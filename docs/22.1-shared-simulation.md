## Experimental physics ownership (#438)

L0 (#442) is a separate explicit equipment selection ([14](14-match-rules.md)).
`shared/item-instances.ts` owns payload/identity admission and deterministic ID
allocation; `shared/equipment.ts` owns instance pickup and rearrangement alongside
the unchanged legacy helpers. `simulation/loot.ts` owns drop planning (`planDrop`,
pure: placement, capacity, mandatory logical overflow and IDs) and
`dropInventorySlot`, the one drop entry point that commits a plan and removes the
slot; `dropEquipment` is legacy emission only. `simulation/interactions.ts` stages
a full-inventory swap on a detached inventory and pickup, plans the outgoing drop
against the real map, and commits both or neither. The ordinary coordinator
orders L0 players lexically and assigns missing carried identities before actions.
Snapshots retain the private allocation counter; live projections omit it and
only the owner receives carried identity. No backend handle or physics dependency
enters the equipment model. P5 owns future item/body attachment.

L1 (#443): `shared/loot-tiers.ts` owns the pure spatial selector (virtual zone,
mix, novelty band), the quality draw, match-creation assignment and the damage
multiplier. `simulation/state.ts` calls the assignment after L0 initialization,
and `simulation/combat.ts` reads the multiplier when firing.

L2 (#444): `simulation/ammo.ts` owns map-weapon magazine seeding, the reload
state machine (`advanceReload`), the ammo-drop intent and death drops of reserves.
`shared/equipment.ts` owns ammo pickup and duplicate-weapon reserve feeding;
`simulation/loot.ts` plans ammo-pickup records with the same bounded placement
(`scatterPoint`) as `planDrop`. The ordinary coordinator calls `advanceReload`
after selection, move and drop and before attack; `combat.ts` refuses attacks
while a reload is in progress. Bots reload through the same input.

`createGame` and default rooms retain the legacy fixed-tick loop. The explicit
async `createPhysicsGame` / server `createPhysicsMatch` factories pin
`last-exit-0.8-physics-1` without a P2 character profile, or the P2 identity in [14](14-match-rules.md) with one, and initialize one Rapier world per match. The server's
optional `matchFactory` composition seam can await that factory; no client command
selects it. Human joins/seat changes reconcile stable body IDs before the first
snapshot. Finished/idle/closed rooms dispose their world; final detached frames
remain readable. A closing service awaits pending engine initialization but does
not wait for a map generator whose promise may never resolve.
The match boundary exposes a pinned, read-only `hasPhysics` capability, selected
by the factory and retained after disposal. Recording compatibility uses that
capability instead of enumerating experimental rules-version strings ([26](26-recording-contract.md)).

`shared/physics/` owns native handles, bounded commands/events, canonical IDs and
contact order, fixed substeps, speed guards, ropes and disposal. Public types are
engine-free. `simulation/phased.ts` owns the separate seven-phase coordinator;
`physics/presentation-geometry.ts` only adapts stored bodies to shared shapes
and anchor radii. `view.ts` owns shape visibility, using `shape.ts` geometry;
the physics adapter has no dependency on view or movement policy.
`physics-runtime.ts` holds each world's private association and captured pending
effects. `physics-geometry.ts` translates shared shape descriptors and playable
boundaries; `physics-projectiles.ts` owns fast swept queries against moving actor
paths and static geometry. Legacy projectile advancement never runs in that mode.

Static obstacle/mask geometry and gate footprints are pinned at creation. Gate
open/closed state commits through the world boundary. P8's
`simulation/dynamic-navigation.ts` overlays observed bodies on local bot steering
([22.6.4](22.6.4-dynamic-overlay.md)); it does not add mutable obstacle
authoring, which still requires a shared revision transaction. P1 uses
existing radii/speeds and experimental bounded motor acceleration (3,600 world
units/second squared); P2 adds optional pinned profiles and floor motor modifiers through `simulation/actor-physics.ts`; [29.1](29.1-physics-design.md) owns those contracts and [14](14-match-rules.md) their experimental numbers. Human feel remains unverified.
The timing/state contract and executed phase traces are [29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md)
and [42.11](42.11-physics-p1.md). No default activation or client prediction is added.

Compatibility exports (`WEAPONS`, `KITS` and the four timing defaults) are
detached copies of the shipped content; named interaction, respawn and name
rules live in `simulation/rules.ts`; `shared/gate-query.ts` owns gate
selection; and `shared/presentation.ts` owns display descriptors. The contract
is [22.7](22.7-content-and-presentation.md).

## Shared simulation

`movement.moveBody` owns SAT sliding for an explicit radius and speed.
`movement.movementSpeed` owns role, boost, stun and sneak tuning for both
coordinators. `movePlayer` uses its default world-units-per-tick result; the
physics motor supplies `HZ` as a base-unit scale before the same modifiers,
preserving each mode's existing floating-point order. `movePlayer` delegates to `moveBody`;
the micro lab passes its selected profile without changing live player state.
`movement.bodyRadius` is a player's collision radius, read by movement and by
anything that places a body.

`simulation/combat.ts` owns `utilityApplies` and the pure `utilityOutcome`:
actual attacks apply that outcome and bot intent collection previews it. Bot
thresholds for *choosing* a utility remain bot policy. Intent collection can
update private routes, pursuits, sliced searches and their per-tick allowance;
it does not apply equipment, healing, shield or drop effects.

`simulation/tick-rules.ts` owns tick/hazard progression, effects decay, player
countdowns, environment damage rules and discovery cadence. The legacy and
physics coordinators call these shared rules at their distinct, versioned
ordering boundaries; the helper extraction does not move those boundaries.

`shared/simulation/dev.ts` holds development commands, which no tick runs on its
own: `teleport` puts an active player's body on a point it can occupy, by that
same radius, and drops a bot's route ([24](24-networking-privacy.md#dev-view)).

Simulation internals live in `shared/simulation/`: `rules` defines constants, `content` owns the tunable set a match is pinned to — the roster, weapon, kit and trap tables, and the tick-denominated rules — and hands each match a frozen copy at creation, so a balance change is a versioned event rather than an ambient one; `state` creates players/games and validates joining, `input` owns what a client may send and the per-player queue a tick spends exactly one of, `rewind` owns recent positions and how far back a given attacker's view ran, `loot` owns events/effects/drops/automatic pickups, `lifecycle` owns elimination/respawn/completion, `combat` owns damage/abilities/weapons/projectiles, `interactions` owns doors/transit/charging/extraction, `bots` owns decisions/navigation, including each bot's private pursuit state (its frontier target, the cells it has given up and the items it has set aside, #230), held off the player so it never reaches a snapshot (its route search is `shared/map/route.ts`, which the Map Lab measures routes with, 53.1; under content flag `navigation: 'tiles'` it plans with `shared/navigation/knowledge.ts` instead, keeping each bot's plan off the player the same way, 22.6), and `visibility` owns gameplay sight and snapshots/projections. Functions receive the existing state/player objects directly. No internal module imports the simulation facade. Dependencies flow from bots to combat to lifecycle to loot; lifecycle never imports combat. Rules and geometry helpers are leaves.

Tick order is part of gameplay: advance time/effects, then iterate players in array order. For each player, attempt respawn, decrement timers, obtain input — one queued input for a client, a fresh decision for a bot, including bot utility use and equipment drops — move, apply slot/move/drop/skill/attack/interact/charge actions, collect loot/reveal at sensors, and apply hazards. Traps, projectiles, and match completion follow the player loop. Extraction preserved that order, mutable state shapes, public exports and wire fields, at the simulation version it was performed under, `last-exit-0.6`. The version has since advanced deliberately; see [24](24-networking-privacy.md).

Power cells are normal unstackable equipment entries with retained charge, not a separate player field. Charging takes 100 ticks. Drop is a one-shot press like interact: spent by the tick that consumes it, and never re-applied by a repeat. Projectile hits can damage other contestants; killed gladiators enter a 400-tick respawning state and return only when a safe transit station is available. These states were introduced under simulation version `last-exit-0.6`; old recordings retain a display fallback for their dedicated cell field.

`shared/recording.ts` owns recording compatibility — the `schema`/`minSchema` pair a header carries and the decision about whether this build can play a given archive — so `room-service` stamping a recording and `client.js` opening one cannot drift apart on it; see [26](26-recording-contract.md). `shared/vector.ts` and `shared/numbers.ts` are dependency-free leaves: the first owns world-point distance, which map generation, routing, hazards and the simulation had each defined separately; the second owns the numeric guards that client input is validated through. Both are re-exported by `simulation/geometry` and `map/context` so existing consumers import them from the module that owns their stage.

`shared/math.ts` owns every non-exact function the simulation uses: `sin`, `cos`, `atan`, `atan2` and two-argument `hypot` (#566). Engines may round `Math.sin` and the like differently, and V8 does on ARM and x86. The server and every client must compute the same bits, so the module is written with exact operations only (`+ - * /` and `Math.sqrt`): ports of fdlibm, and V8's own `hypot` algorithm. On x86 V8 the results equal `Math.*` bit for bit, so the frozen characterization fixture did not move. `tests/math.test.js` forbids transcendental `Math.*` and non-literal `**` elsewhere in `shared/`, and pins a digest of output bits that must hold on every platform. Rapier runs as WASM, which has no fused multiply-add and carries its own trig, so physics already agreed on ARM. A Rust port must reproduce these bits, and these tests are its first acceptance check ([27](27-rust-path.md)).

`shared/discovery.ts` owns contestant map discovery: the coarse 192-unit grid over map dimensions (`map.width` x `map.height`), line-of-sight polygon testing, authoritative state on contestants, the split between geometry learned from the view window and dynamic state learned by line of sight, the landmark beacon radius, a bot's line-of-sight cells and items (`memory.seen`, `memory.items`, #230), keyframe-then-changes delta wire encoding, frame fold reconstruction, and client decoding (#275). Gladiators bypass discovery memory. Simulation content flag `discovery` enables it for `content-2` through `content-5`; `content-1` keeps `discovery: false` specifically to preserve the frozen characterization fixture `behavior-nav-1.json.gz` projections unchanged.

## Frames

A published frame shares what did not change since the previous frame instead of copying it (#655). The
design and its stages are on #655; the measurements are [42.22](42.22-tick-cost-attribution.md). Stage 1
covers the ground items:

- `simulation/ground-items.ts` owns the ground list's representation. `snapshot` publishes `s.map.items`
  by reference, after freezing any record not yet frozen and marking the list as published; an unchanged
  tick freezes and copies nothing. The list is marked rather than frozen, because V8 runs `filter` and
  `JSON.stringify` over a frozen array more slowly: at 36x18 that cost the per-item scans about 1.4 ms a tick
  (42.22). The rest of the frame is still cloned.
- A rule never edits a published record or list. `addItems` appends, `removeItem` takes one record out and
  `replaceItem` puts a new record in place of an old one; each copies a published list first, pointers only.
  `groundList(s)` returns the list for any other edit and drops the indexes below. Pickups edit a `draftItem`
  copy and keep it only when `settledItem` finds that it changed, so a refused pickup leaves the shared record
  alone. Map setup, before the first frame, still edits in place.
- `itemsNear(s, at, reach)` returns the records that could be within `reach`, in list order, from a 64-unit
  cell grid kept per list beside the id index (#641). It is a superset: the caller's own distance test still
  decides, and a record with a non-finite position is always offered. Automatic pickups and `interact` read
  it, so their cost follows what is near rather than the map. List order is restored from a sequence number
  each record gets on entry and its replacement keeps, which sorts as positions do because the list only
  appends, removes and replaces in place. Only frozen records sit in cells; one not yet published may still
  move in place, so it waits in a bucket every query reads. Each record also sits in a 256-unit cell, which a
  query wider than eight fine cells reads instead, so a viewer's reach touches about 200 cells rather than
  thousands.
- A player's view takes its items through `framedItems(s, frame.items, viewer, POTENTIAL, inRange)`: exactly
  `frame.items.filter(inRange)`, in list order, but testing only the grid's candidates. The sight rule in
  `shared/view.ts` and `inRange` still decide; the grid only narrows, so it cannot gain or lose a viewer
  anything (`tests/view-items.test.js`). A frame whose item poses differ from the ground (physics) is built
  by `poseItems`, which notes the swapped records so the query still reads the ground's grid and offers every
  swapped record. Any other list, such as an older frame, is filtered whole. The directed view is unculled.
- `groundItem(s, id)` finds a record by id through an index kept per list, so physics reads item bodies
  without walking the ground. `writePhysicalItemPoses` walks the item bodies and replaces a record only
  when its body moved. The record keeps its `x`/`y`, because frames and recordings carry them.
- A frame's items are therefore read-only. A caller that needs to change one copies it first; writing to a
  frozen record throws. Nothing stops a push onto a frame's list, so a caller arranging a frame gives it a
  new list instead. A pushed list would also record stale, through the kept JSON below.
- `itemsJson(list)` is `JSON.stringify(list)`, kept per published or posed list and per frozen record (#655
  stage 3). The replay writer composes each frame line from the state's keys in order, with the ground from
  `itemsJson`, so the recorded bytes and digest are what `JSON.stringify({ state, commands })` gives (26) and
  an unchanged ground costs nothing to serialize. Any other list is serialized whole.

Players, bodies, gates, traps, projectiles and effects are still copied every frame (#655 stage 2), and the
recording serializes them every tick.

P5's `simulation/world-physics.ts` owns physical ground-item reconciliation,
authored crate admission, capacity preflight and centre-sampled floor drag.
`simulation/explosion-physics.ts` owns static-cover-tested, next-boundary blast
impulses independently of damage. The opt-in identity, tuning and lifecycle rules
are owned by [14](14-match-rules.md) and [29.1](29.1-physics-design.md); networking
and engine-free presentation remain [29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md).

P6's `simulation/hook.ts` owns swept flight, pending attachment, accumulated
pull targets and cancellation. `physics/constraints.ts` owns descriptor validation
and anchor-force calculation; `physics/world.ts` applies those bounded reciprocal
forces at each substep. Public ability records stay behind the existing
`physics-presentation.ts` / `projection-contract.ts` authorization boundary.
Tuning belongs to [14](14-match-rules.md), not renderer or backend defaults.
