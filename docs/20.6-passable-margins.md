## Purpose and invariants

Automatically keep a clear margin around guaranteed-passable segments, then
allow themed geometry to fill intentionally up to the resulting fill boundary.
This permits denser fills close to portals. A builder receives the original
region boundary and portals as well as the reduced fill boundary.

Every original portal must remain reachable from every other original portal
by a hunter, with the required crossing coverage. Clear approaches do not excuse
a missing connection. Opposite portals separated by a wall must fail the
accessibility check. The region's interior, core elements and loot remain its
builder's business; this policy adds no general requirement to reach them.

The new fill perimeter is **not guaranteed passable**. Builders are encouraged
to place content deliberately up to it, according to their theme. This does
not require a wall. A type can intentionally leave more space empty. The new
perimeter creates neither external portals nor further automatic margins.

Contracts remain promises and SDK validation remains elective
([51.1](51.1-principles-and-names.md)). Automatic fill constraints reduce reliance
on every builder inventing its own clearance rule; they do not introduce uniform
output enforcement, geometry repair or a whole-map flood to replace inference.

## Guaranteed segments and the clear footprint

Work from **resolved segment guarantees**, not literal authored values. Explicit
`passable` and `any` meeting `any` get the same treatment. A hint, unstated value
or other segment without a guarantee contributes no margin of its own.

The clear margin is a deterministic union of whole cells around all guaranteed
segments, including segments internal to a same-class region. Extend each run
one cell on both sides and one cell past each endpoint:

- A horizontal run from `(x, y)` to `(x + length, y)` contributes cells in
  columns `x - 1` through `x + length` and rows `y - 1` and `y`.
- A vertical run from `(x, y)` to `(x, y + length)` contributes cells in
  columns `x - 1` and `x` and rows `y - 1` through `y + length`.

These are cell-aligned end caps. Deriving the same union per unit segment gives
the same result, so grouping or splitting a collinear run does not alter its
clear footprint. Union overlapping contributions, clip to playable cells and
order cells canonically. Reapplying an existing footprint has no further effect.

Both sides of a shared portal receive their portions of the footprint as fill
exclusions. An endpoint can also affect a third owner; that owner receives its
portion too. Opposing hints do not reclaim automatically cleared cells.

Map and mask boundaries supply no crossing guarantee and no usable outside
space. A margin touching them is clipped; access checks cannot pretend the
clipped-away cells exist. The shared scale in [52](52-map-primitives-and-library.md)
has a 0.925-cell hunter clearance radius. Straight one-cell margins on both
sides provide a two-cell corridor. Endpoint cells matter: side strips alone
can leave a corner too narrow for that body to turn.

## Information passed to a builder

Keep the following meanings distinct in the kernel contract (`cells`,
`portals`, `internalGuaranteed`, `fillCells` and `clearCells`):

| Information | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Original owned cells, boundary and external portals | Identity, ownership and original access obligations |
| Internal guaranteed runs | Preserve passability requirements that do not form external portals |
| Reduced fill cells and their perimeter | Geometry placement; newly exposed edges have no passage guarantee |
| Known-clear access context | The automatically cleared space needed to plan and check access around guaranteed runs |

The known-clear context can extend across an original portal. It contains
contractually cleared space, not assumptions about another builder's arbitrary
interior. Accessibility uses the original region together with that context;
geometry placement uses the reduced fill area. A check that still requires the
entire hunter to fit inside the original cells defeats the denser-fill policy.
Original portal stands/crossings must therefore be adapted to this context,
rather than mechanically retaining their old full-body inward offset. A stand
sits on the seam, its body in the clear margin on both sides, so a clear stand
is also the crossing: a wall on the boundary overlaps it.

A brief that arrives without margins, built by hand rather than by macro,
derives them from its own portals and internal runs (`withClearMargins` in
`map/kernel/margins.ts`). It knows only its cells and the one-cell strip across
each portal, so it leaves out end caps past a portal's span: nothing says that
space is playable. Macro's briefs keep the margins macro derived. Every builder
therefore has one path.

Original ownership is retained even when the fill is empty or split. Do not
silently turn disconnected clear pieces into one connected region, duplicate
core quotas, or lose an obligation when a child has no remaining fill cells.

## Same-class aggregation and internal requirements

Macro groups side-connected cells with one resolved class before deriving
portals ([51.5](51.5-regions.md)). It does not join existing regions through
historical portals. Its resolved segment data already retains internal
guarantees, so no historical portal registry is needed.

Derive internal guaranteed runs from segments whose two cells have the same
final owner, and external portals from guaranteed boundary stretches between
different owners. This naturally regroups external remnants. Corners remain
separate straight runs; short external fragments still undergo the external
portal validity rule. Internal runs retain their clear footprint without
being promoted to external portals or acquiring external portal-pair semantics.
Changing the external minimum portal length is not part of this policy.

The new internal requirement supersedes the old assumption that same-region
guarantees impose no obligation (17.2.5 M8). Authors can now use internal
passable segments to guide passability through a larger aggregate fill.

## Decomposition and inference

Each child handoff carries its original boundary/portals and allowed fill area,
with inherited clear constraints. Genuine child portals introduce their own
clear footprint. Already inherited constraints are applied idempotently.

A parent's internal run stays internal to a child only where that child owns
both its sides. Where a child boundary splits it, that piece is internal to no
child and does not become a child portal. Nothing is lost: internal runs carry
no pair-connectivity, and its footprint, the one obligation it carries, stays
in the inherited clearance. `block` keeps lots two cells off each portal, not
one: a lot attached to the boundary beside a portal would otherwise leave the
alley a single cell wide past the portal's end cap.

Clear margins are constraints on existing ownership, not ordinary themed child
regions that generate more portals. The parent and children keep their original
connectivity promises. Existing macro region-graph inference can remain valid:
each region still connects all its portals using its interior and guaranteed
clear space. No weaker disconnected-approach policy is introduced.

## Implementation and acceptance route

#313 owns kernel and brief derivation, SDK access/placement helpers, strategy and
child-builder adaptation, Map Lab inspection and diagnosis, and compatibility
decisions. Its issue contains the staged implementation brief and acceptance
cases. Follow [31](31-verification.md) and [53](53-map-artifacts-and-tools.md).

Key acceptance witnesses include a shared two-cell corridor at the same hunter
radius used by actual movement; the disconnected-wall base case; turning with
and without endpoint clearance; internal seams across tiles; empty/split fill;
and nested child handoffs without repeated inset or fabricated portals.
Full map samples must honor the same contract, not just hand-built fixtures.
Every registered builder meets it over briefs made as the chain makes them, with
cleared strips inside the owned cells, in `tests/micro-builder-contract.test.js`
([20.9](20.9-builder-structure.md), #540).

Derived semantics use macro version 3. The margin checkpoint used `types-22`
(`types-21` until `block` restored its two-cell approach), and `types-23`
followed after depot route tuning (#325, 20.3.4). Later builder changes advanced
it further; [53](53-map-artifacts-and-tools.md#current-compatibility-domains)
owns the current value and its source declaration.
macro-version-2 saves are refused by name. JSON/BSON round-trips recompute the
new brief views. The #313 content checkpoint pins the unchanged authored library
under this policy. #214's authored shapes/tuning and final content checkpoint
are merged (52, 53); preserve the frozen characterization fixture named in
[31](31-verification.md#the-frozen-fixture).

Builders keep core siting in original owned cells; access planning can use
known-clear context. Clear margins exclude collision geometry, not pickups.
When a themed fill is too fragmented for its structures, its remaining fill
patches can still offer zone loot. `open` continues to offer none. Compact huts
can use the existing single-room design in 4×3, 3×4 or 3×3 cells. Rubble and
factory scatter retain their original-area eligibility and test individual
geometry against the reduced fill. Internal tile guarantees can intentionally
split larger buildings or remove pipe connections; #214 authorship must account
for them. No library prescriptions are weakened by #313.
