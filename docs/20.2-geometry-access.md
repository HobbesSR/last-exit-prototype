# 20.2. Geometry and access

Back to [20](20-micro-generation.md).

For chain briefs, [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md) defines automatic
guaranteed-clear margins, reduced geometry fill and known-clear access context.
Original portals and owned cells retain their identities and obligations.

## Geometry and reachability

Region boundaries emit nothing. They are ownership boundaries, not automatic
walls or fences, and a portal is never walled. A contestant-only squeeze is 34
world units in the live profile and 1.5 cells in the cell profile.
An opening permits crossing, while an actual door remains an interactable object.
Roofs belong to room assemblies, never to a whole concave region bounding box.

A strategy that protects routes while it builds joins its portals inside the mask
first. The local planner tries direct visibility, then a bounded half-cell search.
Every edge is checked as a swept disc: two circles and a convex strip, using
`shape.ts`'s SAT dispatch. It cannot accept a thin wall between clear endpoints.
Routes and entrance approaches are protected from subsequent placements.
This is a conservative sampled planner: finding a path establishes a continuous
clear route; failure does not prove no continuous route exists.

Start nodes remain within three half-cell steps of the entrance. Their selection
enumerates only a local grid collar (#722), retaining the original exact distance
filter, row-major candidates and stable distance tie ordering. The exported
`routeStarts` helper is shared by the planner and direct equivalence tests.
It does not scan
the whole region to discover that neighbourhood. Clearance, endpoint connectors
and subsequent search order are unchanged.

`validatePortalReach(input, { search: 'thorough' })` optionally retries missed
grid joins through the shared free-space mesh (#644), including adjacent stands
along a long portal. The default remains `grid`. Successful grid routes are
retained. A mesh is built lazily, at most once per call, from final blockers and
the access mask, keeping shared erosion defaults. Endpoint attachment searches
within one cell of each original seam stand; every stand and every path segment,
including endpoint leads, must pass the same full-radius `travelClear` proof.
This bounded attachment may miss a valid route, especially near conservative
erosion corners; it never grants extra clearance or outside access.

The result adds `joins` (endpoints, points and `direct`, `grid`, `mesh` or `none`
discovery provenance) and `meshBuilds`. These include along-portal joins;
`routes` retains its inter-portal meaning. Existing error wording is retained;
an unreachable/divided search report means no witness found, not proof of physical
impossibility. Neither search promises a globally shortest path or universal speed
advantage. The fewer-than-two exemption and malformed-input checks are unchanged.

The builder canvas applies whole-assembly placement atomically. Every collider,
roof footprint and reservation must fit the owned cells. Containment checks the
whole shape against missing cells, so a diagonal body cannot bridge a hole merely
because its vertices are inside. Convex polygons and off-grid rotation are supported;
concave collider decomposition and elevation remain separate work. Rectangular
architecture can select contained subrectangles without forcing the whole region
to be rectangular. Invalid placements do not consume IDs or leave half a building.

Loot follows each cell's zone (52, "Tier zones"): at most one site per cell, clear
of every piece. A constrained region may place fewer, and the result reports the
actual count. Routes through rooms assume unlocked doors can be opened; preview
collision starts with doors closed. There is no claim to validate dynamic
player-created obstructions.

The public interface is bounded by `limits.ts`: a region of up to 65,536 cells and
512 per axis, with up to 4,096 portals of up to 512 segments each. These are tool
limits, not desired match dimensions. The region bounds were raised from 4,096 cells and 64 per
axis for macro's regions, whose open ground can be a whole map wide (17 M7, 51 C2).
One check, `checkedRegionCells`, holds them, and `buildRegion` applies it to every
brief before any strategy runs (20.1).
These are agent-selected defaults from #93 (`000584f`), chosen as headroom over
the then-current roughly 29,500-cell fixture, not measured maximum resource
budgets or user requirements. #330 removes the unrelated ±512 absolute-address
ceiling: translating a fixed region does not increase its work. Coordinates
must be safe integers with representable bounded run/margin offsets. Regional
count, span and portal limits remain unchanged; revising them needs workload
and resource evidence rather than fitting another map by increasing a constant.

### Addressed area

`createAddressedArea(mask)` in `map/micro/addressed.ts`, exported by the SDK,
is a builder's record of the areas it has addressed with intent (#404): what
it built into, and what it chose to leave as it is. It is not occupancy or
reachability, so a deliberately empty court is addressed. Areas are
`shape.ts` shapes (`mark`, or atomic batches with `markAll`) or whole-cell squares (`markCells`, all or none).
Two areas never overlap; an overlapping one is refused, records nothing and
returns `false`. Several areas may still share a cell. A cell has only two
states: unaddressed, touched by no area, or addressed by any area overlapping
it with positive area (touching an edge does not count). So a sliver keeps its
cell with that cell's owner when the rest is offered for delegation
([20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md) D2). Areas reaching past ownership address
only owned cells, but conflict checks cover the complete recorded shapes,
including portions outside ownership. Edge, point and curved tangency do not
count as overlap; the area checks use `shapesShareArea` (`geometry.ts`), `shape.ts`'s `sharesArea` with
the geometry tolerance of `1e-7` world units for penetration. Collision contact semantics are unchanged.
`unaddressed()` and `unaddressedComponents()` follow owned
cell order; the components use the `unusedComponents` walk. The record is pure,
with no random draws or IDs.

Hut and compound mark explicitly: their placeable body and clear demand (20.8), remaining bands and
reserved routes, then offer unaddressed components (20.7). Their placed pieces also commit
through the production canvas below, as cover and its post-filters, depot, park, checkpoint,
hall, ruins, rubble and plant (#490) do ([20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md)).
It differs from `measureFill` (20.3), which counts cells no obstacle touches
after building.

### Production canvas

`createBuilderCanvas(brief, protectedShapes)` in `map/micro/canvas.ts`, exported
by the SDK, places region-2 assemblies through `commit({ elements, areas?, access? })`.
Elements are already positioned. A commit checks that geometry, addressed shapes,
footprints, roofs, reserves and spots lie in the reduced fill. Only what stands (geometry,
reserves and spots) is kept off the supplied protections: roofs only conceal sight
([15](15-information-rules.md)), so a route may pass beneath one, and intent is not
occupancy, so it may include a route. A commit rejects geometry that shares area with an
earlier assembly's (`shapesShareArea`, as for intent), so separate assemblies may stand flush:
a pipe may end on its machine's surface (#489). Rapier colliders that only touch cause no
problem ([29.1](29.1-physics-design.md)). Protections keep collision semantics, contact included: what
stands, a reserve or access shape is kept off them by `shapesOverlap`, as `fits` is. A commit
checks optional standing/swept access shapes against the access mask and geometry.
Committed access shapes protect later placements. Route discovery and portal proof
remain the builder's responsibility; the canvas does not search or retry.

Siting asks the canvas rather than keeping its own ledger (#460). `fits(shape)` says
whether a shape lies in the reduced fill and off every protection, committed access and
reserves included. `spaced(shape, clearance)` says whether the shape's bounds, grown by
`clearance`, stay clear of every committed extent. Extents default to each element's
template box; an assembly of world-absolute records passes `extents`, its pieces' shapes,
since its template boxes span the region. Clearance between pieces, such as depot's and
cover's `AISLE`, is a relation between them rather than intent, so `spaced` never reads
the addressed record. Neither query draws, searches or changes the canvas, and a strategy
still creates its canvas only after its early returns and protections.

Every successful commit records nonempty addressed intent. By default it uses each
element's template box; a builder may supply individual colliders, a convex hull,
or other nonoverlapping world-space shapes that express its intent. Intent is not
an occupancy mask and need not trace every collider. Parts within one atomic assembly
may overlap. `markEmpty(areas)` explicitly addresses intentionally empty fill, including
protected routes. Failed commits leave both elements and addressed areas unchanged.
The canvas owns copies of committed inputs and allocates no IDs or random draws.
Its elements retain commit and part order; the strategy still owns loot and manifests.

The example builders' own `BuilderContext` retired with them (17.2.6 M36, #483), so
the canvas is the one placement contract.

### Segment requirements across hierarchy levels

A child is a brief like any other: it gets passability obligations only, with no
ceilings and no seals ([51](51-generation-chain.md) stage 6). Brief decomposition
carries the parent's portals whole to their owners and pairs each new crossing
(`inheritBriefMargins`, `prepareDelegation`). `validateBriefPartition` then checks the
partition by inference, assuming each child keeps its own portal promise. Every two
children that share a boundary need a crossing, and the crossings must join them all
(20.7). Geometry stays an elective, separate check (`validatePortalReach`).

The SDK's earlier floor/ceiling toolkit (`resolveAccessRequirements`,
`validateRegionAccess`, `inheritBoundaryPorts`, `validateBoundaryComposition`) worked
on walled `RegionPort`s, sealed runs included. Its last users were the example builders
and the decomposition demo, and it retired with them (#483).
