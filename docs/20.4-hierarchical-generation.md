## Production boundary

Production builders use brief children and builder delegation
([20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md)). `block` primarily uses its guillotine planner;
when no lot is selected, it calls the flat `allocateCandidates` machinery for at
most one smaller lot. Its work limits and physical circulation admission are
owned by [20.3.5](20.3.5-district-and-core-element-builders.md). No production
builder imports the tree explorer or the example policy.

The #610 alongside comparison was a separate opt-in multi-lot block policy,
not adoption of this tree explorer. It did not justify replacing guillotine;
#929 retired that policy and its comparison runner. Measurements and provenance
remain in
[42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md#alongside-sdk-block-planning-comparison-610--535-2026-10-27).

## Experimental inspection tooling

`decomposition/explore.ts` and `decomposition/example.ts` remain runnable research
and development inspection tools. The Decomposition Lab's Tree panel
(`public/decomposition-tree-panel.js`) calls the explorer; its Flat panel and
`tools/decompose-region.mjs` use the example policy. Their unit tests preserve
structural ownership, deterministic search and work-budget contracts. They emit
allocation artifacts, not built geometry. Keeping these consumers does not
schedule production promotion or general recursive execution.

The associated helpers have different consumers:

- `findNeckCuts`, `growRegion` and `validateDecompositionPlan` support these
  inspection tools. `shortestCellPath` and the `createWorkBudget` convenience
  factory are retained experimental utilities exercised only by analysis tests.
  They are not required production generation stages.
- `measureState` is part of production allocation scoring, despite having no
  external production caller. `interfaces.ts` also participates in that scoring;
  neither is an unused research-only module.
- The explorer's `generatorFitness` metric is an objective heuristic tied to the
  example generator IDs and weights. It is separate from the allocator's contract
  feasibility/utility function of the same name; changing either score changes
  search results. Per-branch non-budget failures currently leave terminal choices;
  this experimental failure policy is not a production error-handling contract.

### Bounded hierarchical exploration

`decomposition/explore.ts` is an experimental tree explorer over the example
policy and flat allocator. Each refinement stores its local plan and partitions its parent exactly;
assigned pieces and residuals can be considered for further refinement. Reserved
and forbidden ground stays terminal. Retaining a piece is an explicit alternative
to subdividing it. Whole-footprint no-op refinements are rejected.

The lab retains a small portfolio under Balanced usefulness, Smaller room groups,
or Preserve large shapes and holes. Objectives score terminal ownership, rather
than double-counting both a parent and its children. The lab's Tree mode keeps its
objective, depth, alternative and branch selection
when switching to Flat inspection and back; Flat retains its own policy, view and
selection. Both use the active central preview with the relevant inspector beside
it. Each selected branch exposes its depth and reason for stopping or refinement. The example proposal family adds
midpoint rectangle splits to the existing room/courtyard/lobe proposals; this is
not a universal rectangle decomposition rule.

Search depth is capped at three, retained alternatives at eight and expansions at
64 (lab defaults: two, four and 24). A `decomposition-exploration-1` export preserves
the retained trees and named score components, with explicit non-optimal search
diagnostics. This explores structural allocation hierarchies, not recursive physical
generation. General recursive dispatch and automatic portal negotiation are
unimplemented; the production macro-to-brief pipeline is separate.

The explorer shares a counted work budget across nested context, analysis,
example allocation and validation work (#605, 20.3.2). On exhaustion it returns
the last fully evaluated frontier, or reports an error if it cannot establish
the initial owner. Its search diagnostics include work used and limit alongside
the separate expansion count.

### Physical realization (retired)

Until #483, a demonstration turned flat plans into geometry. `negotiatePortals`
gave each interface between two children a walled `RegionPort` crossing, with a
passage floor and ceiling, and `sealed` or `unstated` runs elsewhere.
`executeDecomposition` dispatched each piece to an example builder and composed
the pieces through `composeMicroRegions`. `realizeDecomposition` mapped rooms to
depots, courtyards to courtyards, lobes to ruins and corridors to empty entry
ground. Its `realized-decomposition-1` artifact, previewed by
`decomposition/preview.ts`, was the generation demo's default example.

It retired with the example builders and `micro-1` (17.2.6 M36). Its seals and
ceilings predate the rule that children get passability obligations only (51.8,
2026-09-29), and no production type used it. Real decomposition runs through
briefs instead: `block` writes its children's portals itself, and builder
delegation (20.7) prepares and checks brief children (`prepareDelegation`,
`validateBriefPartition`). The experimental planner above remains an inspection tool.
A production consumer would need explicit brief-child conversion, physical access
obligations and validation; its existence does not provide generic recursive
physical execution or automatic portal negotiation.
