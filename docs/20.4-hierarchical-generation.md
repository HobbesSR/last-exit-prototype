## Combined decomposition and generation

### Bounded hierarchical exploration

`decomposition/explore.ts` is a strategy-level tree explorer alongside the flat
allocator. Each refinement stores its local plan and partitions its parent exactly;
assigned pieces and residuals can be considered for further refinement. Reserved
and forbidden ground stays terminal. Retaining a piece is an explicit alternative
to subdividing it. Whole-footprint no-op refinements are rejected.

The lab retains a small portfolio under Balanced usefulness, Smaller room groups,
or Preserve large shapes and holes. Objectives score terminal ownership, rather
than double-counting both a parent and its children. The lab's Tree mode keeps its
objective, depth, alternative and branch selection
when switching to Flat inspection and back; Flat retains its own policy, view and
selection. Both use the active central preview with the relevant inspector beside
it. Each selected branch exposes its depth and reason for stopping or refinement. The example proposal family adds
midpoint rectangle splits to the existing room/courtyard/lobe proposals; this is
not a universal rectangle decomposition rule.

Search depth is capped at three, retained alternatives at eight and expansions at
64 (lab defaults: two, four and 24). A `decomposition-exploration-1` export preserves
the retained trees and named score components, with explicit non-optimal search
diagnostics. This explores structural allocation hierarchies, not recursive physical
generation. General dispatch, portal negotiation and macro integration remain separate.

The explorer shares a counted work budget across nested context, analysis,
example allocation and validation work (#605, 20.3.2). On exhaustion it returns
the last fully evaluated frontier, or reports an error if it cannot establish
the initial owner. Its search diagnostics include work used and limit alongside
the separate expansion count.

### Physical realization (retired)

Until #483, a demonstration turned flat plans into geometry. `negotiatePortals`
gave each interface between two children a walled `RegionPort` crossing, with a
passage floor and ceiling, and `sealed` or `unstated` runs elsewhere.
`executeDecomposition` dispatched each piece to an example builder and composed
the pieces through `composeMicroRegions`. `realizeDecomposition` mapped rooms to
depots, courtyards to courtyards, lobes to ruins and corridors to empty entry
ground. Its `realized-decomposition-1` artifact, previewed by
`decomposition/preview.ts`, was the generation demo's default example.

It retired with the example builders and `micro-1` (17.2.6 M36). Its seals and
ceilings predate the rule that children get passability obligations only (51.8,
2026-09-29), and no production type used it. Real decomposition runs through
briefs instead: `block` writes its children's portals itself, and builder
delegation (20.7) prepares and checks brief children (`prepareDelegation`,
`validateBriefPartition`). The planner above stays. A region type that builds
from its plans would hand each piece on as a brief child in the same way. That is
the next general boundary (20.5). The bounded production `block` pilot (#580,
20.3.5) now consumes a flat SDK allocation when its ordinary planner selects no
lots, retaining its existing child briefs, paired frontages and dispatch. Generic
recursive physical execution and automatic portal negotiation remain unimplemented.
