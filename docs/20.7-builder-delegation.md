## Recommended starting point

A builder may offer explicit, whole-cell patches it cannot use to named child
strategies. It keeps the rest, including intentional open space. The SDK prepares
ownership, paired portals and inherited constraints; a micro executor dispatches
the children and gathers their output. The containing builder still owns its
original portal promise.

Start with **hut yard patches delegated to cover** as the first consumer (D10).
Recursive patch splitting is depth-bounded, with remaining patches given to
the terminal empty builder at the limit (D6); the default depth is three.
Keep the house, its doorstep, circulation
and original portals in the retained area. This is optional authorship, not an
automatic operation on every empty rectangle. An infeasible patch proposal can
leave its cells retained. If a region cannot fulfill its work by building and
splitting, it may hand the complete region only to the empty builder (D7).
The accepted pilot sequence is not evidence that
every hut has suitable spare ground or that cover is always its best theme.

For example, a hut in a broad, concave yard could retain its house and a hunter-wide
route joining its external portals, then offer a side lobe to cover. Cover receives
that lobe's ownership and a paired crossing to the yard. A narrow strip serving as
the house's doorstep stays with the hut even if it contains no obstacle.

## Decisions to review separately

| Decision | Recommended default | Benefit | Cost / useful alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1. Who identifies spare space? **Accepted** | Only the owning builder identifies patches it cannot fill. Offers may carry builder-defined reason tags, available for debugging and optionally to the child. | Preserves builder intent and explains relinquishment without requiring child-specific tuning. | Each builder authors its patch selection and tag vocabulary; child use of tags is optional. |
| D2. Ownership unit **Accepted** | Disjoint whole-cell patches plus retained cells exactly partition original ownership. A patch must meet its receiving builder's requirements; a default empty builder handles irregular/awkward shapes. | Reuses masks, zones, margins and interface tools; nothing disappears. | Sub-cell slivers stay with their cell owner. Irregular whole-cell footprints do not require shape-level ownership. |
| D3. When to settle the handoff? **Accepted** | Each builder decides timing. The child must be buildable entirely from its interface; all necessary collaboration details belong there. | Supports different builder workflows while keeping children independent of adjacent regions' internals and origins. | The offering builder must supply complete, compatible input. A planning/finalization split is an optional technique, not a universal requirement. |
| D4. Who chooses child content? **Accepted** | The delegating builder decides, optionally informed by information passed in from the original decomposer. | Preserves builder authorship while allowing decomposer intent to inform the choice; the SDK stays about mechanics. | Builders own their selection policy. Guidance representation and any per-builder allowlist remain design details, not accepted requirements. |
| D5. Access responsibility **Accepted** | Preserve the original macro region's reachability obligations through decomposition and any later builder-created patches. Descendant portals retain the required reachability relationships through composed interfaces. | Keeps macro's region-graph inference valid regardless of partition history. | Local child connectivity alone is insufficient if the partition disconnects original relationships. The proposed pilot routing and crossing selection below are implementation choices, not separately accepted rules. |
| D6. Recursion **Direction accepted; default selected** | Limit patch splitting; further patches at the limit go to empty, which always succeeds and never splits. Default max depth3, initial builder depth0, configurable. Initial decomposition is separate. No four-child cap. | Terminates recursive delegation with explicit owned empty output. | A depth bound alone is not a total work budget; separate measured work limits would need their own justification. |
| D7. Unable to fulfill region work **Accepted** | A region may fully delegate when building and splitting cannot fulfill its work. Complete-region handoffs go only to the empty builder. | Provides a terminal fallback without full-region delegation loops. | The complete region may end empty. Proper-subset patches can use other content builders; full-region retries between content builders are excluded for now. |
| D8. Core sites and loot **Accepted** | Defer loot policy to its overhaul. Add no special core delegation machinery now; genuinely incompatible core builders fall back to empty, leaving required-feature checks to post-generation validation. | Keeps delegation focused and exposes missing mechanical features through final checks. | An empty fallback can leave a map without required sites. Existing reports diagnose; live admission rejects missing essentials. A universal generation-failure channel remains unspecified. |
| D9. Result representation **Direction accepted; starting format selected** | Recover details from a versioned owning-layer snapshot of inputs, semantic decisions/outcomes and nested generation objects. Derive views; preserve algorithm versions. | Supports inspection and replay without duplicating the hierarchy in every downstream result. | Flat geometry alone cannot recover historical decisions. The concrete API/encoding must demonstrate complete recovery. |
| D10. Adoption order **Accepted** | Mechanics, then one hut-to-cover pilot, then one measured builder at a time. | Reveals actual value without changing every strategy together. | Slower broad coverage; each adoption gets its own measured review. |

## Handoff mechanics and remaining execution design

### Implemented preparation contract (#328)

`prepareDelegation(parent, offers, policy)` in `map/micro/delegation.ts`, exported
by the SDK, prepares briefs without dispatching a builder. Each offer supplies a
nonempty unique `key`, connected whole `cells`, explicit `type`, optional scalar
`parameters`, `reasonTags` and `guidance`. The caller's pure `receiving(brief,
offer)` policy accepts with `null` or returns a nonempty infeasibility reason.
It sees the complete inherited interface, including the final clearance and
portals. Preparation rechecks all receiving policies when another candidate
would change their interfaces. There is no SDK import of the builder registry
or macro application code.

If adding offer B would invalidate an already accepted A's receiving interface,
B is rejected with the reason `Receiving builder for A: ...`. The reason names
the affected receiver; it need not name the rejected offer's own receiver.
Preparation rebuilds the partition for each candidate and may make quadratic
receiving-policy calls in the number of offers. The #330 pilot measured those
calls for the hut workloads; its results are recorded in
[42](42-performance-history.md). This is not a new work cap.

Malformed ownership (duplicates, overlap, outside cells), keys and interface
input throw. Policy exceptions and invalid policy return values also throw;
they are not disguised as ordinary patch rejection. Canonical key order resolves
competing feasible offers. A refused offer remains retained and is reported in
`rejected`; accepted `children`, `retained` and `crossings` account for every
original cell exactly once. There is no child-count cap. The helper uses the
brief SDK limits, including 65,536 owned cells, rather than the structural
explorer's smaller limits. Complete-region empty replacement is handled by the executor below;
preparation rejects a patch that would leave no retained ownership.

The current preparation supports one connected retained owner. Its default
`externalPortals: 'retained'` keeps all original runs there; the explicit
`'whole-child'` policy can transfer a whole run to exactly one child. Neither
policy permits splitting, shortening or dropping a run. Retained **ownership**
connectivity does not imply retained-only **portal routing**: a child's local
portal promise may participate in the original region's route.

`validateBriefPartition(parent, localBriefs)` checks exact ownership, whole
external inheritance, matching opposing paired crossings, a crossing on every
adjacent owner pair, and the connected interface graph. It applies equally to
an initial decomposition and each later patch split. Rechecking each level, or
the flattened leaf partition against the original brief, preserves the original
macro obligations by inference from the leaves' portal promises. It does not
validate final collision geometry or guarantee passage through arbitrary
interior points. `validatePortalReach` and `validateFillConstraints` remain
elective geometry checks.

Crossings come from kernel `boundaryRuns`, longest eligible straight run first,
ties by axis, row and column. The centered minimum hunter-wide run uses kernel
scale; `portalStands` and swept-body checks against declared playable/clear
context test crossing feasibility, including both sides and clipping at ends.
The caller can declare `protectedCells`, `protectedRuns` (reserved clear
footprints), and already placed `blockers`. Offers cannot take reserved cells;
new crossing footprints cannot claim them, and placed geometry must remain in
retained fill. This supports plan-first and post-placement handoffs through
explicit input alone. Original guarantees are applied before clipping into
children; `inheritBriefMargins`, with the retained owner included, then carries
fill/clear constraints, third-owner endpoint effects and internal guarantees
idempotently. Empty or split fill does not change owned cells or obligations.
Zones preserve tier, bonus and chance; only original hints are clipped to each
new owner. Child parameters are explicit, and core quotas stay with the parent.

Advice travels in the child's `RegionBrief`: `guidance` is a scalar record
supplied explicitly by the offer; parent guidance stays with the delegating
builder unless it deliberately forwards that advice. This keeps one builder's
advice vocabulary from reaching a different builder implicitly. `delegation`
contains the offering `sourceBuilder` and sorted unique `reasonTags`. Both are
advisory input, not access policy. Children need no adjacent implementation
state. These optional fields change no existing brief or builder output.

Child IDs use `${parent.id}/delegate/${encodeURIComponent(key)}`; invalid Unicode
keys are refused. Automatically offered footprints can use `patchFootprintKey`,
which encodes all canonical row-major `x,y` coordinates after `cells:`, separated
by semicolons. This full encoding avoids hash collisions; duplicate offer keys
are rejected. Crossing IDs encode the ordered owners and exact run as a JSON
tuple after `${parent.id}/interface/`. Named seeds use the shared
`map/micro/random.ts` helper: UTF-8 key bytes hashed with unsigned 32-bit FNV-1a
(offset `0x811c9dc5`, multiplier `0x01000193`) supply the x input to the existing
per-place `draw(parentSeed, hash, 0, 0x64656c67)` mixer, then
`floor(draw * 0x7fffffff)`. Seed collisions are possible in a finite seed space;
identities use full keys, not the seed hash. No RNG stream is consumed. The
existing scatter `draw` is re-exported unchanged, so `block` keeps its previous
channels, seeds and output. Execution and snapshot versions are specified below.

### Execution contract (#329)

`executeDelegation(brief, registry, options)` in
`map/micro/delegation-execute.ts` dispatches through an explicit `ExecutionRegistry`.
Each entry records its `version`, `build(brief, context, observe?)` and optional
pure `receiving(brief, offer)` feasibility callback. The executor is outside the
SDK and does not import the production registry. The production hut adopts this
executor in #330 under `types-24`; other strategies remain independently versioned.

The context supplies a cloned `brief` getter, synchronous
`delegate(offers, policyData?)`, and terminal `empty(reason)`. A builder may submit
one patch batch during its invocation, before or after its own placement. After
preparation, the getter supplies its retained interface; it must return a
`region-2` result with that equivalent brief (object key order is immaterial).
A second batch is refused. This is an
execution API boundary, not a requirement that all builders use a planning pass.
Post-placement delegation supplies explicit blockers/protected cells/runs. The
executor does not infer reservations from private geometry or inspect neighbors.

The root builder starts at depth zero, independently of any initial decomposition.
Each patch child increments depth. `maxDepth` defaults to three and accepts any
nonnegative safe integer, including zero. At the bound the current builder may
still build its own retained work, but accepted further patches are terminal empty
children. Their original requested type and complete interface remain recorded;
the actual builder is `empty`, with `depth-limit` as the stopping reason. Receiving
content feasibility is bypassed at the bound; ownership, protection and interface
feasibility are still checked. There is no child-count cap or depth reset.

`empty` is built in, cannot be overridden by a registry, emits no geometry, loot or
core sites, and never splits. An explicit whole-footprint offer can target only
empty; a complete offer to content is rejected and stays retained. `empty(reason)`
can abandon work after child dispatch. Original cells, portals and requested core
counts remain intact, while all attempted parent/child geometry and observer events
are discarded from runtime output. Catching the terminal control signal inside a
builder does not cancel the recorded empty decision or allow another dispatch.
Attempted requests, rejection reasons and child
generation objects remain available as historical decisions. Unexpected builder or
policy exceptions propagate; they are not converted to content fallback.

A child's complete input is its interface, including inherited zones, margins,
portals, guidance and advisory reason tags. It receives no adjacent builders'
private state. Every partition retains the original reachability obligations
([17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md) D3-D5). The containing region owns its
original portal promise; elective final geometry checks remain unchanged.

`executionLeaves(node)` returns the surviving owners. `flattenDelegationResult(node)`
derives one ordinary `region-2` result with the original root brief and unchanged
world coordinates. Child element and building-observer labels use the absolute
child ID as their prefix exactly once, including at deeper levels. Observer
events are buffered until complete replacement is
settled. The manifest counts original owned cells once, derives loot/core totals
from surviving arrays, and sums only the common additive counters `structures`,
`obstacles`, `gates`, `attempted` and `rejected`. Strategy-specific metrics are not
implicitly summed; adopters must define any additional aggregation explicitly.

Sensitive core builders can use complete empty fallback for genuinely incompatible
input. Requested core counts stay in the containing brief, so existing measurement
reports missing features and live admission rejects missing essential sites
([51.10](51.10-measurement.md)). This adds no loot policy, fabricated sites or
universal generation failure API (D8).

### Owning snapshot and recovery (#329)

`saveDelegationSnapshot`, `readDelegationSnapshot` and `delegationSnapshotResult`
in `map/micro/delegation-snapshot.ts` own the JSON encoding
`builder-delegation-snapshot-1`. Execution objects use algorithm version
`builder-delegation-1`. This is an optional owning-layer artifact; it does not
change the chain container, production results or wire versions.

The snapshot records complete canonical root/child inputs, root execution settings,
versions of every built or consulted registry entry (including rejected receivers),
requested and actual builder provenance, semantic requests with tags/guidance,
protection data, accepted keys, actual rejection reasons, semantic fallback kinds
and builder-authored fallback reasons,
nested generation objects and retained results. Discarded attempts remain history,
while derived runtime views exclude them. Observer traces are optional local
execution metadata; they are not embedded in flattened region results.

Saved data must use finite JSON values and plain objects. Optional fields must
be omitted rather than explicitly set to `undefined`, including on elements or
observer traces. Saving refuses values that JSON would silently drop or alter;
adopting builders must satisfy this encoding contract.

Preparation views are not saved. Reading reconstructs retained ownership,
fill/clear margins and paired crossings from accepted semantic requests through
`prepareDelegation`, using recorded rejection reasons for rejected attempts. It
checks the reconstructed child inputs and each local partition/interface graph.
Nested promises compose through their retained owners; a descendant touching an
ancestor's neighbor need not acquire an additional direct portal to that neighbor.
Flattened output is
also derived through the shared executor helper. Full saved results load without
consulting or executing today's registry.

With `omitResults: true`, the snapshot keeps complete inputs and authored decisions
but omits retained generation results. A read then requires matching recorded
algorithm and builder versions; mismatches name the algorithm or builder and are
refused. Recovery reruns the owning executor from the original root input/settings,
checks every recorded decision, and checks any results kept in a partial save.
Builders and receiving policies must be deterministic functions of their interface
and versioned implementation. This refuses silently changed decisions even if an
author forgot a version bump. It does not reconstruct historical decisions from
flat geometry or invoke unversioned upstream data.

### Adoption and verification

Mechanics are covered by synthetic recursive builders and snapshot recovery tests.
The completed #330 production pilot is in `map/micro/strategies/hut.ts`:
`buildHut` remains the flattened-result adapter over the owning
`buildHutGeneration` execution object, with `HUT_EXECUTION_REGISTRY` recording
the hut and cover builder versions. The adapter preserves the supplied root
brief for chain saving; the owning object uses canonical inputs. House siting
and design remain first and
unchanged. The hut retains its house expanded by two cells, plus a two-cell
perimeter band, and standing-block paths from the doorstep to every original
portal. The path search favors existing retained yard with zero-cost steps.
Connected unused yard components
of at least nine cells are offered to cover with reason `unused-yard-lobe`; each
key is `yard:<x>,<y>`, and the owning snapshot records the full footprint.
Within one `buildHutGeneration` invocation, the receiving check and child build
reuse a cover preview cached by the exact serialized child brief. An offer is
rejected if that preview has no usable placement. A hut with no usable fill or
no house with a reachable doorstep takes the terminal empty path. Core quotas
and reachability obligations remain with the original hut; no core exclusion
or new loot system was added. Retained hut loot is clipped to retained
ownership and rolls in canonical cell order; cover uses its existing minimal
loot behavior. These are intentional content changes under `types-24`.
In particular, a 2 by 3 hut region that cannot fit a house now produces terminal
empty output; its former open-zone loot is lost. This is a consequence of the
current full-region fallback, not a new loot policy. The registry's
`cover-types-23` names the cover executor implementation and must advance when
that implementation changes; the aggregate `types-24` content version has a
different scope.

Nested `block` lots use the same hut builder, but small lots may have no
suitable cover lobe; no nested hut lots occurred in the three audited maps, so
the measured uptake does not establish nested adoption. A mechanical
preparation optimization reuses ownership sets and already-normalized private
inputs, avoiding repeated unchanged-owner partitions while retaining geometry
checks.
The authored behavior uses `types-24`. The 20-seed direct cases and three
diagnosed full maps are summarized in [42](42-performance-history.md): broad
yards gained cover, but the generated maps accepted only 6 of 137 offers, mostly
because cover had no usable placement. Preserve the frozen netcode fixture.
One 172 by 172 pilot yard offered two lobes of 25 and 27,969 cells; the larger
alone covered 94.5% of its owned cells. This leaves nearly all its otherwise unused yard available
for cover. That aggressive footprint selection is the current hut pilot's authorship choice, and its
visual/content balance warrants product review before treating it as a policy
for other builders.
#331's candidate review selected compound exterior yards for
[#338](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/338), based on separately
measured exterior space and a compatible cover witness ([42](42-performance-history.md)).
The compound adopter exposes `buildCompoundGeneration` and the versioned
`COMPOUND_EXECUTION_REGISTRY` (`compound-yard-1`, `cover-types-23`), with
`buildCompound` as its flattened adapter. Its existing ring siting and design
run first. Siting verifies the centered outer door's whole-cell stands, rather
than accepting an arbitrary stand elsewhere in a widened passage; this prevents
a gate beside a concave corner from falsely claiming actual yard access.
It retains the complete box, including intentional court and rooms,
a two-cell approach band, non-fill cells and a two-cell perimeter band. A
deterministic standing-block tree reserves swept hunter routes connecting every
original portal entrance and each realized outer gate's yard stand. Half-cell
gate centers reserve both neighboring whole-cell stands. Zero-cost steps favor
the already retained bands. If any required stand cannot be reserved, the yard
stays retained rather than weakening access.

Only remaining connected exterior components of at least nine cells are offered
to cover, tagged `unused-exterior-yard`. Their canonical first cell names the
disjoint footprint (`exterior:x,y`); snapshots store every cell. This keeps IDs
bounded instead of repeating a large footprint in every element label. Complete
ring ownership, inherited non-fill cells and swept route cells are explicitly protected, and already
placed ring geometry is declared to preparation. Inherited clear strips can be
too narrow to connect a new crossing to the retained hunter network, so they
are unavailable as new crossing footprints. Exact inherited cover previews
must contain usable elements; rejections remain with compound. Previews are
cached only by the exact child interface during one invocation. A compound
without a usable ring or accessible gates takes complete terminal empty, never
a full-region cover retry. Its original core requests remain available to final
diagnostics. Retained loot uses canonical/clipped ownership and existing rolls;
cover keeps its current loot behavior. The adapter preserves the supplied root
brief and the retained ring's `rooms`/`compoundGates` counters; other shared
counters are aggregated by the executor. Nested block lots invoke this same
adapter. Owning snapshots and omitted-result recovery use the existing executor
contract above. Measurements and content balance are in [42](42-performance-history.md).
Depot remains unselected because its free cells also include authored aisles
and density choices. Further adopters need their own demonstrated problem and
bounded issue. Use [31](31-verification.md) for focused and integration gates.
