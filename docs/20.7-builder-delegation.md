Corey's verbatim answers are in [17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md).
Issue [#289](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/289) supplies the

The unaccepted recommendations below are starting decisions to weigh individually.

## Recommended starting point

A builder may offer explicit, whole-cell patches it cannot use to named child
strategies. It keeps the rest, including intentional open space. The SDK prepares
ownership, paired portals and inherited constraints; a micro executor dispatches
the children and gathers their output. The containing builder still owns its
original portal promise.

Start with **hut yard patches delegated to cover**, one delegation level,
at most four children per invocation. Keep the house, its doorstep, circulation
and original portals in the retained area. This is optional authorship, not an
automatic operation on every empty rectangle. A failed proposal leaves the
existing hut strategy available. The pilot is a recommendation, not evidence that
every hut has suitable spare ground or that cover is always its best theme.

For example, a hut in a broad, concave yard could retain its house and a hunter-wide
route joining its external portals, then offer a side lobe to cover. Cover receives
that lobe's ownership and a paired crossing to the yard. A narrow strip serving as
the house's doorstep stays with the hut even if it contains no obstacle.

## Decisions to review separately

| Decision | Recommended default | Benefit | Cost / useful alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1. Who identifies spare space? **Accepted** | Only the owning builder identifies patches it cannot fill. Offers may carry builder-defined reason tags, available for debugging and optionally to the child. | Preserves builder intent and explains relinquishment without requiring child-specific tuning. | Each builder authors its patch selection and tag vocabulary; child use of tags is optional. |
| D2. Ownership unit **Accepted** | Disjoint whole-cell patches plus retained cells exactly partition original ownership. A patch must meet its receiving builder's requirements; a default empty builder handles irregular/awkward shapes. | Reuses masks, zones, margins and interface tools; nothing disappears. | Sub-cell slivers stay with their cell owner. Irregular whole-cell footprints do not require shape-level ownership. |
| D3. When to settle the handoff? | After selecting parent structure candidates, before committing final geometry or loot. | New portal margins cannot silently invalidate an already committed house or wall. | Requires a planning/finalization split in adopting builders. Post-build carving is easier initially but needs rollback or very conservative buffering. |
| D4. Who chooses child content? | Parent strategy names a child type and explicit parameters from its own allowlist. | A hut yard remains authored as a hut yard; the SDK stays about mechanics. | Each builder needs policy. A general scored dispatcher could improve reuse later but may erase regional identity. |
| D5. Access responsibility | Each local region keeps its portal promise; parent keeps the containing promise. Paired crossings on every adjacent pair. | Preserves inference and existing child semantics. | New crossings and margins consume fill. Treating a child as decoration inside the parent's routes is cheaper, but would be a separate contract rather than a region. |
| D6. Recursion | Pilot: one extra delegation level, four children, bounded proposal attempts; children are terminal. | Predictable work and no builder cycles. | Misses useful nested refinements. Later bounded recursion can admit them after real examples justify it. |
| D7. Failed delegation | Reject before execution when infeasible; leave those cells retained. Do not hide malformed output or thrown errors. | Optional delegation cannot remove ownership or conceal a broken builder. | Less fill in difficult shapes. Retrying another type is possible, but needs an explicit bounded candidate policy. |
| D8. Core sites and loot | Pilot excludes core-bearing parents. Child-owned loot offers more local style, but changes RNG and accounting across partitions. |
| D9. ## Proposed handoff mechanics

### Planning and ownership

Use a micro-internal delegation plan, separate from `RegionResult` and the macro
brief. Conceptually it contains retained cells, tentative parent placements,
protected cells/runs, and child requests `{ key, cells, type, parameters, reasonTags }`.
These are design fields, not a finalized TypeScript API. SDK helpers prepare the
cell partition and interfaces; strategy code supplies content and protection policy.
Only the owning builder identifies offered patches. It may attach its own reason
tags explaining why it is not retaining those cells. Carry the tags and their
source builder through the debug trace and an advisory channel to the child.
They are separate from child-type parameters; a child may ignore them or use
them as guidance, but they impose no ownership or access obligations. The SDK
does not define a universal tag vocabulary or derive offers on the builder's
behalf. The exact metadata transport remains an implementation design detail.

The executor lives outside the SDK and uses an explicit registry/context, rather
than strategies importing `buildRegion` and creating a registry import cycle.

Every original cell is either retained or owned by one accepted child. Rejected
and unoffered cells remain retained. A child must be one four-connected area;
the pilot also requires the retained area to be connected. Before submission,
each offered patch must meet its receiving builder's requirements. A default
empty builder remains available for irregular and awkward shapes that other
builders cannot handle. It still keeps ownership and applicable access promises;
empty geometry cannot make an inherently impassable footprint traversable.
Empty or disconnected
**fill** is valid and never changes ownership. Original margin cells are not
invented child regions. If subtracting a patch disconnects the retained area,
reject that patch in the pilot; supporting several retained pieces is later work.

The parent declares the footprint and clearance it needs for structures, doors,
core sites where applicable, and intentional routes. Retained cells include whole
cells needed to contain its geometry, including geometry crossing a cell edge.
Do not calculate this solely by subtracting obstacle footprints: a room interior,
yard, turning space or roofed assembly may be intentionally retained too.
Conservative cell reservations can sacrifice some fill; that is preferable to
letting independent children occupy a house interior or block its doorway.

Preparation must establish paired portals and apply margins to **both** retained
and delegated ownership before the parent finalizes its placements. A candidate
that removes required fill or conflicts with protected geometry/approaches is
rejected, not repaired by moving existing geometry after execution. No universal
empty-rectangle scan runs during generation; the existing fill diagnostic remains
an offline measurement tool (#288).

### Portals and inherited information

In the pilot, the retained area owns every original external portal, whole.
Do not allow a child to split a run or take its inside cells. This avoids new
external fragmentation policy. Later, a whole external run could pass to one
child if the containing promise is still supported; it must never be shortened,
dropped or assigned to two owners.

Discover actual shared boundaries among accepted children and the retained area.
Every adjacent pair gets at least one paired hunter crossing, as required by
[20.4](20.4-hierarchical-generation.md). Use maximal straight runs with a
deterministic order: longest eligible run first, ties by axis, row and column;
choose a centered minimum hunter-wide crossing using the kernel scale and
existing portal feasibility mechanics. Minimum width alone is not a clearance
proof. Check both sides and endpoint footprints against known playable cells
and declared protection. If a required pair has no eligible crossing, reject
the candidate. Non-selected seam stretches are unstated, not automatically
walled or guaranteed passable. Point contact is not an interface.

Require the required-crossing graph to connect every child to the retained area.
A one-portal child is allowed: it still clears the crossing although it has no
pair-connectivity promise. Requiring two frontages as `block` does is useful when
a child should be a through-route, but is unnecessary for every yard pocket.
The parent must keep all its external and new interface portals connected within
its retained ownership and known-clear context. Thus it does not assume it can
walk through arbitrary child interiors. This is intentionally conservative;
using children to carry the parent's main route would need a richer plan policy.

Reuse `inheritBriefMargins(parent, localBriefs)` with the retained brief included.
It already handles new crossing footprints affecting third owners, inherited
fill exclusions and internal-run clipping. It checks disjoint/subset ownership,
so preparation additionally checks exhaustive coverage and correct paired runs.
Preserve [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md)'s rule: splitting an internal guaranteed
run preserves its clear footprint, without manufacturing a child portal.

Restrict zones by owned cells without changing tier, bonus or chance. Clip original
boundary hints to the new owner; do not hint every newly cut edge. Child parameters
are explicit child-type configuration, not a copy of unrelated parent parameters.
Known-clear context contains only inherited or newly guaranteed clearance;
unbuilt retained ground is not automatically safe context for a child.

`negotiatePortals` currently uses explicit `RegionPort` floors/ceilings from the
demonstration executor. Reuse its interface analysis where appropriate, but do
not silently reinterpret that contract as kernel `Portal` runs. A brief-oriented
preparation helper or explicit adapter must own this distinction.

### Determinism and work bounds

Each request has a unique stable strategy key, such as `yard-east`, used in the
child path `${parent.id}/delegate/${key}`. Automatically enumerated patches use
canonical normalized cell order and a deterministic footprint-derived key;
the implementation must specify its encoding and reject collisions. Do not use
acceptance-order numbering that changes every later child's identity when one
proposal is rejected. Derive seeds with a named delegation stream from parent
seed and child key; never consume the parent's structural or loot RNG stream.
Interface IDs derive from the ordered owner keys and crossing run. Canonicalize
proposal processing and output independently of input cell/list order.

Proposed pilot work limits: at most eight candidate patches considered, four
accepted children, one direct child build per accepted request, no child
delegation. Each accepted child is a strict smaller subset of its parent.
Stop with an explicit diagnostic reason when the budget is exhausted; cells
stay retained. These are proposed work caps, not measured optimums or SDK-wide
region-size reductions. Avoid routing large briefs through the structural
explorer's smaller 4,096-cell limit without an explicit bounded adapter.

Keep depth/budget/geometry-only options in a micro execution context, not numeric
region-type parameters or macro-authored data. The pilot allowlist contains only
terminal cover, so a hut inside a `block` lot can use the pilot without allowing
unbounded registry recursion. Before general recursive dispatch, route all
delegating calls through one context, count existing nested work as well as new
work, and decide shared root budgets. Structural tree exploration's depth caps
are not automatically physical execution caps.

### Sites, loot, results and diagnosis

For the pilot, a parent with any positive core quota is ineligible. Generic future
adoption should keep quotas with the parent and explicitly reserve its sites;
children must not inherit copies. Delegating actual core duties is a later choice
requiring quota partition and shortfall accounting. `block` currently reports
no core sites, so its aggregator cannot be copied as a generic quota solution.

Give adopting builders an internal geometry-only path; a child must not roll
loot and then have its output discarded. Preserve the original zone context.
After parent and child geometry is gathered, the parent rolls each original
owned cell once using its normal named loot stream and tests sites against the
combined blockers. This keeps loot ownership and RNG ordering independent of
child acceptance; actual eligibility may change with geometry. Hut's current
loot behavior is the first example, not a new universal loot algorithm. The
implementation must also check inter-site spacing and any retained reservations
used by the adopting parent. More fill may change total loot; measure it explicitly.

Flatten elements under stable child label prefixes, preserve coordinates and
forward building observers with those prefixes. Return the original parent
brief, parent plus child geometry, final loot and preserved core sites. Recompute
aggregate `cells`, `loot` and `coreElements`; sum only known additive counts.
Strategy-specific manifest fields need documented aggregation rather than blindly
summing metrics with different meanings. Add delegation/retained cell counts only
with explicit semantics. Do not emit children as extra macro-owned regions that
overlap the parent in `composeRegions`.

A tool-only trace records offered/accepted/rejected patches, builder-defined
reason tags and their source builder, preparation rejection reasons, types,
keys/seeds, paired crossings, protected areas, before/after fill and stopping
conditions. It is not a required saved tree or an automatic repair mechanism.
Contract checks remain elective under [19](19-decomposition-design.md). Adopted
builders should test final combined geometry with fill and portal validators,
including parent external coverage and local paired crossings. Malformed plans,
unknown types and execution errors fail explicitly. Only normal pre-execution
infeasibility invokes the unchanged parent fallback.

## Proposed implementation sequence and acceptance

1. **Mechanics without production adoption.** Add a brief-oriented plan/preparation
   helper and micro executor; define execution options and geometry-only hooks.
   Test exact partition, malformed/overlapping/outside requests, disconnected
   retained ownership, paired interfaces, external portal preservation, internal
   guarantee inheritance, third-owner endpoint clearance and idempotent margins.
   Existing builders and the chain baseline must remain unchanged.
2. **Hut-to-cover pilot.** Offer at most four useful yard patches while retaining
   the house, doorstep and external circulation. Include a useful lobe witness,
   a thin/non-delegable yard, a margin-conflict rejection, nested block lot, empty
   fill, deterministic reordered input, exhausted budget and no-core eligibility.
   Check the final containing portal promise and child crossings physically;
   show that delegation adds themed content without losing the house/access.
3. **Review evidence before expansion.** Compare the same representative seeds
   with `npm run fill`, record loot changes and view the patches in Micro Lab or
   Map Lab. Report attempted/accepted delegation and time as well as fill; fewer
   empty rectangles alone does not establish a better yard. Run focused timing
   checks per [31](31-verification.md), relevant integration/browser checks and
   the 224-case sweep. Bump `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` and deliberately capture changed
   built/report content with provenance at the adoption checkpoint. Preserve the
   frozen netcode fixture; reassess artifact versions only if output shape changes.
4. **Separate later adoptions.** Evaluate compound yards, depot leftovers and
   other builders individually. Then consider external portals owned by children,
   multiple retained pieces, child loot policies and true recursive execution.

This proposal changes no runtime behavior, versions or baselines. As decisions are
accepted, record Corey's answers verbatim in [17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md) and update this
document's status and the owning contracts before implementing them.
