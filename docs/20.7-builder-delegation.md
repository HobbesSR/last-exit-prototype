# 20.7. Builder delegation proposal

Status: **design direction recorded and implementation defaults selected,
2026-10-10; documentation checkpoint only; nothing implemented**.
Corey's verbatim answers are in [17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md).
Issue [#289](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/289) supplies the
design boundary; #242 supplies the direction to use space in a builder's style.
Unsettled mechanics below are implementation recommendations, constrained by the
recorded decisions; they are not all independently accepted API requirements.

## Recommended starting point

A builder may offer explicit, whole-cell patches it cannot use to named child
strategies. It keeps the rest, including intentional open space. The SDK prepares
ownership, paired portals and inherited constraints; a micro executor dispatches
the children and gathers their output. The containing builder still owns its
original portal promise.

Start with **hut yard patches delegated to cover** as the first consumer (D10).
Recursive patch splitting is depth-bounded, with remaining patches given to
the terminal empty builder at the limit (D6); the default depth is three.
Keep the house, its doorstep, circulation
and original portals in the retained area. This is optional authorship, not an
automatic operation on every empty rectangle. An infeasible patch proposal can
leave its cells retained. If a region cannot fulfill its work by building and
splitting, it may hand the complete region only to the empty builder (D7).
The accepted pilot sequence is not evidence that
every hut has suitable spare ground or that cover is always its best theme.

For example, a hut in a broad, concave yard could retain its house and a hunter-wide
route joining its external portals, then offer a side lobe to cover. Cover receives
that lobe's ownership and a paired crossing to the yard. A narrow strip serving as
the house's doorstep stays with the hut even if it contains no obstacle.

Offers may carry builder-defined reason tags, available for debugging and optionally to the child. | Preserves builder intent and explains relinquishment without requiring child-specific tuning. | Each builder authors its patch selection and tag vocabulary; child use of tags is optional. |

| D4. ## Proposed handoff mechanics

### Handoff and ownership

Use a micro-internal delegation plan, separate from `RegionResult` and the macro
brief. Conceptually it contains retained cells, protected cells/runs, and child
requests `{ key, cells, type, parameters, reasonTags }`. Parent placement plans
may remain private to the offering builder; children cannot depend on them.
These are design fields, not a finalized TypeScript API. SDK helpers prepare the
cell partition and interfaces; strategy code supplies content and protection policy.
The delegating builder chooses child content and may use guidance received from
the original decomposer through its interface. The guidance is available input,
not a decision imposed by a generic dispatcher. Its vocabulary and transport
remain to be designed; no builder reads private decomposer or adjacent-region state.

Only the owning builder identifies offered patches. It may attach its own reason
tags explaining why it is not retaining those cells. Carry the tags and their
source builder through the debug trace and an advisory channel to the child.
They are separate from child-type parameters; a child may ignore them or use
them as guidance, but they impose no ownership or access obligations. The SDK
does not define a universal tag vocabulary or derive offers on the builder's
behalf. The exact metadata transport remains an implementation design detail.

The executor lives outside the SDK and uses an explicit registry/context, rather
than strategies importing `buildRegion` and creating a registry import cycle.

Every original cell is either retained or owned by one accepted child. Rejected
and unoffered cells remain retained. A child must be one four-connected area;
the pilot also requires the retained area to be connected. Before submission,
each offered patch must meet its receiving builder's requirements. A default
empty builder remains available for irregular and awkward shapes that other
builders cannot handle. It still keeps ownership and applicable access promises;
empty geometry cannot make an inherently impassable footprint traversable.
Empty or disconnected
**fill** is valid and never changes ownership. Original margin cells are not
invented child regions. If subtracting a patch disconnects the retained area,
reject that patch in the pilot; supporting several retained pieces is later work.

The parent declares the footprint and clearance it needs for structures, doors,
core sites where applicable, and intentional routes. Retained cells include whole
cells needed to contain its geometry, including geometry crossing a cell edge.
Do not calculate this solely by subtracting obstacle footprints: a room interior,
yard, turning space or roofed assembly may be intentionally retained too.
Conservative cell reservations can sacrifice some fill; that is preferable to
letting independent children occupy a house interior or block its doorway.

The offering builder decides when it delegates: before, during or after its own
geometry generation. At handoff, the child must have complete interface input to
build independently. Required collaboration, such as crossing runs, clear areas
or placement exclusions, must be stated through that interface rather than
obtained by inspecting adjacent builders, their private plans or their origins.
This does not require passing all parent geometry; it requires passing whatever
constraints the child actually needs. The precise representation of additional
collaboration information remains an implementation design question.

Paired portals and margins still apply to **both** retained and delegated
ownership. The offering builder must make the handoff compatible with its own
geometry and promises. A builder may prepare those constraints before finalizing
placements, or derive a compatible handoff after placing geometry. Neither timing
is mandated. A proposal that conflicts with protected geometry/approaches must
not be dispatched with incompatible input. No universal empty-rectangle scan runs
during generation; the existing fill diagnostic remains an offline measurement
tool (#288).

### Portals and inherited information

**Accepted reachability invariant (D5).** Macro treats each original region as a
node and joins two nodes when their shared boundary has a guaranteed portal
([51.5](51.5-regions.md)). Its proof in `map/macro/src/chain/proof.ts` computes
the resulting connected components; it relies on all portals of each node being
mutually hunter-reachable ([51.1](51.1-principles-and-names.md)). Micro partitioning
cannot invalidate that assumption.

Decomposition may first partition the original region into sub-regions. Builders
of those regions may later split off further patches. Both operations preserve
the original obligations, all the way down; a handoff never starts a fresh,
weaker reachability contract. Child portals must still connect into the same
required reachability structure through the final composed region geometry.
For example, if original portals A and B must connect and a route now passes
through children X and Y, paired interfaces and each child's internal passage
must together preserve A-to-B access. X need not own B or know Y's implementation.
It receives its necessary local obligations through its interface (D3).

This does not make every interior point reachable or require a whole-map flood.
The portal promise and elective geometry validation remain as in 19 and 20.1.
The pilot routing and crossing-selection recommendations below are one way to
preserve that promise, not a requirement that original paths stay in retained
parent cells or that decomposition and patch delegation use the same algorithm.

In the pilot, the retained area owns every original external portal, whole.
Do not allow a child to split a run or take its inside cells. This avoids new
external fragmentation policy. Later, a whole external run could pass to one
child if the containing promise is still supported; it must never be shortened,
dropped or assigned to two owners.

Discover actual shared boundaries among accepted children and the retained area.
Every adjacent pair gets at least one paired hunter crossing, as required by
[20.4](20.4-hierarchical-generation.md). Use maximal straight runs with a
deterministic order: longest eligible run first, ties by axis, row and column;
choose a centered minimum hunter-wide crossing using the kernel scale and
existing portal feasibility mechanics. Minimum width alone is not a clearance
proof. Check both sides and endpoint footprints against known playable cells
and declared protection. If a required pair has no eligible crossing, reject
the candidate. Non-selected seam stretches are unstated, not automatically
walled or guaranteed passable. Point contact is not an interface.

Require the required-crossing graph to connect every child to the retained area.
A one-portal child is allowed: it still clears the crossing although it has no
pair-connectivity promise. Requiring two frontages as `block` does is useful when
a child should be a through-route, but is unnecessary for every yard pocket.
For the proposed pilot, keep all original external and new interface portals
connected within retained ownership and known-clear context. This is a conservative
pilot choice, not D5's general rule. A broader partition may carry the original
region's routes through children when their composed portal promises preserve
the original relationships. No strategy assumes passage through arbitrary
child interiors beyond the obligations supplied by their interfaces.

Reuse `inheritBriefMargins(parent, localBriefs)` with the retained brief included.
It already handles new crossing footprints affecting third owners, inherited
fill exclusions and internal-run clipping. It checks disjoint/subset ownership,
so preparation additionally checks exhaustive coverage and correct paired runs.
Preserve [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md)'s rule: splitting an internal guaranteed
run preserves its clear footprint, without manufacturing a child portal.

Restrict zones by owned cells without changing tier, bonus or chance. Clip original
boundary hints to the new owner; do not hint every newly cut edge. Child parameters
are explicit child-type configuration, not a copy of unrelated parent parameters.
Known-clear context contains only inherited or newly guaranteed clearance;
unbuilt retained ground is not automatically safe context for a child.

`negotiatePortals` currently uses explicit `RegionPort` floors/ceilings from the
demonstration executor. Reuse its interface analysis where appropriate, but do
not silently reinterpret that contract as kernel `Portal` runs. A brief-oriented
preparation helper or explicit adapter must own this distinction.

### Determinism and work bounds

Each request has a unique stable strategy key, such as `yard-east`, used in the
child path `${parent.id}/delegate/${key}`. Automatically enumerated patches use
canonical normalized cell order and a deterministic footprint-derived key;
the implementation must specify its encoding and reject collisions. Do not use
acceptance-order numbering that changes every later child's identity when one
proposal is rejected. Derive seeds with a named delegation stream from parent
seed and child key; never consume the parent's structural or loot RNG stream.
Interface IDs derive from the ordered owner keys and crossing run. Canonicalize
proposal processing and output independently of input cell/list order.

Bound recursive builder patch splitting by depth (D6). When further content
delegation would exceed that bound, dispatch remaining patches to the default
empty builder instead. It always succeeds and never splits; ownership and
interface obligations remain with those terminal regions. Record the stopping
reason in the trace. Use a configurable default maximum patch depth of three,
counting the initial builder as zero and incrementing for each content patch
handoff. A builder at depth three may do its own building, but further offered
patches dispatch to empty. A maximum of zero gives immediate empty patch fallback.
Optional initial decomposition does not consume patch-splitting depth; each of
its resulting initial builders starts at zero. Do not reuse
structural tree exploration's depth caps as implicit physical execution limits.

The previously suggested four-child and eight-candidate pilot caps are withdrawn;
they were not derived from requirements or measurements. A depth bound does not
bound total branching work. Any additional work budget needs explicit justification
and a reviewed stopping policy, rather than silently limiting useful child patches.
Each content-bearing patch child remains a strict smaller subset of its parent.
A complete-region handoff is allowed only to the terminal empty builder (D7),
so it cannot create a same-footprint content-builder cycle. Avoid routing
large briefs through the structural explorer's smaller 4,096-cell limit without
an explicit bounded adapter.

Proposed execution mechanism: carry depth options in a micro
context, not numeric region-type parameters or macro-authored data. Route recursive
patch dispatch through that context so nesting cannot reset the bound. A hut
inside a `block` lot follows the same patch-splitting rule. The empty builder needs
no access to adjacent internals and does not attempt to repair invalid input or
geometry elsewhere; interface checks and the original reachability promise are
unchanged.

### Sites, loot, results and diagnosis

Loot policy is deferred to the planned loot-system/generation overhaul (D8).
This design no longer prescribes parent-only loot rolls, geometry-only child
hooks or a new quota/accounting framework. Carry existing output through ordinary
regional aggregation; decide only the minimal integration needed by each adopting
builder, without using this issue to redesign loot.

For sensitive core-site builders, add no special delegation machinery now beyond
rejecting building when the received region is truly incompatible. Such a rejection
delegates the entire region to empty (D7/D8). Preserve original core requests in
the containing brief; do not clear them to make the empty result appear complete,
copy quotas to children or fabricate replacement sites. There is no blanket
exclusion solely because a brief requests core elements. `block` currently reports
no core sites, so its aggregator is not a generic quota solution.

Check required core mechanical features after generation. Existing macro
measurement (`map/macro/src/chain/measurement.ts`, [51.10](51.10-measurement.md))
reports per-brief count mismatches and missing per-instance core features. Map
tools expose those defects through `checkMap` and its validity flag. Measurement
does not reject or repair the map. The live adapter's `liveMapFromBuilt` already
rejects missing required spawns/exits, chargers and transit sites and checks site
occupancy. This is the existing route to the desired failure for a map missing
mechanical features; no universal generation error API is specified here.

Proposed runtime result view: flatten elements under stable child label prefixes, preserve coordinates and
forward building observers with those prefixes. Return the original parent
brief, parent plus child geometry, final loot and preserved core sites. Recompute
aggregate `cells`, `loot` and `coreElements`; sum only known additive counts.
Strategy-specific manifest fields need documented aggregation rather than blindly
summing metrics with different meanings. Add delegation/retained cell counts only
with explicit semantics. Do not emit children as extra macro-owned regions that
overlap the parent in `composeRegions`.

An inspectable delegation view records offered/accepted/rejected patches, builder-defined
reason tags and their source builder, preparation rejection reasons, types,
keys/seeds, paired crossings, protected areas, before/after fill and stopping
conditions. Under D9, these details should be deterministically recoverable from
the owning generation layer's snapshot. Follow
[51.12](51.12-map-and-saving.md) and [53](53-map-artifacts-and-tools.md): store
decision-bearing objects with their inputs/version, derive views rather than
duplicating them, and refuse regeneration with incompatible algorithms.

Do not assume flattened final geometry can reconstruct patch ownership, reason
tags or rejected attempts. The implementation must identify the complete owning
inputs and decisions and demonstrate recovery by reading its snapshot or rerunning
the matching deterministic layer. Start with a micro-owned `builder-delegation-1`
snapshot recording complete owning inputs (or resolvable versioned upstream
references), execution settings, semantic requests with metadata, accepted/rejected
or fallback outcomes, and nested child generation objects/results. Derive retained
ownership, margins, interface views and flattened runtime output from those objects
when possible. Actual authored choices that cannot be reconstructed belong in
the snapshot. Reading a fully saved snapshot must not depend on today's builder
registry; regeneration of omitted objects requires the recorded matching algorithm.
The executor issue owns the concrete TypeScript/JSON API and versioned encoding.
The proposed flat
runtime result is compatible with a recoverable layer snapshot, but is not an
independently accepted restriction on storage. Inspection remains diagnostic,
not an automatic repair mechanism.
Record complete-region fallback separately from patch rejection and depth-limit
fallback. When a builder cannot fulfill its work through building and splitting,
it may hand its whole input region to empty (D7). The handoff preserves the full
footprint and interface obligations; it does not shrink the region, restart
dispatch with another content builder or reset recursion to attempt another split.
The final result represents the chosen empty fallback, rather than combining it
with the abandoned attempt's geometry and child output. The proposed executor
should gather regional output before publishing it so complete replacement does
not leave partial content behind. Failure signaling and handling of unexpected
implementation exceptions remain implementation design details; D7 does not
choose an exception API or require a catch-all fallback.

Contract checks remain elective under [19](19-decomposition-design.md). Adopted
builders should test final combined geometry with fill and portal validators,
including parent external coverage and local paired crossings. Malformed plans
and unknown types remain invalid input. Normal patch infeasibility can leave
cells retained; inability to fulfill the region's work permits complete empty
delegation. Preserve diagnostic reasons instead of concealing failed attempts.

## Proposed implementation sequence and acceptance

Ready tasks on Forgejo, in dependency order:

| Step | Issue | Checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [#328](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/328) | Baseline-neutral patch/interface preparation |
| 2 | [#329](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/329) | Baseline-neutral execution, empty fallback and snapshot recovery; depends on #328 |
| 3 | [#330](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/330) | Intentional hut-to-cover content adoption and measured checkpoint; depends on #328/#329 |
| 4 | [#331](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/331) | Later measured adoption tracker; ready after #330 evidence |

Implementation begins after this documentation checkpoint is merged. Each issue
owns the exact APIs within the recorded behavioral invariants. Later builder
issues are selected from evidence, not created as an automatic registry-wide rollout.

1. **Mechanics without production adoption.** Add a brief-oriented plan/preparation
   helper and micro executor; define execution options without new loot hooks.
   Specify the owning-layer snapshot and recovery path. Check round-trip or
   matching-version regeneration of patch ownership, tags, child IDs/seeds,
   paired crossings and fallback/rejection reasons; refuse incompatible-version
   regeneration. Downstream flattened geometry must not be the sole provenance
   if it loses information needed to recover these details.
   Verify that equivalent interface input supports the same child build regardless
   of adjacent regions' creation history or private implementation. Include a
   handoff after parent geometry as well as a plan-first handoff, with all needed
   collaboration constraints supplied explicitly.
   Test exact partition, malformed/overlapping/outside requests, disconnected
   retained ownership, paired interfaces, external portal preservation, internal
   guarantee inheritance, third-owner endpoint clearance and idempotent margins.
   Include an original A-to-B route passing through decomposed children, a later
   patch split from one child, and a negative case where children each satisfy
   local promises but their interface graph breaks the original A-to-B connection.
   Existing builders and the chain baseline must remain unchanged.
2. **Hut-to-cover pilot.** Offer useful yard patches while retaining
   the house, doorstep and external circulation. Include a useful lobe witness,
   a thin/non-delegable yard, a margin-conflict rejection, nested block lot, empty
   fill, deterministic reordered input and depth-limit empty fallback.
   Verify that terminal empty output always succeeds on valid awkward
   patches and never requests another split; include repeated nested splitting
   that cannot reset the depth counter.
   Include complete-region fallback after an unsuccessful building/splitting
   attempt: only empty is eligible, the original footprint and portal obligations
   remain, no abandoned partial geometry is emitted, and no further split occurs.
   Include a genuinely incompatible core brief falling back to empty with its
   original request preserved: post-generation checks must expose the missing
   feature, and live admission must reject missing required mechanical sites.
   Check the final containing portal promise and child crossings physically;
   show that delegation adds themed content without losing the house/access.
3. **Review evidence before expansion.** Compare the same representative seeds
   with `npm run fill` and view the patches in Micro Lab or
   Map Lab. Report attempted/accepted delegation and time as well as fill; fewer
   empty rectangles alone does not establish a better yard. Run focused timing
   checks per [31](31-verification.md), relevant integration/browser checks and
   the 224-case sweep. Bump `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` and deliberately capture changed
   built/report content with provenance at the adoption checkpoint. Preserve the
   frozen netcode fixture; reassess artifact versions only if output shape changes.
4. **Separate later adoptions.** Evaluate compound yards, depot leftovers and
   other builders individually. Then consider external portals owned by children,
   multiple retained pieces and broader recursive execution. Loot policy belongs
   to its separate overhaul.

This proposal changes no runtime behavior, versions or baselines. As decisions are
accepted, record Corey's answers verbatim in [17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md) and update this
document's status and the owning contracts before implementing them.
