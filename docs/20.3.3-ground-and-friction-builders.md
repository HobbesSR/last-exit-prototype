# 20.3.3. Ground and friction builders

Part of [20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md), which holds the shared builder layer, the design guidelines and what
every catalogue builder shares. Moved from 20.3 without changing its content (#510).

## `cover` builder

- **Slots:** they lie on a lattice one tile (6 cells) apart. Each slot is
  taken with chance `density`, by a cluster of one to three crates,
  barriers and containers inside a 4 × 4 box.
- **The aisle:** a cluster's bounding box keeps 2 clear cells from the next
  cluster. Its footprint must lie in the reduced fill, off every clear margin (20.6).
  Hinted boundary sites may use a perimeter cell instead of the old blanket
  edge ring.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the clear margins keep every portal's
  approach, and masks with up to 32 portals also protect a sampled hunter
  corridor while placing pieces. Above
  32 portals, a conservative boundary rule allows attachment along one
  hinted side or two adjacent hinted sides, but retains a 2-cell owned ring
  elsewhere and forbids attachment across opposite sides. This bounded mode
  is a spacing argument, not a proof that every non-portal pocket remains
  connected; `validatePortalReach` remains the independent promise check.
  The bounded #361 audit and its contrasting park/depot findings remain in [54.1](54.1-ground-and-friction.md#cover).
- **Delegated residual infill (#531, first installment):** the shared cover
  receiver keeps a successful ordinary cluster result unchanged. If that pass
  places nothing, density is positive, and the patch has at most 32 portals,
  it tries individual placeables over a seeded shuffle of canonical fill cells.
  A chosen large piece may give way to a smaller piece at the same cell. Whole
  footprints stay in the inherited fill, off protected hunter routes, and two
  cells from other pieces. No synthetic crossing margin is removed. Every
  candidate takes an independent density roll; a positive density does not
  guarantee placement. Above 32 portals, an empty cluster result stays empty,
  bounding route-search work. Standalone cover and the core-site post-filters
  keep their existing cluster behavior. Existing cover loot rolls are retained
  only where their three-by-three standing area remains clear of the new pieces.
  Receiver versions and the hut/compound adoption boundary belong to [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md).

## `ruins` builder

- **Rooms:** slots lie on a lattice 6 cells apart, at a seeded phase. A slot
  is taken with chance `density` by a roofless room 3 or 4 cells a side,
  wholly on owned cells, so at least a doorway's width (52) parts two rooms.
- **Walls:** a quarter cell thick, as `hut`'s are, laid a cell at a time
  along the inside of the room's box. Each span falls with chance `decay`;
  half of the fallen lie as a rotated slab inside their cell, and the rest
  are gone.
- **The doorway:** two cells wide, on a side whose cells beyond are owned
  two deep, so no room is sealed off. A doorway at a corner takes the
  meeting wall's end span too, to stay two cells wide.

- **Residual pockets (#624 / #531):** after rooms and hinted walls, try
  one-cell crates on canonical unaddressed cells with independent channels
  100?102 and chance `density / 2`. Every geometrically eligible lattice room
  retains its drawn rectangle plus two cells on all sides, including slots
  skipped by density or with no standing span. This keeps room interiors,
  door approaches and intentional open slots clear. Crates require a fully
  owned two-cell access ring, inherited fill and protected-route clearance,
  and two-cell separation from existing extents. Primary room/debris/hint
  geometry and counts remain unchanged. Loot checks final obstacles when
  crates are added; zero-density and small-region fallbacks retain their
  existing behavior. This conservative pass does not promise to populate
  every pocket or change room grammar.

## `hall` builder

- **The rectangle:** the largest rectangle in the reduced fill (20.6) with
  both sides at least 6 cells. Ties go to the first in row order.
- **Pillars:** each stands a quarter cell inside a 2 × 2 block of cells, so
  it is 1½ cells square. Blocks lie `spacing` cells apart, centred across
  the rectangle, and the aisle between two pillars is `spacing` less 1½
  cells.
- **The stagger:** each line of blocks is offset from the last along one
  axis, drawn from the seed, by as near half a period as whole cells allow
  while sharing no factor with it: 5 cells at spacing 6, 7 at 10. So the
  lines visit every phase within a period's worth of them, and a sightline
  down the other axis meets a pillar. A stride sharing a factor would visit
  only some phases and leave a lane open the whole way (PR #176 review).
  That axis keeps clean lanes. Staggering both would
  pinch pillar corners together.
- **The roof:** with `roofed`, the rectangle is one enclosing element, so a
  roof hides what's under it as a building's does.
- **Residual wings (#619 / #531):** after the primary assembly, `hall` alone
  tries one-cell crates on canonical unaddressed cells with an independent
  seeded probability of 0.5. The largest rectangle remains excluded even when
  it emitted no element. Crates keep two cells from that rectangle, hint walls
  and earlier crates, stay off protected routes and clear margins, and require
  a fully owned two-cell bypass ring in the access mask. The main lattice,
  roof and deliberately open pillar lanes retain their original geometry.
  Loot uses all final obstacles. The `market` stand-in retains its old behavior;
  this is neither a second roofed wing nor the bespoke market builder.
