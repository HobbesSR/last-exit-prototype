# 20.5. Tools and next boundaries

## Inspect and verify

The micro SDK's regional work limits are described in [20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md).
Absolute position does not consume more regional work: #330 removes the old
±512 address ceiling. Cells instead require safe integer coordinates with room
for the SDK's bounded run/margin offsets. The existing cell-count, span and
portal limits remain agent-selected tool defaults, not gameplay requirements.

Run `npm run dev` and open the **Region Builder Explorer** at `/dev/micro` on the
address it prints: 3100 by default, a worktree's assigned port from
[34](34-forgejo-workflow.md) otherwise. Choose a region type, shape, size and seed,
inspect roofs, portals and clearance routes, walk as either body with WASD, open
doors with E, and download the result. The lab displays the diameters, doorway and
squeeze in cells. This page is a development preview.
The desktop workspace keeps generation parameters on the left, the preview in
the center, and generation diagnostics, ports and building details in a separate
scrolling inspector on the right. The top action bar holds generation and JSON
import/export; overlays, walking and view controls sit beside the preview.
Panels scroll independently so inspecting a long result keeps the region visible.
The workspace fills the space below navigation at its natural height, and the
preview renders at the display's pixel density while keeping controls and pointer
coordinates in CSS pixels, including after window or display-scale changes.
Smaller windows stack the panels; desktop ergonomics are the acceptance target.
The region type list holds every registered game region type, defaulting to
`depot`; aliases are labelled with their first registered shared strategy
(currently `market` uses hall). The SDK's example builders and their explicit-port
mode retired with `micro-1` (#483). Region types take a hand-authored brief from
`map/micro/region-examples.ts`: rectangle, L, hole, neck, cross or stair, with
width and height from 8 to 48 cells, two boundary portals and an optional north
portal, and one zone at the selected loot tier with a 0.04 loot chance per cell.
Arrival requests eight contestant spawns, departure three exits and three hunter
spawns, charging one charger, and transit one warp; the preview labels their
emitted sites. These are lab presets, not changes to live map recipes.
The lab's own examples walk at cell proportions, and their download is the `region-2`
result, suitable for importing here or attaching to builder feedback. This is
local collision exploration, with doors, rather than a multiplayer match.
Scroll or use the zoom buttons to inspect details, drag to pan, and Fit region
to reset the view. Shift-click clear ground to move the walker there; placement
checks the selected body's collision and respects a game region's holes and
concave boundary. With the preview focused, WASD or arrow keys move and E
operates nearby doors. Leaving preview focus clears held movement keys and
restores normal page-key handling.
Toggles draw each building's design
graph, allocation, spans with their owners, and openings, from the design held
in memory ([56](56-buildings.md) L4). Guidance a building didn't meet is
listed as a warning. A broken portal promise is the one error.
**A chain region's brief** opens in the micro lab from the Map Lab's
drill-down ([53.1](53.1-map-lab-and-tools.md)), by link, or as a file:
"Load region JSON" takes a brief, or a `region-2` result, which is rebuilt
from its brief and compared to the file. The link carries the brief in the
URL fragment, `#brief=` and its JSON, deflated and base64url-encoded
(`map/micro/brief-link.ts`, which owns the format). A fragment never reaches
a server and survives any origin the link is opened on, and keeps a
`block`'s 60 KB brief to a few KB. The brief is built through the game's
region types, as the map built it, so the result equals the map's stored
one. It is walked with the game's collision at the game's own scale, not
cell proportions. It stays until a control makes a new example. The
building views draw through `map/micro/building/draw.ts`, which the Map Lab
shares.
Controls apply automatically. An imported brief stays until a control generates
a new example or another file is imported.

```powershell
node tools/micro-region.mjs --type=depot --seed=42 --out=test-results/region.json
node tools/micro-region.mjs --type=compound --shape=hole --width=36 --height=30
node tools/micro-region.mjs --validate=test-results/region.json
node tools/micro-region.mjs --type=ruins --shape=hole --batch=20
node tools/micro-region.mjs --brief=brief.json --out=region.json
node tools/decompose-region.mjs --shape=neck --out=test-results/decomposition.json --beam=8 --residual-penalty=10 --piece-penalty=3
node tools/decompose-region.mjs --validate=test-results/decomposition.json
node --test tests/micro-*.test.js
node tests/micro-lab.mjs
node tests/region-explorer.mjs
node tests/decomposition-lab.mjs
node tests/generation-demo.mjs
```

`npm run fill` (`tools/builder-fill.mjs`) measures what each region type leaves
empty over a seed set: per type, the free share of cells and the disjoint free
rectangles of at least 3×3, taken largest first (`measureFill` in
`map/micro/fill.ts`). It is a design guideline's measure, enforced nowhere and
refusing nothing, and the sweep's report never calls it, so it moves no baseline.

The CLI and the browser lab share the briefs (`region-examples.ts`), the region
types and the portal check. `micro-region` builds the lab's example brief for
`--type`, `--shape`, `--width`, `--height` and `--seed`, or a saved `--brief`. With
`--batch=N` it builds N seeds and reports each manifest, its time and its portal
check. `--validate` checks a saved `region-2` result's portal promise from its
geometry (`validatePortalReach`), so it exits 1 on a broken one. Broader acceptance
is in [31](31-verification.md). `decompose-region` emits or validates a
`decomposition-1` plan; `--shape`, `--spec`, `--beam`, `--residual-penalty` and
`--piece-penalty` select its example/context and caller policy. The decomposition
lab at `/dev/micro/decomposition` is an allocation visual inspector: its seams and
portal opportunities are structural, not physical geometry. `/dev/micro`
continues to inspect and walk actual micro geometry. Decomposition controls apply
automatically; presets demonstrate different allocation policies, while status
explains unchanged winners. Advanced search settings are not style controls.

All browser-based development tools (`/dev/micro`, `/dev/micro/decomposition`,
`/dev/micro/generation`, and the Map Lab at `/dev/map`) are now hosted directly
by the game server and carry a shared global navigation bar. There is no separate
lab server or `MAPGEN_PORT`; everything runs through `npm run dev`.

Open `/dev/micro/generation` for Region, Decomposition, Generated and Walk stages
of one `block`. A block shows as Region, its lots and alleys (`planBlock`) as
Decomposition, the block's own result as Generated, with each child's elements
taken by their label prefix, and Walk. Without a link, the example controls build
the lab's block brief, 36 by 30 cells, from a shape and seed. A `block` brief from
the Map Lab's link (same fragment) shows the map's block instead. Any other region
type's brief is refused by name, and the example shows instead. Inspect each
child's assignment and geometry, show routes and roofs, and download the block's
`region-2` result. Walk uses the game's actual collision movement; WASD/arrows move
and E operates doors. The earlier demo realized 19's room, courtyard and lobe plans
through the example builders with walled seams. It retired with them (#483); none
of today's decomposing types uses the 19 planner yet.
Ownership outlines distinguish region perimeters from decorative grid gutters;
these are drawn separately from the generated walls and roofs.
`npm run test:micro` runs the focused unit tests and all three browser previews,
including a walk through an example block and a map's block.

## Development previews

For the full procedural pipeline, open `/dev/micro/generation` on the address the dev server printed
([default](http://localhost:3100/dev/micro/generation)). Step through Region, Decomposition, Generated and
Walk: the default example is a block of lots and alleys. Vary the shape and seed,
inspect child assignments, then walk with WASD/arrows and open doors with E. This
is a standalone development preview.

For the decomposition SDK, open `/dev/micro/decomposition` on the address the dev server printed
([default](http://localhost:3100/dev/micro/decomposition)). Compare candidate footprints, neck cuts, depth and
generator assignments, or use **Explore decomposition trees** to compare bounded
hierarchies. Choose an objective, depth and retained alternative, then select a
branch to inspect its descendants and stopping reason. Tree exploration is
structural; no region type builds from those hierarchies yet.
The flat inspector below still lets you compare candidate footprints and
generator assignments; inspect residuals and change policy weights. Controls apply
automatically; try the Balanced and Prefer fewer pieces presets. Status explains
when settings change but the winning allocation stays the same. This is
separate from the walkable micro geometry preview. See [docs/19](19-decomposition-design.md)
for the design and [20.4](20.4-hierarchical-generation.md) for implemented limits.

For procedural architecture development, open `/dev/micro` on the address the dev server printed
(http://localhost:3100/dev/micro by default; an agent worktree uses its assigned port). Generate
any registered region type over the lab's example shapes, inspect clearance routes
and core element sites, walk with WASD as either role, use E at doors, and export
or import a brief or `region-2` result.
The normal arena remains unchanged. CLI/custom-region usage and the macro contract
are in [20.5](20.5-tools-and-next-boundaries.md) and
[20.1](20.1-region-contract.md), respectively.

## Next boundaries

Placing core element regions beyond local entry spacing, primitive material sets, and
authored segment/vertex metadata still need expansion. The next decomposition
boundary is a region type that builds from the 19 planner's plans or explored
trees, its pieces handed on as brief children with portals (20.7). The planner
proposes cells, and nothing yet turns its plans into geometry.

The `map/macro/` adapter this section once listed as a next boundary has shipped: live
rooms run the generation chain through `map/live.ts`
([22.2](22.2-map-generation.md#live-map-assembly), [51.14.5](51.14.5-switch-over.md)).
How it meets this SDK, as built:
- **Briefs** (51 stage 5) are expressed in the macro/micro contract in the
  shared map space, described under [Briefs](20.1-region-contract.md#briefs) (C1, #84).
- **Region type ids** replace the closed builder list.
- **Portals** are derived stretches that must stay passable. They are used for
  checking and never laid as geometry ([17](17-open-questions.md) M3, M4).
- **Not yet done:** the 19 planner's decomposition contexts keep their
  4,096-cell and 64-per-axis bounds. Its walled `RegionPort` negotiation
  retired with `micro-1` (#483), since M4 gives children passability
  obligations only. B3's one decomposer, `block` (#161), uses neither: it
  splits its region by spacing and writes its children's portals itself
  ([54](54-region-types.md) `block`).
- **mapgen's flat cell indices** are translated into this SDK's global integer
  cell coordinates, with an explicit `cellSize`.
- **Whole-map composition** is `composeRegions` (51 C2), described under [Child-region decomposition](20.3.2-child-region-decomposition.md#child-region-decomposition).
  Reachability isn't measured over the whole map. It is inferred from the macro
  proof and from each builder keeping its promise (51 principles 8 and 9).
  Nothing enforces that promise. This SDK's validators are elective utilities
  a builder may use to check its own work, for example in its tests.
- **The contract, body scale and the definition of a run** are in the shared
  map space, `map/kernel/`, which neither level owns (51 C0).
  `types.ts` re-exports the contract, `microMetrics` reads the scale, and
  `buildInterfaces` builds on the shared run finder.

The adapter must not treat `map/macro/`'s graph or sampled connectivity as proof
of this game's physical geometry. mapgen's own micro layer and its planned path,
which duplicated this directory's work, were deleted at the switch-over (#146).
Where macro and micro solve similar problems, such as boundary runs and
connectivity, each keeps a version fitted to its own scope ([50](50-map-generation.md)).
The legacy street generator in `shared/map/` is no longer live; it remains
`createGame`'s default for explicit legacy callers and the frozen
characterization fixture ([31](31-verification.md)).
