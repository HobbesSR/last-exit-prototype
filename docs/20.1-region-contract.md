# 20.1. Region contract

Back to [20](20-micro-generation.md).

## Input and output

`generateMicroRegion(spec)` takes a serializable `RegionSpec`:

- Stable region `id`, integer `seed`, and builder class: `open`, `depot`,
  `courtyard`, `ruins` or `entry`.
- `cellSize` in game world units and a contiguous, non-overlapping list of cell
  coordinates. Masks may be concave, holed or span arbitrary tile boundaries.
  There is no six-cell tile restriction in this layer.
- Explicit perimeter runs (`ports`): side, first inside cell, length in segments,
  passage floor (`required`) and ceiling (`allowed`). Passage classes are `none`,
  `contestant`, `hunter`. A horizontal run advances x; a vertical run advances y.
- Macro-owned rectangular `reservations`, which must remain free of geometry and
  loot, plus optional loot budget/tier.
- Reversible class tuning: `density` in [0,1], integer `roomCells` in [4,12], and
  `decay` in [0,1]. Density adjusts proposals rather than guaranteeing a coverage
  fraction; decay affects ruins. The loot tier is metadata, not implemented weapon
  tier statistics or trap scaling.
- Optional `bodyProfile: 'live' | 'cell'`. Omission retains the legacy `live`
  profile. An `entry` region accepts `entry.count`, from 0 through 64, and uses
  24 when omitted.

Outputs are JSON-compatible `micro-1` artifacts containing the input, resolved
ports, proven clearance routes, placed `ElementTemplate` assemblies, budgeted
loot candidate positions, and counts of actual output and rejected placements.
An entry result also carries `entry.points`, `requested`, `shortfall`, and
`minimumSpacing`; a shortfall is explicit rather than represented by invented
positions.
There are no timestamps, runtime IDs or shared random draws. Named RNG streams
separate structural and decoration draws; cell input order is normalized.

The current defaults are 40 world units per cell, 8 loot candidates, tier 1,
density 0.55, room size 6 cells and decay 0.35. `live` preserves current game
radii of 12/23 and navigation clearance of 14/25 from `map/navigation.ts`.
`cell` is an explicit preview/profile choice. Its contestant and hunter sizes,
its doorway (2 cells) and its contestant-only squeeze (1.5 cells) are stated
once, in 52, "Units and scale". This is not a conversion of `map/macro/`'s abstract scale. A local artifact
made with `cell` must not silently change the live game's bodies, clearance,
capacity, or spawn rules.

### Briefs

`buildRegion(brief)` in `region-types.ts` takes a `RegionBrief`, the macro/micro
contract in `map/kernel/contract.ts` ([51](51-generation-chain.md) stage 5). It
checks the brief's own shape, then dispatches on its region type to a strategy
in the `REGION_TYPES` registry. A brief carries:
- the region type and its class rule's parameters
- optional scalar `guidance` and builder-supplied `delegation` reason metadata
  for a prepared child handoff (20.7); both are advisory
- cells, with `cellSize`
- optional `fillCells`, the owned area allowed for geometry, and `clearCells`,
  guaranteed clear access context near ownership; macro always derives both
  for its briefs (20.6)
- each cell's zone (tier, bonus and loot chance)
- core element counts
- portals
- optional `internalGuaranteed` runs, which preserve same-owner segment
  guarantees without turning them into external portals (20.6)
- hinted perimeter runs (`hints`, absent when nothing is hinted): guidance only, never an
  obligation (decided 2026-10-08; 52, "Segment prescriptions"; #287)

Bodies are the kernel's body scale, the `cell` profile.

The strategy returns `region-2`: its elements, core element sites, loot and a
manifest. It is not checked, because a builder's promise is its own to keep
(51 principle 9).

`hut` also exposes its owning `buildHutGeneration` object for the versioned
delegation snapshot in [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md). Production and nested
block lots still receive the ordinary flattened `region-2` result; the owning
object records retained house work, accepted/rejected yard offers and child cover.
The production adapter preserves the supplied root brief exactly for chain saves;
canonical ordering and derived margins remain in the owning execution object.
Full snapshots recover that result without today's registry; omitted-result
snapshots recover by rerunning the version-matched owning executor (20.7).

**Portals** are straight runs on the region's perimeter. `resolvePorts` never
walls them. The promise: every part of every portal is reachable by a hunter
from every other portal through original ownership and explicitly known clear context
(20.6). Fewer than two portals carry no requirement at all, stands included
(Corey, 2026-09-29, [17.2.4](17.2.4-contract-questions.md); 51.7). A lone portal
still receives its automatic margin, which is a fill constraint rather than part
of the promise.

**Hints** are maximal runs of the region's perimeter where a side states
`not-passable-hint` and the two sides guarantee no passage (51.4). Like a portal,
a hint lies on the perimeter with the region's cells on one side along its whole
length, and it names no other region: a run ends where the region passes to the
other side of the line (a concave region can), or where the owner across changes,
and the map's edge
and masked-out cells count as one owner. A hint never overlaps a portal, since a
guaranteed segment is a portal and wins. Both regions on a boundary get the same
runs. `briefErrors` checks them as input. No builder is obliged to use them, and
the elective fill diagnostic may report a hinted stretch left empty, never
refuse. They are derived from the same segments as the portals, so no wire or
macro version moves: briefs are a view, and a brief with no hints is unchanged.

**`portalStands`** gives hunter positions that cover a portal, on the original
seam and at most half a cell apart. A stand's body straddles the seam into the
clear margin on both sides (20.6), so a clear stand is also the crossing.
**`validatePortalReach`** is the elective check over final collision geometry,
within the original cells and the known-clear context. With two or more
portals, each stand must be clear, the stands of one portal must join, and each
portal must reach the first. With fewer it skips stand clearance, within-portal
joins and between-portal routes (#373). Existing input checks still apply,
including collision-shape validity, bounded portal and internal guaranteed runs,
and portal placement on the perimeter. If those checks pass, it reports no errors
and no routes. Geometry left in a
lone portal's margin is `validateFillConstraints`'s to name. **`diagnoseBuiltMap`** runs it on
every region of a composed map, also runs `validateFillConstraints`, and names
the regions that fail, as the
report's diagnostic ([51](51-generation-chain.md) stage 8).

Like the other route checks, it is sampled, on whole and half cells. A gap
with its edges on whole cells and a doorway wide fits a hunter; one that is
narrower, or whose edges lie off the samples, can be refused though a route
exists.

**Registered types.** A catalogue type ([54](54-region-types.md)) is registered
under its own name once B3 writes its strategy, in `strategies/`. 54's
drafted type (`market`, #169) is registered
under its own name too, bound to the nearest built strategy until its
own is written. So far:
- **`open` (#126)** builds nothing (17 M25): no geometry, core elements or loot.
- **`cover` (#127)** scatters clusters of small cover on a slot lattice. It
  keeps between-cluster aisles, places only in the reduced fill, and protects a
  hunter corridor on masks with up to 32 portals. Larger masks use a
  conservative boundary rule; hinted boundary attachments are allowed only
  where the remaining two-cell ring stays owned ([54](54-region-types.md) `cover`).
- **`rubble` (#128)** protects hunter routes between portals, then places
  seeded debris that can leave contestant-only squeezes. It rolls zone loot
  after geometry.
- **`ruins` (#157)** protects the same routes, through the same helpers in
  `strategies/scatter.ts`, then stands roofless rooms with broken walls and
  fallen slabs around them ([54](54-region-types.md) `ruins`).
- **`hall` (#158)** protects the same routes, then stands a staggered
  lattice of pillars on the reduced fill's largest rectangle, roofed or
  not ([54](54-region-types.md) `hall`).
- **`depot` (#159)** keeps aisles between pieces by spacing, clears portal
  approaches, and prioritizes hinted warehouse sites. It protects a hunter
  route tree at any portal count; the conservative boundary rule let
  warehouses seal portals on large masks (#347, [54](54-region-types.md) `depot`).
- **`plant` (#219)** keeps aisles between pieces by spacing: machines on a slot
  lattice, pipe runs between neighbours that block movement but not sight
  and leave one door-wide gap each, and an optional shed built as `depot`'s
  warehouse. It clears portal approaches, protects a hunter corridor on
  masks with up to 32 portals, and uses a conservative boundary rule on
  larger masks ([54](54-region-types.md) `plant`).
- **`checkpoint` (#220)** stands pieces across the route,
  so it keeps the promise by pruning during the solve instead of by spacing:
  it checks the empty shape with `validatePortalReach`, then keeps cached swept
  routes between portals and along each portal. Only paths touched by a new
  piece need another search. Barrier stations now begin one cell from the
  portal ends; post footprints keep the one-cell portal margin clear and try
  hinted perimeter boxes first ([54](54-region-types.md) `checkpoint`).
- **`park` (#221)** keeps aisles between pieces by spacing: round tree clumps
  and hedge runs, which block movement but not sight. It clears portal
  approaches, guides tree placement toward hints, and protects a hunter
  corridor at any portal count: first-portal routes up to 32 portals, a
  nearby-portal tree past that. The conservative boundary rule let clumps
  seal portals on large masks (#361, [54](54-region-types.md) `park`).
- **`block` (#161)** is the one decomposer. It splits its region into
  rectangular lots kept apart by 3-cell alleys, with no route search, and keeps
  lots two cells off each portal, so the alleys stay a hunter wide where they
  leave it. It hands
  each lot to `hut`, `depot`, `compound`, `ruins` or `cover`, with two
  frontage portals onto the alleys, and the alleys to `open`
  ([54](54-region-types.md) `block`).
- **`compound` (#160)** sites one walled compound by a bounded cell-level
  yard search. It keeps portal approaches clear and requires accessible gates;
  hinted perimeter boxes are tried first. It needs a contained 10-by-10 box
  for its 3-cell room ring and 4-by-4 court, without an additional yard inset
  ([54](54-region-types.md) `compound`).
- **`hut` (#129)** stands one house in its yard. It tries hinted boxes first,
  then uses cell-level yard connectivity and doorstep checks; it needs only
  the house's contained footprint, not an extra two-cell extent margin
  ([54](54-region-types.md) `hut`). After siting, it retains house, yard and
  circulation and offers suitable unused lobes to cover (20.7, #330). A hut
  unable to site a house takes terminal empty, preserving requested obligations.
- **`arrival` (#130)** uses the whole region as its one spawn child and sites
  `brief.coreElements.spawn` contestant points with the SDK's farthest-point
  spread before cover and loot claim space. Its optional `spacing` parameter is
  a minimum point-to-point distance in cells; without it, body clearance is
  the minimum. Cover keeps its aisle spacing and any piece that occupies a
  spawn or cuts its route to the first portal is omitted. A constrained region
  can site fewer points; the macro report names the shortfall.
- **`departure` (#131)** takes its region whole. It spreads
  `brief.coreElements.exit` exits first, farthest-first, so they sit as far
  apart as the region allows; the optional `exitSpacing` parameter (cells) is
  only a floor. It then spreads `hunter-spawn` points over the ground at least
  `setBack` cells from every exit, 6 by default, a proposal. Every site is
  reachable from the first portal, and cover and loot fill around them through
  the cover strategy's `coverAround`, which `arrival` uses too. A region that
  can't hold them all sites fewer, for the report to name.
- **`charging` (#132)** takes its region whole. It sites
  `brief.coreElements.charger` chargers with `spreadPoints` as near the
  region's middle as a contestant fits, falling back to the first portal's
  side when the middle can't be walked to. `coverAround` keeps cover and loot
  off `standing` cells around each (2 by default, a proposal), while the
  route check uses a contestant's radius (`ClearSite.reach`). Then the cover
  strategy's `coverNear` adds one container within 2 cells of that ground if
  no cover stands there, keeping cover's aisles so it can't divide a hunter's
  ground. A region too small for it gets none.

Today's builders stand in for the rest, with the ids `example-open`,
`example-depot`, `example-courtyard`, `example-ruins` and `example-entry`. The
prefix keeps a library from binding to them. `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` names what
the registry builds. Automatic margins use `types-21` (20.6), and `types-22`
restores `block`'s two-cell portal approach under them. `types-23` connects
depot portals through a nearby-portal tree instead of reserving a diagonal
from the first portal to every other one (#325). `types-24` records the hut-yard
to cover pilot (#330); `types-25` records compound exterior-yard adoption (#338,
[20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md)). The previous perimeter-placement update used `types-20`.
The version records changed builder outputs, while saved maps retain their
captured results instead of silently rebuilding them with another strategy (51
"Saving"). `types-21` adds fill exclusions, inherited child constraints and
known-clear access planning. The all-portals connectivity promise remains;
its validator now uses the original boundary and explicit clear context. A
brief without margins (one built by hand) derives its own (20.6), so every
builder has one path.
It does not edit library grids. `types-15` (#175) moved the route search's samples to whole and half cells,
and with them `hut` (no inset), `cover` and `depot` (2-cell aisles) and `compound`
(2-cell passages, centred doors). `types-16` (#170) rounds `ruins`' fallen slabs to a thousandth of a
world unit, so a browser and the server build the same slab. `types-17` (#219) gives `plant` its own
strategy, and takes in #218's warehouse designs in `depot`, which didn't bump it. `types-18` (#220) gives `checkpoint` its own strategy, and `types-19` (#221) gives `park` its own. `types-20` records the perimeter-placement updates to `cover`, `depot`, `plant`, `park`, `checkpoint`, `hut`, `compound`, and `block`. Each example builder:
- protects its portals' stands and a hunter route between them while it builds
- sites the brief's core elements before the loot fill, as the entry builder does,
  kind by kind, reachable from the first portal
- rolls each cell's loot chance, and gives loot its cell's tier, with no
  budget but the 64-slot tool limit

A brief is not the `RegionSpec` in "Input and output". The explicit-port spec and
its `micro-1` result (`MicroResult`) stay for the examples, the tools and
decomposition.

For brief decomposition, `inheritBriefMargins(parent, children)` carries the
original child cells and portals, inherited fill exclusions and new child portal
footprints. It rejects overlapping/outside child ownership and applies inherited
constraints idempotently. `block` uses it even though it dispatches child builders
directly. The explicit `RegionPort` floor/ceiling examples remain a separate
contract; they are not silently converted into whole-run macro guarantees.
