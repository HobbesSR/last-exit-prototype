# 42.84. WebTransport join latency against WebSocket under delay and loss (#1201)

Up: [42](42-performance-history.md). Transport design: [24.5](24.5-transport.md). Earlier lossy run: [42.59](42.59-split-delivery-under-loss.md).

## Finding

WebTransport join latency is dominated by delivering the `ready` envelope (~642 KiB uncompressed JSON, 98.5% of it `ready.map`), whose reliable-stream throughput collapses when RTT, jitter, and packet loss rise (with candidate causes in Quinn congestion control, per-datagram ACK/pacing behavior under the ordered relay, and the edge's 256 KiB `SEND_WINDOW`).

- **What crosses the stream before and at `ready` (`12x6`, `seed 4217`):**
  - Before `ready` (`16.5–21.0 KB` total across the ~9 s map-generation wait): `welcome` (`~3,450 B`: waiting-yard `map` `1,598 B`, `presentation` `709 B`, `state` `539–540 B`, `constants` `128 B`, `roster` `112 B`), `131–178` waiting-yard `state` frames (`~15–16 KB`), `lobby` (`~873 B`), and `ping` frames.
  - `ready` itself: `657,830–657,861 B` (`ready.map` `648,207–648,228 B`, `state` `7,738–7,740 B`, `roster` `940 B`, `presentation` `709 B`, `constants` `128 B`).
- **Stream throughput under ordered delay and loss (3 runs per profile/transport, median `p50`):**
  - `lan` (`rtt 4 ms`, `0%` loss): WT delivers `ready` in `137 ms` (`4,689.3 KB/s`), WS in `7 ms` (`91,775.8 KB/s`). End-to-end join is ~`9.6 s` on both, set by `~9.1–9.3 s` map generation.
  - `typical` (`rtt 80 ms`, `jitter 12 ms`, `1%` loss): WT `ready` takes `3,286 ms` (`1,823–4,998 ms`, `195.5 KB/s`), adding ~`3.1 s` over WS (`80 ms`, `8,030.3 KB/s`).
  - `wifi` (`rtt 120 ms`, `jitter 35 ms`, `3%` loss): WT `ready` takes `17,143 ms` (`15,549–19,402 ms`, `37.5 KB/s`), taking total join from `9.7 s` (WS) to `26.8 s`.
  - `poor` (`rtt 220 ms`, `jitter 70 ms`, `5%` loss): WT `ready` takes `34,141 ms` (`34,016–40,932 ms`, `18.8 KB/s`), taking total join from `9.7 s` (WS) to `43.6 s` (`max 50.2 s`).
  - `poor-lossless` (`rtt 220 ms`, `jitter 70 ms`, `0%` loss): WT `ready` still takes `5,174 ms` (`4,565–5,177 ms`, `124.2 KB/s`) against WS's `336 ms` (`1,912.0 KB/s`).
- **Observed transfer breakdown and candidate causes (and what this setup does not isolate):**
  - On `poor-lossless` (`0%` loss, `~371 ms` measured RTT), `edgeMetrics.readyDeliverToWrittenMs` (`4,465 ms` on run 0) accounts for 86% of `readyDeliveryMs` (`5,174 ms`). Note what the edge tap actually timestamps: Node's `DELIVER` write to `edgeLink` and the edge's cumulative `WRITTEN` report, not direct `send.write_all` call durations, Quinn congestion-window (CWND) state, flow-control stalls, or retransmissions.
  - Because `poor-lossless` carries `0%` configured drop while `edgeMetrics.readyDeliverToWrittenMs` stays high, candidate causes are `last-exit-edge`'s `SEND_WINDOW = 256 KiB`, QUIC slow-start CWND ramp, and per-datagram ACK delay under the ordered UDP relay; adding `3%–5%` packet loss (`wifi`, `poor`) across ~500 QUIC packets of `ready.map` cuts throughput further to `15.7–41.3 KB/s`, while `fast` datagrams (`297` on `wifi`, `639` on `poor` in run 0) arrive and queue in Chrome waiting for `ready`.
  - `#1201`'s realistic-path discrimination between QUIC windowing/slow-start and the synthetic relay paths remains open until an equal-path or fixed-delay control and direct Quinn / `send.write_all` instrumentation are added.

## Method

`node tools/perf/join-latency-1201.mjs --profiles=lan,typical,wifi,poor,poor-lossless --runs=3 --out=docs/reports/issue-1201/join-latency.json`

- **Environment.** Windows 11, Node `v24.15.0`, Chrome `157.0.8103.51` headless, `last-exit-edge` built in release, `roomThreads: false`, under the bench machine lock on 2026-11-26.
- **WebTransport path.** Chrome connects to `startLossyRelay` (`tests/helpers/lossy-relay.js`) with `ordered: true` forwarding UDP to the edge, and a zero-delay loopback TCP tap on `edgeLink` records Node's `DELIVER` timestamps and the edge's `WRITTEN` completions.
- **WebSocket path.** Chrome loads HTTP assets over direct loopback and connects `WebSocket` through `startTcpDelayProxy` (`tests/helpers/tcp-delay-proxy.js`, rewriting the initial `Host:` upgrade header so `server/websocket.js`'s origin check matches) using the same ordered per-direction RTT and exponential jitter formula as `lossy-relay.js`.

## Limits

- **Effective path models differ between the ordered UDP relay and the TCP delay proxy.** The userspace TCP delay proxy draws delay per loopback read chunk and leaves kernel loopback TCP congestion control and zero packet loss intact, whereas the ordered UDP relay draws per-datagram delay on both data packets and QUIC ACKs. Consequently, WS `wifi` and `poor` rows are lossless RTT+jitter baselines, and comparing WT `poor-lossless` (`5,174 ms`) against WS `poor-lossless` (`336 ms`) shows a slow `ready` transfer in this setup without isolating `SEND_WINDOW = 256 KiB` or slow start as the sole cause.
- **`12x6` only.** Larger maps (`24x12`, `36x18`) send larger `ready.map` payloads and were not measured here.
- **Decision checkpoint.** WebTransport join latency hits a clear payload + reliable-stream throughput blocker on `wifi` (`17.1 s` `ready` delivery) and `poor` (`34.1 s` `ready` delivery) before WebTransport can become the default; #1171 / envelope compression is the immediate size lever before #1207, while any Quinn `SEND_WINDOW`/congestion tuning remains a candidate follow-up requiring an equal-path/fixed-delay control and Quinn write/CWND instrumentation.
