# 42.56. WebTransport through a lossy relay, Node to Chrome (#1095)

Up: [42](42-performance-history.md). Design: [24.5](24.5-transport.md); the split it carries is
[24.2](24.2-lossy-transport-deltas.md), whose offline model is [42.50](42.50-lossy-deltas.md). This record checks
the transport itself: a real WebTransport session, a candidate server stack, Chrome's own QUIC and real packet
loss. It does not measure our codec, deltas or acknowledgements.

The server stack measured here is the Node addon. The edge has since been decided as Rust (27), so its own
server-side numbers come from 24.5's stage 1. The browser's and QUIC's behaviour recorded here does not depend on
the server library.

## Finding

**Real QUIC behaves as 42.50 modelled.** Datagrams lose frames, but the age of the newest frame a client holds
stays bounded. A reliable stream delivers everything, but its tail grows with loss, because each loss holds every
later frame until the retransmission arrives.
- **Staleness p95, datagrams against the reliable stream** (medians of three runs):
  - typical: 110 ms against 130 ms;
  - wifi: 174 ms against 300 ms;
  - poor: 345 ms against 1,463 ms, with the stream's runs spanning 1,036–2,514 ms;
  - bursty: 144 ms against 258 ms.
- **Against 42.50:** its model gave split-delivery datagrams a p95 of 99 ms on typical and 249 ms on poor, and
  today's reliable layers 199–249 ms and 599 ms.
  - Each frame here is sent whole every tick, so a lost frame costs one tick, as the model's
    acknowledged-baseline deltas do.
  - The probe's staleness also includes the up-to-50 ms gap between ticks.
  - The real reliable stream does worse on poor than the model's: see "Delay on poor" below.
- **The cap.**
  - **Reported sizes:** Chrome 157 reported `maxDatagramSize` 1,211 bytes, and the server 1,213, in every run.
  - **Oversize fails silently both ways.** A 1,300-byte datagram resolved without an error and never arrived,
    from Chrome to the server and from the server to Chrome. Every sender must check sizes itself.
- **Connection setup** costs about two round trips without loss: 17–24 ms on lan, 95–123 ms on good, 195–238 ms
  on typical. Lost handshake packets wait for QUIC's probe timeout: 0.55–1.0 s on wifi and 0.8–3.5 s on poor. A
  WebSocket join pays TCP's and TLS's equivalents, which this loopback probe could not load with loss.

### Delay on poor

For the jitter model, the expected medians are:
- **One way:** half the rtt plus jitter × ln 2.
- **Round trip:** the rtt plus about 1.678 × jitter, since the sum of two exponential legs has that median.
  Twice the jitter is the mean, not the median (review 906).

Measured medians match within 1–4 ms through wifi. Poor is the exception:
- **With loss:** one-way p50 is 190–199 ms against 158.5 expected.
- **Lossless control:** poor's delays without loss still read 171–185 ms. They also delivered only 99.5–99.7% of
  datagrams, although the relay dropped none.

The likely cause is reordering. Every packet draws its own exponential delay with a 70 ms mean, so packets
overtake each other far more often than on a real path. QUIC's loss detection then declares spurious losses, its
congestion window shrinks, and the sender queues or drops datagrams. That also explains why the reliable stream
collapses on poor (1–2.5 s at p95). This is an inference: the probe does not read the QUIC stacks' loss counters.

The datagram and stream rows share each session and the same relay, so their comparison holds either way. Absolute
numbers on poor are pessimistic for both.

## Method

`node tools/perf/transport-1095-probe.mjs --module-dir=DIR --profiles=lan,good,typical,wifi,poor,bursty,poor-lossless
--seconds=20 --runs=3 --out=docs/reports/issue-1095/transport-probe.json` (installation in the script's header).
- **Environment.** Node 24.15.0 and Chrome 157.0.8103.51 headless, on the development machine under the bench
  lock. Seed 1095.
- **Raw results:** [transport-probe.json](reports/issue-1095/transport-probe.json).
- **Server.** `@fails-components/webtransport` 1.6.12 with its prebuilt quiche binary, in the probe's Node
  process. It uses a self-signed ECDSA P-256 certificate, accepted by its hash (`serverCertificateHashes`).
- **Sending.** Every 50 ms, on absolute deadlines (400 frames in 20 s), the server sends one 350-byte frame on
  each of three channels. 350 bytes is the size of a 42.55 steady D message for a player view.
  - a datagram;
  - a length-prefixed message on one reliable unidirectional stream;
  - a WebSocket message.
- **Relay.** A UDP relay between Chrome and the server, on its own thread, applies 42.48's profiles (and 42.50's
  bursty one) to every QUIC packet in both directions: half the rtt plus exponential jitter, independent loss, and
  bursty outages per 50 ms slot. Handshakes, acknowledgements and retransmissions all cross it.
- **Measurement.** Every 10 ms the page records the age of the newest frame each channel has delivered
  (staleness). Every 100 ms it sends a ping datagram, which the server echoes, for the round trip.
- **Timing fixes after review 906.**
  - The first version scheduled the relay on the main thread. An armed timer held back packets due sooner, which
    inflated poor's delays.
  - The spinning also slowed the frame timer. Lan sent about 347 frames in 20 s instead of 400.
  - Windows' `setInterval` alone also loses cadence.
  - The relay now has its own thread, and senders tick on deadlines.

## Limits

- **One machine, loopback.** Server, relay and Chrome share the CPU. No real network path, NAT, or path MTU
  discovery was involved.
- **Reordering is overstated.** Independent per-packet jitter reorders far more than real paths do, which
  penalises both QUIC channels on poor (above).
- **WebSocket under loss is not measured.** TCP loss cannot be injected on loopback from user space. The reliable
  QUIC stream stands in for it. Both are reliable and ordered, and TCP's minimum retransmission timeout (200 ms or
  more) suggests the WebSocket would stall at least as long.
- **Chrome only.** Firefox and Safari were not run.
- **Server CPU** per session and per datagram was not measured. It belongs to stage 1 of 24.5, on the Rust edge.
- **Whole frames, one size.** 350 bytes never fragments. Fragmentation and 24.2's whole-frame recovery are stage 3's
  measurements.
