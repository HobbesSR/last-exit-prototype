# 24.3. Engine data model for the wire (#1139)

**Implementation update (#1153).** The measurements and per-field inventory below
record the pre-relational wire. [24.4](24.4-relational-data-model.md) now owns the
implemented numeric relations, separate self/observed records, roster delivery,
body-owned poses, dynamic-only gates, baseline shape compression and structured
localized events (`WIRE_VERSION = 4`, recording schema/minimum reader 20).
The binary codec, further constants removal and quantization remain pending.

Up: [24](24-networking-privacy.md). Encoding direction: [24.1](24.1-wire-encoding-design.md).
Codec evidence: [42.49](42.49-wire-codec-selection.md), [42.51](42.51-protobuf-head-to-head.md).
**Historical findings and remaining proposals.** The measured inventory predates
#1153; the implementation update above distinguishes landed contracts from later
codec/schema work. The live protocol remains JSON (24).

Corey, 2026-11-16 ([17.4](17.4-live-service.md) #17), verbatim: "It sounds like for us to truly
assess the wire model, we have to visit the engine's data model." The codec studies typed every
number as a generic `num`, because the projections declare nothing more specific. This record gives
every field of the live `state` message a type taken from what the engine produces, and lists the
engine-side choices that shape the wire, for Corey's decision.

## Summary

The engine's data is far more regular than its JSON suggests. Typing it field by field needs no
engine change for most fields; a handful of choices are Corey's.

- **Body records are Rapier's float32.** Every body position and velocity is exactly a Rapier
  float32 metre times `SCALE` = 40 (`shared/physics/world.ts`): `fround(v / 40) * 40 === v` held for
  100% of body values at both sizes. Sent as float32 metres, each takes 4 bytes and decodes
  exactly.
- **Other positions are logical values that usually equal the physics pose.**
  - **Players:** the round trip held for 99.9% of positions. Every failure was an integer spawn
    position such as 36, which the body holds as 35.999999046325684.
  - **Items:** 21–46%, because placements sit on integer coordinates.
  - **Effects and hook flights** can be JS arithmetic, for example slash effects.

  These need an exact form with a fallback, never silent canonicalization.
- **Counters, ticks, serials and indices are integers by construction.** Hit points, shields and
  damage are not: content's weapon-quality factors (1.08, 1.16, …; `content.ts`) are applied
  unrounded (`combat.ts`). They were integers in these traces only because the bots' weapons had no
  quality bonus. Content-derived quantities (cooldowns, radii, maximum hit points) are integers
  only as long as content says so.
- **Genuine float64 values** are heading (copied from the client's aim), projectile motion (JS
  trigonometry), some body radii (content arithmetic) and some item drop points.
- **Match constants travel every tick.** `version` and `physicsProfile` are the same 21-character
  string, sent twice per frame. `duration`, `cellChargeTicks` and the four prediction-motor values
  are content constants.
- **IDs are serial numbers wearing strings,** but a prefix does not name a field. Item identity is
  one domain with two allocators (`item-<n>` from map generation, `instance-<n>` from the match), and
  it moves between the ground ID, `instanceId`, `lineageId`, body `itemId` and reload `instanceId`.
  Player IDs are `c<i>` or `g<i>`. Body IDs repeat their actor or item ID with a prefix. Effects,
  projectiles and events already use numeric serials.
- **Enums are closed unions sent as strings.** Event text is a formatted sentence.
- **Some fields have no reader or no writer.** No client code reads 10 projected fields. Two own
  fields are declared in the projection but exist nowhere on `Player`. `cloak` is never set above 0
  yet, and no production code places traps yet.
- **Poses are sent twice.** A player's position travels in its player record and again in its
  actor body, and a physical item's in its item record and its item body. The player copies
  disagree only at spawn, where the player record holds the logical integer point and the body
  its float32 rounding.
- **"None" has two spellings** (absent and `null`). The player list is a union chosen by whose view
  it is.

## Method

- **Runtime probe** (`tools/perf/wire-data-model.mjs`). It runs the production match on 42.47's
  retained tuned caches (seed 4217, content-7, 12x6 and 36x18) for 1,200 ticks. Each tick it calls
  production `match.project` for every player's own view and for the directed (dev) view: 14,412
  projections per size. It walks every projected record and reports, per field and per record path:
  - value kinds and presence (absent, `null`);
  - for numbers: the share that are integers, float32-exact, **scaled float32** (a Rapier float32
    metre times `SCALE` = 40) and genuine float64, with ranges. Separately, every number is put
    through the proposed float32-metre round trip, `fround(v / 40) * 40 === v`, with the share that
    pass and examples of failures (review 868 on PR #1140);
  - duplicate pose copies: each player's and physical item's position against its body's;
  - for strings: cardinality, length and sample values;
  - coverage against the projection declarations (`projection-contract.ts`, `visibility.ts`).

  The probe refuses a cache whose map, simulation or server sources changed since it was generated,
  except for the reviewed declaration-only exports, which are pinned by the hash of their diff.
  Frame layers and the discovery encoder are a transport detail on top of the projection and are
  not walked. Delayed spectators receive the dev view's records later, so they add no fields.
- **Static inventory.** For each declared field: its TypeScript type, its producer and whether
  client prediction reads it, from the source.
- **Unobserved families.** The bot traces produced no traps, heard cues, tethers, hook flights,
  reloads, carried cells, or box and convex shapes. Content-7 has traps and the hook kit, but the
  bots never used them. Those fields are typed from the source alone and marked unobserved.

## Findings

**Numbers.**
- **Body records.** Rapier stores metres as float32, and the engine multiplies by 40, which is not
  a power of two. A body position therefore reaches the projection as a float64 such as
  9820.670166015625. The float32-metre round trip held for every body `x`, `y`, `vx` and `vy` at
  both sizes, about 172,000–184,000 bodies each. A codec that sends `fround(v / 40)` and multiplies
  by 40 on decode is exact for them: IEEE multiplication is deterministic in Node and Chrome alike,
  and 24.1 requires that exactness for prediction.
- **Logical positions** come from the gameplay owners, not straight from Rapier, so the round trip
  is not guaranteed:
  - **Players:** 99.9%. Every failure is an integer spawn or transit point (36, 516, 4572, …),
    and its body holds the float32 rounding of it.
  - **Items:** 21–46%. Placements are integers, and drop points are JS math; only physical items
    copy their body's pose.
  - **Effects:** 100% in the traces, where every effect was `loot` at a player's pose. Slash
    effects are placed by JS math (`combat.ts:55`), and the bots never produced one.
  - **Gates** are integers; the round trip does not apply.
- **Genuine float64:**
  - `heading`, copied from the client's `input.aim`;
  - projectile `x`, `y`, `dx` and `dy`, from `cos`/`sin` times speed;
  - 28–38% of shape radii, content-derived per kit, such as 11.2875;
  - 0.3% (12x6) and 3.7% (36x18) of ground-item coordinates, from drop points (`loot.ts`).
- **Integers by construction:** ticks, counters, countdowns, serials, slot and roster indices, and
  discovery cells. Negative values occur only in `hazardX` (−80) and `lastSeq` (−1 for none).
- **Not integers, although the traces showed only integers:**
  - **Hit points, shields and projectile `damage`.** Weapon-quality damage factors (1, 1.08, 1.16,
    1.24, 1.32; `content.ts:102`) multiply shot damage (`combat.ts:78`), and damage is applied to
    shields and hit points unrounded (`combat.ts:30–31`). A quality-2 pistol deals 10.8. The bots'
    weapons had no quality bonus.
  - **Content-derived quantities** such as maximum hit points, cooldowns, reload durations and
    radii. They are integers only while content's numbers are.
- **Large integers.** Body `generation` reaches about 32,000 by tick 1,200, because it is a world
  serial. Discovery cell indices run to about 21,600.

**Identifiers.**
- **Strings around serials:** players `c<i>` or `g<i>`; gates `door-<n>`; buildings `building-<n>`;
  map nodes `<kind>@<x>,<y>`.
- **Item identity is one transferable domain**, not one namespace per field (review 870 on PR #1140):
  - **Allocators:** map generation names ground items `item-<n>` from the map's shared serial.
    During a match, `allocateItemId` (`item-instances.ts:38`) issues `instance-<n>` from
    `itemSerial`, skipping any identity already in use.
  - **Moves:** picking up a whole ground item keeps its ground ID as the carried `instanceId`
    (`equipment.ts:82–83`). Dropping reuses the carried `instanceId` as the new ground ID
    (`loot.ts:58`). A reload copies the weapon's `instanceId` (`ammo.ts:76`). `lineageId` keeps the
    originating identity.
  - **Consequence:** a ground item's ID can be `instance-42`, and a reload's `instanceId` can be
    `item-3506`. The same number can appear under both prefixes (`item-42` and `instance-42`),
    naming different items.
  - **Consumers of the domain:** ground `id`, carried `instanceId` and `lineageId`, body `itemId`
    (and the body ID `item:<id>`) and reload `instanceId`.
- **Player identity** has two prefixes for one domain: `c<i>` for contestants and `g<i>` for
  gladiators. `c0` and `g0` are different players.
- **Body IDs** are always `actor:<actorId>` or `item:<itemId>`. In every frame checked, the ID was
  derivable from the other field.
- **Numeric serials:** effects, projectiles and events use the game serial.
- **Mixed declarations:** `itemId` on a body is declared `string | number`.

**Constants and slow data.**
- **Content and match constants** sent every tick: `version`, `physicsProfile` (equal to `version`
  once physics attaches, `game.ts:111`), `duration`, `cellChargeTicks` and `predictionMotor`.
- **Roster data** sent in every player record: `name`, `role`, `kit` and `seat`.
- **Map placement** sent with every gate record: gate geometry and `buildingId`. Frame layers already
  send gates as a keyframe plus changes, so the steady cost is small, but each keyframe resends all
  of it. A 36x18 dev view holds 787 gates.

**Duplicates.** The same pose travels in two records:
- **Players:** a player's `x`/`y` and its actor body's `x`/`y`. The copies are identical in 99.9%
  of pairs (66,944 of 67,019 at 12x6; 80,451 of 80,535 at 36x18). Every differing pair checked at
  12x6 was at tick 0: the player record holds the logical integer spawn point, and the body holds
  Rapier's float32 rounding of it (36 against 35.999999046325684). From the first step onward, the
  player record is copied from the Rapier transform (`tick.ts`), so the copies agree. No rule says
  which copy is authoritative, and client prediction reads both.
- **Physical items:** an item's `x`/`y` and its item body's are identical in every pair (117,087
  and 92,004).
- **Players without a body,** for example after elimination, keep their only pose in the player
  record (10,692 and 15,756 records).

**Presence.**
- **Two spellings of none.** `reload`, `charging` and `respawnAt` are `null` when present and
  missing otherwise. Empty inventory slots are `null` (84% of slots), which is positional and
  meaningful.
- **Kind-dependent fields.** `weaponType`, `ammo`, `charge`, `count` and `family` depend on the
  item's `kind`, which already serves as a discriminant.
- **The player list** holds the viewer's own record, with 35 possible fields, among observed
  records with 15. Which is which follows from the viewer's ID, not from the record.

**Dead or unread fields**, verified by search:
- **No client reader:**
  - item `droppedBy`, `pickupAfter`, `nodeId`, `tier`, `instanceId`, `lineageId` and `quality`;
  - own `attackCd`;
  - body `omega`;
  - header `directed` (the client derives its own, `public/client.js:79`).

  The map lab reads `tier` from map data, not from state.
- **No declaration or writer:** own `cell` and `nodeId` are in `OWN_FIELDS` but are neither declared
  on `Player` nor written. The HUD finds the carried cell from the inventory.
- **No writer above zero yet:** `cloak` (declared and read).
- **No producer yet:** traps. `map.traps` starts empty and only tests and tools fill it.

**Corey's direction on these** (2026-11-16, [17.4](17.4-live-service.md) #17):
- **Cloak stays, as a planned mechanic.** "I'm imagining producing a variety of mechanics with
  powers, abilities, items, weapons, utilities, physics, and environmental interactions."
- **Item `tier` and `quality` stay.** They "may have a number of UI representations", and today's
  item look "may be just one of a number of classes of renderings both on the map and in
  inventory".

The schema must therefore be ready to grow. New mechanics will add fields, record kinds and enum
values, so the codec has to version and extend them cleanly rather than be sized to today's
content.

**Prediction.** Client prediction (`public/physics-prediction.js`, `input-prediction.js`) reads:
- the tick, the motor, the tether flag and `physicsProfile`;
- each player's `id`, `status`, `boost`, `stun`, `x`, `y` and `role`;
- each gate's `id`, `x`, `y`, `w`, `h` and `open`;
- each body's `id`, `x`, `y`, `angle`, `vx`, `vy`, `generation`, `actorId`, `itemId`,
  `discontinuity` and shape;
- each tether's `a` and `b`.

It also copies the whole own record into its predicted state. These are the fields 24.1 keeps
exact, and every one has an exact wire type below. Since #1096 prediction reconciles by the frame's
tick against each input's tick stamp, not by `lastSeq`, and the client's input clock
(`shared/input-clock.ts`) reads the own `inputMargin`.

**Limits.**
- **Bot traces.** 1,200 ticks of a 12,000-tick match, played by bots. The phase never reached
  `finished`, the hazard had not advanced, and no client sent input sequences, so `lastSeq` stayed
  −1.
- **Unexercised content.** Traps, hook flights, tethers, heard cues, reloads, box and convex shapes
  and stack items never occurred. Those rows are typed from the source.
- **What the probe can't see.** It reads projected values, not the engine's intent. Integer-valued
  traces do not make a field an integer: hit points proved fractional under supported content. The
  proposed types below follow the producers, and proposal 2 declares only integers by
  construction.

## Per-field inventory

"Prediction" marks fields read by client prediction, which must stay exact (24.1, 29.2). "Proposed
wire type" is a proposal for #1131's schema, not a decision.
- **"float32 metres"** means `fround(value / 40)`, multiplied by 40 on decode. It is used only where
  the round trip held for every value: body records.
- **"Exact number"** keeps any JS number exact: integers as varints, float32 metres when the round
  trip returns the same value, otherwise float64. Nothing is canonicalized.
- **"Metre round trip"** in the observed column is the share of values that would survive the
  float32-metre form.

Fields not listed are the frame-layer and discovery encodings (24), which wrap these records
unchanged. Inventory entries and the carried cell use the item record. Families the traces never
exercised are typed from their producers and marked unobserved. Where a producer is missing, the
type is marked open.

## Proposals for Corey

Each proposal is independent. Each needs a decision before #1131 closes its schema. Proposals 1 and 2
change no behaviour. The others change the projection, the client or both, and so the wire
contract (`WIRE_VERSION`).

1. **Type positions by their producer, exactly.**
   - **Body records** (`x`, `y`, `vx`, `vy`) travel as float32 metres: 4 bytes, exact for every
     observed value.
   - **Logical positions** (players, items, effects, hook flights and traps) use the exact number
     form: an integer, float32 metres when the round trip holds, else float64. A value is never
     rounded to fit.
   - **Affects:** wire only; no engine change.
   - **Not proposed:** making `SCALE` a power of two or using metres as the world unit. Either would
     make Rapier values float32 in world units, but would change Rapier's inputs and so the physics
     fixture.
   - **With proposal 11,** player and physical-item poses move to their bodies, and most remaining
     positions become pure Rapier values.
2. **Declare the integers by construction.** Give ticks, counters, countdowns, serials and slot and
   roster indices integer types in `shared/types.ts`, with a debug assertion at projection, so the
   wire can use varints safely.
   - **Excluded:** hit points, shields, damage and every content-derived quantity, which keep exact
     number types. Supported quality content already makes hit points fractional.
   - **Affects:** types and projection only; no behaviour change.
3. **Move match constants out of the per-tick state** into the welcome or ready message: `version`,
   `physicsProfile`, `duration`, `cellChargeTicks` and the prediction motor.
   - **Roster data:** a further step would send `name`, `role`, `kit` and `seat` in a roster
     message when it changes, and identify players by their roster position in each tick.
   - **Affects:** welcome handling, the HUD and prediction's reads.
4. **Number the identifiers.** Two options:
   - **(a) Wire only.** The wire codec encodes each identity domain, not each field.
     - **Encoding:** an ID becomes a namespace tag plus its serial (item identity: `item` or
       `instance`; players: `c` or `g`), so `item-42` and `instance-42` stay distinct. Every field of
       a domain uses the same encoding.
     - **Refusal:** a form outside the domain's declared namespaces is refused, so a nonconforming
       ID fails loudly. IDs with no reviewed representation (stations and chargers, map node IDs)
       stay exact strings.
     - **Body ID:** dropped, and rebuilt from `actorId` or `itemId`.
   - **(b) Engine.** The engine itself moves to numeric IDs per family. That reaches map
     generation's shared serial, the fixture, recordings and many tests.

   Option (a) is the smaller step and keeps the engine unchanged.
   - **Reference:** option (b) is where the DOOR primer's stable identifiers and explicit
     relations point: one entity identity shared by a player, its body and its records, with
     owner, contents and building links as integer references (`docs/references/door-primer.md` (PR #1141)). This proposal does not
     depend on it.
5. **Enum tables.** Give every closed union an ordered runtime table in its owner (as `JOIN_MODES`
   already is), and send indices. `weapon` is already an index, into the weapon table's key order,
   so a content reorder silently changes its meaning; it should index a declared table.
   - **Events:** sending events as structured records (kind, subject, object) for the client to
     format would replace free-text sentences. That affects the event producers, the HUD and
     recordings.
   - **Reference:** the primer's distinction between system events and committed model events
     (`docs/references/door-primer.md` (PR #1141)) points further: deaths, pickups and door changes published as deterministic model
     events, from which the client derives its presentation, such as today's `loot` effect
     records. That is a candidate for its own decision, not part of this proposal.
6. **One spelling of "none".** Optional values are absent rather than `null` (`reload`, `charging`,
   `respawnAt`). Inventory slots stay positional.
   - **Affects:** those types, their writers and their few readers.
7. **Separate the viewer's own record.** Send it as its own field, `self`, with `players` holding
   observed records only, so the player list is no longer a union.
   - **Affects:** the projection (`visibility.ts`), the client's `me()` lookups, and the discovery
     delta wiring in `room-views.js`.
8. **Remove dead and unread fields from the projection.** That serves privacy minimization (24) as
   well as size.
   - **Clear cases:** own `cell` and `nodeId`, gate `kind` and header `directed`.
   - **Kept, per Corey:** `cloak` (a planned mechanic) and item `tier` and `quality` (future
     renderings).
   - **Still open:** the other unread fields, which are item `droppedBy`, `pickupAfter`, `nodeId`,
     `instanceId` and `lineageId`, own `attackCd` and body `omega`. Each either gets a planned
     reader or leaves the projection.
9. **Send static geometry once.** Gate placement belongs with map delivery, and only `open` and
   `locked` change during a match. A body's shape is constant for its generation, so it could
   travel only when the body appears or changes.
10. **Leave the genuine float64 values to their owners.** Heading, from client aim, could be
    quantized where input enters (#1096); that would change the authoritative shot direction, a
    gameplay change. Projectiles move in straight lines from spawn and could be sent once.
    - **Recommendation:** neither here. Keep them float64 on the wire until #1096 and #1094 decide.
11. **One pose per entity on the wire.** Where a player or physical item has a body, its pose
    travels once, in the body. The player and item records drop `x`/`y`, and a player without a
    body keeps them.
    - **Why:** the duplicate copies above, which can disagree, with no stated rule for which one
      wins. The primer warns against two authoritative copies of the same state (`docs/references/door-primer.md` (PR #1141)); the
      measurement is the justification.
    - **Affects:** the projection, and the client's reads of player and item positions in the
      renderer, HUD, minimap and prediction.
    - **Why the copies disagree, now answered:** only at spawn, where the player record keeps
      the logical integer point and the body holds its float32 rounding. Sending the body's pose
      makes the physics value authoritative on the wire.

**Recommended order.**
- **Without behaviour change:** 1 and 2 can start now.
- **Relational prerequisite (#1146, #1147):** see [24.4](24.4-relational-data-model.md)
  for the accepted design direction resolving proposal 4's relational strategy (including
  implicit dense indices), proposal 9's static geometry split and proposal 5's structured
  localized events ahead of #1130's field-typed codec comparison and #1131.
- **Remaining wire-contract changes:** the remaining constants from 3, 6 and the clear cases of 8 belong in
  #1131's schema closure under one `WIRE_VERSION` bump (with 4, 5 and 9 shaped by 24.4).
- **Waiting on product or later work:** the rest of 8, plus 10 (heading and projectile
  quantization deferred cleanly until #1094/#1096 revisit them).

**What this enables.** With these types, the field-typed codec comparison on #1130 can be run fairly.
D and protobuf would both be built from the same per-field types, measured on bytes, Chrome decode
and a deployable bundle (42.51, review 866).
