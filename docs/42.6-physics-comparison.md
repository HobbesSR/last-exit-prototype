## Decision: neither candidate is qualified

Do not select or activate either candidate for production. Both fail unchanged
contact correctness gates, including a legal dense-doorway start. The shared
spring integration also misses its reference; that is not engine-selection evidence.
The lab is useful negative evidence, not a completed production engine. P1 (#438)
needs a reviewed response to these failures before proceeding. The frozen fixture,
game tick order, content IDs, protocol and playback implementation are unchanged.

Rapier remains the candidate to investigate first if a further bounded evaluation
is authorized. That is a remediation recommendation, not an engine selection:
its moving-pair CCD and contact penetration failures must be resolved on these
same witnesses. Do not trade those failures for a faster timing score. The custom
candidate additionally carries its own narrow phase, contact impulses, sweeps,
angular response, constraints and resource lifetime; expanding it into a general
engine exceeds P0's scope. Planck remains the reserved alternative from 29.1,
not a tested replacement.

## What is compared

Both candidates receive the same seeded body descriptions and tick-boundary
actions at 20 Hz, with 2/4/8 fixed substeps. Rapier uses zero gravity and 40 world
units per metre; native solver iterations, internal PGS iterations, contact error,
prediction distance and CCD settings are recorded. The custom solver uses eight
contact iterations, a 12-event CCD cap and 0.001-unit positional slop. No tuning
changes occur during a run.

Both have the same bounded velocity-target motor and constant floor deceleration.
Centre springs use explicit Hooke/damping forces, cleared each substep; ropes use
predictive, force-limited maximum-distance impulses. Native Rapier joints are
**not evaluated** by this candidate. The spring errors measure this game-owned
integration policy, not either engine's native spring capabilities. Switching to
native joints alone would not remove the first-order integration error: an
implicit first-order spring also misses this reference at 2/4/8 substeps.
A native-joint experiment would require an explicit force-cap/break policy and
its own accuracy evidence.

The legal-start witnesses cover pair/slide/blocker contacts, a three-body corner,
a narrow queue, ice/slow terrain, steering during an impulse, pushes, resting and
rotating props, waking, thin walls, moving pairs, a boxed mask hole, coordinates
near one million units, spring decay, slack/unequal/blocked/opposing ropes, joint
deletion and deferred door closing. Coincident spawn and zero-distance spring
probes intentionally begin with overlap and are labeled separately. The caller
defers closure until the door footprint is clear; this is not crushing behavior.

Independent geometry records maximum penetration at each observed substep.
The circle path test is analytic for straight relative movement. Box paths sample
at most 1024 interpolated points; zero detected crossings does not prove arbitrary
continuous containment, especially within an engine's internal CCD trajectory.
The spring witness compares the absolute displacement from its 40-unit rest
length at one second with the analytic two-unit-mass oscillator: expected
0.11297352133810565 units, tolerance 5% of that reference. No initial-amplitude
tolerance is substituted to hide integration error.

Representative failures from legal starts (world units unless marked percent):

| Candidate / metric | 2 substeps | 4 substeps | 8 substeps | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Custom doorway static penetration | 0.0877 | 0.0242 | 0.0150 | <= 0.01 |
| Rapier doorway static penetration | 1.0167 | 0.2750 | 0.2511 | <= 0.01 |
| Rapier moving-pair pass-through: sampled overlap | 16.0 | 16.0 | 16.0 | <= 0.5; no pass-through |
| Rapier mask-hole static penetration | 1.0422 | 1.0422 | 0.7988 | <= 0.01 |

The moving-pair failure is complete pass-through at all three counts, not a
contact that settles with 16 units of residual penetration. Both radius-eight
bodies retain velocities +1400/-1400, swap sides, and finish at x=245/-245
after four ticks. Symmetric starts put a substep sample at exact coincidence,
giving the 16-unit overlap diagnostic. It does not describe the severity of the
full tunnel. This confirms the adapter's documented limitation for CCD with two
moving bodies.
The metric returns no crossing when either endpoint overlaps, so these intervals
are counted as penetration and `crossings: 0`. That counter does not establish
containment; the sample overlap already fails the gate.

Rapier's thin-wall case also exceeds 0.01 at two/four substeps (0.1436), and the
million-coordinate case reaches 0.0625 at two/four substeps. Eight substeps pass
those two particular probes but do not fix the other failures. No observed state
was non-finite. Rope witnesses remain within the one-unit bound, and deferred
closure waits nine ticks before adding the clear door. These are observations on
the retained fixtures, not guarantees over arbitrary geometry or loads.

The strengthened mixed-role scene adds Rapier static penetration failures at
four/eight substeps (0.1253/0.1247). Blocked-pull rope excess is at most 0.6615
custom and 0.6604 Rapier, within the one-unit bound.

### Shared spring integration limitation

The unchanged spring gate fails in both adapters, but does not distinguish the
engines. Measured relative errors at 2/4/8 substeps are 49.66/27.51/14.43% custom
and 65.54/33.74/17.10% Rapier. Force is computed once per substep in both; the
custom result matches a bare symplectic Euler oscillator to four decimal places.
The reduced mass is 0.5, so relative displacement obeys
`x'' + 8x' + 40x = 0`, with `x(0)=10` and `x'(0)=0`.

A focused calculation integrates that equation for 20 ticks. For
`h=0.05/substeps`, symplectic Euler updates
`v += h*(-40*x-8*v); x += h*v`; backward Euler updates
`v = (v-40*h*x)/(1+8*h+40*h*h); x += h*v`.

| Substeps at 20 Hz | Symplectic Euler error | Backward Euler error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 14.43% | 18.78% |
| 16 | 7.38% | 9.70% |
| 32 | 3.73% | 4.93% |

For these two first-order schemes, error decreases approximately linearly with
substep duration, and about 32 substeps are needed for this particular gate.
At one second the analytic absolute displacement is only 1.13% of the initial
10 units, so the 5% tolerance allows about 0.00565 units absolute error.
Native implicit integration alone would not resolve that requirement.
#451 must put the intended time point, tolerance, or dedicated spring substep
rate to the user before changing the contract in 41.1. Keep the existing gate
and recorded failures until that decision; contact failures independently
prevent either candidate qualifying.

Node and Chrome repeats and checkpoint continuation match all 78 scenario/substep
traces for each candidate. Rapier also matches all 78 hashes across runtimes. The custom Node/Chrome hashes differ for `rotating-long-box`
at all three substep counts. At two substeps the maximum final-state scalar
difference is about 1.07e-14; at four/eight the final state agrees but an earlier
trace sample differs. Thus exact cross-runtime custom determinism is unsupported
even where the visible difference is negligible. Checkpoints are a debug
capability; recorded-state playback neither initializes nor depends on this lab.

## Costs and limits

The browser run used Windows 10.0.26200, an AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (24 logical CPUs),
and installed headless Chrome **155.0.8059.85**. It retained 100 measured ticks
after 20 warm-up ticks per timing cell. At four substeps, clustered workloads
gave these **step-only p95 milliseconds**:

| Actors | Props | Ropes | Custom | Rapier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | 0 | 0 | 0.1 | 0.2 |
| 27 | 0 | 0 | 0.9 | 0.3 |
| 64 | 0 | 0 | 4.8 | 0.5 |
| 11 | 128 | 8 | 13.4 | 0.9 |
| 27 | 128 | 8 | 16.8 | 0.9 |
| 64 | 128 | 8 | 33.5 | 1.2 |
| 11 | 512 | 32 | 213.2 | 2.6 |
| 27 | 512 | 32 | 235.0 | 2.6 |
| 64 | 512 | 32 | 264.7 | 3.1 |

These are timing observations, **not supported envelopes**: both candidates fail
correctness. For 64 actors/512 props/32 ropes, Chrome p99 step cost was 266.2 ms
custom versus 3.3 ms Rapier. Additional p95 query/serialization costs were
6.3/0.4 ms custom and 0.2/2.0 ms Rapier. Those original query paths run
along the northern fringe, so they underrepresent populated casts. Different serialization costs include
each adapter's state extraction and numeric JSON representation. The same case's
p95 lab frame was 104,497 versus 96,435 bytes; that is not a protocol-size promise.

Node v24.15.0 retained 171 timing cells per candidate (342 total), plus six
legacy controls; Chrome retained 21 per candidate (42 total). The full Node
matrix includes all population combinations at 2/4/8 substeps and separate
sleeping populations. At four substeps, the clustered 64/512/32 case had
step p95/p99 of 250.45/254.77 ms custom and 3.10/3.27 ms Rapier.

The supplemental query benchmark freezes a scene after one common 12.5 ms step,
then casts 128 identical paths (96 through initial body centres and 32 offset)
for 100 samples after 20 warm-ups across 18 diagonal populations. Input hashes
match across both candidates and runtimes, and every cell records at least 96
hits. At clustered 64/512/32, cast p95 is 7.91/1.68 ms in Node and 6.30/1.10 ms
in Chrome (custom/Rapier). These are separate query microbenchmarks, not costs
to add to a different evolved scene as an exact total.

Cold browser module import plus first empty world took 2.6 ms custom and 37.9 ms
Rapier (22.5 ms import, 15.4 ms first world). This is local loopback delivery,
not WAN download latency. The pinned compat ESM file is 3,409,863 uncompressed
bytes; the packaged WASM is 2,404,467 bytes. Browser clock quantization produces
zero for some sub-0.1-ms lifecycle operations; zero is not zero actual work.

Across the four synthetic RTT profiles, Chrome p95 prediction work was at most
0.2 ms custom and 0.3 ms Rapier. Maximum authority error was about 14.52 world
units for both, despite zero hidden-ID fields in the pre-authorization payloads.
This demonstrates why exact backend determinism cannot remove hidden-contact
corrections. Only zero-to-two replay ticks were exercised by delivered corrections;
the configured eight-tick cap is not stressed by this witness.

Timing excludes geometric diagnostics, hashes and report I/O. Step time, 128 swept
circle queries, state serialization and UTF-8 frame bytes are separate series.
The serialized lab frame includes static poses and is not a production wire or
recording schema. Synthetic capacity maps use four bounds and one pillar per
hundred bodies; they do not represent generated production geometry or full-room
AI, visibility, networking and recording cost. Sparse and clustered workloads are
distinct. Requested sleeping populations are separate from awake populations;
the custom solver exposes sleeping metadata but has no automatic sleeping engine.

The legacy timing control calls actual `moveBody` once per tick on the identical
actor-only layout. It has no rigid-body contacts, constraints or projectile casts,
so it is a control for the old movement path, not a feature-equivalent competitor.
No room-capacity claim or historical slowdown fix follows from these measurements.

Lifecycle checks repeatedly create/dispose worlds and perform 128-body synthetic
drop/pickup bursts, endpoint removal and ID reuse. Counts reach zero and access
after disposal is rejected. Forced Node GC and unforced browser heap readings
measure wrapper/runtime retention; they are not a WASM allocator census.

Prediction uses only a projected island. Its synthetic ordered connection has
0/50/100/200 ms RTT, one-tick jitter, a stalled delivery batch, hidden contact,
delayed door knowledge and body removal. Corrections rebuild from an authorized
snapshot and replay at most eight ticks. The checkpoint comparison restores that
same projected island, never the hidden authoritative world. These measurements
do not validate production fog/protocol privacy, renderer smoothing, reconnect or
background-tab behavior; those remain P3/P4 integration gates.

## Required next decision

Follow-up #451 owns this decision and blocks #438.

Keep the existing numeric gates and gameplay unchanged. Review a bounded follow-up
that addresses contact/CCD containment and large-coordinate precision, then
rerun these witnesses before P1. Separately, ask the user whether the spring
reference time, tolerance or a dedicated integration rate expresses the intended
requirement in 41.1; the current first-order integration misses the gate regardless
of engine choice. A local coordinate origin,
native joint experiment or changed collision policy is a contract decision, not
an unrecorded fix. If the supported scope or tolerance changes, record it in
29.1/41.1 with the reason rather than modifying a witness until it passes.

Rapier's Apache-2.0 artifact adds WASM startup, binary pinning, allocation disposal,
world/handle lifecycle and browser loading work. It offers a maintained rigid-body
engine and snapshot facility, but wrappers still own motor, filtering, privacy and
failure policy. The custom option avoids that external runtime while making this
project responsible for every unsupported solver feature and numerical failure.
Source size is not a selection criterion. See the pinned
[Rapier package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@dimforge/rapier2d-compat/v/0.21.0),
[CCD documentation](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_body_ccd/)
and [snapshot API](https://rapier.rs/javascript2d/classes/World.html).
