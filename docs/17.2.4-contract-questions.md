2. **M2. When the proof gates generation.** Reachability is inferred (M6), so
   the macro proof is a link that has to hold, and it gates placement.
   Still open: what it must connect. *Assumption:* every layout region must
   be in the spawn region's component. That is the pessimistic reading of
   "everything must be reachable by a hunter", and it means designs prescribe
   passable segments wherever regions meet, so that portals derive there. Your September 27 option, "we may make
   perimeter segments default to passable with explicit nonpassable
   indicators", would ease that authoring burden. It is not adopted, and an
   unstated segment stays no guarantee.
3. **M3. What a portal obliges of geometry.** Today's SDK ports are
   supplied by the caller, and `resolvePorts` walls each one except for a
   centred gap (a doorway of 2 cells, or a squeeze of 1.5) before any builder
   runs.
    - (2026-09-29) "Ahh,I'm not sure if we've got like first class things here. resolvePorts, is that... like trying to identify contiguous passable segments? I guess Port is a fair term for it. Maybe we can make it Portal or something. Anyway, they should be derived things and used for checking and validation. At the macro level, passable sections that become derived portals would be authored. In micro, the SDK would provide utilities to help arrive at such reachability conclusions, but it doesn't have the benefit of the rigid grid system, as geometry can be placed arbitrarily."

    - (2026-09-29) "Authors don't write portals, but they definitely think about how their passable sections will build portals."

   Recorded in 51:
   - A **portal** is derived, never authored: the guaranteed-passable stretch
     of a boundary. Authors write passable prescriptions, designed with the
     portals they will form in mind, and the Map Lab previews them (B1).
   - Portals state what must stay passable, and are what validation checks.
     They place no geometry, so no jambs.
   - The SDK supplies utilities for reaching reachability conclusions over
     free-form geometry.

    - (2026-09-29) "It's the builder's responsibility to ensure that from that portal all other portals in its (sub)region is reachable. So if they put something next to it that effectively make it not passable into the rest of the region, then they are promising there are no other portals on its perimeter to reach. A portal is passable if a hunter can pass through its geometry. so two segments that meet at a right angle, I haven't done the math, but I think that would mean a hunter couldn't pass through that, so that set of passable segments would not count as a portal."

   Recorded in 51 stages 3 and 5:
   - A portal is straight and at least a hunter's diameter long. A right-angle
     pair doesn't count.
   - From each portal, every other portal of the (sub)region is reachable.
     Blocking a portal promises there's no other portal to reach.

Ahh but this still gets to the rule of reachable from other portals.    - A region with one portal has no reachability requirement.
   - Neighbours compose without coordinating, and two one-portal regions
     sharing their portal are rejected by the proof.

   Enforcement and validation:
    - (2026-09-29) "We're not enforcing anything on the builders or validating at all. The prescriptions are for macro reasoning purposes. Validation support for a variety of things can be provided by the micro SDK, but it is elective.    **M3 reconsideration during #214 (2026-10-09), unresolved.** Corey:
   "I suppose the map can be more interesting if a portal is reachable by passing through portals if we make the policy all passable segments must have a passable / empty cell width of area around them. Maybe that's a later constraint that can relax what meets the passable requirement. But it significantly effects authorship so it's a policy to decide sooner than later.    The initial lead analysis misread this as primarily a relaxation for narrow
   regions and discussed disconnected portal approaches as a policy alternative.
   Corey corrected that interpretation:

   "Hmmm boy there's so much wrong with that it's hard to know where to start. If there is a portal on one edge, and one on the other edge of some rectangle region/subregion, for example, And a wall splits them off, then their is no path for a hunter to get from one portal to the next or the other. That base case failing means that algorithm is wrong regardless of policy. This isn't an issue of oh they can get past if they partially pass through some space in an adjacent region they can reach through a portal. The what the one segment cell / margin allows is not narrow irregular regions, but rather, placement of geometry very close to portals while still allowing accessibility to other portals, allowing for denser region fills. If this is a policy, essentially that means the region the builder is passed would need to automatically put that margin in to reduce reliance on builders obedience. It also then creates another policy decision that has to be made, what do the segments on the new perimeter become? Not passable is being worked on as fairly prescriptive. Any is meaningless, and passable kind of implies a recursion / forms new portals. That being said. I think I'm ok with not passable. not passable means \"not [guaranteed] passable\" and builders are encouraged to take as much advantage as that as possible in filling their regions. Some region types may naturally be more empty, thematically. But by theme, region types are encouraged to place content, whether passable or not, intentionally up to those not guaranteed passable borders."

   Corrected direction:
   - Accessibility between the original portals remains required. Clear portal
     approaches are not a substitute for that route. A direct 8 by 6 rectangle
     probe accepts an empty region and rejects a full-height separating wall
     with "Portal east is unreachable from portal west." The earlier example
     did not identify a defect in the existing validator.
   - The margin is automatically excluded from the area handed to geometry
     filling, instead of every builder independently deciding to leave it clear.
     Its purpose is denser intentional fills close to portals while preserving
     access. Distinguish the original access area and its portals from the
     reduced fill area; simply shrinking the former loses the obligation.
   - Corey's preferred new fill perimeter is not guaranteed passable. This is
     not a physical wall requirement. It encourages themed content up to that
     edge, and creates neither new guaranteed crossings nor recursive margins.
   - Region types can intentionally be more empty where their theme calls for
     it. Incidental emptiness does not create a passability guarantee.

   Corey further clarified: "Hmmm I guess they need to know both the old and new boundary, because they need to know where the portals are."

   Builders therefore retain the original boundary and its portal obligations
   as well as the reduced fill boundary. Geometry placement uses the fill area;
   access planning includes the automatically reserved margin and refers to the
   original portals. The new not-guaranteed-passable fill edge does not erase
   those obligations or itself create portals. The same distinction is needed
   when a region passes a subregion to its builder.

   The controlled movement witness in 17.2.5 remains evidence of conservative
   local mask containment, not a guarantee about arbitrary neighbouring builds.
   Implementation of automatic margins remains pending. Width, endpoint and
   turn treatment, and propagation through subregions must be specified without
   discarding original portal connectivity. The existing kernel brief has only
   one cell mask; existing portal-margin helpers are opt-in builder heuristics
   and even omit hinted cells. Some strategies invoke child builders directly,
   so changing top-level dispatch alone would not cover subregions.
   No contract or validator change has been implemented.
