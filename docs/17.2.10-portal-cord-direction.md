# 17.2.10. Portal cord direction

Up: [17.2](17.2-map-questions.md). The proposed design is in
[20.10](20.10-portal-cords.md). This record preserves source direction; it does
not claim the capability is implemented.

## Original direction

Corey's prompts, 2026-10-28, recorded in [issue #643](/corey/astra_test/issues/643):

> I have a new idea for builders / micro generation. It's an SDK that essentially tracks cords between all portals in a region that can merge or seperate and with them in place then not guaranteed passable perimeter areas can fill from there edges into the interior of the region "pushing" cords around. And with the area addressed marking feature, not sure if this is the "canvas" that is referred to, that should help track areas intended to be addressed that push those cords around. There's probably a better way to describe this, but hopefully you get what I'm driving at. And again, not suggesting this must be used by builders, but a lot of builders would want this tooling available in ther SDKs.

> So while addressed claims make no guarantees one way or another about passability, before geometry is finalized, I would be pessimistic. At the moment I'm not sure there is a distinction between reserving an area as intending to address and marking and area as addressed. I think currently these areas are only used for the latter, however for what we're conceiving here, the intent is more the former.
>
> I'm thinking continuous cords at the moment. We also have to consider like swept space for gladiators, I've just been htinking about it in its most primitive form. Essentially the core strategy is to grow from perimiter placement hints while preserving some path between each pair of portals.
>
> I imagine inward perimeter growth is kind of case by case strategy, but builders are going to want an SDK that lets them use arbitrary polygons and polyonimoes, some using one or the other, some using both, others using neither, either because they don't use them at all or they have their own custom solution for growth.
>
> and 4 gets into detail I don't understand well enough to comment on, but my prior responses may already inform how the architecture and interface ought to change / grow.

Corey's concerns in [comment #7075](/corey/astra_test/issues/643#issuecomment-7075):

> I have a couple of concerns about this approach.
>
> 1. I don't want this to force everything that gets placed to touch the edge of a builder wants to employ these tools. That is to say, it should be compatible with its own interests in starting from arbitrary places in the interior.
> 2. When it comes to builders deciding to release / decompose their spaces, this may make it so they release arbitrary strangely shaped interior regions and prevent them from chopping off hunks of regions on the periphery of their region, which I believe makes for better regions for the hand off to work with both in shape for the hand off and in location relative to the original region for switching to a different builder type.

## Hut retained-yard policy after adoption evidence (2026-10-29)

PR #654 review 493 requested a percentage-based yard-retention bound or an
explicit decision after seeing the allocation distribution. Corey was asked:

> Review #493 asks for an explicit retained-yard policy. The updated 78 production huts retain 67.9%–100% of yard outside the house (median 77.3%); the synthetic minimum is 43.1%, with house/access/circulation preserved. Which rule should the hut milestone use?

Corey's selected answer, verbatim:

> Keep the current geometry/access policy and accept these measured shares (recommended).

Recorded decision: retain hut's current geometry/access-based authorship policy;
do not introduce a percentage floor. Preserve the house, actual entrance access
and circulation through every accepted handoff. The measured allocation shares
are accepted evidence for this hut milestone, not a universal bound or a policy
for compound. Current mechanics are in 54.2 / 20.10; full evidence is in 42.3.
