# 29.4. Drawing

Part of [29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md), which holds the shape description, collision and sight. Moved from 29 without changing its content (#510).

## Drawing

Phaser renders everything. There are three kinds of drawn content, and they differ in how often they
are rebuilt:

1. **Static world geometry** — terrain, obstacles, buildings, stations, hazards. Recorded once in
   `recordStatic()` (from `buildMap()`, and again when the palette changes) as a list of drawings
   indexed by 512-unit cell, then rasterised into a
   `RenderTexture` per tile the first time a tile comes near the view. A frame costs a textured quad
   per visible tile.
2. **Dynamic content** — loot, traps, projectiles, effects, the hazard band, gates. Cleared and
   redrawn every frame into two `Graphics` (`dynamic`, `fixtures`), because it changes every frame.
3. **Actors** — one `Container` per player holding an SVG image, ring, health bar and name. Retained
   and repositioned, never rebuilt.

**A retained `Graphics` is not a cache.** Phaser 3 keeps a `Graphics` as a list of commands and
replays it on every render: each rounded rectangle becomes a path and each filled polygon is
triangulated again with Earcut, every frame. This document used to say the opposite, and the static
layer was culled per 1024-unit chunk on that belief. The headed-Chrome comparison and its limits are retained in
[42.2](42.2-runtime-performance.md#retained-graphics-observation).
`render.draw` now times Phaser's render pass separately, so this cost can't hide again.

How the tiles are kept:

- **Recorded once, by bounds.** `staticAt(box)` records a drawing's calls once and indexes it under
  every cell its box touches, padded by `SEAM`. A tile replays every drawing indexed in its cells, in
  recorded order and without duplicates, and its texture clips them to its own square. So a shape
  across a seam is drawn whole on both sides, and overlapping translucent shadows are not doubled.
  The old anchor-keyed chunks let a large shape spill a whole chunk past its anchor.
- **Tile size follows zoom.** A tile spans `512 << level` units, and the level rises as the camera
  pulls back, keeping a tile at least `MIN_TILE_PIXELS` across. A player's view uses 512-unit tiles.
  The whole-arena view uses about fifteen 4,096-unit tiles; at 512 units it would need nearly 600,
  and building those together froze the page for over 15 seconds under software GL.
- **One texel per screen pixel.** The resolution is the camera zoom, rounded up to a multiple of
  1/16, so a tile is always a whole number of texels and a window resize rarely rebuilds anything.
  Each texture overlaps its neighbours by one texel. Without that, filtering blends a tile's edge
  with nothing, and the grid shows as faint lines in the whole-arena view.
- **Built under a frame budget.** Each frame builds tiles for up to `BUILD_MS`, and always at least
  one. Holes in the view go first, then visible tiles at a stale resolution, then the ring one tile
  beyond the view, nearest first. A stale tile stays on screen, scaled, until it is replaced.
  Textures more than `RETAIN_TILES` beyond the view, or at another level, are released, so texture
  memory is bounded by the view, not the map.
- **Recreated, not resized.** A texture whose size changes is destroyed and made again.
  `RenderTexture.resize` in Phaser 3.90 left the redrawn content clipped and offset.

Textures drop the canvas's multisampling, but at one texel per pixel a before-and-after screenshot
differed in fewer than 20 pixels. Anything that changes per frame belongs in the dynamic layer, not
in a tile, because a changed tile costs a full rasterise.

An obstacle carrying `points` is drawn from its own outline, so generated geometry needs no matching
branch in the renderer and cannot be drawn as something other than the body the simulation collides
with. Per-kind styling — palettes, highlights, hatching — stays presentation and stays in the scene.

## Drawing planes

Drawn content belongs to a plane, and the plane decides what it occludes:
- **Ground:** floors, markings and thresholds. Door and window decals (`map.decals`, 56.2) reach
  past a roof's overhang, so an entrance shows while its roof is drawn.
- **Objects:** walls, trunks and actors.
- **Overhead:** roofs and canopies, above the actors.

Overhead items occlude what the viewer can already see; they never change what
the viewer may *know*. Sight stays the shared rule in [29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md#sight) (`seesPoint`, `roofConceals`),
so fading a roof or a crown is cosmetic. A tree keeps its trunk as the collision
and sight shape; its translucent crown is drawn at 1.9 times the trunk radius on
the overhead plane and partly hides a player who steps under it. This styling
reads `kind` and radius from existing records; explicit asset parts remain future
work. Ground and static objects share the static tile cache in recorded order;
actors are retained objects above it, and overhead textures sit above actors.

The overhead cache uses the same camera-dependent tile level, resolution and
frame budget as static geometry. Roofs have one alpha group per building; crowns
share a canopy group. Roof textures are cropped to the roof's own extent, which overhangs
the walls by `ROOF_OVERHANG` on exterior sides, dropping overhang, perimeter fill insets, and seam
strokes where physically abutting another roof or building regardless of fade group (`roofCellBorders`,
comparing boundary contact intervals across unequal cell sizes and staggered origins).
Group visibility and alpha update retained `RenderTexture` quads without replaying
the drawing commands. Offscreen textures are released beyond the retention ring,
and map replacement clears both caches. No overhead `Graphics` is rendered each
frame.

Roof presentation (`shared/view.ts`, so the renderer reads one rule). Roof groups
are declared directly on `map.roofs` via `groupId` by the building SDK (`roofGroups`),
retiring the client-side door-edge derivation when declared roofs are present; legacy
maps without declared roofs fall back to deriving groups from abutting building boxes
sharing a door edge. A builder states its groups. The compound's rule is reachability: roofs
fade together when one can walk between their spaces without leaving the structure
(`reachableRoofGroups`, 56.1). A group fades together, and its target is binary
(`roofRevealed`): revealed (alpha 0) while the viewer is inside any room of the group or within
`ROOF_REVEAL_RANGE` (32 world units) of one of the group's doors, windows or doorway threshold
cells (`openingDistance`), and covered (alpha 1) otherwise. The reveal zone is therefore the
threshold cell outside each exterior door, plus a band about a body's width outside each door and
window; approaching an opening reveals nothing partially. The client moves alpha toward the target
at a constant rate (`stepRoofAlpha`), so a complete fade takes `ROOF_FADE_MS` (300 ms) and a target
flip mid-fade reverses from the current alpha, taking the remaining share of that time. A roof that
reaches zero stops being drawn. An unfogged directed view reveals every roof; a fogged view with no
eye covers them. While the viewer is indoors a player camera zooms in by
`INSIDE_ZOOM`, eased; a directed camera is not touched. None of these presentation changes alters `roofConceals` or sight.

## Palettes

The arena draws its world in a palette (#582). `public/palette.js` holds 11.1's
`1.0-working` swatches and a set of themes. A theme maps the renderer's roles to colors: ground,
street, floor, wall, fence, crates, vegetation, roof, roof rim, out-of-sight shade, background and the
details derived from them. The roles describe what is drawn, never which swatch fills it, so a palette
changes without touching a draw call. `prototype` is the pre-11.1 look and stays the default until
the signature production target passes [41.2](41.2-art-direction-plan.md)'s promotion gate. `signature` is Corey's favored combination, and `neonAftermath`,
`sodiumGraveyard`, `deadMall` and `broadcastLockdown` are his four scene identities. Which
of a scene's six swatches fills which role was assigned here, and is prospective (11.1 §5.3).

- **Presentation only.** A palette is a viewer's choice, made with `?palette=<id>` or the dev view's
  menu and remembered in that browser. Simulation, wire, recordings and saved maps never see
  it, and a test holds that `shared/`, `server/` and `map/` do not import it.
- **Restyled in place.** Switching records the static drawings again (`recordStatic`) and drops
  every tile, which rebuilds over the next frames within `BUILD_MS`. Sight, gate memory, the eye,
  actors and each roof's fade are kept; only `buildMap()`, for a new map, resets those.
- **Gameplay colors are not themed yet.** Objectives (stations, chargers, exit), hazards, traps,
  items, gates, effects and actors keep fixed colors, and so do ground decals. Their meanings are
  #587's, and their material treatment is #585's.
- **A limited color regression guard.** Opaque outlined wall, fence and crate colors are compared
  against ground, street and floor, lit and under the out-of-sight shade. Their fill or outline
  must meet the prototype's ratio, capped at 1.5; streets must separate from ground by 1.2.
  These are sampled-color checks, not proof of rendered or perceptual contrast. Special drawing
  branches (glass, pipes, hedges, ruins, trees, rocks), actors and hazards are not certified by this
  guard. Alpha, line width, tile resolution and camera scale need the rendered witnesses in
  [41.2](41.2-art-direction-plan.md). A saturated full-value shade failed the limited guard,
  which is why night shades are dark tinted washes.

## Direction for the art passes

11.1 holds the creative direction and #581 tracks the stages. These are the constraints the tile
renderer above places on them:

- **Decoration never changes the outline.** Collision and sight always come from `shapeOf`
  ([29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md#one-description-three-consumers)). Cosmetic decay and damage
  (cracks, stains, scorch marks, craters, weathered paint, storefront markings) and color washes are
  drawn on the ground or roof planes ([56.2](56.2-ground-decals.md)). They must never look like a
  wall or hide one. Destruction that changes walkability or sight, such as a breach, a collapsed wall,
  a rubble pile or a wreck, is convex obstacle geometry emitted by the region builder
  ([20.3.1](20.3.1-canvas-adoption.md), [29.3](29.3-shape-construction-and-assembly.md)) and drawn
  from its own outline (#585).
- **Few, flat shapes.** Materials read through flat fills and sparse graphic cues recorded once into
  the tiles, not high-frequency noise that competes with silhouettes and inflates rasterisation
  within `BUILD_MS` (#585).
- **Lighting without a lighting engine** (#583). The candidates that fit the tiles are region and
  building palette variants chosen while recording, translucent wash polygons and directional rim
  strokes baked into ground or roof tiles (a wash across a seam is not doubled; see above), and
  event-driven washes in the dynamic layer. Screen-space overlays and gradients would have to
  show they fit the frame budget first.
- **Virtual lighting** (Corey's idea, 2026-10-26, advanced; #583). Moving objects may receive the
  local mood used by static materials. The implementation direction below supersedes an earlier
  suggestion to multiply a light tint into the whole kit: multiplication alone can darken or
  destroy identity colors. Gameplay markings remain protected, and rendered witnesses must
  establish the acceptable tint strength before promotion.
- **Value before hue** (#587). Walkable from blocked, cover from rubble, and actors and hazards from
  the scene must separate by luminance, silhouette and outline, not by hue alone. They must still
  separate under every wash, under `this.shade` ([25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md)) and at each
  zoom tier (`LOD_LOW_ZOOM`, `LOD_MEDIUM_ZOOM`,
  [25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md#level-of-detail-and-camera-culling)).

## Selected implementation direction

The final #591 review chooses **static material resolution first, spatial lighting second,
moving-object tint third**, in [41.2](41.2-art-direction-plan.md)'s sequence. `public/appearance.js`
implements the static material and spatial lighting resolver for step 2 (#585, #583); generator-emitted
appearance descriptors and building adopters (#584 step 3) are defined in `shared/types.ts` and adopted in
`map/micro/building/service-frontage.ts`; moving-object virtual tint (#583 step 4) remains a future step.

**Keep appearance as data resolved before drawing.** A small client presentation resolver (`public/appearance.js`)
takes an existing geometry record, optional appearance identity (`map.appearance`) and the viewer's selected art mode
(`palette`, `lighting`, `wear`).
It returns material fills, structural edges and a bounded list of decorative marks (`resolveBuildingAppearance`,
`resolveObstacleAppearance`, `resolveGroundAppearance`). It does not
allocate game IDs, consume simulation RNG, change `shapeOf`, or infer unseen dynamic state.
Use stable identity plus a versioned presentation-only hash (`APPEARANCE_RECIPE_VERSION`, `appearanceHash`) for repeatable variation; never camera
position, traversal order or frame time. Old maps without appearance metadata get a neutral
material fallback. The eventual serialized descriptor belongs to 52/53's reviewed contract;
do not add arbitrary renderer colors throughout the wire format to prototype the slice.

**Compose in a fixed order.** Resolve base material and surviving paint, then wear/scars, then
the static theatrical treatment. Preserve a structural edge after that treatment. Existing
ground/objects/overhead planes, information masks and roof alpha still govern occlusion.
The first slice uses color resolution and bounded flat shapes recorded into existing tiles;
no full-screen filter, lighting render target, per-frame static `Graphics` or per-object shadow map.
Decoration stays inside its material surface except for explicitly ground-owned marks. All
stroke/shadow extents must be included in recording bounds; `SEAM` is not an arbitrary-effects pad.

**Spatial coherence precedes random variety.** Choose a district treatment once for a meaningful
area; do not randomly assign a scene palette to every tile or obstacle. For the first slice author
two fields explicitly. Each has a stable ID, footprint, tint and intensity. Later procedural
assignment consumes #584's independent lighting identity. Use one deterministic spatial resolver
for both static samples and moving objects. At boundaries blend normalized influences with a
neutral remainder; define stable tie handling and bounded overlap before expanding the pilot.
Long surfaces crossing fields must be split or sampled spatially rather than tinted solely from
their center. No doubled overlap at cache seams and no color change merely because a tile rebuilt.

**Protect information and identity.** Static fields can describe known architecture. Dynamic
light events may use only viewer-authorized projected events, never an unseen actor's presence.
Visible actors/props may receive a bounded material tint after the static pass is proven; rings,
health bars, names, hazard boundaries, objective symbols, loot glyphs and role identifiers keep
their semantic treatment. If body identity and material color cannot be separated in the current
sprite, leave that sprite untinted. Spatial boundary blending must work without temporal state;
any additional easing resets on teleport, replay seek, subject/view change or map replacement so
color cannot depend on a previous camera path. This affects appearance only, never perception.

**Cache by what changes.** Static appearance is resolved when recordings are made; cache identity
includes the appearance recipe/version and art selection. Keep roof fade progress outside those
recordings as now. Pure movement must not invalidate ground or roof tiles. Begin with #582's
explicit full restyle for rare user selection changes, within the existing tile-build budget;
regional invalidation is warranted only when a measured use case requires it. Event animation
belongs in a bounded dynamic layer and must not rebuild static fields every tick.
