# 29.4. Drawing

Part of [29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md), which holds the shape description, collision and sight. Moved from 29 without changing its content (#510).

## Drawing

Phaser renders everything. There are three kinds of drawn content, and they differ in how often they
are rebuilt:

1. **Static world geometry** — terrain, obstacles, buildings, stations, hazards. Recorded once by
   `recordStatic()` (`public/static-drawing.js`, from `buildMap()`, and again when the palette changes) as a list of drawings
   indexed by 512-unit cell, then rasterised into a
   `RenderTexture` per tile the first time a tile comes near the view. A frame costs a textured quad
   per visible tile.
2. **Dynamic content** — loot, traps, projectiles, effects, the hazard band, gates. Cleared and
   redrawn every frame into two `Graphics` (`dynamic`, `fixtures`) by the layer functions in
   `public/frame-drawing.js`, because it changes every frame.
3. **Actors** — one `Container` per player holding an SVG image, ring, health bar and name. Retained
   and repositioned, never rebuilt.

**A retained `Graphics` is not a cache.** Phaser 3 keeps a `Graphics` as a list of commands and
replays it on every render: each rounded rectangle becomes a path and each filled polygon is
triangulated again with Earcut, every frame. This document used to say the opposite, and the static
layer was culled per 1024-unit chunk on that belief. The headed-Chrome comparison and its limits are retained in
[42.2](42.2-runtime-performance.md#retained-graphics-observation).
`render.draw` now times Phaser's render pass separately, so this cost can't hide again.

How the tiles are kept:

- **Recorded once, by bounds.** `TileCache.record(box)` (`public/tile-cache.js`) records a drawing's calls once and indexes it under
  every cell its box touches, padded by `SEAM`. A tile replays every drawing indexed in its cells, in
  recorded order and without duplicates, and its texture clips them to its own square. So a shape
  across a seam is drawn whole on both sides, and overlapping translucent shadows are not doubled.
  The old anchor-keyed chunks let a large shape spill a whole chunk past its anchor.
- **Tile size follows zoom.** A tile spans `512 << level` units, and the level rises as the camera
  pulls back, keeping a tile at least `MIN_TILE_PIXELS` across. A player's view uses 512-unit tiles.
  The whole-arena view uses about fifteen 4,096-unit tiles; at 512 units it would need nearly 600,
  and building those together froze the page for over 15 seconds under software GL.
- **One texel per screen pixel.** The resolution is the camera zoom, rounded up to a multiple of
  1/16, so a tile is always a whole number of texels and a window resize rarely rebuilds anything.
  Each texture overlaps its neighbours by one texel. Without that, filtering blends a tile's edge
  with nothing, and the grid shows as faint lines in the whole-arena view.
- **Built under a frame budget.** Each frame builds tiles for up to `BUILD_MS`, and always at least
  one. Holes in the view go first, then visible tiles at a stale resolution, then the ring one tile
  beyond the view, nearest first. A stale tile stays on screen, scaled, until it is replaced.
  Textures more than `RETAIN_TILES` beyond the view, or at another level, are released, so texture
  memory is bounded by the view, not the map.
- **Recreated, not resized.** A texture whose size changes is destroyed and made again.
  `RenderTexture.resize` in Phaser 3.90 left the redrawn content clipped and offset.

Textures drop the canvas's multisampling, but at one texel per pixel a before-and-after screenshot
differed in fewer than 20 pixels. Anything that changes per frame belongs in the dynamic layer, not
in a tile, because a changed tile costs a full rasterise.

An obstacle carrying `points` is drawn from its own outline, so generated geometry needs no matching
branch in the renderer and cannot be drawn as something other than the body the simulation collides
with. Per-kind styling — palettes, highlights, hatching — stays presentation and stays in the scene.

## Drawing planes

Drawn content belongs to a plane, and the plane decides what it occludes:
- **Ground:** floors, markings and thresholds. Door and window decals (`map.decals`, 56.2) reach
  past a roof's overhang, so an entrance shows while its roof is drawn.
- **Objects:** walls, trunks and actors.
- **Overhead:** roofs and canopies, above the actors.

Overhead items occlude what the viewer can already see; they never change what
the viewer may *know*. Sight stays the shared rule in [29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md#sight) (`seesPoint`, `roofConceals`),
so fading a roof or a crown is cosmetic. A tree keeps its trunk as the collision
and sight shape; its translucent crown is drawn at 1.9 times the trunk radius on
the overhead plane and partly hides a player who steps under it. This styling
reads `kind` and radius from existing records; explicit asset parts remain future
work. Ground and static objects share the static tile cache in recorded order;
actors are retained objects above it, and overhead textures sit above actors.

The overhead cache uses the same camera-dependent tile level, resolution and
frame budget as static geometry. Roofs have one alpha group per building; crowns
share a canopy group. Roof textures are cropped to the roof's own extent, which overhangs
the walls by `ROOF_OVERHANG` on exterior sides, dropping overhang, perimeter fill insets, and seam
strokes where physically abutting another roof or building regardless of fade group (`roofCellBorders`,
comparing boundary contact intervals across unequal cell sizes and staggered origins).
Group visibility and alpha update retained `RenderTexture` quads without replaying
the drawing commands. Offscreen textures are released beyond the retention ring,
and map replacement clears both caches. No overhead `Graphics` is rendered each
frame.

Roof presentation (`shared/view.ts`, so the renderer reads one rule). Roof groups
are declared directly on `map.roofs` via `groupId` by the building SDK (`roofGroups`),
retiring the client-side door-edge derivation when declared roofs are present; legacy
maps without declared roofs fall back to deriving groups from abutting building boxes
sharing a door edge. A builder states its groups. The compound's rule is reachability: roofs
fade together when one can walk between their spaces without leaving the structure
(`reachableRoofGroups`, 56.1). A group fades together, and its target is binary
(`roofRevealed`): revealed (alpha 0) while the viewer is inside any room of the group or within
`ROOF_REVEAL_RANGE` (32 world units) of one of the group's doors, windows or doorway threshold
cells (`openingDistance`), and covered (alpha 1) otherwise. The reveal zone is therefore the
threshold cell outside each exterior door, plus a band about a body's width outside each door and
window; approaching an opening reveals nothing partially. The client moves alpha toward the target
at a constant rate (`stepRoofAlpha`), so a complete fade takes `ROOF_FADE_MS` (300 ms) and a target
flip mid-fade reverses from the current alpha, taking the remaining share of that time. A roof that
reaches zero stops being drawn. An unfogged directed view reveals every roof; a fogged view with no
eye covers them. While the viewer is indoors a player camera zooms in by
`INSIDE_ZOOM`, eased; a directed camera is not touched. None of these presentation changes alters `roofConceals` or sight.

## Palettes

The arena draws its world in a palette (#582). `public/palette.js` holds 11.1's
`1.0-working` swatches and a set of themes. A theme maps the renderer's roles to colors: ground,
street, floor, wall, fence, crates, vegetation, roof, roof rim, out-of-sight shade, background and the
details derived from them. The roles describe what is drawn, never which swatch fills it, so a palette
changes without touching a draw call. `signature` is the default arena theme, promoted following
completion of the art plan's promotion gate ([below](#reviewing-an-art-change); evidence in [42.45](42.45-art-delivery-evidence.md)) and readability verification (#587, #593). `prototype` is retained for global comparison, and `neonAftermath`,
`sodiumGraveyard`, `deadMall` and `broadcastLockdown` are his four scene identities. Which
of a scene's six swatches fills which role was assigned here, and is prospective (11.1 §5.3).

- **Presentation only.** A palette is a viewer's choice, made with `?palette=<id>` or the dev view's
  menu and remembered in that browser. Simulation, wire, recordings and saved maps never see
  it, and a test holds that `shared/`, `server/` and `map/` do not import it.
- **Restyled in place.** Switching records the static drawings again (`recordStatic`) and drops
  every tile, which rebuilds over the next frames within `BUILD_MS`. Sight, gate memory, the eye,
  actors and each roof's fade are kept; only `buildMap()`, for a new map, resets those.
- **Gameplay colors are not themed yet.** Objectives (stations, chargers, exit), hazards, traps,
  items, gates, effects and actors keep fixed colors, and so do ground decals. Their meanings are
  #587's, and their material treatment is #585's.
- **A limited color regression guard.** Opaque outlined wall, fence and crate colors are compared
  against ground, street and floor, lit and under the out-of-sight shade. Their fill or outline
  must meet the prototype's ratio, capped at 1.5; streets must separate from ground by 1.2.
  These are sampled-color checks, not proof of rendered or perceptual contrast. Special drawing
  branches (glass, pipes, hedges, ruins, trees, rocks), actors and hazards are not certified by this
  guard. Alpha, line width, tile resolution and camera scale need the rendered witnesses in
  [42.45](42.45-art-delivery-evidence.md). A saturated full-value shade failed the limited guard,
  which is why night shades are dark tinted washes.

## Direction for the art passes

11.1 holds the creative direction and #581 tracks the stages. These are the constraints the tile
renderer above places on them:

- **Decoration never changes the outline.** Collision and sight always come from `shapeOf`
  ([29](29-geometry-and-drawing.md#one-description-three-consumers)). Cosmetic decay and damage
  (cracks, stains, scorch marks, craters, weathered paint, storefront markings) and color washes are
  drawn on the ground or roof planes ([56.2](56.2-ground-decals.md)). They must never look like a
  wall or hide one. Destruction that changes walkability or sight, such as a breach, a collapsed wall,
  a rubble pile or a wreck, is convex obstacle geometry emitted by the region builder
  ([20.3.1](20.3.1-canvas-adoption.md), [29.3](29.3-shape-construction-and-assembly.md)) and drawn
  from its own outline (#585).
- **Few, flat shapes.** Materials read through flat fills and sparse graphic cues recorded once into
  the tiles, not high-frequency noise that competes with silhouettes and inflates rasterisation
  within `BUILD_MS` (#585).
- **Lighting without a lighting engine** (#583). The candidates that fit the tiles are region and
  building palette variants chosen while recording, translucent wash polygons and directional rim
  strokes baked into ground or roof tiles (a wash across a seam is not doubled; see above), and
  event-driven washes in the dynamic layer. Screen-space overlays and gradients would have to
  show they fit the frame budget first.
- **Virtual lighting** (Corey's idea, 2026-10-26, advanced; #583). Moving objects may receive the
  local mood used by static materials. The implementation direction below supersedes an earlier
  suggestion to multiply a light tint into the whole kit: multiplication alone can darken or
  destroy identity colors. Gameplay markings remain protected, and rendered witnesses must
  establish the acceptable tint strength before promotion.
- **Value before hue** (#587). Walkable from blocked, cover from rubble, and actors and hazards from
  the scene must separate by luminance, silhouette and outline, not by hue alone. They must still
  separate under every wash, under `this.shade` ([25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md)) and at each
  zoom tier (`LOD_LOW_ZOOM`, `LOD_MEDIUM_ZOOM`,
  [25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md#level-of-detail-and-camera-culling)). Readability is governed by a
  3-tier combined framework joining gameplay role priority with visual channel separation (fill and edge):
  - *Tier 1: High-Speed Gameplay & Threat Signals* (actors, projectiles, motion mines, traps, objectives).
    Immediate peripheral recognition; protected identity hues; strict flat fill contrast ≥ 1.80 against
    underlying pavement/ground. Cannot rely on stroke/outline alone.
  - *Tier 2: Physical Geometry & Dynamic Clutter* (walls, buildings, closed doors, cover, physics props).
    Communicates navigability, collision, and movable cover. Targets flat fill contrast ≥ 1.50 against
    underlying ground/street/apron, or composite separation with flat fill ≥ 1.35 when accompanied by a
    high-contrast perimeter stroke ≥ 2.00 (e.g. dynamic props under high-luminance amber washes).
  - *Tier 3: Surface History & Cosmetic Texture* (pavement cracks, patch repairs, scorches, drainage stains,
    doorstep thresholds). Conveys surface age and architectural history without competing with collision
    geometry. Fills remain intentionally subdued (1.10 ≤ contrast ≤ 1.35) so they never mimic obstacles.
  The tiers' code statement is planned as `public/readability.js` (#778, [22.8](22.8-development-tools.md#appearance-tools));
  until it lands the thresholds are literals in `tests/appearance.test.js` and the art witness.

## Reviewing an art change

This was the promotion gate of the technical art plan (#581; moved here from the retired 41.2 on 2026-11-13, #1054). It stays the standard for reviewing a change to art or appearance.

Record the commit, fixture/seed, theme, viewport, pixel ratio, camera/zoom, fog and
roof state beside captures. Keep reference/candidate captures paired. Use a small
contact sheet for review, with original gameplay-scale images retained; a close-up
concept image is not evidence. Review grayscale and color-vision simulations as
additional checks, not as a substitute for ordinary color/motion inspection.

- **Readability:** demonstrate all special obstacle branches in step 1, visible
  actor identities and threats against both quiet and saturated backgrounds.
  Door/window openings and narrow passages remain recognizable. Decorative wear
  cannot be mistaken for blocking rubble; theatrical color cannot be mistaken for
  a hazard boundary or objective. Architectural graphics must explain former use
  without requiring symbol decoding; contiguous material fields remain legible
  after wear and at gameplay scale. Log remaining ambiguity as a failed gate.
- **State and privacy:** test palette changes during roof fades, camera movement,
  view changes and replay seeking. Remembered gates, discovery, actor containers
  and fade progress survive restyling. Appearance must never reveal a withheld
  actor, trap, item or event, even through a light or shadow.
- **Compatibility:** changes confined to presentation must leave frozen behavior
  fixtures and map geometry unchanged. Any serialized appearance field requires
  its own 53/26 compatibility decision; no opportunistic replay or map bump.
- **Performance:** use 31's relevant browser checks and `npm run bench:client` on
  the same scene/viewport before and after, sequentially without competing work.
  Compare `render.draw`, frame p95/hitches, tile-build cost/count, retained textures
  and memory across first view, pan/zoom, restyle and steady motion. Retain `BUILD_MS`
  and view-bounded caches. Repeat suspected regressions three times. Do not claim
  lighting is cheap merely because its colors were calculated once.

Promotion requires resolved findings and recorded visual sign-off by the art
reviewer, as well as relevant automated gates. It does not require inventing a
new universal contrast ratio or an unmeasured performance budget. The prototype
remains available for comparison after promotion.

## Selected implementation direction

The final #591 review chooses **static material resolution first, spatial lighting second,
moving-object tint third**, in the art plan's sequence (#581). `public/appearance.js`
implements the static material and spatial lighting resolver for step 2 (#585, #583); generator-emitted
appearance descriptors and depot warehouse adoption (#584 step 3) are defined in `shared/types.ts` and adopted in
`map/micro/strategies/depot.ts`; spatial lighting composition across neighboring areas, long surface splitting,
and bounded dynamic-object virtual tinting for physics props and separated actor body materials while protecting untinted actor identity (#583 step 4, #736)
are implemented in `public/appearance.js` (`sampleSpatialLight`, `getCanonicalFields`, `resolveActorTint`, `resolvePropAppearance`,
segmented ground paving) and applied by the renderer (`public/static-drawing.js`, `public/frame-drawing.js`, and actor tints in `public/arena-scene.js`).

**Keep appearance as data resolved before drawing.** A small client presentation resolver (`public/appearance.js`)
takes an existing geometry record, optional appearance identity (`building.appearance`, `obstacle.appearance`, `map.appearance`)
and the viewer's selected art mode (`palette`, `lighting`, `wear`).
Recordings and saved maps store appearance directly on their domain objects (`building.appearance`, `obstacle.appearance`),
leaving `map.appearance` for template-local/map-level surfaces, marks, and lighting fields; older readers safely ignore them.
It returns material fills, structural edges and a bounded list of decorative marks (`resolveBuildingAppearance`,
`resolveObstacleAppearance`, `resolveGroundAppearance`). It does not
allocate game IDs, consume simulation RNG, change `shapeOf`, or infer unseen dynamic state.
Use stable identity plus a versioned presentation-only hash (`APPEARANCE_RECIPE_VERSION`, `appearanceHash`) for repeatable variation; never camera
position, traversal order or frame time. Old maps without appearance metadata get a neutral
material fallback. The eventual serialized descriptor belongs to 52/53's reviewed contract;
do not add arbitrary renderer colors throughout the wire format to prototype the slice.

**Compose in a fixed order.** Resolve base material and surviving paint, then wear/scars, then
the static theatrical treatment. Preserve a structural edge after that treatment. Existing
ground/objects/overhead planes, information masks and roof alpha still govern occlusion.
The first slice uses color resolution and bounded flat shapes recorded into existing tiles;
no full-screen filter, lighting render target, per-frame static `Graphics` or per-object shadow map.
Decoration stays inside its material surface except for explicitly ground-owned marks. All
stroke/shadow extents must be included in recording bounds; `SEAM` is not an arbitrary-effects pad. When a building has several declared roof pieces, roof graphic marks (commercial signs, wear panels, drain stains) are drawn once on the primary roof piece containing or closest to the building origin (`resolveDeclaredRoofs` in `public/appearance.js` owns that choice and the roof's appearance fallback to its own descriptor, #725), while roof fill, rim, eave, and ridges apply across all pieces. Cosmetic wear and scar marks (`public/appearance.js`, #585) provide a flat 2D destruction vocabulary recorded into ground and roof tiles: kinetic impact craters (`impact-crater` with pulverized halo `mix(coolConcrete, dirtySidewalk, 0.35)`, dark basin `structuralVoid`, core pit, and radial fracture streaks sampled against spatial light), asphalt patch repairs (`patch-repair` with fresh dark slate asphalt pad `mix(nightAsphalt, structuralVoid, 0.55)` and tar perimeter seam), pavement cracks (`pavement-crack` with angular hairline fractures and `coolConcrete` chip highlights), vegetation encroachment (`vegetation-patch` / `vegetationEdge` with irregular olive tufts in `urbanVegetation` along pavement joints and roof edges), drainage stains, faded panels, and scorch groups. Glazed window obstacles (`o.kind === 'window'`) receive diagonal specular reflection sheen streaks using resolved window fill and outline. Toggling `wear: false` skips all cosmetic wear and scar marks, cleanly reverting surfaces to un-worn base materials while strictly preserving collision boundaries (`shapeOf`), visibility polygons (`seesPoint`), and simulation RNG.

**Spatial coherence precedes random variety.** Choose a district treatment once for a meaningful
area; do not randomly assign a scene palette to every tile or obstacle. For the first slice author
two fields explicitly. Each has a stable ID, footprint, tint and intensity. Later procedural
assignment uses an independent presentation identity. The #771 recipe (adopted by #878)
`map/micro/lighting.ts` (`district-light-1`) consumes canonical region IDs/seeds on a
named stream, never the simulation RNG. At emission, `stampBuiltMap(context, built, origin)` appends at most six broad district fields to `map.appearance.lighting.fields`.
`generateLiveMap` and `liveMapFromBuilt` include these fields by default under `types-67`;
the experimental fourth-argument switch was removed. Older maps remain neutral when fields
are absent; there is no automatic two-field demonstration fallback.

Districts form a 3 by 2 grid (2 by 3 for tall maps), spanning the composed cell extent.
Regional cell counts vote into four treatment buckets: cobalt for `compound`, `hut`,
`depot`, `transit` and `arrival`; magenta for `market`, `charging` and `landmark`;
amber for `rubble`, `ruins`, `checkpoint` and `departure`; cyan for `park` and `plant`.
Unmapped types, including `open`, `cover`, `hall`, `block` and `empty`, vote cobalt.
This fallback participates in the majority vote and can dominate production districts;
the recipe does not guarantee that every treatment appears on a map.
Ties and bounded intensity
variation use the independent identity; landmark/core districts also carry an amber edge.
Empty districts emit nothing. Rectangle washes may extend over holes in an irregular
map; these are presentation extents, never playable-area or knowledge geometry.
Falloff is one quarter of the shorter district side. Generation is linear in owned cells;
fields are stored once, adding no simulation tick work. Existing client sampling sees
at most six additional fields, rather than an unbounded field per region or obstacle.

Use one deterministic spatial resolver
for both static samples and moving objects. At boundaries blend normalized influences with a
neutral remainder; define stable tie handling and bounded overlap before expanding the pilot.
Long surfaces crossing fields must be split or sampled spatially rather than tinted solely from
their center: surfaces split only when lighting is enabled and fields overlap the surface bounds.
Segment size (`segmentSpan`, default 256) bounds the luminance step across the surface; flat fills
are drawn within the existing tile recording architecture rather than continuous gradient shaders.
No doubled overlap at cache seams and no color change merely because a tile rebuilt.

**Protect information and identity.** Static fields can describe known architecture. Dynamic
light events may use only viewer-authorized projected events, never an unseen actor's presence.
Visible actors and props receive bounded material tinting while rings,
health bars, names, hazard boundaries, objective symbols, loot glyphs and role identifiers keep
their semantic treatment. Actor sprites decouple neutral base body equipment, kit/team identity
markings, and forward facing cues into a three-layer container (`[ring, sprite, identity, visor, bar, name, geometry]`, #736):
`actor.sprite` renders base jumpsuit clothing and mechanical chassis (`contestant-body.svg`, `warden-body.svg`)
modulated by bounded virtual lighting (`resolveActorTint` with `actorScale = 0.25`, capping regional luminance modulation to ~10–14% without value collapse or destructive darkening).
`actor.identity` renders broad white-and-dark outline markings covering prominent silhouette areas (contestant helmet dome, warden shoulder pauldrons and chest plate in `contestant-identity.svg`, `warden-identity.svg`) tinted exclusively
with the actor's saturated kit/contestant identity tint (`actorBaseTint`, reading authoritative kit colors from `MatchContent` via `describeContent`: Warden Coral Red `#ff6b70`, Striker Golden Amber `#ffba62`, Specter Lavender `#b39aff`, Hook Cyan `#77dddd`, unassigned/unknown kits falling back to neutral plate `UNKNOWN_KIT_COLOR` `#d0d5cc`; Contestants: Self White `#ffffff`, with non-self contestants distributed across variant hues Emerald Green `#48c774`, Royal Blue `#3a86ff`, and Vivid Lime `#8ac926` via the recorded roster place `seat` (#792), falling back to the roster bot's `c${i}` id and then a callsign hash where no seat is present (waiting-yard frames and recordings written before the field), guaranteeing zero non-self contestants collapse to self white), strictly untinted by theatrical lighting washes to prevent hue distortion or darkening.
`actor.visor` renders forward equipment and facing cues (`contestant-visor.svg` cyan slit `#8cb8af`, `warden-visor.svg` yellow dashes `#fae6a2`) directly above identity, guaranteeing facing cues remain visible and untinted.
`contestant.svg` and `warden.svg` serve as pre-composited reference renders for external DOM UI (`public/index.html`), identical to the composited three-layer stack in canonical default appearances (default white contestant, default coral red warden) to prevent asset drift. Physics props receive bounded virtual lighting (`propFillScale = 0.35`, `propStrokeScale = 0.25`),
governed by the Tier 2 readability standard: props must maintain ≥ 1.50 luminance contrast separation
against the underlying pavement and ground (theme street, theme ground, and authored slate paving), or
composite separation with fill ≥ 1.35 when accompanied by a high-contrast perimeter stroke ≥ 2.00
(verified across all six authored theme palettes and regional/focal lighting fields under lit and unlit
conditions, #737). Spatial boundary blending must work without temporal state; any additional easing
resets on teleport, replay seek, subject/view change or map replacement so color cannot depend on a
previous camera path. This affects appearance only, never perception.

**Cache by what changes.** Static appearance is resolved when recordings are made; cache identity
includes the appearance recipe/version and art selection. Keep roof fade progress outside those
recordings as now. Pure movement must not invalidate ground or roof tiles. Begin with #582's
explicit full restyle for rare user selection changes, within the existing tile-build budget;
regional invalidation is warranted only when a measured use case requires it. Event animation
belongs in a bounded dynamic layer and must not rebuild static fields every tick.

**Event light** (#594). `public/event-light.js` turns the viewer's projected frame into at most
12 glows per frame. Sources are combat and arena effects (`shock`, `slash`, `rail`, `scan`,
`charge`, `grapple`), shots in flight and firing flame traps. Pickups (`loot`, `upgrade`) don't light.
- **Privacy.** Effects and shots reach the client by range, not by sight, so a source lights
  anything only if it passes the same `lit` rule that decides whether the renderer draws it. A
  flame trap is judged at its nozzle, the only point the renderer tests before drawing its jet, so
  a seen flame firing into a wall still lights. An unseen event never lights seen ground.
- **Drawing.** Each glow is four stacked flat 24-sided discs (Phaser's own circles cost about
  0.2 ms more `render.draw` in the crowded client bench) on an additive layer (`eventLight`) between
  the fixtures and the shade. The added light falls off about linearly, so no edge reads as a zone,
  and there is no outline. Walls don't occlude a glow: a seen event's light also falls on ground
  out of sight, where the shade dims it like the rest of that terrain. It reveals nothing there
  that the dimmed ground doesn't already show.
- **Protected layers.** Actors, items, traps and effects draw above the layer and keep their
  colors; physics props, which already take virtual light, take the same lift as the ground
  beneath them (`eventLightAt`, `liftByEventLight`) so they keep their separation from it.
- **Bounds.** Peak additive alpha is 0.18 (a flame at 0.65 of that) and radius at most 240. The art
  witness holds this: signature's prop-on-street contrast beside the firing flame stays at or
  above 1.50 (1.59 without event light). Glows fade with the effect's life and use only the frame
  and interpolation alpha, so seeking, cuts and view changes need no reset. `lighting: false`
  removes them. Hues are theatrical swatches, never a gameplay signal color.
