# 20.3.1. Canvas adoption

Part of [20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md), which holds the shared builder layer, the design guidelines and what
every catalogue builder shares. Moved from 20.3 without changing its content (#510).

Production canvas adoption (#430) starts with `cover`: each accepted cover piece
commits its whole assembly and template footprint through `createBuilderCanvas`
([20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#production-canvas)). Existing lattice, cluster-box,
boundary, route and loot decisions remain in the strategy. Further strategy
migrations are separate behavior-preserving steps; `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` and
cover's executor version remain unchanged for this output-preserving extraction.

Depot is the next adopter: each warehouse and container row commits as a whole
assembly, with its template box recording addressed intent. Its existing lane,
aisle, hint, route-tree, design-observer and loot decisions stay in the strategy.
A refusal after siting is an invariant failure, rather than another placement
fallback. This migration also preserves output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION`.

Hut, compound, park, checkpoint, hall, ruins and rubble follow depot (#465): siting
accepts a piece, then it commits, and a refusal is an invariant failure. A park clump and
a ruins room each commit as one assembly. Checkpoint commits a piece once its route proof
holds. Hall, ruins and rubble keep legacy world-absolute records, whose template boxes
are not their intent, so they pass shapes as areas, built with shape construction
([29.3](29.3-shape-construction-and-assembly.md#shape-construction), #488). A hall and its hint walls
commit as one assembly whose intent is the hall's rectangle and the walls, less the
protected routes it keeps clear. A ruins room's intent is its spans rather than its box.
`commitHintWalls` commits a builder's hint walls as one assembly. Walls meet at corners,
and ruins spans and debris meet in a room, so each piece's area is its shape less the
pieces before it (`disjoint`). Output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` are unchanged: the
addressed record is intent, and neither strategy reads it back.

A ruins room's box less its routes is not yet its intent: a hint wall may stand in a
fallen span's cell inside the box, and its commit would then address nothing new, which
the canvas refuses (20.2, "Every successful commit records nonempty addressed intent").

Plant was the last to move (#490). Its pipe runs used to reach into the machines they
join, which a commit refuses as a collision with an earlier assembly. Each stub is now its
run less both machines' shapes, as convex pieces (shape subtraction, #488), so it ends on
the machine's surface, and a commit accepts that contact between assemblies (#489,
[20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#production-canvas)); #489 alone did not move the chain sweep.
The shed, each machine and each run commit as assemblies, and a refusal is an invariant
failure. A run addresses its stubs' cells; its gap stays unaddressed, as the aisle it is.
This is a content change: pipe geometry moved, so `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` went to `types-35`
([42.4](42.4-chain-checkpoint-history.md)).
Cover's post-filters commit through a fresh canvas with cover's protections (#491).
`coverAround` commits each piece it keeps as its own assembly, as `buildCover` did, and
`coverNear` commits the pieces it is given, then its guard. `coverAround`'s route check
stays in the filter, and a refusal is an invariant failure.

Siting then moved onto the canvas's queries (#460, [20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#production-canvas)).
Depot's `taken` grid, `buildCover`'s footprint grid and `coverNear`'s `AISLE` grid gave way
to `fits` and `spaced`, as did checkpoint's barrier spacing for its post. Park, hall, ruins and
rubble test candidates with `fits` instead of the fill mask and their protections. Cover's
loot standing room asks `spaced` too. Lattice slots, cluster boxes, hints, route proofs and
boundary spacing stay in the strategies. Output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` are unchanged.
Plant adopted them when it moved onto the canvas (#490): its fill and protection checks
became `fits`, and machine and shed spacing `spaced` at a doorway. A run's owned-aisle ring
stays a check on the access mask, since a run touches the machines it joins.
