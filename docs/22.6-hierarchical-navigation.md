# 22.6. Hierarchical navigation and optimistic bot planning

Status: direction recorded 2026-10-21 for #353. Stage 1 (the portal graph with full
knowledge, #420) is built and measured in [42.5](42.5-portal-graph.md); stage 2 (the
free-space mesh as its bottom level, #422) is built and measured in
[42.7](42.7-navigation-mesh.md). Stage 3 (the knowledge filter and optimistic planning, #423) is
built; its tiled design replaces the truth-graph filter drafted here first (Corey, 2026-10-22).
Stage 4 (#424) adopts it: under the default `content-3`, contestants and gladiators plan over the
tiles, measured in [42.8](42.8-bot-adoption.md). Up: [22](22-ownership.md). The first research pass (#364, 2026-10-15) is in
[42.1](42.1-navigation-experiment.md); this revision replaces the design it tested.

## Purpose and scope

Give bots a navigation structure derived from the finished map, revealed to
contestants as they discover the world, and able to plan long routes that the
current fixed search box reports as "no route". Gladiators see the whole structure.
Movement and interactions stay authoritative. This document does not change
collision, movement, destructibility, floors or transit AI.

## Direction (Corey, 2026-10-21)

Recorded on [#353](http://localhost:3000/corey/astra_test/issues/353), in answer to
the open choices of the earlier draft:

- **Reachability is a graph; "have seen" is cell based.** The two kinds of
  knowledge are separate. Discovery cells (192 units, 4 × 4 map cells; `shared/discovery.ts`)
  stay the unit of seen knowledge. The graph's own units are whatever suits
  navigation, and a cell filter decides which parts a contestant may use.
- **Derive, don't save.** Saved maps stay minimal, each layer minimal for that layer.
  What can be derived deterministically is derived. Baking is justified only when
  the data is small and the computation measurably expensive. The structure must be
  in memory by the time players are loaded and the world runs.
- **Portals form the top of the progressive mesh.** The region portals generation
  already guarantees are the top level. Finer levels lie beneath, refined only where
  a bot is about to walk.
- **A polygon navmesh at the bottom.** Built from the same shapes collision uses,
  owned by the shape module as a derived index, not by navigation privately.
- **`canOccupy` and movement stay on shapes.** Moving bodies along the mesh is a
  later, separate gameplay change, judged then on feel and measurement.

Corey's addition of 2026-10-15 still applies verbatim:

> oh, one thing we might also want to do is "speculative" or optimisitic planning by the bots.

## Findings that constrain the design

- `shared/simulation/bots.ts` routes with `gridRoute` over a box spanning the bot
  and its target plus 600 units. The legacy block graph is empty on chain maps, so
  long routes are one large grid search and a detour outside the box fails.
- The 40-unit grid samples `canOccupy` at clearance 14/25 (`shared/map/navigation.ts`).
  #364 showed sampling at 24 or 40 units disconnects physically traversable passages;
  rebuilding at 6 recovers them. Sampling is the weakness, not the swept test.
- `BuiltMap` (`map/kernel/contract.ts`) holds each region's brief (cells, portals)
  and `pairs`, the two regions on each side of every shared portal. A region with
  two or more portals promises every part of every portal is reachable by a hunter
  from every other ([20.1](20.1-region-contract.md), [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md));
  `portalStands` gives hunter stands covering each portal (`map/micro/portals.ts`).
  Contestant clearance (14) is below the hunter's (25), so hunter promises hold for both.
- `validatePortalReach` is elective and sampled: a found route is real, a miss
  proves nothing. The promise's strength on live geometry, including gates, is
  therefore measured, not assumed.
- Portals are not every crossing: clear margins and `not-passable-hint` stretches
  may still be physically open (20.6). Missing them lengthens routes, never breaks them.
- Delegated child results (20.7) are flattened into their parent's elements; no
  child portals survive into `BuiltMap`. A middle level needs them retained first.
- `GameMap` carries no regions, and `shared/` never imports `map/` (22.2).
- Bot planning, `accessible()` and `exploreTarget()`'s `insideMap` read truth.
  Restricting the route finder alone would leave those channels open. Under
  `content-3` all three read knowledge instead (see "Bot adoption").
- Discovery has two sets: `discovered`, from the view window through walls and
  roofs (the minimap's), and `seen`, from line of sight (loot). Layout knowledge is
  `discovered`; door state, like loot, needs sight.

## Architecture

| Level | Nodes and edges | Derived from | Refined for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Top | Portals (with stands); an edge for each pair of portals in one region, and each shared portal joins its two regions | `BuiltMap` briefs and pairs | The whole trip |
| Middle (later) | Child-region portals inside a region | Retained delegation children (20.7) | The current region's leg |
| Bottom | Free-space triangles, eroded by one body's clearance | The shape module's obstacles, mask and gate footprints | The next stretch |

A bot plans the trip at the top, then refines only the leading leg downward and
replans as it goes (partial refinement). Detail is fine near the bot and coarse
far away. A level may be absent: where no middle level exists the top refines
straight to the bottom.

| Concern | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| Graph records, levels, queries, knowledge filter | `shared/navigation/` (new) |
| Free-space mesh, its corridors and funnel paths | `shared/free-space.ts`, the shape module's derived index over `shared/shape.ts` outlines |
| Swept-disk predicate, the judge of every path | `shared/movement.ts` (`sweptClear`) |
| Bottom level (`Reach`) over the mesh | `shared/navigation/mesh-level.ts` |
| Free-space tiles on the discovery grid, each from its own reach | `shared/free-space.ts` (`freeSpaceTile`) |
| Knowledge filter, optimistic planner, result states | `shared/navigation/knowledge.ts` |
| Top-level graph from chain results, at live assembly | `map/live.ts`, through `shared/navigation/` types |
| Geometry reveal (`discovered`, `surveyed`) and door observations | `shared/discovery.ts` |
| Goals, exploration scoring, stall policy | `shared/simulation/bots.ts` |
| Inspection | `map/tools/` and the Map Lab routes tool |

The graph is attached to the live map as a runtime resource, never one of its
fields: `liveMapFromBuilt` calls `attachPortalGraph`, which holds it in a
module-private WeakMap keyed by the map object, read with `portalGraphOf`. It is
therefore never in player projections, snapshots, the replay header's map or any
clone. The map worker posts it beside the map and `createMapGenerator` attaches it
again on the main thread; `createGame` keeps the map object, so the game holds it. Bots under
`content-3` plan over tiles on any map, regions or not; `content-1` and `content-2` keep the grid
planner, which keeps the frozen `behavior-nav-1` characterization on its existing path.

### Top level: the portal graph

Built by `buildPortalGraph` (`map/navigation.ts`) into `shared/navigation/portal-graph.ts`
types: plain data, so the map worker posts it beside the map. An owner grid of map
cells answers which region holds a point.

**Nodes and stands.** A portal has several hunter stands along its line
(`portalStands`), and 20.1 requires the stands of one portal to join in a region
with two or more portals. A node is a portal crossed at a representative, its middle
stand (the line's midpoint when no hunter fits), and keeps all its stands. Joining is
checked for each body at the bottom level, from the representative within each
promising region (`standJoins`). The node stands for the group of stands joined to
its representative; a stand outside that group is reported as unconfirmed (see
"Safety") and is not used. Splitting a divided portal into one node per group would
make the graph differ per body, and is deferred until a finer check confirms one:
dropping a crossing can only lengthen routes. Because every stand of a node reaches
its representative, connectivity measured between representatives holds for every
stand pair it keeps.

**Edges.** A shared portal joins its two regions, so the graph spans the map. Within
a region, every pair of nodes is an edge, measured once between representatives by
the bottom level when a planner is made (`regionTables`): one flood per portal
reaches all the others in its region, about k × area per region rather than one
search per pair. That length is an upper estimate: a refined leg may cross the
portal at any of its stands and is usually shorter. An unreached pair is Infinity
and unconfirmed (see "Safety"), reported and not repaired. An edge's support (for the knowledge filter) is the discovery cells
its measured witness crosses, plus the cells under every stand of both nodes. A
region with one portal hangs off that portal. Gate state will be an edge condition;
today a door change means a new planner.

**Safety.** Connectivity counts only over moves the swept-disk test accepts: each
bottom-level move, from cell centre to cell centre or along a mesh corridor, is
checked against obstacles, mask voids and closed gates at the body's navigation
clearance (`sweptClear` in `shared/movement.ts`, over the shape module's
`segmentShapeDistance`). A walkable sample at each end does not establish a move: a
wall thinner than the gap between two clear sample disks can lie between them. The
connector from every endpoint to the sample it snaps to is swept too, or that
endpoint is unreachable: a portal's stands and crossing at navigation clearance,
and any other point, such as a bot's own position, at the body's collision radius,
since a body pressed against a wall is closer to it than navigation clearance but
is already there. Snapping takes the first sample whose connector sweeps clear, so
a fallback sample across a wall never counts. On the mesh, an endpoint outside the
eroded free space connects to the nearest point of it, and that connector is swept
the same way. A bottom level without swept moves
cannot confirm a connection and may only report one as unconfirmed.

The evidence is one-sided. A connection found over swept moves is confirmed: the
body can travel it. A pair the bottom level fails to connect is inconclusive, not a
broken promise: the break may be real, or the passage may be narrower than the
level's sampling. Only a check that cannot miss a passage (continuous free space,
or a sampled search refined until the passage is resolved) can establish that a
promise is broken.

The first stage measures, over a seed sweep and all authored sizes, both bodies,
whether every promised pair is connected on final live geometry with gates closed
and openable, and whether each portal's stands join (42.5). Every pair is reported
as confirmed or unconfirmed. An unconfirmed pair is investigated with a finer check
before it is attributed to a builder as a generation defect, and the planner never
hides it.

Query: flood from the start over its region's portals and from the goal over its
region's (distances are symmetric), then A* over the portal graph with a
straight-line heuristic, ties to the larger g then the lower node. It returns the
crossings then the goal, and a status: `complete`, `no-route` or `invalid-endpoint`.
No fixed detour box. Refining the first leg into walkable steps is the bot's,
at adoption.

### Bottom level: free-space triangulation

Built in #422. `shared/free-space.ts` cuts each region's playable ground, out to the
same 96-unit margin, by the outlines of every obstacle, mask void and closed gate
(Clipper, on a 1/256-unit integer grid), erodes the result by the body's navigation
clearance plus 0.2 units, rounding convex corners, and triangulates it as a
constrained Delaunay mesh (Delaunator, Constrainautor). The eroded ground is the
body's configuration space, as Recast erodes by an agent's radius: a disk centred
anywhere in it is clear, so a path inside it needs no further clearance reasoning,
and a passage narrower than the body's diameter is simply absent. Narrow passages
are kept exactly rather than lost between samples. Circles are triangulated as
circumscribed polygons, so the mesh never includes ground a circle covers; the
sweep uses the analytic circle.

A path is a corridor of triangles from one search over edge crossings, each costed
from the crossing before to the nearest point of its edge, pulled taut by the simple
stupid funnel algorithm (Mononen): any-angle, so no staircase straightening is
needed. A portal the apex lies on, as a stand on a mesh edge does, is skipped rather
than seen edge-on. Every segment is swept with `sweptClear` at navigation clearance:
collision geometry is the judge, and a disagreement is a defect, recorded in the
reach's `defects` and counted as unreached, not a fallback. Lengths are of those
paths, not upper estimates through sample centres.

The mesh is a derived index, built on first use and kept per map, region and
clearance. It is rebuilt only when the closed gates within its domain change, so a
door rebuilds the regions it borders; a change to the obstacle list rebuilds all of
them. Building reads geometry only: no RNG, no IDs, no map field.

The design first asked for one mesh serving every radius, each triangle recording
its clearance (Demyen and Buro, "Efficient Triangulation-Based Pathfinding", AAAI
2006). Built that way, its funnel paths (tangent to clearance circles at corners)
were refused by the sweep on live maps: triangle widths miss obstacles beyond a
corridor's edges (the "disturbances" of Kallmann's local clearance triangulations),
and the circle funnel's tests turn unstable when the funnel is nearly flat. Eroding
per body gives paths that are clear by construction, at the cost of one mesh per
body radius, two today. Measured in [42.7](42.7-navigation-mesh.md).

`shared/navigation/grid-level.ts` (the 40-unit grid within each region, with every
move and connector swept) remains as the independent test reference: tests and
`tools/navigation/mesh-report.mjs` compare the two on the same maps.

### Middle level (later)

When delegation retains its children and their portals, they become a middle level
inside their parent. Nothing above or below changes. Extra crossings found by an
HPA-style scan of region borders can also be added as top-level edges later. Both are
deferred until a planner consumes the portal graph (see "Implementation sequence", stage 5).

## Knowledge and optimistic planning

Built in #423. A contestant plans with
`createKnowledgePlanner` (`shared/navigation/knowledge.ts`) over three things: tiles of the map's
free space it may know, the free-space hypothesis for the rest, and its own door observations.
It never sees hidden region membership, component counts or measured costs of unseen ground.

**Why tiles, not a filter over the truth graph.** The first draft gave every node, edge and
triangle of the truth structure a support set and filtered it. That cannot pass the information
test, because the truth structure depends on hidden geometry. A hidden shortcut moves an edge's
measured witness into unknown cells, so a pair the bot has fully seen becomes unknown. A
constrained Delaunay triangle depends on every vertex in its circumcircle, and Delaunator breaks
the many cocircular ties of rectangle corners using the whole point set, so known ground
triangulates differently depending on what lies behind discovery. Recast-style tiles avoid both
(Corey chose them, 2026-10-22).

1. **Tiles and support.** `freeSpaceTile` triangulates one discovery cell (192 units) at a time
   for one body. It reads the ground and obstacles within the cell widened by its reach: the
   clearance, plus the 0.2 erosion margin, plus 1. The eroded ground is cut to the cell, then cut
   again into door zones (below), and its rings are put in a canonical order before triangulation.
   A tile is therefore a function of the geometry within its reach alone: neither the obstacle
   list's order nor anything beyond the reach changes it. A tile's support is every cell its reach
   touches; at 192 units that is always the 3 × 3 block around it, clipped to the public world
   bounds. A tile is known only when its whole support is. Tiles are built on first use and kept
   until the obstacle list changes; door state never rebuilds one. Whole-region reveal cannot
   happen, since a region is not a unit here at all.
2. **Known cells.** A cell is known when it is `discovered` or `surveyed`. `surveyed` is every
   cell the view window has covered, void or not. `discovered` (the minimap's set) leaves out cells
   whose centre is off the playable ground; reading only it would leave the tiles beside every void
   unknown for ever. Beacon cells are discovered, but without their neighbours they support no tile,
   so they stay disconnected facts until joined.
3. **Free-space hypothesis.** Every unknown cell inside the public world bounds is an open square.
   The search runs over known triangles and unknown cells together. It enters each place through a
   portal: a triangle edge, a cell side, or the stretch of a tile's side that a triangle edge covers.
   Each portal is costed from the crossing before it to the portal's nearest point, with a
   straight-line heuristic, and the funnel (`pullTaut`) pulls the corridor taut. Costs through
   unknown ground are therefore straight lines from public dimensions, never a measured hidden
   edge. A plan may cross unknown ground toward a beacon or a remembered objective and be wrong;
   that is intentional.
4. **Prefix and replan.** A query returns the tentative route and its confirmed prefix. The
   prefix runs up to where the route first enters an unconfirmed place (an unknown cell, or a
   triangle behind an unobserved door), so it ends at a point on the edge of known free space, at
   the frontier. The bot walks only the prefix and replans when its knowledge changes.
   `memory.revision` counts every discovery sample that changed anything and every door
   observation; `checkRoute` reports a route as `stale` if it was planned under another revision or
   with other keys held. Repair is replanning; incremental repair (D* Lite) waits until
   measurement shows a need.
5. **Stable goals** (built in stage 4). Keep a goal while progress is made; a disproved
   approach tries another frontier. Never permanently abandon a goal because a hypothesis failed
   or a budget ran out. Keep the separate need to explore loot sight frontiers.

Scoring is known cost plus optimistic unknown cost, with no tuned risk penalty until playtests
justify one.

A query reports one of six states:
- `complete`: every place on the route is confirmed.
- `speculative`: the route crosses unknown ground or an unobserved door.
- `no-known-route`: the goal is unreachable even with unknown ground open.
- `invalid-endpoint`: an endpoint is outside the world, or inside known obstacles.
- `budget-exhausted`: the search ran out of budget.
- `stale`: reported through `checkRoute`, as above.

A budget is a count of expanded places (`ROUTE_BUDGET`, 200,000), never a clock. When it runs out,
the query returns the route to the place it searched nearest the goal, with its prefix. A budget
failure does not establish unreachability. The search's order is keyed by cell index and by
triangle number within a tile, both known, so ties never depend on hidden geometry.

**Information test** (`tests/navigation-knowledge.test.js`). The twin maps are identical in what
a bot knows and differ behind it: obstacles added, removed and reordered, voids, doors, and a
hidden wall that blocks truth's only way through. They give identical routes, prefixes, statuses,
expansions, tiles read and budget results, on hand-built twins and on a live chain map. The same
walk through discovery also observes and plans the same on both. Stage 4 extends it to the bot
itself (see "Bot adoption").

**Limits.** These costs were measured once each on seed 1, not benchmarked. Building every tile
eagerly costs about 0.25 s per body at 12 × 6 and 2.8 s at 36 × 18; a contestant builds only the
tiles it knows. A fully known spawn-to-exit query took 8 ms warm (2,677 expansions) at 12 × 6 and
75 ms (32,068 expansions) at 36 × 18, within 0.3% of the region mesh planner's length.

- Tile sides are cut from erosions made in different windows, so about 0.1% of seam edges have an
  end one step of the integer grid (1/256 unit) away from their neighbour's. Crossings between
  tiles therefore match edges by their overlap along the side, not by shared points.
- A slanted obstacle edge that crosses a tile's reach is clipped from its endpoints, so a hidden
  endpoint on the same line could round a clip point differently. Live maps are axis-aligned boxes
  today.
- A corridor of unknown cells is a staircase that the straight line may leave. With nothing known,
  a route came out 0.6% longer than the straight line at 36 × 18.
- A known tile's edge along its side counts as open toward an unknown neighbour, even where known
  geometry within the reach shows it blocked just across the side. This is optimistic, and rare.
- Planning is flat over tiles and cells; contestants have no portal hierarchy yet. If budgets bind
  at 36 × 18 on adoption, a hierarchy over known tiles is the next step.

## Bot adoption (stage 4)

Built in #424. The default content, `content-3`, is `content-2` with `navigation: 'tiles'`
(Corey chose a new content id, 2026-10-23, 17.3); `content-1` and `content-2` keep the grid
planner. In `shared/simulation/bots.ts`:

- **Who plans with what.** A contestant plans with `createKnowledgePlanner` over its own
  knowledge. A gladiator plans with the same planner over the whole map, every cell known and
  doors as they stand, rather than the portal planner. The portal planner's region tables cost
  about 6 s per body at 36 × 18 (42.7) and would need rebuilding as doors unlock, while one flat
  planner serves both roles, treats doors as edge states and works on maps without regions. The
  portal graph stays the hierarchy to add if budgets bind.
- **Truth reads replaced.** A building's loot is judged reachable from the doors the bot last saw
  locked (`memory.gates`), not live locks. An exploration frontier is ground if the bot has
  surveyed its cell and its centre is ground, or if the cell is unsurveyed (open by hypothesis);
  a target found to be void is settled. Objectives are ranked by straight-line distance; the
  street graph is truth, and chain maps have none.
- **Routes are world points.** The route is the planner's whole tentative path in `route`, walked
  past its confirmed prefix: stepping into unknown ground is how the bot learns what is there. A
  taut path turns at corners a clearance away, so a bot turns onto the next leg only when the
  first 24 units of it are clear for its body, or when it is on the corner.
- **When to replan.** Unknown ground is planned as open, so learning more can only close a route
  or lengthen it, never open a shorter one elsewhere. A route therefore stands until its goal
  moves a tile, the keys held or door observations change, or a cell it was planned through as
  unknown gets a known tile; a standing route is also replanned every 5 s. Replans run on each
  bot's turn, every 20 ticks staggered by its place in the player list. A bot whose route was
  cleared, or that walked out a route that stopped short, plans at once; a first plan and a retry
  of a failed one wait for its turn.
- **Budget.** A repath may expand 12,000 places. A query across a 36 × 18 map with everything
  known makes about 60,000 (about 110 ms warm); at the budget the bot walks to the place searched
  nearest its goal and plans on from there. A known dead end (`no-known-route`, or an endpoint in
  known obstacles) gives up an exploration cell and sets an item aside for 30 s, as a stall does.
- **Information test.** `tests/bot-discovery.test.js` walks a bot for 500 ticks on a live map and
  on a twin with no obstacles, doors or ground beyond what it knew and could see. The bot
  remembers a cell 6,000 units away, so its route crosses ground the twins disagree about. Under
  `content-3` the walks agree tick for tick in position, heading, route and inventory; under
  `content-2`, whose grid search reads the truth, they part.
- **Tiles built ahead.** A gladiator's first plan reads every tile of the map; built then, on the
  first tick it plans (tick 10), that tick took 200 to 300 ms at 36 × 18. The map worker builds them
  instead (`prebuiltTiles` in `bots.ts` names which: a gladiator's, under `navigation: 'tiles'`)
  and packs them with `packTiles` into three buffers. Posted as objects, the 36 × 18 tiles would
  cost the main thread 150 to 200 ms to deserialize; packed, they transfer without a copy, and
  `createMapGenerator` adopts them with `adoptPackedTiles`. `freeSpaceTile` unpacks a tile into
  views of the buffers the first time it is asked for it, so adopting costs nothing up front. A
  packed tile equals the tile built in place, which `tests/navigation-knowledge.test.js` checks for
  every cell. Contestants' tiles are still built as they discover, a few at a time. Maps generated
  inline (tests, tools, `createMatch` without a map) build tiles as before. 42.8 has the
  measurements; #470 takes replanning further.
- **Sliced replanning** (`content-4`, #470 part 1). Under `replanning: 'sliced'` a repath is one
  search (`planner.search`) of up to 100,000 expansions, advanced at most 2,000 a tick, while the
  bot walks the route it has; whole-tick searches keep `content-3`'s 12,000. All bots' searches
  share 3,000 expansions a tick, taken in player order: searches that coincide queue rather than
  add up on one tick, which a per-bot slice alone let reach 6,000 (42.9). The search runs over
  `frozenKnowledge`, the cells and door observations as they stood when it started, so it returns
  what a whole search would have then: sliced or whole, it expands the same places in the same
  order (`tests/navigation-knowledge.test.js`). What was learnt meanwhile reaches the adopted plan
  through the usual checks: its doors and watched cells are compared with the bot's knowledge now.
  A due repath leaves a running search alone unless the goal has moved a tile or the keys changed.
  The bot has moved on by the time a route arrives, so it takes the route up from the end of the
  segment it is nearest, among those it could have walked, when the first 24 units toward it are
  clear (as `passed` sweeps); otherwise from the route's start. A search in progress, like a plan,
  is bot-private and never snapshotted.

## Doors and changing geometry

Doors are edge states. A tile is built with every door open. The triangles where a disk of the
body's clearance would meet the door if it were closed form separate pieces carrying the door's
id; that zone is the door's outline widened by the erosion and squared off. The planner can then
apply each door's state without rebuilding geometry. Pieces share their points along the seams
between them, so they join edge to edge even where a zone's side lies along a wall's eroded edge.

Each contestant's planner reads its own last observation (`memory.gates`), never live gate bits.
`discover` samples a door when the sample point is in range, in line of sight and not
roof-concealed; the sample point is the point of the door's outline nearest the eye, moved one
unit toward the eye. `interact` reports any door a contestant tries, whatever the result.
Unobserved state is optimistic: passable but unconfirmed, so the prefix stops before it. It is a
private hypothesis, never a displayed observation (F-10's presentation stays).

How each state counts:
- **Open:** passable.
- **Closed and unlocked:** passable, opened on the way.
- **Locked:** needs a key, and keys are consumed. The search state carries the keys spent, so a
  route through several locked doors spends one key per door and lists them in the route's
  `doors`. A plan may keep an optimistic tail past an unobserved lock but cannot call it confirmed.

One bot's observation never changes another's costs.

Limits:
- A door zone is squared off, so a closed door blocks a little more corner ground than collision
  does.
- Leaving a door's zone and re-entering it spends a second key, which can only lengthen routes.

Future removable geometry needs a change operation (stable obstacle ID, old and new bounds,
monotonic revision) that rebuilds the touched tiles, since removal can create links that did not
exist. Physical revisions stay separate from each bot's observed revision. No destructible kinds are
added by #353.

## Derivation, saving and compatibility

Derive every level at live assembly, in memory, from the chain results and final
collision geometry. Nothing is added to chain saves (51.12). A saved cache is
considered only if measurement at 36 × 18 shows assembly cost that justifies it, and
then as an explicitly versioned sidecar specified in 51.12 and 53.

The grid planner's paths are 40-unit tile steps in `path`; the tile planner's are world points
in `route`, a separate optional field, so neither type changes. Snapshots and recordings carry
it; live projections leave both out, and no renderer or replay reader uses either. The new
recorded field raised the recording schema to 7 with `minSchema` unchanged
([26](26-recording-contract.md)). Playback keeps using recorded frames and never needs the graph.

Adoption intentionally changes gameplay, so it is `content-3` (Corey, 2026-10-23); rules stay
`last-exit-0.7`. `content-1` stays on its planner and `behavior-nav-1.json.gz` is never regenerated.

## How this compares with established practice

| Practice | Here |
| --- | --- |
| Hierarchical abstraction: clusters, entrances, precomputed intra-cluster paths (HPA*, Botea, Müller and Schaeffer, 2004) | Regions, portals, portal-pair edges; irregular designer clusters, as in shipped games |
| Partial refinement (PRA*, Sturtevant and Buro, 2005) | Plan at the top, refine the leading leg |
| Freespace assumption with incremental repair (D* Lite) | Optimistic planning with prefix execution |
| Tiled navmesh eroded per agent radius, with area flags (Recast/Detour) | Per-region mesh eroded per body clearance, a door rebuilding the regions it borders; for contestants, tiles on the discovery grid with doors as triangle flags |
| Planning separate from steering | Already so in `bots.ts` |

Differences: entrances come from generation's guarantees rather than a border scan
(stronger, but not exhaustive); bots are knowledge-limited, a robotics-style choice
most games skip. Hierarchical paths are slightly longer than optimal; measure
stretch against an unrestricted fine search rather than assume it.

The #364 lattice (uniform 24-unit samples in 192-unit chunks, every boundary sample
a node) was rejected: it lost narrow passages and left about 43% of samples as
boundary nodes (42.1). Recast through WebAssembly was considered for the bottom
level; its voxelization reintroduces sampling, so a small 2D builder is preferred.

## Implementation sequence

#353 stays the umbrella. Each stage is its own issue and PR.

1. **Portal graph, full knowledge.** `shared/navigation/` graph and query; built in
   `map/live.ts`; bottom level is the existing grid bounded by the region, with
   every move swept. Measure promise connectivity and stand joins across a seed
   sweep, sizes, bodies and gate states; assembly
   time and memory at each size; route agreement and stretch against an
   unrestricted fine search, including detours that leave today's box. Bots unchanged.
2. **Free-space triangulation.** In the shape module, eroded per body, with funnel
   paths and swept validation; replaces the interim grid. Measure build time at
   36 × 18 before adoption and compare passage witnesses from 42.1. Built in #422,
   measured in [42.7](42.7-navigation-mesh.md).
3. **Knowledge and optimistic planning.** Support sets, the discovery filter,
   gate observations, result states; the information test with hidden-map twins. Built in
   #423 as tiles on the discovery grid (see "Knowledge and optimistic planning").
4. **Bot adoption.** Replace truth reads in `bots.ts` together; choose the content
   version; waypoint type and consumer audit; playability on live chains. Built in #424 (see
   "Bot adoption"), measured in [42.8](42.8-bot-adoption.md). Carried from stage 3 (#423):
   - **Path quality first.** Measure path aliasing from the tiles over the 42.7 sweep, with full
     and partial knowledge. Compare against the stage-2 mesh planner and the unrestricted fine
     search, and report length stretch and extra kinks. Two candidate fixes: straighten runs of
     a path that lie wholly in unknown cells (open by hypothesis), and shortcut corners where a
     straight segment stays inside known free space. Watch for bots stalling where a prefix ends
     on a cell line.
   - **Replanning cost.** A flat query costs about 75 ms at 36 × 18, so stagger replanning
     across bots, as discovery staggers its samples, or slice a search across ticks. The budget
     stays an expansion count. If budgets bind, add a hierarchy over known tiles.
   - **Information test.** Extend it to target choice, ranking, `insideMap`, `accessible()` and
     inputs. `accessible()` reads live gate locks today; it should read `memory.gates`.
5. **Middle level and extra crossings,** when delegation children are retained. Deferred
   (Corey, 2026-10-23, #425): after stage 4 no bot plans over the portal graph, so this waits
   until #470's sliced and local replanning (its parts 1 and 2) show whether the expansion budget
   still binds. If it does, #470's coarse level over the tiles is the consumer and #425 resumes
   to feed it. After part 1 it does not: no plan ran out of budget across 42.9's 13 matches.
