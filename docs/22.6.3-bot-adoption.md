# 22.6.3. Bot adoption

the levels and their owners, derivation and compatibility. Moved from 22.6 without changing its content (#510).

## Bot adoption (stage 4)

Built in #424. The default content, `content-3`, is `content-2` with `navigation: 'tiles'`
(Corey chose a new content id, 2026-10-23, 17.3); `content-1` and `content-2` keep the grid
planner. In `shared/simulation/bots.ts`:

- **Who plans with what.** A contestant plans with `createKnowledgePlanner` over its own
  knowledge. A gladiator plans with the same planner over the whole map, every cell known and
  doors as they stand, rather than the portal planner. The portal planner's region tables cost
  about 6 s per body at 36 × 18 (42.7) and would need rebuilding as doors unlock, while one flat
  planner serves both roles, treats doors as edge states and works on maps without regions. The
  portal graph stays the hierarchy to add if budgets bind.
- **Truth reads replaced.** A building's loot is judged reachable from the doors the bot last saw
  locked (`memory.gates`), not live locks. An exploration frontier is ground if the bot has
  surveyed its cell and its centre is ground, or if the cell is unsurveyed (open by hypothesis);
  a target found to be void is settled. Objectives are ranked by straight-line distance; the
  street graph is truth, and chain maps have none.
- **Routes are world points.** The route is the planner's whole tentative path in `route`, walked
  past its confirmed prefix: stepping into unknown ground is how the bot learns what is there. A
  taut path turns at corners a clearance away, so a bot turns onto the next leg only when the
  first 24 units of it are clear for its body, or when it is on the corner.
- **When to replan.** Unknown ground is planned as open, so learning more can only close a route
  or lengthen it, never open a shorter one elsewhere. A route therefore stands until its goal
  moves a tile, the keys held or door observations change, or a cell it was planned through as
  unknown gets a known tile; a standing route is also replanned every 5 s. Replans run on each
  bot's turn, every 20 ticks staggered by its place in the player list. A bot whose route was
  cleared, or that walked out a route that stopped short, plans at once; a first plan and a retry
  of a failed one wait for its turn.
- **Budget.** A repath may expand 12,000 places. A query across a 36 × 18 map with everything
  known makes about 60,000 (about 110 ms warm); at the budget the bot walks to the place searched
  nearest its goal and plans on from there. A known dead end (`no-known-route`, or an endpoint in
  known obstacles) gives up an exploration cell and sets an item aside for 30 s, as a stall does.
- **Information test.** `tests/bot-discovery.test.js` walks a bot for 500 ticks on a live map and
  on a twin with no obstacles, doors or ground beyond what it knew and could see. The bot
  remembers a cell 6,000 units away, so its route crosses ground the twins disagree about. Under
  `content-3` the walks agree tick for tick in position, heading, route and inventory; under
  `content-2`, whose grid search reads the truth, they part.
- **Tiles built ahead.** A gladiator's first plan reads every tile of the map; built then, on the
  first tick it plans (tick 10), that tick took 200 to 300 ms at 36 × 18. The map worker builds them
  instead (`prebuiltTiles` in `bots.ts` names which: a gladiator's, under `navigation: 'tiles'`)
  and packs them with `packTiles` into three buffers. Posted as objects, the 36 × 18 tiles would
  cost the main thread 150 to 200 ms to deserialize; packed, they transfer without a copy, and
  `createMapGenerator` adopts them with `adoptPackedTiles`. `freeSpaceTile` unpacks a tile into
  views of the buffers the first time it is asked for it, so adopting costs nothing up front. A
  packed tile equals the tile built in place, which `tests/navigation-knowledge.test.js` checks for
  every cell. Contestants' tiles are still built as they discover, a few at a time. Maps generated
  inline (tests, tools, `createMatch` without a map) build tiles as before. 42.8 has the
  measurements; #470 takes replanning further.
- **Sliced replanning** (`content-4`, #470 part 1). Under `replanning: 'sliced'` a repath is one
  search (`planner.search`) of up to 100,000 expansions, advanced at most 2,000 a tick, while the
  bot walks the route it has; whole-tick searches keep `content-3`'s 12,000. All bots' searches
  share 3,000 expansions a tick, taken in player order: searches that coincide queue rather than
  add up on one tick, which a per-bot slice alone let reach 6,000 (42.10). The search runs over
  `frozenKnowledge`, the cells and door observations as they stood when it started, so it returns
  what a whole search would have then: sliced or whole, it expands the same places in the same
  order (`tests/navigation-knowledge.test.js`). What was learnt meanwhile reaches the adopted plan
  through the usual checks: its doors and watched cells are compared with the bot's knowledge now.
  A due repath leaves a running search alone unless the goal has moved a tile or the keys changed.
  The bot has moved on by the time a route arrives (`joinRoute`). The planner keeps a route's
  legs `navigationClearance` from walls (14 for a contestant, 25 for a gladiator), 2 units wider
  than the body. So when the bot is within that margin of a segment it could have walked along,
  the way straight to the segment's end stays inside the cleared space (its distance from the
  segment only shrinks along the way) and it joins there. Otherwise nothing it knows says the way
  onto the route is clear, so it walks back along its trail, one place a tick since the search
  started, to the route's start: its body has crossed that way already. Being near a clear
  segment is not enough: 10 units off a leg that runs along a wall, the diagonal to the leg's end
  meets the wall. Sweeping a longer leg from here would read geometry it may not know, and
  dropping the route to search again could repeat while it walks on (#473 review). The same
  margin governs turning: under `content-4`, `passed` lets a bot turn short of a waypoint only
  within the margin of the next leg, or it would cut onto that diagonal at the next corner
  instead. Within 9 units of a waypoint steering slows to land on it, so a bot reaches the
  corner rather than circling it. A search in
  progress, like a plan, is bot-private and never snapshotted.
- **Local repair** (`content-5`, #470 part 2). Under `replanning: 'repair'` a contestant that
  learns something near itself repairs its route at once instead of walking on until its next
  sliced repath. What it learns is a door observation that changed, or a cell its route crossed
  as unknown that now has a known tile. A change is near when it lies within 192 units (one
  discovery cell) of the bot or of the next 384 units of its route. The repair is one whole-tick
  search of at most 500 expansions from where the bot stands to the waypoint 576 units along the
  route past the furthest near change, or the route's end; the rest of the route is kept. Short
  searches are cheap on tiles (a 1,200-unit detour costs about 100 expansions with everything
  known). A repair reserves its whole 500 from the tick's shared allowance before it searches, and
  what it spends comes out of it, so sliced searches wait rather than the tick growing; when less
  than 500 is left, the repair waits for the next tick with the change still pending, and the
  tick's search work stays within the allowance. The plan then takes up the change. It stays marked stale, so
  the sliced repath still follows at the bot's next turn, when a change was further off, when a
  search was running from before the change (it is dropped, since it would bring back a route
  that ignores the change), when the repair found no way through within its budget (the sliced
  repath then starts at once), or when a door was seen open or unlocked after being seen locked.
  Learning ground can only close or lengthen a route (unknown ground is planned as open), so a
  local repair is enough for it; an opened door can shorten the route anywhere. A goal that moved
  or keys that changed are not local and wait for the sliced repath as before. Gladiators plan
  over the doors as they stand and watch nothing, so they do not repair.
