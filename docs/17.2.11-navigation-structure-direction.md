## Region content is a stand-in

Corey, in chat with Claude, 2026-10-30, verbatim:

> Another thing is that, up to this point, I haven't really given any design or art direction to the authorship of what is in regions, or even what the set of region types is. What we have has come organically as I've asked for stand ins as needed to grow from macro to micro generation in detail. However, in many ways I expect a lot of it to get scrapped as I work shop things. But it's been important to me to get the tools needed for that authorship in place. And I think I'm approaching this the right way. But I AM also trying to get some authorship that produces things with mechanics that are functional for exercising other elements of the game, and in that respect there's kind of more refinement in the wrong directions and not enough refinement on the right. I was hoping to get to the end of the basics much much faster, but this is dragging on and on. But I've got a lot of big systems open in tickets right now and half implemented, so I am reluctant to add in another digression or pivot by providing more authorship direction. But perhaps a pass across all the issues to recalibrate scope is inorder.

## Detour, not density

Corey, same conversation, verbatim:

> Now what's important is that, while I'm not so much focused on density, I am focused on having much less empty space, and also requiring some exploration to get around things and even needed to back track at times, because there are emergent culdasacs (not like street culdasacs, just abstractly in the pathing). Pretty much if you have any target on the map you can get there with a fairly straight progression with minimal deflection in your path. So even when things are "densly" filled, that's not the same as having to navigate around things that serve as barriers relative to where you're trying to get to.

## Recorded direction

- The current catalogue and each type's content are stand-ins, likely to be
  revised or scrapped once Corey gives authorship direction. Authorship tools
  remain the durable investment.
- Judge content work by what it lets the rest of the game exercise, not by how
  full a region is. The goals named are **much less empty space** and **paths
  that must go around barriers relative to their target**, including emergent
  dead ends that sometimes force backtracking. "Dead end" means a pocket in the
  walkable space, not a street cul-de-sac.
- High density does not satisfy this by itself.

Not chosen by Corey: the explanation of why current maps are straight, the
proposed levers and the proposed measures in 54 and 51.10 are Claude's analysis
from the same conversation. They are proposals for review, not requirements.
Epic #529's density framing was an agent's, not a user requirement.

## Independent region edges and useful fallback

Corey, in the follow-up discussion of #705 / PR #713, 2026-10-30, verbatim:

> I especially would like to see more instances of different regions sharing a not guaranteed passable section with content that butts up against each other. If only to show it happens often enough that you'll encounter it at least once traversing the map. After that it's tuning of various authorship details, but at the momoent we don't have authorship strategies that can meaningfully incidentally put up unpassable sections that span regions not as a collaboration, but incidentally.

> Also, I suspect maybe the issue is that we are ignoring across all builders perhaps duplicated policy of maintaining a passable moat around the entire regions perimiter.

> Also, I feel like we have generators that fill in nothing still. I thought they were supposed to fall back to other generators that do stuff until we had an implementation

Applied direction:

- Establish incidental obstacles spanning independently authored, different
  regions at unguaranteed seams, with ordinary traversal encounter evidence.
  A coordinated macro curtain or an isolated touching fixture does not establish
  this capability. [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md) still owns guaranteed clear
  margins and access obligations; unguaranteed edges do not acquire a compulsory
  wall or a compulsory bypass.
  The historical default-passable perimeter answer in
  [17.2.2](17.2.2-boundaries-and-prescriptions.md) does not govern these seams:
  use 20.6's resolved guarantees; hints and unstated segments contribute no
  margin of their own.
- Audit duplicated perimeter-bypass assumptions before adding new authorship
  machinery. The moat explanation is a user hypothesis, not an established
  universal cause. Initial code inspection found several local rings/setbacks,
  while the shared canvas and other strategies allow edge contact. Their
  real-map contribution remains to be measured.
- Prefer useful bounded alternative content when a themed strategy cannot
  place its primary content in viable fill. Preserve intentionally authored
  open space and genuine no-fit outcomes; historical M24/M25 remain in
  [17.2.6](17.2.6-catalogue-questions.md). All registered types now have strategies;
  empty geometry can result from placement rejection without a missing stub.

Open implementation owners are #716 (edge-policy audit and a bounded two-family
incidental seam capability) and #717 (empty-output attribution and productive
fallback). Their family counts, proposed checks and sequencing are implementation
scope, not additional verbatim user requirements. [41](41-roadmap.md) and #529
own priority and the relationship to older composition/tooling issues.
