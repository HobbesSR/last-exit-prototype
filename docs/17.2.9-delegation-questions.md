# 17.2.9. Builder delegation decisions

Up: [17.2](17.2-map-questions.md). Proposal and individual decision IDs:
[20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md), issue #289. D1-D10 are proposal decision
IDs, not M-numbers. Accepted answers do not imply implementation.

## D1. Identifying patches and explaining relinquishment

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> Let's see. D1 only the builder can identify cell patches it can't fill. When offering cells, it can have a per builder set of tags indicating why the builder is not retaining ownership of those cells, for debug reasons. I'm less concerned about those as being parameters the new sub-region builder utilizes, but might as well have a channel for it.

Recorded decision: only the owning builder identifies patches it cannot fill.
Offers may carry builder-defined reason tags explaining why it relinquishes
ownership. Preserve those tags for debugging and provide a channel to the
receiving child builder. Child builders need not use them; the tags are advisory
metadata, not child configuration or access/ownership obligations. Tag vocabulary
belongs to the offering builder rather than a universal SDK reason taxonomy.

## D2. Whole-cell ownership and an empty fallback

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D2 seems good, not sure what you mean by delegating tiny gaps, but regions that meet a region builder requirements can't be submitted, and we'll always have a default empty builder for irregular shapes and other awkward things.

Eligibility wording clarified by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> Yes, it must meet the receiving builder’s requirements.

Recorded decision: accept disjoint whole-cell patches and retained cells that
exactly partition original ownership. A patch can be submitted to a particular
receiving builder only when it meets that builder's requirements. A default empty
builder is always available for irregular shapes and other awkward footprints
that content builders cannot handle. Empty output still retains cell ownership
and applicable portal/clearance obligations; it does not excuse a region whose
required crossings cannot physically connect.

The proposal's "tiny gaps" meant sub-cell slivers beside freeform geometry,
not irregular whole-cell footprints. Whole-cell delegation leaves those slivers
with their cell's owner; awkward whole-cell patches can use the empty builder.
