# 17.2.9. Builder delegation decisions

Up: [17.2](17.2-map-questions.md). Proposal and individual decision IDs:
[20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md), issue #289. D1-D10 are proposal decision
IDs, not M-numbers. Accepted answers do not imply implementation.

## D1. Identifying patches and explaining relinquishment

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> Let's see. D1 only the builder can identify cell patches it can't fill. When offering cells, it can have a per builder set of tags indicating why the builder is not retaining ownership of those cells, for debug reasons. I'm less concerned about those as being parameters the new sub-region builder utilizes, but might as well have a channel for it.

Recorded decision: only the owning builder identifies patches it cannot fill.
Offers may carry builder-defined reason tags explaining why it relinquishes
ownership. Preserve those tags for debugging and provide a channel to the
receiving child builder. Child builders need not use them; the tags are advisory
metadata, not child configuration or access/ownership obligations. Tag vocabulary
belongs to the offering builder rather than a universal SDK reason taxonomy.

## D2. Whole-cell ownership and an empty fallback

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D2 seems good, not sure what you mean by delegating tiny gaps, but regions that meet a region builder requirements can't be submitted, and we'll always have a default empty builder for irregular shapes and other awkward things.

Eligibility wording clarified by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> Yes, it must meet the receiving builder’s requirements.

Recorded decision: accept disjoint whole-cell patches and retained cells that
exactly partition original ownership. A patch can be submitted to a particular
receiving builder only when it meets that builder's requirements. A default empty
builder is always available for irregular shapes and other awkward footprints
that content builders cannot handle. Empty output still retains cell ownership
and applicable portal/clearance obligations; it does not excuse a region whose
required crossings cannot physically connect.

The proposal's "tiny gaps" meant sub-cell slivers beside freeform geometry,
not irregular whole-cell footprints. Whole-cell delegation leaves those slivers
with their cell's owner; awkward whole-cell patches can use the empty builder.

## D3. Builder-controlled timing and a self-contained interface

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> I guess for D3, this is up to the individual builder, the resulting sub-region the patch becomes should be buildable entirely from the information passed in to its interface. So any collaborating details must be part of the interface and otherwise the builders should be agnostic to the details of adjacent sub-regions, no matter how they come to be.

Recorded decision: delegation timing belongs to the individual builder. There
is no universal requirement to delegate before committing parent geometry.
The resulting child region must be buildable entirely from its interface input.
Any details necessary for collaboration must be expressed in that interface;
builders otherwise remain agnostic to adjacent regions' internals and origins.
This replaces D3's proposed mandatory planning/finalization split. Builders may
still use such a split internally when useful, but it is not the delegation contract.

## D4. Builder-selected child content with decomposer guidance

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D4 I'm going to say th builders decide the child content. It perhaps can be informed by information passed in from the original decomposer.

Recorded decision: the delegating builder decides child content. Its decision
may be informed by information passed in from the original decomposer. Such
guidance does not transfer content selection to the decomposer or a universal
SDK dispatcher. Per D3, any guidance used by the builder must arrive through
its interface, rather than by inspecting the decomposer's private state.
The guidance representation and a per-builder allowlist are not settled by
this answer; builders may choose their own selection policy within the
receiving builder's requirements (D2).

## D5. Preserve original reachability through every partition

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D5 reachability ultimately must satisfy the reachability assumptions the macro generator makes about the original region before decomposition (which to be clear, is a separate potential partitioning step of a region into sub-regions, who themselves can split of new sub-region patches). In the macro strategy, I believe there is something about how regions can join together for the macro reachability algorithm and passability must be maintained. So anyway, the point is, if a patch is spun off into a sub region, its portals must still be able to reach the same portals they were required to meet before, before the original region began being partitioned in any manner.

Recorded decision: preserve the reachability obligations of the original macro
region across all partitioning. Decomposition is a separate optional partition
step; its resulting builders may subsequently relinquish patches into further
sub-regions. Neither operation resets or weakens the original obligations.
Portals affected by a patch handoff must retain the reachability relationships
required before any partitioning of the original region began.

Macro's region graph joins regions through guaranteed portals (51.5), and its
proof computes connected components from those joins. It relies on each original
region connecting all its portals at hunter size (51.1). Partitioning must keep
that inference valid. Descendant portal connections compose through local
interfaces to preserve the original relationships; a child need not physically
contain every original portal. Necessary collaboration information is supplied
through interfaces per D3, not adjacent-region private state. New child portals
must join the inherited reachability structure rather than create isolated
islands that happen to satisfy a local one-portal rule.

This accepts the end-to-end obligation, not the proposal's specific retained-area
pilot routing, one-versus-two-frontage choice, or portal selection algorithm.
Existing elective validation policy remains unchanged.

## D6. Recursion limit and terminal empty builder

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D6, I'm not sure where the 4 children come from, but yes maybe we put a maximum recursion on sub-regions from sub-region splitting, where what remains is then just given empty builder, which should always succeed and never split.

Recorded direction: limit recursive sub-region patch splitting by depth. At the
limit, remaining patches are assigned to the default empty builder, which always
succeeds and never splits. The four-child cap was an arbitrary proposed pilot
limit, not an existing requirement, and is removed. No child-count cap is accepted
by this answer. The numerical depth and precise counting convention remain open;
the bound concerns builder patch splitting, distinct from the optional initial
decomposition step described in D5.

The empty builder is a terminal content operation, not a repair pass. Existing
interface validity and inherited reachability obligations remain in force; an
empty builder adds no geometry and does not fail or split in an attempt to fill
an awkward region.

## D7. Complete-region delegation goes only to empty

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D7 If a region for whatever reason cannot fullfil its work through a combination of building and splitting, it may fully delegate to another builder, but since we don't want to worry about loops right now, a delegation of the complete sub-region must go to the empty builder.

Recorded decision: when a region cannot fulfill its work through building and
patch splitting, it may relinquish the complete region. A complete-region handoff
must go to the empty builder, never another content builder, to avoid delegation
loops. This differs from a proper-subset patch handoff, whose content is selected
by its offering builder (D4), and complements D6's depth-limit empty fallback.
The empty builder always succeeds and never splits; original ownership and
interface obligations remain unchanged.

## D8. Loot deferred; incompatible core regions fall back to empty

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D8, we're generally unconcerned with loot at the moment as the loot system and generation will get a complete overhaul. But yes, sensitive regions like core sites for the time being we will not code in anything save rejecting building if the region it receives is truly not compatible. Ideally this would cause the map to fail. I don't know if we have an error channel for that. Perhaps best just to leave it for a post generation validation step for the map having core mechanical features placed. If they do have to reject building, they should just delegate to the empty region.

Recorded decision: defer new loot-generation/accounting policy to its planned
overhaul. Do not add special core-site delegation machinery now. Sensitive core
builders reject building only when their received region is truly incompatible;
if they reject, delegate the complete region to empty. Required core mechanical
features are checked after generation rather than repaired or fabricated by the
delegation mechanism. Preserve original requests so missing features are visible.

Current channel, checked in code: macro measurement reports `broken-promise`
counts per original brief and `missing-core-element` counts per set piece
instance (51.10). Map tools expose defects and a validity flag. The report itself
diagnoses without discarding the map. `liveMapFromBuilt` rejects missing required
spawn/exit counts, chargers and transit sites, and checks core-site occupancy.
Making generation itself fail through a new universal error channel is not
implemented or specified by this answer; the existing post-generation checks
provide the starting point for the desired map failure.

## D9. Recover details from the owning layer's snapshot

Answered by Corey, 2026-10-10:

> D9 not sure quite what this is saying, but hopefully these are details that can be deterministically reclaimed from the snapshot format of any generation layer that has them.

Recorded direction: delegation details should be deterministically recoverable
from the snapshot format of the generation layer that owns them. This follows
the existing object/view and saving rules in 51.12 and 53: derive views from saved
objects; preserve inputs and algorithm versions for regeneration. Do not require
duplicated hierarchy data in every downstream result, but do not declare the
hierarchy unrecoverable or permanently tool-only either.

The exact snapshot representation is still an implementation design task. A
flat geometry result alone is not generally enough to infer historical patch
choices or rejection reasons. Recovery may need an owning-layer snapshot or
deterministic regeneration from its complete inputs with the matching algorithm.
This answer does not independently freeze the proposed flattened `region-2`
shape or prohibit a versioned snapshot extension if needed.

## Implementation design defaults

All D1-D10 have recorded direction. The lead selected a default maximum of three
patch-splitting levels, with initial builder depth zero; optional initial
decomposition does not consume that depth. At the limit, further offered patches
go to empty. The execution setting is configurable; this is an implementation
default, not a new user requirement or a child-count cap.

For D9, start with a versioned micro-owned `builder-delegation-1` snapshot of the
owning inputs/settings, semantic requests and outcomes, and nested generation
objects/results. Derive ownership/interface/flattened views when possible; retain
non-derivable authored decisions. The concrete API/encoding is owned by the
executor/snapshot issue, constrained by deterministic recovery and version rules.
See [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md) and its implementation sequence.
