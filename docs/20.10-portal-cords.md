# 20.10. Portal cords and prospective parcels

Back to [20](20-micro-generation.md). Source direction and provenance:
[17.2.10](17.2.10-portal-cord-direction.md). Discussion: [#643](/corey/astra_test/issues/643).

Status: design reviewed in #648, 2026-10-28; optional validator fallback
implemented in #644. The optional retained-content coordinator is implemented
in #645; handoff coordination, growth helpers and builder adoption remain pending.
User direction
establishes optional tooling, pessimistic claims, continuous hunter clearance,
interior starts and useful peripheral handoffs. The mechanics below are the
proposed synthesis. Current canvas and delegation behavior remains in
[20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md) and [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md).

## Purpose and freedom of composition

A builder can reserve a parcel before knowing its geometry while the SDK finds
passages around it. Claims may be anywhere: interior islands, boundary parcels,
corner lobes, or extensions of an earlier parcel. Edge attachment is never an
admission requirement. Builders can use polygons, polyominoes, both, their own
growth loop, or none of this toolkit.

Carve-first is a primary workflow: choose useful peripheral hunks for another
builder, prepare their interfaces, then populate the retained space. Interior-first
and perimeter-first workflows are equally supported. Delegation must not depend
on exhausting perimeter growth and handing off whatever interior scraps remain.
The strategy selects candidate shape, location, recipient and order. Narrow
circulation remnants can stay deliberately empty with the parent.

For interior-first handoffs, register the placed structure's envelope and its
required doorstep/gate terminals before probing candidate peripheral lobes,
corner boxes or edge slabs. Let those candidates push movable yard cords around
the structure. Do not first freeze a route tree into addressed/protected cells
and then treat raw unaddressed components as the only available handoffs. That
would recreate fixed-route refusal and could leave wrap-around interior scraps.
Actual fixed access anchors remain protected; candidate usefulness is builder
policy, not a universal compactness constraint.

## Separate allocation, obstacles and proofs

An addressed record remains intent, not collision. A deliberately empty court
stays nonblocking. A prospective parcel also has a pessimistic routing envelope:
until explicitly relaxed, the whole envelope is unavailable to cords. Settling
does not implicitly replace it with actual geometry or reroute through a building.

The coordinator owns parcel handles and envelope state alongside the existing
intent ledger. Reserved parcels are unavailable to unrelated infill and delegation.
Population through a handle does not double-mark its intent or collide with its
own reservation; every other canvas check still applies. The full relevant physical
footprint must remain inside its envelope. Do not substitute origin/template-box
containment for physical containment. Existing colliders, reserves and fixed
access obligations remain effective.

Retained content parcels can be populated or settled intentionally empty.
Delegation is a separate whole-cell ownership operation, not a settlement outcome
of a content-claim handle. The coordinator shares transaction machinery, but
uses distinct admission domains and proposals for claims and handoffs.
Cancellation of an unpopulated claim can
release allocation and envelope together; cancellation after population must not
erase live occupancy. The first implementation may refuse that cancellation.
Relaxing a settled envelope to actual geometry is deferred; retaining it is safe
and useful. A private releasable reservation layer may be needed because today's
AddressedArea is append-only; do not change legacy mark semantics to simulate it.

## Atomic operations

Illustrative names, not a frozen API:

- `probeClaim(area)` computes a detached retained-content proposal against a
  state revision, constrained to reduced fill.
- `acceptClaim(proposal)` publishes allocation and repaired witness together;
  a stale proposal is refused or recomputed. `tryClaim(area)` combines them.
- `populate(handle, assembly)` enforces envelope containment and canvas rules.
- `settle(handle, outcome)` finalizes populated or intentionally empty retained
  intent; it does not transfer ownership.
- `probeHandoff(offers)` prepares a hypothetical whole-cell partition and its
  routing obligations; `acceptHandoff(proposal)` accepts that plan atomically
  into coordinator planning state. Neither operation executes child builders.
  The final plan is published through the single execution batch below.

An extension replaces the same parcel's envelope atomically rather than conflicting
with its own old footprint. Rejecting a proposal leaves elements, allocation,
envelopes, witness, IDs and RNG unchanged. Diagnostics may count attempted work.
Malformed input is distinct from ordinary candidate refusal and budget exhaustion.
Initialization failure is distinct from a ready tracker with no required routes.

Live cords are not immutable canvas protections: those would veto movement of the
cords. The coordinator evaluates tentative placement and routing together. Builders
can freeze settled passages afterward. A door facing a current cord is not promised
future access: realize frontage after routing settles or explicitly anchor its
approach. Optional interior terminals preserve builder-specific doorstep/gate
access; failures are reported separately from the external portal promise.

## Continuous connectivity witness

Keep [19](19-decomposition-design.md)'s portal promise and the fewer-than-two-portals
exemption. When that promise applies, reconstruct every required stand through
`portalStands`, including coverage along long portals. Connect them by continuous
polylines whose every segment passes `travelClear` at `hunterClearance(cellSize)`.
Optional builder terminals can impose additional connectivity even with fewer
external portals; they do not alter the public promise.

Plan in the access mask; physical content envelopes fit the reduced fill mask.
Keep [20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md)'s automatic clear margins and original portal
identities. Internal guaranteed runs keep their clear footprint without acquiring
external pair-connectivity semantics. Threshold clearance is not interior
connectivity. Margins protect the doorway from content constrained to reduced
fill; cords protect connections further inside. Verify stands and connector
sweeps rather than assuming margins
make every hand-built or clipped brief geometrically feasible.

Use `shared/free-space.ts` for candidate discovery, followed by swept proof.
Whole-mesh rebuilding per trial is the initial baseline, not a demonstrated
production performance guarantee. `freePaths` chooses triangle corridors and
funnels them; it does not establish globally shortest Euclidean paths. Its lengths
are witness lengths. Shared search ancestry does not itself produce identical
geometric trunks. Normalize genuinely coincident segments for shared graph edges;
capsule overlap alone does not establish a junction. Test merging and splitting.
Root-to-terminal paths suffice for connectivity without all-pairs searches.

Reuse valid witness geometry where practical. A rebuild may choose a different
side of an island; stability and detour preferences belong to policy. Neither
fixed homotopy nor optimal shared trunks is promised.

Retain the original seam stands. Reuse `entry`/`FreePath.leads` under a bounded
connector policy and sweep every lead as well as every stand. Preserve shared
navigation erosion defaults. The extra erosion/arc approximation can exclude
valid points; a connector bound is not simply the erosion margin at corners.
Attachment/search failure means no witness was found, not physical impossibility.

Existing precedents are `map/micro/strategies/block-circulation.ts`
(`createCirculationCheck`, #614) for cell-claim admission and checkpoint's cached
`Evidence` for incremental swept proof. Reuse their lessons; migrating either
builder is not a prerequisite.

## Validator search modes

Corey's quoted direction in [17.2.10](17.2.10-portal-cord-direction.md) keeps both
searches with parameterized fallback. Implemented validator API (#644):

`validatePortalReach(input, { search: 'grid' | 'thorough' })`

The default `grid` preserves prior verdicts; it is Claude's
compatibility choice, not a default selected by Corey. `thorough` retains
successful grid
routes and tries mesh discovery plus sweeps for missed connections, including
joins along a portal. Both use final collision geometry, the same access mask and
the same clearance checks. Reuse a mesh within a validation call. Diagnostics
identify the discovering search; neither mode's miss proves impossibility.
Do not call the grid universally faster without representative measurements.
The exact result diagnostics and bounded endpoint policy are in
[20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md).

Cord adopters use thorough final-geometry validation in tests. Negative fixtures
exercise thorough mode too, but their expected geometric obstruction must be
justified independently of a search miss. A supplied-witness verifier is deferred;
the first validator discovers its own routes, independent of tracker state.
Changing the default is a separate decision.

## Geometric inputs and ownership

### Implemented retained-content API (#645)

`createProspectiveParcels(brief, { fixedAccess?, terminals?, budget? })` returns
`{ ok: true, coordinator, counters }` or an explicit initialization failure.
An empty witness is successful only when no connectivity obligations require
paths. Invalid inputs throw; geometric/search refusal and deterministic budget
exhaustion return failures. This API owns its private canvas; callers route all
subsequent canvas writes through it rather than sharing a mutable external canvas.
It consumes no builder RNG or runtime ID allocator.

The coordinator exposes `probeClaim(shapes, extendHandle?)`,
`acceptClaim(proposal)`, `tryClaim(shapes, extendHandle?)`,
`populate(handle, assembly)`, `settle(handle, 'populated' | 'empty')`, and
`cancel(handle)`. Proposals are detached snapshots backed by private identity and
revision checks; editing their displayed geometry does not change the transaction.
Handles likewise require object identity. IDs advance only for accepted new
parcels. Extension unions new shapes with the same parcel's prior envelope;
settled parcels cannot extend. One atomic population assembly may contain many
elements; a second population of the same handle is refused. Prepare that assembly
in the builder's own loop before publishing it. Population must fit both its
intent and complete physical footprint (including gates, reserves, spots, enclosed
interiors and roofs) into the canonical envelope and satisfy every canvas check.
Population never marks its own reservation twice. The parcel's canvas position is
its claim acceptance order. Cancellation is supported for unpopulated parcels,
including intentionally empty settlements; populated cancellation is refused.

`commit`, `markEmpty`, `fits`, `spaced`, `fill`, `access`, `elements` and
`addressed` support custom builder loops. Returned state is detached; mutating it
does not mutate the coordinator. The allocation view includes live reservations;
the legacy append-only `AddressedArea` API is unchanged. Fixed access and committed
approaches remain protected, including during extension. Live witness paths do
not become canvas protections. Settling retains the whole pessimistic envelope.
Ordinary `commit` proves routing first, then uses the live canvas's atomic commit;
`markEmpty` directly uses its atomic intent write. These operations append history
without replaying prior assemblies. Claim trials, extension, population, settlement
and cancellation retain detached replay. Successfully committed ordinary assembly
blockers are cached privately in append order; failed writes never enter that cache.
Parcel populations remain represented by their pessimistic envelopes, so parcel
replay and cancellation do not invalidate the ordinary-assembly cache.
Every geometry commit still pays witness
validation/search cost; this is not raw-canvas performance parity. Measured limits
are in [42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md).

`parcelCells(cells, cellSize)` and `parcelArea(shapes)` produce canonical,
disjoint convex pieces from cell squares, boxes and simple world-space polygons,
including concave inputs and overlapping compound pieces. Holes in a union stay
holes. Shape fields determine canonical codepoint ordering independently of object
property insertion order or locale. They use the existing construction topology (1/256 world unit) and restored
world-space edges from 29.3, rather than rasterizing into cells. Sub-grid slivers
can be lost: the returned pieces, also displayed in proposals, are the authoritative
envelope for routing and containment. Circular envelopes are explicitly unsupported;
circular physical content is conservatively checked with a circumscribed polygon.
`areaContains` subtracts convex pieces in world space rather than relying on
rounded clipping to admit physical escape. Conservative containment may refuse
near-boundary geometry. There is no exact-arithmetic or arbitrary sub-grid topology
guarantee.

`discoverCordWitness` is separately usable. It connects all required portal stands
and optional interior terminals to a canonical root, reuses swept-valid previous
paths, otherwise tries a direct sweep and then shared free-space mesh discovery.
Every stand and segment, including attachment leads, receives hunter-clearance
swept proof. A miss reports `external` or `interior`, separately from `budget`.
Coincident collinear path segments are split into canonical shared graph edges;
capsule overlap creates no junction. This provides explicit geometric merge and
split witnesses without promising an optimal trunk or homotopy.

Default per-discovery limits are one mesh build, 65,536 swept checks and 8,192
attempted root paths (`maxMeshBuilds`, `maxSweeps`, `maxPaths`). Counters report
actual work; failed routing probes retain them. Hard limits bound input geometry
and normalization: 16,384 blocker vertex equivalents, terminals or prior path
points; 4,096 accepted path segments; 256 parcels; 4,096 aggregate envelope pieces;
1,024 external canvas operations; and 4,096 populated elements. A parcel input
polygon has at most 256 vertices; the area adapter limits a union to 4,096 input
pieces and 16,384 vertices. Unsupported adapter sizes throw `RangeError`; claim
probes translate geometry-size exhaustion to `budget`. These are conservative
tool limits, not established production capacity.

Representative deterministic witnesses in `micro-prospective-parcels` use a
10-by-8-cell region and two two-cell portals: initialization uses zero meshes,
seven sweeps and three paths; an intersecting interior island uses one mesh,
57/63/93 sweeps at cell sizes 16/40/80, respectively, and three paths. A 20-by-16
region with two six-cell portals and a four-piece holed envelope uses one mesh,
181 sweeps and 19 paths, including every long-portal stand. A 20-sweep budget
refuses the repair atomically. These small witnesses justify deterministic
coverage and expose costs; they do not establish whole-builder performance.
The focused `micro-parcel-area`, `micro-cord-witness` and
`micro-prospective-parcels` tests include independent thorough final-geometry
validation. No production builder uses the coordinator yet.

Provide explicit cell/box and world-space shape adapters to one area representation.
Compound pieces form a union: internal overlaps are legal, holes remain holes,
and inter-parcel overlap is checked separately. Use existing shape-construction
machinery to validate/decompose concave input and normalize disjoint ledger pieces.
Do not rasterize continuous polygons for routing or silently feed overlapping
compound pieces to today's `markAll`.

Physical claims and delegation patches are different domains. A delegated patch
is connected whole-cell ownership under [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md), and may
include inherited non-fill/clear cells. It cannot be modeled simply as a solid
content claim required to fit reduced fill. Preserve sub-cell touched-cell
ownership semantics; no partial-cell ownership transfer is introduced.

## Peripheral handoff transaction

Prepare the candidate partition before publishing it: exact retained/child
ownership, whole external-portal inheritance, all paired crossings, inherited and
new clear margins, and receiving feasibility remain governed by 20.7. Recheck any
earlier recipient whose interface changes. No cells or obligations disappear.

Current preparation proves connected retained cells, interface composition and
crossing feasibility, and checks declared geometry against new fill constraints.
It does not prove hunter connectivity through the retained interior. The cord
coordinator must perform that additional check on the prepared retained brief
**after** `inheritBriefMargins`, using its resulting fill/access masks, all new
crossing stands, retained original terminals and builder-specific doorsteps/gates.
Test both existing geometry and pessimistic envelopes against the new constraints;
cell connectivity or a clear crossing alone cannot substitute for this check.

`context.delegate` currently executes one patch batch per builder invocation.
Multiple candidate trials belong in hypothetical coordinator/preparation state,
not repeated calls to that executor. Probe cumulative offer sets; when a later
candidate changes earlier interfaces, recheck receivers and retained routing.
Interleaved content claims must invalidate stale handoff proposals. Before the
single execution call, reproduce and verify the final partition using the same
canonical offer order, policy, blockers and receiver semantics as execution.
Do not publish a different accepted subset or interface than the one verified.
If deterministic reproduction cannot be guaranteed, stop for an explicit
executor/snapshot contract change under 20.7 rather than bypassing the one-batch
rule. Rejected hypothetical candidates never dispatch children or change live
ownership. Historical recovery must still reconstruct the executed partition.

Route the retained owner's new obligations using its new brief and explicit
known-clear context. A whole original portal transferred to a child is removed
only from the retained owner's terminal set, not from the containing region's
promise. Add all required stands of every new retained/child crossing. The child
connects its inherited external portals and paired interfaces through its own
promise. Validate composition, not just the parent or child in isolation.

A plain solid claim over a portal-bearing hunk would obstruct that original
terminal and be rejected before transfer. Therefore do not reserve the hunk by
the ordinary content-claim path and try to change terminal ownership afterward.
Partition, terminals, allowed access context, envelopes and intent must be tested
as one hypothetical handoff and committed together. Routing may use guaranteed
clear interface context, never an assumed path through a child's arbitrary interior.
An existing parent collider/access anchor in the proposed patch or a new margin
must be resolved or cause refusal before ownership changes.

The containing region retains its original promise by interface composition.
A child with only one interface and no inherited external portal keeps the
existing one-portal exemption; do not impose an interior-access promise on it.
The v1 retained owner follows preparation's connected-ownership requirement.
General routing through child interiors, beyond promised interface composition,
does not follow from pessimistic envelopes and is not required here.

## Optional growth and bounded work

Helpers can extend frontage bands, grow corner/lobe parcels or expand interior
seeds. The builder chooses order, sizes, stopping rules and recipient suitability.
Round-robin fronts and witness-detour limits are optional. Root stretch is not an
all-pairs detour bound; define its baseline and zero-length cases explicitly.

As a conservative candidate filter, helpers can use block's two-cell setback
near portals: a boundary lot closer to the portal can pinch the turn beyond its
end cap (20.6). This is optional pruning, not a new universal claim restriction;
swept proof still decides clearance.

Nested physical growth only removes free space, but rounding, attachment and
bounded search can make the operational success predicate non-monotone. Bisection
is at most a conservative heuristic: probe one revision, keep a swept-verified
feasible lower bound, and do not claim maximum feasible depth. A budget miss is
not proof that all deeper claims fail.

Bound candidate attempts, input/mesh complexity and route work deterministically;
do not make acceptance depend on elapsed time. The public SDK's large limits in
20.2 are not measured planner capacity. Report unsupported workload or exhaustion
explicitly. Measure representative stand counts, holes and obstacle complexity
before selecting defaults. Do not turn one synthetic timing into a guarantee.

## Acceptance and delivery boundary

The first capability accepts a useful parcel intersecting an initial route by
rerouting around it, and refuses a true cut without mutation. Include interior
islands, union/holes, long portal coverage, scale variation, hand-built versus
macro margins, endpoint connectors, stale probes, mixed input forms, cancellation,
own-claim population, envelope escape, fixed frontage, deterministic shuffled
inputs and budget failures. Thorough validation includes a valid off-lattice
witness and a genuine separating wall.

The composition milestone releases a useful boundary-flush corner/lobe before
core filling, with both no-transfer and whole-external-portal-transfer cases.
Validate ownership, receiving feasibility, final retained and child geometry and
the containing region. Include multiple cumulative probes followed by exactly
one execution batch, with final interfaces equal to those verified, and rejection
when new margins conflict with existing content/envelopes. Rejecting handoff
leaves all original state intact.

The adoption milestone replaces hut/compound's pragmatic whole-perimeter collars
only after equivalent circulation and actual doorstep/gate access are protected.
Include an interior-first fixture whose initial yard route crosses a useful
peripheral candidate: the route must move to admit the handoff while the placed
structure and actual entrance access remain valid. Removing only the collar,
while freezing the old route tree and offering raw residual components, does
not establish this capability.
Preserve each type's theme; demonstrate useful peripheral child shapes instead
of counting arbitrary residual cells as success. Adoption is intentional content
change, with seed comparisons and compatibility/snapshot review under
[53](53-map-artifacts-and-tools.md) and 20.7. A content bump alone does not preserve
historical crossing reconstruction. Never regenerate the frozen characterization
fixture for this work. Verification follows [31](31-verification.md).

Sequencing and implementation issue status belong in [41](41-roadmap.md).
