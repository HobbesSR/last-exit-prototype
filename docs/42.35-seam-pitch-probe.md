**Caps cannot change structural run length.** Clipping them releases 4,189
additional both-sided fillable seam units at 36, but other guaranteed strips
and caps still split every fillable stretch below six cells. This also holds
at 12 and 24 wide.

**Removing guarantees can expose long runs, but these masks break macro
connectivity.** Across the panel the complementary masks put 49.64–55.15% of
unguaranteed length in structural runs of 6+, but leave 2–5 graph components.
No variant has a short portal. The thinning removes all guaranteed halves on
selected sides; many baseline sides already have only one. It does not reduce
two halves to one everywhere, and is not a rule for an adopted tile library.

## Turning is a real constraint

The existing passable-margin corner witness is exercised with actual hinted
continuations at cell sizes 40 and 48, using the SDK's 0.925-cell hunter
clearance. Full caps supply clear swept legs and a found route, replayed
successfully through `moveBody` and `canOccupy`. Stopping caps at hints removes
the outside corner cell from guaranteed context, and the route search fails.
A third owner can then fill that cell; the diagonal pinch is only `sqrt(2)`
cells, below both the 1.85-cell clearance diameter and the 1.75-cell body
diameter. This witnesses why universal clipping cannot inherit 20.6's turning
argument unchanged, without claiming that every shortened cap fails.

The decision enabled is whether to pursue selective connectivity-preserving
authorship or a geometry-aware replacement for caps. Neither is chosen here.
Physical blockage, builder adoption, long-route detour and frequency remain
unmeasured. One seed per size is a bounded probe, not a seed sweep. No library,
production margin, version, chain baseline or frozen fixture changes.

## Reading after the 2026-11-06 discussion

The probe's candidate A thins guarantees on a fixed layout, so the solver never reacts. Three
facts found afterwards change how to read it:

- **The half-and-half pattern is authored.** Every themed `*-ground` tile in
  `common-primitives@5` states the same perimeter on all four sides: three hinted segments
  beside three passable ones (`hhhPPP`), aligned so that neighbouring tiles meet half to
  half. `field` and the rubble banks state `any`, and `hut-quarter` mixes both.
- **Placement ignores passability.** `placement.ts` says "Passability can't conflict, so it
  never constrains the solve". 51.4's table lets `passable` on either side win. Tile
  difficulty counts only adjacency prescriptions.
- **Corey wants passability matched (17.2.2, 2026-11-06).** `any` fits anything; otherwise
  like with like. On the 36-wide panel map, only 24 tile-seam segments put `passable`
  against a hint today. #849 implements it behind a library switch.

With matching, a fully hinted tile side can sit only against another hinted side or `any`.
Placement's open-face rule already refuses a placement that seals a component and backtracks.
So authored variants would be placed only where connectivity survives, with today's tiles
as the fallback. The disconnected graphs above are therefore an upper bound on harm from
blind thinning, not a verdict on authoring.

A closed duplicate probe (PR #847, branch `claudecode/761-seam-probe`, commit `75a4879`)
skipped, greedily, each side whose thinning macro's validator would refuse. At 36 wide, that
kept every map valid while leaving 52.6% of seam length in runs of 6+ cells (every other
side, 1.5% of sides skipped) or 65.8% (every side, 17% skipped). One-portal regions rose from
13 to 186 or 551. A connectivity-preserving selection exists, and it costs portals rather
than validity.

The direction this points to, which is Corey's to choose: implement the matching rule, then
author weighted fully hinted tile variants with today's tiles as the fallback, measured on
this panel alongside #838's builder adoption as one batch. Cap clipping stays shelved, given
the turning failure above.
