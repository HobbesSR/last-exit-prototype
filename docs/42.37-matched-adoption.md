## Differences from step 7

The adopted content resolves to exactly the experiment's step 7 library, with one addition:
- **`rubble-court`'s ring.** With `clear-rubble-center`'s edges `any`, only the cover tiles'
  three-segment crossings faced the court's one-cell open ring, and a hunter can't follow a
  one-cell ring between them. Every court broke its portal promise (`validate --diagnose`: 7
  courts in 3 maps at 12 wide). The four cover tiles beside the centre are now
  `cover-ground-court-{n,e,s,w}`, `fillable: false`, each facing the centre with a passable side,
  so the ring is guaranteed all round, as the court was authored.

## Placement's heavy tail, and the fix

At step 7 some seeds didn't place in reasonable time. Compared on the same seeds:

| Seed | Before | Step 7 content | With the fix |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 × 12 `library-1` (the slow content test's) | 1.1 s | over 25 min | 9.3 s |
| 36 × 18 `seed-3` | 12.6 s | over 240 s | 5.7 s |
| 36 × 18 `seed-6` | 3.1 s | over 240 s | 6.7 s |

On 10 seeds each, step 7 stalled on none at 24 wide (all about 2 s) and on 2 of 10 at 36 wide.
Each stall was one slot from done: the open-face rule refused a sealed component the size of the
whole map, cut off from a small pocket, and named every slot in it as the cause. The solver could
then backjump only to the latest placement, so it backtracked one slot at a time, flooding the
map-sized component at each node until the iteration cap, and then started the next attempt.

The fix (`open-face.ts`) names a sealed component by its **cut**: the faces between it and the
cells outside it, every path out crossing one, plus the closed slots' links. Changing a slot
inside the component can't reopen the cut, so the cause stays sound, and the solver backjumps to
the slots that closed it. The slow content test (`map/macro/tests/slow/chain-content`) went from
2,299 s to 20 s.

**A tail remains.** The cut makes each refusal's cause small (8–14 slots), but some solves still
churn among the last few slots around one pocket:
- 36 × 18 `size-36-5` (one of `tools/large-briefs.mjs`'s seeds) hadn't placed after 300 s: five
  slots left, the same park pocket refused over and over, each check about 45 ms because the
  map-wide component floods most of the map before it reaches an open face. At the 84,240-node
  cap an attempt would take about an hour.
- A 36 × 18 playground with the 12 × 6 library, `wfc-large-1`: about 115 s (before: 2.1 s). Two
  attempts reach the cap among small sealed pockets, at about 1 ms a node, before a third places.
  `wfc-large-2` and `-3` take 5 s, so the test now uses `wfc-large-2`.
- 24 × 12 seeds `size-24-1` to `-5` and `library-1` place in 2–9 s; 36 × 18 `size-36-1` to `-4`
  in 5–16 s. The 228 sweep maps (12 × 6 and smaller, plus four macro entries at 24 and 36) all place.

Bounding it is a placement decision: restart an attempt sooner (a node or refusal budget well
under the cap), look ahead for pockets as the fill closes them, or make the end-of-solve flood
cheaper. None is in this batch.

## For Corey

- **Adopt?** The bundled libraries are step 7 plus the court fix. The largest effects: three
  quarters of unguaranteed boundary physically closed (from a fifth), one-portal regions up
  from 6–14 to 51–446 a map, long routes a little longer (no route above 1.5), dead-end area up
  by a third to a half, and 36-wide generation 2.2 times as long.
- **Placement's tail.** The cut fix removed the stalls seen at step 7, but some seeds still
  churn near the end (`size-36-5`, a 36 × 18 playground). Live games use 12 × 6, where none of the
  228 sweep maps or the 12 sampled diagnosis maps stalled. Three ways to bound it, none of which
  changes what a valid layout is, the matching rules or the content:
  1. **A restart budget** (the cheapest): abandon an attempt past a refusal count or a time budget
     well under today's cap, and start the next on a fresh stream, as placement's 50 attempts
     already do. Successful 36-wide attempts made about 4,400 open-face checks for 8,424 slots,
     and the stalled ones churned at up to 45 ms a check, so a node cap alone could still leave
     minutes. It changes layouts only for seeds that would reach the budget, and it would unblock
     `large-briefs --check`.
  2. **Connectivity kept up to date** as tiles are placed, instead of re-flooding the map at each
     check: cheap checks, but real engineering, since backtracking removes tiles.
  3. **Filtering options by connectivity**, dropping one that would seal a pocket. The local
     form (a slot whose placed neighbours can't be joined) never fired: the stalled pockets span
     several slots.
  Each could come before or after adoption, as its own change.
- **Hut quarters** nearly vanish from the fill. Keeping them is a content decision (a weight, or
  hut tiles with stated sides).
- **Workflow cost.** The sweep takes about ten minutes, against the two to three minutes 31
  quoted, and `npm test` about 275 s against 150–170 s, with seventeen files over the fast
  tier's 15 s. Both come from building cost (cover and park edge walls, more regions).
