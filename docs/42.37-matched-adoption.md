# 42.37. Matched libraries adopted: comparison report (#849)

Back to [42](42-performance-history.md). Stage 4 of #849: the content Corey approved in the Map Lab
([42.36](42.36-matched-library-experiment.md), step 7) as the bundled libraries,
`common-primitives@6`, `common-set-pieces@6` and `district-set-pieces@5`, under
`passabilityRules: "matched"` ([51.4](51.4-resolution.md); what changed is listed in
[52](52-map-primitives-and-library.md), "Matched adoption"). `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` is `types-64`.
This report is for Corey's adoption decision. Definitions are [51.10](51.10-measurement.md)'s;
the retained data is in [reports/issue-849](reports/issue-849/README.md).

**Workload.** #688's panel and a seed batch: `size-{12,24,36}-{1,2,3}` with #688's parameters
(two exits, eight contestants, three hunters, loot chance .04, tier step .09, cell size 48). Nine
full maps for each library: **before** is `main` at `0a917ba` (`common-primitives@5`, default
rules), **after** is this branch. `map/tools/library-comparison.mts` measures each, and
`map/tools/edge-clearance.mts --maps` the saved maps. Portals, one-portal regions and fill need only
macro (placement through the briefs); `library-comparison.mts --macro` measures them without
building, about 6 s for a 36 × 18 map against 2 minutes built. Physical blocking, detour and dead
ends are measured on built geometry, since hints only suggest what builders do. Figures are means over the three seeds of a
size unless they say otherwise.

## Differences from step 7

The adopted content resolves to exactly the experiment's step 7 library, with one addition:
- **`rubble-court`'s ring.** With `clear-rubble-center`'s edges `any`, only the cover tiles'
  three-segment crossings faced the court's one-cell open ring, and a hunter can't follow a
  one-cell ring between them. Every court broke its portal promise (`validate --diagnose`: 7
  courts in 3 maps at 12 wide). The four cover tiles beside the centre are now
  `cover-ground-court-{n,e,s,w}`, `fillable: false`, each facing the centre with a passable side,
  so the ring is guaranteed all round, as the court was authored.

## Placement's heavy tail, and the fix

At step 7 some seeds didn't place in reasonable time. Compared on the same seeds:

| Seed | Before | Step 7 content | With the fix |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 × 12 `library-1` (the slow content test's) | 1.1 s | over 25 min | 9.3 s |
| 36 × 18 `seed-3` | 12.6 s | over 240 s | 5.7 s |
| 36 × 18 `seed-6` | 3.1 s | over 240 s | 6.7 s |

On 10 seeds each, step 7 stalled on none at 24 wide (all about 2 s) and on 2 of 10 at 36 wide.
Each stall was one slot from done: the open-face rule refused a sealed component the size of the
whole map, cut off from a small pocket, and named every slot in it as the cause. The solver could
then backjump only to the latest placement, so it backtracked one slot at a time, flooding the
map-sized component at each node until the iteration cap, and then started the next attempt.

The fix (`open-face.ts`) names a sealed component by its **cut**: the faces between it and the
cells outside it, every path out crossing one, plus the closed slots' links. Changing a slot
inside the component can't reopen the cut, so the cause stays sound, and the solver backjumps to
the slots that closed it. The slow content test (`map/macro/tests/slow/chain-content`) went from
2,299 s to 20 s.

**A tail remains.** The cut makes each refusal's cause small (8–14 slots), but some solves still
churn among the last few slots around one pocket:
- 36 × 18 `size-36-5` (one of `tools/large-briefs.mjs`'s seeds) hadn't placed after 300 s: five
  slots left, the same park pocket refused over and over, each check about 45 ms because the
  map-wide component floods most of the map before it reaches an open face. At the 84,240-node
  cap an attempt would take about an hour.
- A 36 × 18 playground with the 12 × 6 library, `wfc-large-1`: about 115 s (before: 2.1 s). Two
  attempts reach the cap among small sealed pockets, at about 1 ms a node, before a third places.
  `wfc-large-2` and `-3` take 5 s, so the test now uses `wfc-large-2`.
- 24 × 12 seeds `size-24-1` to `-5` and `library-1` place in 2–9 s; 36 × 18 `size-36-1` to `-4`
  in 5–16 s. The 228 sweep maps (12 × 6 and smaller, plus four macro entries at 24 and 36) all place.

**The stall limit (#854, stacked under this batch).** An attempt now gives up after 500 open-face
refusals without reaching a new depth (`PLACEMENT_STALL_LIMIT`, 51.3), and the next of its 50
attempts starts on its own stream. The limit counts refusals, not seconds, so placement stays
deterministic. Validity, the matching rules and the content are unchanged: only how long an attempt
searches. Placement times by limit on this content:

| Seeds | No limit | 1,000 | 500 (adopted) | 200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 × 6, `sweep-1` to `-120` | 85.7 s total, worst 8.4 s | 79.0 s, worst 2.3 s | 73.3 s, worst 2.2 s | 79.7 s, worst 2.6 s |
| 36 × 18 `size-36-5` | over 300 s | 39.7 s | 22.7 s | — |
| 36 × 18 `seed-1` to `-10` | stalls | worst 17.2 s | worst 14.2 s | — |
| 24 × 12 `size-24-1` to `-5`, `library-1`, `-2` | worst 10.7 s | worst 10.0 s | worst 10.3 s | — |
| Playground `wfc-large-1` to `-3` | 115 s for `-1` | 32.3 s for `-1` | 29.9 s for `-1` | — |

No seed failed outright and none used more than three attempts. Successful attempts also stall
long (up to 5,666 refusals at 12 × 6 here, 4,440 at 24 × 12 on `main`'s content), so some layouts
change: on `main`'s content 2 of the 228 sweep maps move (`macro-24x12/size-24-1`,
`playground-6x3/sweep-13`), which is why #854 lands with this batch. None of this report's nine
maps changed (checked with `library-comparison.mts --macro`), so the tables below stand.

The limit is a backstop, not the fix. A later probe (12 × 6, `sweep-1` to `-30`, cap 3,000) found
the stalls are sealed pockets, 1,050 refusals against 2 short-portal ones. A tile that fits any
seam (`field`) is already in the fill, so these are areas walled in by neighbours' closed sides,
caught only when their last face closes. Refusing them as soon as they become certain is #857;
#855 is a Lab view of the solve to watch them form. The same probe found attempts failing before
their first choice because two set pieces were placed side by side with seams that don't fit (#856).

| Size | Library | All boundary (contestant / hunter) | Unguaranteed boundary | Unguaranteed share of boundary | Guaranteed boundary |

| 12 × 6 | before | 17.0% / 18.0% | 20.8% / 23.3% | 37.2% | 1.1% |

This is the largest change. Before, an unguaranteed boundary was usually open in practice; now

park now wall their hinted edges (#838). Open regions build nothing, but 74% of theirs is
closed by what stands beside them (before: 8%). Guaranteed boundary stays clear.

## Generation cost

Whole-map `generate`, three sequential runs each under the `bench` machine lock
(`tools/generation-time.mjs`, `--library` for before), on a quiet machine:

| Size | Library | Runs | Mean | Macro (placement) | Briefs |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 36 × 18 `size-36-1` | before | 51.1, 50.0, 49.9 s | 50.4 s | 3.3 s | 1.3 s |
| 36 × 18 `size-36-1` | after | 112.5, 111.8, 111.7 s | 112.0 s | 5.2 s | 1.3 s |

Each library's three runs produced identical maps. Building takes nearly all of the increase,
from about 46 s to 106 s: 2,597 regions instead of 2,094, and cover and park walling their hinted
edges through the live cord (20.10). It wasn't attributed per builder. Placement takes 5.2 s
here; 42.36 measured 5.6 s at step 7.

**Workflow cost.** A full `sweep --check` takes 540–595 s on this content, against 199 s for
`main`'s content on the same machine. Agents and the post-merge CI tier run it.

A 12 × 6 game map takes about 10 s to generate, against about 3.7 s before. With the content
alone, `npm test` took 275 s of wall time and seventeen files ran over the fast tier's 15 s, every
one building game maps. Corey's direction on review 672 (31): a test that builds maps is slow-tier
work, and a test that needs a map only to play on uses an arena sized to the task. So:
- room and networking tests play the pregenerated room arena, seed 9's live map stored by
  `tools/room-arena.mjs` (`room-directory` 17.2 → 1.5 s, `frame-layers` 19.6 → 2.6 s, `server`
  13.6 → 5.5 s, `room-dev-tools` 11.8 → 3.5 s alone);
- the map-chain tests that build move to `tests/slow/`, whole (`map-container`, `map-sweep-replay`)
  or in part (`view`, `live-map`, `live-map-adapter`, `portal-graph`, `map-sweep`'s game case,
  `map-lab-routes`, `map-generator`, `map-library`, `map-tools`), each slow file under 60 s
  (8.9 to 43.1 s alone, in 31's per-set tables);
- `npm test` then took 146 s (machine 39% busy), and every former offender is under 15 s alone,
  the largest `appearance-descriptor` at 12.9 s.

The slow tier gains about 300 s of serial work (12 files), run after merge. `micro-large-briefs`
went from 26 s to 64 s with the recaptured briefs, as park's 87-portal brief alone takes about 40 s
(33 s without its edge walls), so park's brief has its own file (39.2 s; the rest 24.2 s).

## For Corey

- **Adopt?** The bundled libraries are step 7 plus the court fix. The largest effects: three
  quarters of unguaranteed boundary physically closed (from a fifth), one-portal regions up
  from 6–14 to 51–446 a map, long routes a little longer (no route above 1.5), dead-end area up
  by a third to a half, and 36-wide generation 2.2 times as long.
- **Placement's tail** is bounded by the stall limit (#854, a stacked review PR that lands with
  this batch): `size-36-5` places in 22.7 s and the playground seed in 29.9 s. Its cause, sealed
  pockets refused only once they close, is #857, with #855 to watch it in the Lab.
- **Hut quarters** nearly vanish from the fill. Keeping them is a content decision (a weight, or
  hut tiles with stated sides).
- **Workflow cost.** The sweep takes about nine to ten minutes, against 199 s on `main`'s content.
  The fast tier is back under budget by your tier rule (146 s, every file under 15 s alone); the
  slow tier carries about 300 s more. Both come from building cost: twice the park regions, each
  costlier, and cover and park edge walls. Cutting park's cost is the lever if either matters.
