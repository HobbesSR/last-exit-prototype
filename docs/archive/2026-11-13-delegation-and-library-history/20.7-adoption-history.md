## Crossing offset experiment (#631)

The follow-up `tools/compound-crossing-offset-experiment.mjs` (#631) isolates
one witness seam in a temporary copy of preparation, leaving production
selection unchanged. It checks alternate offsets through complete preparation
and the exact cover receiver; geometric promise checks remain separate from
partition admission. This is experimental evidence, not an execution policy.
Any future crossing-policy adoption must explicitly preserve historical
interface reconstruction: even fully saved snapshots re-prepare accepted
offers in `recover`. Every snapshot is checked against the current algorithm
version, and recovery checks each node's version; omitted-result recovery also
requires matching consulted builder implementations. Bumping the algorithm
version would reject old snapshots, rather than preserve their compatibility.
Preserving them requires reconstructing their old crossing choices. Recovery
prepares accepted offers alone, so a future search must not depend on rejected
offers' footprints unless those dependencies can also be reconstructed.
A content version bump alone cannot make changed crossing reconstruction
compatible. No compatibility or version change is introduced by this experiment.

### Adoption and verification

Mechanics are covered by synthetic recursive builders and snapshot recovery tests.
The completed #330 production pilot is in `map/micro/strategies/hut.ts`:
`buildHut` remains the flattened-result adapter over the owning
`buildHutGeneration` execution object, with `HUT_EXECUTION_REGISTRY` recording
the hut and cover builder versions. The adapter preserves the supplied root
brief for chain saving; the owning object uses canonical inputs. House siting
and design remain first and
unchanged. The original #330 policy retained expanded house/perimeter collars
and standing-block routes, then offered raw residual lobes. #647 replaces that
selection under `types-48`: explicit boundary rectangles, a pessimistic house
parcel, fixed actual doorstep approaches and movable continuous yard cords.
The bounded candidate policy is in 54.2 and the transaction is in 20.10. Keys
now encode rectangle origin and dimensions; snapshots keep the whole footprint.
Within one `buildHutGeneration` invocation, the receiving check and child build
reuse a cover preview cached by the exact serialized child brief (see below). An offer is
rejected if that preview has no usable placement. A hut with no usable fill or
no house with a reachable doorstep takes the terminal empty path. Core quotas
and reachability obligations remain with the original hut; no core exclusion
or new loot system was added. Retained hut loot is clipped to retained
ownership and rolls in canonical cell order; cover uses its existing minimal
loot behavior. These are intentional content changes under `types-24`.
In particular, a 2 by 3 hut region that cannot fit a house now produces terminal
empty output; its former open-zone loot is lost. This is a consequence of the
current full-region fallback, not a new loot policy. Cover owns its
receiver in `map/micro/strategies/cover.ts`: `COVER_EXECUTOR_VERSION`
(`cover-receiver-infill-1`) names the cover receiver implementation and must advance when
that implementation changes; the aggregate `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` content version
has a different scope and does not move it (#384). Both adopters' registries use
the same `COVER_EXECUTION` entry, and each generation invocation takes a fresh
`previewedCoverExecution()`, whose receiving check and child build share previews
keyed by `JSON.stringify` of the exact child brief. Adopters keep their own
registries and orchestration.

#531's first installment changes only this shared receiver: a successful
cluster result is kept unchanged; an empty result can try individual placeables
in the inherited fill with its hunter routes protected (20.3.3). This is
intentional content under `types-36`, including changed retained ownership and
yard loot rolls when formerly rejected patches are accepted. The hut and
compound primary structures, offer selection, court/approach protections and
crossing preparation are unchanged. Full historical snapshots still load their
stored results; omitted-result recovery requires the matching receiver version,
including when that receiver was consulted and rejected. Before/after evidence
is in [42.3](../../42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md). Other builders have not
adopted residual infill in this installment; they remain work under #531.

Nested `block` lots use the same hut builder, but small lots may have no
suitable cover lobe; no nested hut lots occurred in the three audited maps, so
the measured uptake does not establish nested adoption. A mechanical
preparation optimization reuses ownership sets and already-normalized private
inputs, avoiding repeated unchanged-owner partitions while retaining geometry
checks.
The authored behavior uses `types-24`. The measured adoption, sample limits,
fixture context and pilot-yard content balance are in [42.3](../../42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md#hut-pilot-yard-sample).

loot uses canonical/clipped ownership and existing rolls; cover keeps its
