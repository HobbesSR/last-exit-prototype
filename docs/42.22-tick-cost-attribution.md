# 42.22. Server tick cost by stage and map size (#637)

Up: [42](42-performance-history.md). Parent checkpoint: [42.20](42.20-physics-p9.md#default-activation-checkpoint-523) (#635).
Harness: [31](31-verification.md) (`npm run bench:stages`). Siblings that act on this: #638 to #641.

Measurement only: no gameplay, wire or ordering change. Timers were added and nothing else.

## Method

**Timers, not a profile.** The physics coordinator had no timers, so about 60% of a physics tick was unattributed.
`shared/simulation/phased.ts` now times each phase (`sim.bots`, `sim.actions`, `sim.solve`, `sim.poses`,
`sim.projectiles`, `sim.pickups`, `sim.environment`, `sim.sync` with `sim.itemSync` inside it, `sim.discovery`,
`sim.rewind`) inside an inclusive `sim.step`, like the legacy step. The legacy step gained `sim.environment` and
`sim.rewind`. `snapshot` is split into `sim.snapshot.world` (the physics world copy), `.presentation` (body capture
and item poses) and `.clone` (the `structuredClone` of the whole state). Broadcast is split into `loop.encode`
(layer and discovery encoders) and `loop.serialize`, and every keyframe cut counts as `work.rekey.<layer>`. The
profiler is off by default and every hook returns before reading the clock. `profiler.frame()` now returns the
frame's totals, so the harness keeps every tick instead of the 600-sample window. One CPU profile (`--cpu-prof`,
800 ticks at 36x18) was used only to name the functions inside a stage.

**Harness.** `tests/bench-stages.mjs` runs the body of the server loop (`server/room-service.js`) for each room in
turn: `match.advance()` (step and snapshot), the real replay writer with its gzip output discarded, `remember` (the
spectator history) and `broadcast` with fake sessions. Between ticks, outside the measured interval, it waits for
the writer to drain, as the 50 ms gap between live ticks lets it. A run whose writer drops a frame fails, so every
recording-on number here records every frame. The wait averaged 4 ms per tick at 12x6 and 11 ms at 36x18. Sessions
are attached to bot seats, so a viewed seat still runs its bot. Read every multi-viewer row as a full bot room plus
the views; it is an upper bound for that pairing. The harness also records GC pauses (`PerformanceObserver`),
which tick each overlaps, and v8-serialized sizes of the state and the history. The untimed remainder is
0.01 to 0.02 ms per tick at every size, so the stages cover the whole tick.

**Runs.** Seed 4217, Node 24.15.0, this machine, sequential under the benchmark lock. The runs were driven by
`tools/perf/stage-runs.sh`, which starts from an empty directory and stops at the first failed run, and
`tools/perf/stage-report.mjs` produced [the tables](reports/issue-637/stage-tables.txt); it refuses a run that
dropped a frame. Per-run summaries, including the first 80 over-budget ticks of each run, are in
[stage-attribution.json.gz](reports/issue-637/stage-attribution.json.gz). Item counts below are the generated map's
items (893, 3,633 and 7,713). 42.20's 974, 3,853 and 8,608 count the same maps from the started game. These are not
capacity promises.

The lock serializes benchmarks only, and other agents' processes shared the machine. Runs that slowed in every stage
at once were treated as contended and rerun: the 36x18 legacy run, the ladder (both measured while the machine ran
out of memory) and one 36x18 physics repeat whose first 3,000 ticks ran 25% slow. Three physics repeats per size
then read 7.34 to 7.54 ms at 12x6, 17.18 to 19.44 at 24x12 and 33.37 to 34.41 at 36x18.

An earlier pass (PR #653's first head) did not drain the writer, and it silently dropped about a third of recorded
frames. That pass understated recording by about 1 ms and the 36x18 tick by 1.5 ms, and its GC and tail figures
were lower too; nothing from it is used here. The harness mean is about 5 ms above `tests/bench.mjs` at 36x18 (34.4
against 29.4) because it adds recording, encoding and the GC they cause.

### Baseline and physics increment (deliverable 4)

At 36x18 physics costs 14.5 ms more than legacy (34.4 against 20.0). The two parts:

- **Shared baseline, about 20 ms, present on both engines:** the state clone (7.6 to 8.0), recording (3.8 to 4.2),
  the item scans in interact and pickups (3.5 to 3.7), bots (3.0 legacy), discovery (0.9), projection and encoding
  (0.8 to 1.2). Legacy alone is over the 25 ms P9 p99 target at 36x18 (32.8 in this harness).
- **Physics increment, about 14.5 ms:** `sim.solve` 7.21, `sim.sync`/`itemSync` 1.68, `sim.poses` 0.91, the world
  copy 1.01 and the presentation capture 0.87, which sum to 11.7. On top of that, bots cost 1.55 more (dynamic
  navigation observations, `dynamic-navigation.ts`), projection 0.34 more (bodies) and recording and clone 0.7 more

The first 1,200 ticks are heavier than the whole match, which raises the ladder exponents a little.

**O(items) per tick today (exponent about 0.8 or more):** the state clone, recording, the interact and pickup scans,
item sync, item pose write-back, presentation capture, the physics solve, per-view projection and layer encoding. The
CPU profile names the scans. `automaticPickups` (`loot.ts:115`) tests every ground item for every contestant
each tick. `interact` (`interactions.ts:46`) filters every item for each interacting player.
`writePhysicalItemPoses` (`world-physics.ts:115`) and `synchronizePhysicalItems` walk every item. The solve
holds at most 128 dynamic bodies, so its growth goes with the static colliders (7,768 at 36x18); that link is
inferred, not shown. **Sub-linear:** repath (sliced, bounded), discovery, the physics world copy (dynamic bodies
only, `includeStatic: false`) and serialization (the payload is already a delta). Bots grow at 0.4 to 0.7 on real
maps; the profile places that growth in `decideBot`, which copies and filters every remembered item per bot each
tick and checks gates per item (`bots.ts:489`–`494`).

**Calibration of the ladder.** At the same item count the tiled 36x18 matches the real map's first 1,200 ticks
within about 20% for the clone, recording, solve, item sync, poses, presentation, projection and encoding. Pickups and
discovery read 5 to 50% high, repath reads low, and bots read 1.6 times (legacy) to 3.3 times (physics) high. Tiles
are joined only where their edges allow, so routes are not real topology; the bot rows on the ladder, and the
over-budget runs of bot ticks it produces from about tick 550, are not evidence.

**Beyond 36x18.** Physics cannot be built past 36x18: `buildPhysicsGeometry` refuses extents above 51,840 by 25,920
world units (`MAX_WIDTH`/`MAX_HEIGHT`, `shared/simulation/physics-geometry.ts`), exactly 36x18. A larger physics
map needs that limit reviewed first. The ladder measured legacy at a synthetic 48x24 (4x4 tiles), first 1,200
ticks, with bots inflated as above: tick mean 49.6 ms, `sim.snapshot.clone` 15.6, `loop.record` 7.2, 297 of 1,200
ticks over 50 ms. 72x36 was attempted and dropped from the set. There one recorded frame nearly fills the writer's
8 MiB queue, so even a drained writer dropped frames, and the process heap reached about 2.5 GiB, more than the
machine could hold beside other work.

Physics beyond 36x18 is extrapolated, not measured. The stages with an exponent near 1 sum to about 32 ms at 36x18,
which gives about 60 ms at 48x24 and about 130 ms at 72x36. That agrees with #637's straight-line estimate (52 and
118) after adding recording.

## Ticks over 50 ms

Each over-budget tick is classified by its largest stages, whether a keyframe was cut on it and how much GC pause
overlapped it. "GC" below means a pause of more than 5 ms (kinds 1, scavenge, and 4, mark-compact).

**The 12x6 71 to 80 ms tick is tick 1, explained.** It is the only tick over budget at 12x6, and it is tick 1 in all
three runs (77.3, 68.5 and 77.1 ms): the first world step (`sim.solve` 43 to 46 ms), first pickups (12 to 17), item
sync (6 to 10) and bots (4). It is also tick 1 at 24x12 (108 to 138 ms) and 36x18 (141 to 148 ms). Encoding plays no
part: the rekeys counted on tick 1 are each view's first keyframe. Most of the first solve is paid once per process,
not once per room. With four rooms starting together in one process, tick 1's four first solves took 63 ms in total,
18 ms more than one room alone. A live server would pay it on its first room after start, and `tests/bench.mjs` pays
it in every run because each run is a new process. Whether it is engine warm-up or the first broadphase build was
not separated.

**36x18 physics: 59, 45 and 62 ticks over budget after tick 1 (0.46 to 0.64%).** Not periodic at a fixed period.
Ticks 2, 54, 247, 634, 658, 710, 739 and 752 are over in all three runs, so deterministic game events and allocation
drive them.

- **Bot decision bursts are the largest single cause:** `sim.bots` at 15 to 28 ms, often with `sim.repath` at 7 to
  13, is the largest stage on 19 to 30 of each run's over-budget ticks.
- **GC:** 15 to 25 of them overlap a GC pause, of up to 125 ms. The pause lands in whichever stage was allocating:
  the clone, pickups, recording, presentation or projection. About 60% of the over-budget ticks are still over without
  the GC time. GC averages 1.9 ms per tick (about 7,700 pauses per match); legacy averages 1.2.
- **Solve spikes:** `sim.solve` at 15 to 30 ms (typical 7), the largest stage on 9 to 15 ticks per run, often
  with little GC. Cause not found.
- **Clone at two to three times its typical cost:** the largest stage on 10 to 12 ticks per run, some with no pause
  recorded. V8's incremental marking charged to the allocating stage would fit, but that is an inference.
- **Keyframe cuts: none.** No over-budget tick after tick 1 had a cut. Cuts happen on 0.2% of ticks with one
  viewer. With eight player views they happen on 20.8% of ticks and on 20.0% of the over-budget ones, no more than
  chance. #640's keyframe hypothesis does not hold at these sizes.

**36x18 legacy:** only tick 1 (55 ms, the first interact scan). **24x12 physics:** tick 1 and 0 to 3 others (a bot
burst, a solve spike or the clone).

## Memory

v8-serialized sizes, a proxy for retained heap. The spectator history keeps 62 frames per room (`remember`,
`SPECTATOR_DELAY_TICKS` + 2), so its memory is state size times 62:

| Size | State (legacy / physics) | History per room (legacy / physics) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 12x6 | 95 / 138 KiB | 5.8 / 8.4 MiB |
| 24x12 | 376 / 423 KiB | 22.7 / 25.6 MiB |
| 36x18 | 824 / 871 KiB | 49.9 / 52.8 MiB |
| 48x24 (tiled, legacy) | 1,679 KiB | 101.7 MiB |

Linear in items; 72x36 would hold about 230 MiB of history per room. Sampled heap peaked at about 1.9 GiB in a 36x18
physics match. That includes uncollected garbage from a high allocation rate (a full clone and a full JSON frame of
the state per tick), so it is not a retained-size figure. Memory over longer sessions or across matches was not
measured.

Memory, like the per-tick cost, scales with the map because the server copies the whole state each tick, keeps 62
of those copies, and writes each one whole to the recording. It is not set by the number of actors, awake bodies or
what viewers see. Copying only what changed (#638, #639) addresses both before any thought of splitting a match's
world across processes.

## Change fraction per tick (#655)

Stage 0 of #655 asks whether a structurally shared frame would pay: its copy cost follows the entities that change,
not those that exist. `tests/measure-changes.mjs` runs one bot room to the end of the match and compares each
published frame with the previous one, entity by entity, keyed by id. Equal values count as unchanged, so this is a
lower bound on what copy-on-write would copy: a step that writes an equal value still touches its entity unless the
write is skipped. Seed 4217, whole matches (24x12 physics ran to the 12,000-tick limit). Raw output is under
`test-results/655/`, not checked in.

Items are removed (picked up) about 0.02 times per tick. Over a whole match 0.0 to 3.5% of items ever change, and
then only `x`, `y` (physics) and `ammo`. Gates change only `open`; 12 to 26% of them open at some point. Traps and
tethers were empty throughout. Players change `x`, `y`, `heading`, `movedThisTick` and `route` on most ticks,
and bodies their pose, velocity and sleep state.

**By size.** At tick 600 of the 36x18 physics match the v8-serialized frame is 900 KiB: items 766 (85%), gates 77
(9%), the physics world 30, bodies 15, players 11, everything else under 1. Weighting each collection by its
changed fraction, a shared frame would replace about 12 KiB a tick, roughly 1.3% of the frame. That is an
estimate from one tick's sizes, not a measurement of a shared frame.

**Decision: stage 1 (items) goes ahead.** The clone (8.0 ms), recording (4.2 ms) and the 62-frame history copy
about 94% of the frame that never changes between ticks: items and gates. Players change often but there are 11 of
them; the physics body sets change at about 9% of 80. The scans over every item (pickups, interact, item sync,
pose write-back) are a separate cost: they read every item whether or not it changes, so sharing frames alone does
not remove them (#639, #641).

### Encoding by layer and the shared item projection

With temporary per-layer timers inside `loop.encode` (not kept), first 600 ticks at 36x18 physics, seed 4217,
spectator view included, under the lock: items are 2.2 of 2.8 ms with one player view and 2.3 of 4.6 ms with
eight, where gates are another 2.3 ms. Discovery, bodies and tethers are under 0.15 ms together. Each tick's
projection built new item objects, so the encoder compared every item field by field. Gates are cloned with

## Which sibling acts on which rows

- **#638 (snapshot copy):** `sim.snapshot.clone`, the largest stage, 8.0 ms at 36x18 (23% physics, 38% legacy),
  exponent 0.84 to 0.98, baseline. `loop.record` (4.2 ms, one JSON frame of the whole state per tick) and the
  history's memory are the same per-tick whole-state copy. The clone is also the main allocator behind
  GC-overlapped tail ticks.
- **#639 (in-server deltas):** the stages that walk every item each tick: interact and pickup scans (3.65 ms,
  baseline), item sync (1.49), pose write-back (0.91) and presentation capture (0.87; these three are the physics
  increment), encoding and recording. Together about 7.5 ms at 36x18 besides the clone, all exponent 0.77 to 1.1.
  Bots ("Bots by sub-stage", 8.4 ms at 36x18 physics early on, the largest stage after #655) are tracked here too.
  The per-call sight-edge scan in `lineClear` is a spatial lookup in kind (#641's), and the remembered-item and
  frontier scans are this issue's.
- **#640 (tails):** keyframe cuts ruled out as a cause; the 12x6 spike is tick 1's first solve (warm-up, mostly once
  per process). What remains is bot decision bursts, GC overlap (about a third of over-budget ticks), solve spikes,
  the clone at two to three times its typical cost, and rooms lining up when several run at once.
- **#641 (spatial partition):** per-view projection (0.83 ms per player view, 2.4 ms for the spectator view at 36x18,
  exponent 0.80 to 0.90), the per-contestant pickup scan and the interact filter (distance tests over every item),
  and bots' remembered-item filter. Admitting item bodies by distance would bear on `sim.solve` (7.2 ms, 21%) only
  if the growth there is the static colliders' and not the dynamic bodies', which is not established.
