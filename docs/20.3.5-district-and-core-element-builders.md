# 20.3.5. District and core element builders

Part of [20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md), which holds the shared builder layer, the design guidelines and what
every catalogue builder shares. Moved from 20.3 without changing its content (#510).

## `block` builder

- **SDK pilot (#580, #605):** if the existing split selects no lots, and the
  cell scale is positive and at most 200,
  offer square lots of side 12, 9 and 6 at owned cells in row order. Admit at
  most 64 candidates. Each must avoid parent portal approaches, have the same
  safe boundary attachment and two adjacent full-side frontages as ordinary
  lots, and leave exactly one connected circulation remainder. The allocator
  selects at most one rectangle, maximizing lot area; ties follow stable
  candidate IDs. Beam width is one, with at most 64 expansions. No residual,
  seam or piece penalties are used: circulation feasibility is a hard filter,
  not a score tradeoff. The selected piece uses the existing lot type/seed,
  child-brief construction and physical builders below. All remaining cells
  become the open alley child. An empty allocation remains open whole.
  Successful existing plans retain the original path. The fallback's attempted
  boxes, connectivity checks and SDK work share a deterministic work budget;
  `planBlock().fallbackWork` reports its usage and exhaustion. The physical
  builder raises an exhaustion error because its result has no diagnostics
  channel. Removing the old size guard can change custom block content, so
  this behavior is identified by `types-43`. This deliberately bounded pilot does not fill existing alleys,
  search multiple lots, or introduce a universal district policy.
  `planBlock().allocation` exposes the attempted SDK plan, diagnostics and
  residual ownership for tools/tests; it is absent when that path is not used
  and is not stored in region results. SDK interfaces remain opportunities;
  physical obligations use the existing paired full-side child portals.
- **The split:** the region's bounding box is split, guillotine fashion,
  across its longer side at a seeded place. Each split leaves an alley 3
  cells wide between the parts, and splitting stops once no part is longer
  than 24 cells.
- **Lots:** each part tries rectangles at least 6 cells a side that may
  attach to one or two adjacent boundary sides. Hinted attachments take
  preference, then larger area. Other sides keep two owned cells for
  circulation; opposite-side attachments are refused. The three-cell
  splits between lots remain unchanged. Parts choose in row order, and a
  candidate that would leave the alleys in more than one piece is refused
  for the part's next best (#508). A part with no viable rectangle stays
  alley.
- **Children:** each lot is a `hut` (no side over 12), `depot` (sides at
  least 10), `compound` (at least 16), `ruins`, or `cover` (at least 9)
  region. Its type is drawn from those its size suits, and it gets its own
  seed. The alleys, everything left, are one `open` region. Each child's
  zones are the block's, cut to its cells. Parent hints are clipped to the
  child owning each hinted boundary segment. `planBlock` returns the child
  briefs, and the block's result gathers the children's geometry, loot and
  manifest through the delegation executor's initial-partition path. Element
  labels use the full child brief ID as their prefix, and the manifest uses the
  executor's common additive counters rather than a block-specific `lots` count.
- **Frontages:** each lot has two portals onto the alleys, the two whole
  sides at a seeded corner with two owned cells beyond both sides. Two,
  not one, so the lot is a passage its
  strategy must keep joined, rather than a pocket that owes nothing. A `hut`
  still fits in the far corner of the smallest lot. The alleys hold every
  frontage and every one of the block's own portals.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** a convex lot can make a notch at an edge
  or corner, but cannot bridge opposite boundary sides. Its other sides
  keep a hunter-wide path around it. Lots stay separated by three-cell
  splits; parent portal approaches stay in the alleys. Each lot keeps its
  own promise between frontages that face owned alley cells.
- **The alleys stay one area:** on a concave mask, a side a lot attaches to
  can be boundary for only part of its length, and the lot would wall off
  the alleys beyond it (#508). So the alleys are checked connected as each
  lot is chosen. Every child is then one area keeping its own promise, and
  `validateBriefPartition` passes over the lots and alleys (51.8), which
  `tests/micro-block.test.js` and `tests/micro-block-partition.test.js`
  check.

## `departure` builder

Exits are spread farthest-first, so they sit as
far apart as the region allows, and may share one corridor (17 M13's
assumption, keep the tip). Hunters' spawns are spread over the ground at
least the set-back from every exit. Cover and loot fill around both, as in
`arrival`. What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

## `charging` builder

The charger stands as near the region's middle as a
contestant fits and can walk to from the first portal. Cover and loot keep
off the standing room around it. Then, if no cover already stands within 2
cells of that room (*proposed*, a doorway's width), one container is added
there, keeping cover's aisles, where the region has room. A pad too small
for that, like the fixture's 2 × 2, takes its cover from its neighbours.
What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

## `transit` builder

`map/micro/strategies/transit.ts` uses hunter clearance and first-portal access,
then keeps two cells of standing space free of cover and loot. Nearby cover
uses the existing cover strategy. Insufficient space leaves a report shortfall.
