# 20.3.5. District and core element builders

Part of [20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md), which holds the shared builder layer, the design guidelines and what
every catalogue builder shares. Moved from 20.3 without changing its content (#510).

## `block` builder

- **Alongside comparison (#610):** `planBlock(brief, { planner:
  'sdk-experimental' })` explicitly selects an experimental multi-lot policy.
  Production registry calls retain the ordinary planner and its fallback below.
  The comparison offers square sides 24, 18, 12, 9 and 6 at the upper-left
  corner of 27-cell bays, with origins inset two cells from the region bounds
  to avoid boundary portal approaches. Different bays leave
  at least three cells between lots; alternatives in one bay overlap and compete.
  Candidates avoid original portal approaches and reuse the ordinary boundary
  attachment, full-side frontage and connected-remainder checks. Admission stops
  at 32 candidates in bay row order, so later bays can be omitted. The SDK
  maximizes selected lot area, with beam width one and at most eight pieces;
  other score weights are zero. The selected union must leave one connected
  circulation remainder and every available frontage must remain unclaimed.
  Child types, seeds, hints, inherited margins and dispatch use the existing
  block machinery. This is a deliberately limited candidate policy, not a
  comprehensive search or a default-promotion decision.
  `plan.experimental` reports attempted boxes, connectivity checks, counted
  work, exhaustion and any rejected union. The shared default work allowance
  remains 20 million units. Exhausted or rejected plans are unusable;
  `buildBlock(brief, observer, options)` throws rather than building them.
  `experimentalBudget` supplies a custom meter for this mode; `fallbackBudget`
  remains specific to the ordinary planner's single-lot fallback. The union
  rejection checks are defensive and were not triggered by the retained sample;
  the current three-cell bay gap already protects two-deep frontage strips.
  `node tools/block-planning-compare.mjs --out=PATH` compares current-library
  briefs and shape examples for physical density, final parent/child access,
  fill constraints, determinism and sequential planning cost. `--quick`
  reduces the sample; `--no-timing` skips timing. Evidence and limits belong
  in [42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md).
  Version-2 comparison captures pin committed Git blob IDs and SHA-256 digests
  before generation, retain separate checkout-byte digests, and refuse edits
  to the recorded source set other than line-ending conversion. Commit source
  changes before capture.
- **SDK pilot (#580, #605):** if the existing split selects no lots, and the
  cell scale is positive and at most 200,
  offer square lots of side 12, 9 and 6 at owned cells in row order. Admit at
  most 64 candidates. Each must avoid parent portal approaches, have the same
  safe boundary attachment and two adjacent full-side frontages as ordinary
  lots, and leave exactly one connected circulation remainder. The allocator
  selects at most one rectangle, maximizing lot area; ties follow stable
  candidate IDs. Beam width is one, with at most 64 expansions. No residual,
  seam or piece penalties are used: circulation feasibility is a hard filter,
  not a score tradeoff. The selected piece uses the existing lot type/seed,
  child-brief construction and physical builders below. All remaining cells
  become the open alley child. An empty allocation remains open whole.
  Successful ordinary plans retain the original path, subject to the hunter-width
  admission below. The fallback's attempted
  boxes, connectivity checks and SDK work share a deterministic work budget;
  `planBlock().fallbackWork` reports its usage and exhaustion. The physical
  builder raises an exhaustion error because its result has no diagnostics
  channel. Removing the old size guard can change custom block content, so
  this behavior is identified by `types-43`. This deliberately bounded pilot does not fill existing alleys,
  search multiple lots, or introduce a universal district policy.
  `planBlock().allocation` exposes the attempted SDK plan, diagnostics and
  residual ownership for tools/tests; it is absent when that path is not used
  and is not stored in region results. SDK interfaces remain opportunities;
  physical obligations use the existing paired full-side child portals.
- **The split:** the region's bounding box is split, guillotine fashion,
  across its longer side at a seeded place. Each split leaves an alley 3
  cells wide between the parts, and splitting stops once no part is longer
  than 24 cells.
- **Lots:** each part tries rectangles at least 6 cells a side that may
  attach to one or two adjacent boundary sides. Hinted attachments take
  preference, then larger area. Other sides keep two owned cells for
  circulation; opposite-side attachments are refused. The three-cell
  splits between lots remain unchanged. Parts choose in row order, and a
  candidate that would leave the alleys in more than one piece is refused
  for the part's next best (#508). A part with no viable rectangle stays
  alley.
- **Children:** each lot is a `hut` (no side over 12), `depot` (sides at
  least 10), `compound` (at least 16), `ruins`, or `cover` (at least 9)
  region. Its type is drawn from those its size suits, and it gets its own
  seed. The alleys, everything left, are one `open` region. Each child's
  zones are the block's, cut to its cells. Parent hints are clipped to the
  child owning each hinted boundary segment. `planBlock` returns the child
  briefs, and the block's result gathers the children's geometry, loot and
  manifest through the delegation executor's initial-partition path. Element
  labels use the full child brief ID as their prefix, and the manifest uses the
  executor's common additive counters rather than a block-specific `lots` count.
- **Frontages:** each lot has two portals onto the alleys, the two whole
  sides at a seeded corner with two owned cells beyond both sides. Two,
  not one, so the lot is a passage its
  strategy must keep joined, rather than a pocket that owes nothing. A `hut`
  still fits in the far corner of the smallest lot. The alleys hold every
  frontage and every one of the block's own portals.
- **Frontage geometry:** a convex lot can make a notch at an edge
  or corner, but cannot bridge opposite boundary sides. Its other sides
  reserve two cells around it. Lots stay separated by three-cell
  splits; parent portal approaches stay in the alleys. Each lot keeps its
  own promise between frontages that face owned alley cells. Concave masks
  additionally need the physical circulation admission below.
- **The alleys stay one area:** on a concave mask, a side a lot attaches to
  can be boundary for only part of its length, and the lot would wall off
  the alleys beyond it (#508). So the alleys are checked connected as each
  lot is chosen. Every child is then one area keeping its own promise, and
  `validateBriefPartition` passes over the lots and alleys (51.8), which
  `tests/micro-block.test.js` and `tests/micro-block-partition.test.js`
  check.

**Hunter-width admission (#614, `types-44`):** ordinary candidates, SDK fallback
candidates and the experimental selected union must also retain one connected
graph of owned 2 x 2 cell squares, joined at cardinally adjacent centers.
Each edge supports a swept disc of radius one cell, exceeding the hunter's
0.925-cell clearance. Every original portal and selected frontage must have
its complete two-deep inward strip attached to this graph. New frontages use
the same seeded corner as final child construction. Existing cell-connectivity
and experimental frontage checks remain in force.

This is a conservative sufficient witness, not an exact free-space solver:
disconnected square pockets can reject a physically feasible plan, and clear
context outside ownership is deliberately not used to rescue admission.
An intrinsic one-cell neck can therefore cause every lot to be refused, even
when no lot created the pinch; baseline-relative admission is not implemented.
Intrinsically inaccessible input portals cannot be repaired by leaving a block
open; a no-lot result retains that existing input limitation. Margins and portal
promises are unchanged. Each check uses O(N + total portal length) work and
O(N) storage, with relative numeric keys independent of absolute coordinates.
SDK candidate and union checks charge 64N plus claimed area and the summed
original portal lengths (including overlapping runs) before checking,
covering cell connectivity, graph construction/traversal and portal strips;
ordinary search remains bounded by four candidates per guillotine part.
The 36 x 30 seed-1 neck keeps five lots with 488 rather than 508 lot cells and
now passes its final alley-child reach check independently of lot interiors.
The earlier exact barrier diagnosis is retained in [42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md).

## `market` builder

The dedicated strategy (#534, `types-47`) replaces the hall stand-in with
`market-stall` placeables (20.8). Rows follow the original region bounding box's
longer axis (x on a tie). Each stall is two cells deep with a low crate-kind
counter along one long edge; alternating rows reverse that edge. A declared
awning covers each stall by default, without enclosing a building or adding
collision. `roofed: false` removes the awnings.

The longitudinal pitch is `spacing` (integer at least 4, default 6). Stall
length is the smaller of four cells and `spacing - 2`; transverse pitch is
four cells. Complete slots are centered inside the bounding box's two-cell
bypass band. When
there are at least four columns, one seeded column in every four stays empty
across every row, making wider cross-lanes. Two-cell gaps remain between all
other stalls. Candidates cover the whole mask, including wings, rather than
only its largest rectangle.

Every footprint must fit the reduced fill, avoid the nearby-portal protected
route tree and keep two cells from committed footprints. A complete two-cell
bypass ring must fit in the access mask, so a stall cannot close a local neck
or side pocket even with only one portal. The canvas commits each assembly;
refusal after siting is an invariant failure. No fitting stall takes the
terminal `open` path with zone loot. This deliberately conservative siting can
leave thin or highly fragmented wings empty. Perimeter hint walls, mixed hall
sections and elaborate canopy grammar are outside this first capability.

Loot uses the zone chances and final obstacle clearance; canopies do not
exclude loot. The manifest adds `stalls` and `awnings`. Core-element requests
remain unsited for the report. Paired authored-map evidence belongs in 42.3.

## `departure` builder

Exits are spread farthest-first, so they sit as
far apart as the region allows, and may share one corridor (17 M13's
assumption, keep the tip). Hunters' spawns are spread over the ground at
least the set-back from every exit. Cover and loot fill around both, as in
`arrival`. What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

## `charging` builder

The charger stands as near the region's middle as a
contestant fits and can walk to from the first portal. Cover and loot keep
off the standing room around it. Then, if no cover already stands within 2
cells of that room (*proposed*, a doorway's width), one container is added
there, keeping cover's aisles, where the region has room. A pad too small
for that, like the fixture's 2 × 2, takes its cover from its neighbours.
What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

## `transit` builder

`map/micro/strategies/transit.ts` uses hunter clearance and first-portal access,
then keeps two cells of standing space free of cover and loot. Nearby cover
uses the existing cover strategy. Insufficient space leaves a report shortfall.
