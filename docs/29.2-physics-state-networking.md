## Tick order

Authoritative 20 Hz, one consumed input per actor per tick, with the existing queue, expiry and
acknowledgement semantics. Each tick runs four fixed substeps ([29.1](29.1-physics-design.md#settings-and-witness-gates)).

A tick is one synchronous call (`step`), so nothing that arrives while it runs can affect it. Client
inputs are queued per player as they arrive (`setInput`), and each tick consumes at most one per
actor; the rest wait. Outcomes produced inside a tick are collected as facts and resolved in a fixed
phase or deferred to the next tick, never applied during the solve. A few commands are not inputs and
change state between ticks directly: roster changes (join, resume, choose, leave) and developer
teleport, each followed by physics synchronization and a placement marker (29.1). The step mutates the
game and its world in place rather than returning a new state.

1. Advance tick/timers and pending lifecycle transitions. Apply queued body/gate mutations from the
   previous boundary in stable order. Revalidate pending hook attachments and existing pulls after
   lifecycle/body synchronization. IDs are allocated here or in explicitly ordered gameplay phases,
   never from an engine event callback.
2. Consume each actor's input once in stable roster order. Bots decide against the same pre-movement
   state and collect intents without mutating it.
3. Validate movement and ability intents, resolving inventory selection/rearrangement before choosing
   an attack's equipment. Commit utility use once. Build motors, admit actor projectiles and hook
   launches/cancellations, and enqueue previously pending impulses.
4. Advance all active bodies together through the substeps. Motor force acts each substep; an impulse
   acts once. Sweep supported fast bodies. Canonicalize contacts by substep, time of impact, stable
   IDs and event type; solver callbacks only collect facts. Ground item poses are synchronized from
   solved bodies.
5. Resolve hits, ability transitions, damage and lifecycle consequences. Physical impacts resolve by
   substep/time of impact first, then end-of-tick hitscan/melee by stable caster ID on finalized
   positions with the existing bounded rewind policy. Each hit applies damage and its immediate
   death/inactive transition before a hook attachment is validated, so a dead target never attaches.
   Hook flight resolves after projectile damage. A hook impact creates its pull for the next tick;
   explosion and other post-solve impulses queue for the next tick and are captured in state.
6. At final positions in stable actor ID order: interactions, charging and competing pickups, then
   wall/environment damage, then traps. Dead or extracted actors cannot act or pick up. Turrets firing
   here queue their spawn for the next tick. Movement distance for cancellation and trap rules is the
   actual path, including pushes, not the desired input distance.
7. Commit body/joint additions and removals for the next tick, synchronize lifecycle and clean
   constraints, update discovery, decide completion, record rewind, and capture state and projections.
   Completion is decided only after every phase 5/6 outcome, with one terminal result and one captured
   tick.

Simultaneous end-of-tick attacks use stable ID precedence; there is no trade-kill promise. Already
admitted opposing projectiles may kill both casters. Every projectile advances through exactly one
owner. Movement status for phase 4 is taken at solve start; a stun applied in phase 5 or 6 affects the
next tick's motor. Charging starts or advances once at the final position; blocked input without
displacement does not cancel it, and accumulated path below 0.05 world units (contact recovery
jitter) does not either. Mines keep their 0.5-unit motion threshold. `tests/tick-phases.test.js` and
the [paired trace report](reports/physics-438-phases.json) witness these choices; phase-3 utility
activation and next-tick turret admission were implementation choices reviewed in #472.

Swept radius-2 query projectiles use relative actor motion at each substep and the earliest static
contact, mask holes included; geometry wins an equal time of impact. Pending attachment revalidates
endpoint activity, generation, maximum distance and unobstructed reach. Between-tick synchronization
also invalidates removed or relocated endpoints.

A future ballistic profile resolves within phase 5 by actual event time; at a tie within 1e-9
seconds impact wins, then fuse, then ground, with at most one terminal event per projectile. Mask
impact beats tied obstacle impact; equal obstacles use lexical ID. This is implemented only in the
`ballistic-lab-1` lab ([29.1](29.1-physics-design.md#height-and-ballistics)) and has no live phase
owner.

### Bounds

The world admits at most 27 actors, 128 dynamic bodies, eight constraints, 1,024 queued backend
commands and 8,192 retained contact facts; a step that would retain more throws rather than dropping
facts. Pending impulses and pending projectile spawns are each bounded at 128. These are guards, not
production capacity qualification (#436). Numeric representability guards do not extend supported map
extents. Multiple impulses on one target combine per target and generation and clamp to the pinned
limit before the next solve; body generations reject stale pending impulses, including pickup/drop
reuse of an item ID.

## Four state contracts, not one giant snapshot

| Contract | Contents and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Authoritative gameplay state | Stable entity/body/constraint IDs; transform/velocities, active/sleep state, material/profile, motor/ability state, pending spawns/impulses/attachments/mutations, private hook state (`physics.hooks`) and logical item ownership. Repeated static descriptors are omitted |
| Exact simulation checkpoint | Gameplay state plus the backend state needed to resume (contact/warm-start caches, sleep counters, solver internals, handle mapping, version/settings); private to tests and debug |
| Per-view live projection | Only authorized entities and fields, own correction state and consumed `lastSeq`; explicit spawn/remove/discontinuity plus physics profile/version metadata |
| Recorded playback frame | Complete renderable authoritative state, including physical items, angles, hook flights and visible tether state; viewing it needs no engine |

Transforms and velocities alone do not restore a solver exactly, and neither the gameplay snapshot nor
the adapter's debug snapshot (which may include statics) supports exact continuation in production.
Exact restore is a comparison/debug capability; where unsupported it is labelled so, and prediction
uses fresh projected worlds. Production rollback or crash recovery would need its own verified
contract. Engine blobs are opaque, pinned to an exact engine build, size-bounded, not portable between
versions, and never sent to a player as a correction packet. An API that promises exact continuation
gets a separate explicit checkpoint API rather than being silently weakened.

## Live projection and privacy

`simulation/physics-presentation.ts` publishes closed top-level body and tether records with recorded
frames and authorized live projections, through the existing layer delivery protocol. Body shapes are
embedded display descriptors without materials, motors, pending effects or backend settings. Complete
lists and generations express lifecycle; a placement marker cuts presentation. Visibility
(`simulation/visibility.ts`), frame-layer encoding/decoding and client buffers change together. Moving
items cannot sit in a static layer without invalidation; a keyframe or reconnect recreates the
projected world, and deletion, sleep, wake and revision changes survive layer resets. No backend
handles are sent. Client maps omit private `physicsProps` authoring; clients see crates through
authorized body projections, and item-associated bodies keep item authorization regardless of sleep.

Actors, props and items are projected under the existing potential-visibility and observation policy.
Hidden dynamic state and hidden joint endpoints are not prediction dependencies a client is entitled
to. A tether with an unauthorized endpoint is omitted whole, ID included; endpoint IDs, body lists,
force sources and contact debug data never disclose it. A hook flight record carries only its ID,
caster body ID and tip, and is projected only with an authorized caster body and an in-range tip;
pending target IDs and generations stay private. A pull uses the tether projection, which needs both
endpoints. A viewer receives its own `hookActive` boolean (flight, pending or pull) so the HUD can
offer release while cooldown runs. An unexplained correction or physical reaction may be observed;
no secret identity or coordinates are attached to explain it. Static known geometry, remembered doors
and current authorized dynamics stay distinct, and the server enforces actual doors even when a client
knows an older state.

Because props block bodies but not sight, movable cover would have to extend sight, remembered
transforms, navigation knowledge and minimap together. Spectator delay applies to complete physics
state and effects; there is no separate physics channel around it. Local bot detours
([22.6.4](22.6.4-dynamic-overlay.md)) are bot-private state, absent from captures and projections.

## Prediction and reconciliation

`public/physics-prediction.js` loads lazily for a live player and predicts only the local character
motor against known static geometry and authorized nearby dynamic proxies; the full world is never
predicted on clients. Remote actors and props are server-owned; speculative pushes never award
damage, collect loot or attach a constraint. A correction uses the frame's tick, own consumed
`lastSeq`, movement status/timers, own body pose/velocity, profile, generation and placement marker.
The closed, own-view-only `predictionMotor` field adds pinned acceleration, braking, maximum speed and
`surfaces: true`, and contains no impulses, contacts or hidden dependencies; authored motor surfaces
travel with the map. The shared `characterMotor` calculation preserves server arithmetic. An own-only
`predictionTethered` boolean selects server-driven motion while tethered, even when the other endpoint
is hidden, without disclosing it.

Every correction builds a fresh Rapier island; no solver cache is restored. Nearby authorized actors
and solid props are fixed proxies at the correction tick, held for the replay horizon; sensor loot and
every item-associated body are excluded. Proxy history aligns to the correction tick, not the delayed
render state. The client keeps at most 32 projected frames and 32 inputs, numbering replayed inputs
consecutively above the correction tick, and replays only sequences above the consumed acknowledgement
and any discarded-history floor, never once on arrival and again on acknowledgement. A correction
replays at most eight ticks against at most 512 static footprints and 128 proxies. A send applies one
new motor tick; a correction replaces the world with one bounded replay. Correction error is measured
against the retained predicted pose for that tick. This is approximate prediction: a projected world
cannot reproduce hidden contacts or unknown remote inputs, even with a deterministic backend.

Missing frames or inputs, a changed own profile/tuning/generation, placement, tethered motion and
capacity overflow discard pending prediction and hold authority, as does authority older than
`PREDICTION_LIMITS.staleMs` (500 ms; `public/physics-prediction-limits.js` owns it and the
presentation threshold in [25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md)). Prediction resumes from a later complete
correction; discarded sequences are not revived. Proxy membership changes rebuild the island without
discarding pending inputs. Reconnect, map changes and background visibility changes dispose the island
and input history. WASM initialization failure leaves server-driven motion.

Physical state is corrected immediately; only presentation error is smoothed, with bounded
duration/distance and collision-aware snapping where easing would cross a wall. Camera, sprite, local
fog and cover use the same eased eye. Remote bodies interpolate position and shortest-arc angle
between adjacent ticks for bodies matched by ID under the same profile and unchanged endpoint lists.
A body's placement, generation change, spawn or removal holds that body's earlier state until the
boundary without interrupting unrelated untethered bodies; profile or tether changes and gaps hold the
group. Nothing extrapolates or interpolates through walls. Sleep freezes simulation, not visibility.
Authoritative snapshots alone decide pickup, hit, constraint and inventory effects.

## Replay and compatibility

The axes are [26](26-recording-contract.md)'s: simulation version for rules and order; content ID for
masses, radii, materials, loot and ability tuning; schema and minimum reader for representation. 26
owns the schema numbers for physics records (body/tether records, private discontinuity markers,
collision/damping fields and crate authoring, capped constraints and hook flights). Backend
artifact/settings identity is pinned separately for debug and resimulation.

Recordings contain the transforms, angles, lifecycle, item, hook-flight and tether state needed to
draw every retained tick, through display descriptors that do not depend on today's tuning. The
minimum reader rises when old clients would misrepresent required physics visuals; an optional
diagnostic blob alone does not raise it. Old recordings keep their frames, body appearance and map
semantics, with no engine initialization or content migration. 26's frame digest, bounded writer,
whole-frame omission and sparse playback hold: no extrapolation or reconstructed spring motion across
missing frames, and exact retained states through seek and partial tails. Checkpoint blobs, if
recorded for debug, use a separate bounded cadence and are never required for playback.

## Failure and resource policy

Initialize the backend before admitting a match and report a clear load failure rather than selecting
another solver. Dispose room worlds, temporary prediction worlds and WASM allocations on replacement
or retirement. Validate finite positions, forces and materials and bounded arrays at game/API entry.
Capacity rules reject optional spawns without consuming an item or cooldown; mandatory objective/loot
conservation follows [29.1](29.1-physics-design.md#loot-props-and-physical-items).

Non-finite or escaped body state fails a test and emits a bounded diagnostic in development. At
runtime it is contained at a validated last-safe state, affected constraints are cancelled and a
correction is marked; repeated faults fail the affected match rather than changing solver settings.
This is exceptional recovery, not collision handling or permission to teleport across obstacles.

The developer physics lab ([42.20](42.20-physics-p9.md)) may read the adapter's on-demand
`debugContacts()` (at most 256 final-substep solver contact points, world-unit separations and unit
normals in stable body-pair order) and `debugForces()` (external force accumulators at the final
substep, excluding motor velocity changes and collision impulses). Both are private diagnostics,
absent from captures, recordings and projections. The lab connects to no room, needs an enabled
developer-tools policy, and its exports are not a replay format.
