## Current baseline and intentional change

At design checkpoint `8fa07ff`, `shared/simulation.ts` advances each player through
input, movement, actions and pickups before moving to the next player. Bots may
act inside `botInput` (`shared/simulation/bots.ts`). Traps and projectiles run
after the player loop; `recordRewind` captures final positions. Movement is
position-based in `shared/movement.ts`; `moveBody` rounds to three decimals.
The live client replays pending movement inputs through that same function.

A simultaneous contact world cannot honestly preserve this sequential gameplay
order. Introduce physics under a separately selected simulation rules version,
with its own expected traces. Preserve the frozen `behavior-nav-1.json.gz` and
the legacy path needed by characterization; do not regenerate it or describe new
outcomes as a refactor. Existing recordings play stored frames without invoking
either solver. A mode is pinned at match creation, never switched mid-match.

## Experimental new tick order

Keep authoritative 20 Hz and one consumed input per actor per tick, including
existing queue, expiry and acknowledgement semantics. P1 pins four fixed 12.5 ms
physics substeps (80 Hz internally), 40 units/metre and 24-unit speculative
distance from reviewed #472 evidence. A CPU spike cannot change substep count.

1. Advance tick/timers and pending lifecycle transitions. Apply queued body/gate
   mutations from the previous boundary in stable order. Allocate IDs here or in
   explicitly ordered gameplay phases, never from an engine event callback.
2. Consume each actor's input once in stable roster order. Evaluate bots against
   the same pre-movement state and collect intents; split existing bot action
   side effects out of decision making for this new mode.
3. Validate movement/ability activation intents. Build motors, spawn authorized
   hook/projectile bodies and enqueue previously pending impulses. Inventory
   selection/rearrangement is resolved before deciding the attack's equipment.
4. Advance all active bodies together through fixed substeps. Continuous motor
   force acts each substep; an impulse acts once. Sweep supported fast bodies.
   Read and canonicalize contacts by substep, time of impact, stable IDs and
   event type. Solver callbacks only collect facts.
5. Resolve hits, ability transitions, damage and lifecycle consequences in that
   documented order. Mark targets inactive immediately for later event validation;
   suppress already-consumed projectiles and duplicate damage. Hitscan/melee use
   finalized actor positions plus the existing bounded rewind policy. A physical
   hook impact creates its pulling constraint for the next tick. Explosion and
   other post-solve impulses are queued for the next tick and included in state.
6. Resolve interactions, charging and competing pickups at final positions in
   stable actor order. Dead/extracted actors cannot act or pick up. Apply hazard
   and trap outcomes with an explicit priority before match completion. Record
   movement distance from the actual path (including externally caused movement)
   for cancellation/trap rules, rather than desired input distance.
7. Commit body/joint additions/removals needed for the next tick, update discovery,
   finish match, record rewind, and capture authoritative state/projections.

This is the P1 experimental order, including one-tick delayed post-solve impulses.
Executed old/new observations are below and in [42.10](42.10-physics-p1.md).
Resolve physical impacts by substep/time-of-impact first, then
end-of-tick hitscan/melee by stable caster ID. Each hit applies damage and its
immediate death/inactive transition before validating a hook attachment; a dead
target cannot attach. Pending hooks are validated again before the next solve.
Simultaneous end-of-tick attacks use stable ID precedence (no trade-kill promise);
the trace matrix must expose this choice for review. In phase 6, interactions,
charging and pickups precede wall/environment damage, then traps, matching the
legacy broad priority where possible; completion is decided only afterwards.
Do not accidentally apply both legacy projectile advancement and body stepping.
Ballistic extension must later define impact/fuse/landing ties within phase 5;
the default proposed tie is earliest impact, then fuse expiry, then landing,
with one terminal outcome per projectile.

Utility activation in phase 3 and next-tick turret admission were reviewed
implementation choices in #472, not additional verbatim user answers.
The baseline fixtures must not be regenerated to accept these intentional deltas.
State snapshots include pending spawns/impulses/attachments; transform-only restore
does not establish exact backend continuation.

P1 uses 0.05 world units of accumulated path as charging's numerical rest
tolerance: contact recovery jitter below that does not cancel charging. Mines
retain their existing 0.5-unit motion threshold. Already-admitted opposing
projectiles may kill both casters; the stable caster precedence above concerns
later finalized-pose attacks, which validate activity immediately before use.
Swept radius-2 query projectiles use relative actor motion at each substep and
earliest static geometry contact, including mask holes; geometry wins equal TOI.
Pending attachment admission revalidates endpoint activity, generation, maximum
distance and unobstructed reach. Teleport clears velocity, motor and constraints
and advances generation; stale queued effects cannot reach a reused body.

The world admits at most 27 actors and 128 total dynamic bodies, eight ropes,
1,024 queued backend commands and 8,192 retained contact facts. These are bounded
experimental guards, not production capacity qualification. Fast initial body
motion is refused for swept-query/explicit-teleport handling; motors and external
forces/impulses share bounded translation/angular motion before and after each
substep. Numeric representability guards do not expand supported map extents.
Native center ropes have no finite force cap or break policy; P6 owns those
gameplay contracts. Gameplay snapshots omit repeated static descriptors and
include pending state. The separate adapter debug snapshot can include statics;
neither snapshot supports exact backend continuation in production.

## Four state contracts, not one giant snapshot

| Contract | Contents and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Authoritative gameplay state | Stable entity/body/constraint IDs; transform/velocities, active/sleep state, material/profile, motor/ability state, pending impulses/mutations and logical item ownership |
| Exact simulation checkpoint | Gameplay state plus all backend state required to resume (contact/warm-start caches, sleep counters, solver internals, handle mapping and version/settings); private to tests/debug/future recovery |
| Per-view live projection | Only authorized entities and fields, own correction state and consumed `lastSeq`; explicit spawn/remove/discontinuity plus physics profile/version metadata |
| Recorded playback frame | Complete renderable authoritative state, including physical items, angles and visible tether state; no requirement to run an engine to view it |

Transforms and velocities alone may not restore a solver exactly. Evaluate full
checkpoint continuation over contact, sleep/wake, joint breakage and body reuse.
Exact restore is a comparison/debug capability, not a first-release requirement:
when unsupported, label it unsupported and use fresh projected prediction worlds.
Production rollback or crash recovery would require its own verified contract.
Engine blobs are opaque, pinned to an exact engine build, size-bounded and not
assumed portable between versions. They must never be sent to a player as a
convenient correction packet. Playback frames need no solver blob each tick.
If an existing snapshot API promises exact continuation, give it a separate
explicit checkpoint API or extend it correctly; do not silently weaken it.

## Live projection and privacy

Extend `shared/simulation/visibility.ts`, frame-layer encoding and decode, and
client buffers together. Moving items cannot remain in a supposedly static layer
without invalidation. A full keyframe/reconnect must recreate the projected world;
deletion, sleep, wake and revision change must survive layer resets.

Project actors/props/items using existing potential visibility and observation
policy. Hidden dynamic state and hidden joint endpoints are not prediction
dependencies the client is entitled to receive. A visible tether must be clipped
or omitted when its endpoint is unauthorized; endpoint IDs, body lists, force
sources and contact debug data must not disclose it. An unexplained correction
or physical reaction can be observed in gameplay; do not attach secret identity
or coordinates to explain it. Static known geometry, remembered doors and current
authorized dynamics remain distinct. The server continues to enforce actual
doors even when a client only knows an older state.

For first release, physical props that block bodies but not sight simplify this
policy. A later movable-cover feature must extend sight, remembered transforms,
navigation knowledge and minimap together. Spectator delay applies to complete
physics state and effects; it is not bypassed by a separate physics channel.

## Prediction and reconciliation

Do not predict the full authoritative world on clients. First stage predicts the
local character motor against known static geometry and authorized nearby dynamic
proxies. Remote actors/props are server-owned; speculative pushes never award
damage, collect loot or attach another player's constraint. Tethered motion may
temporarily use server-driven correction plus cosmetic input response until the
measured prediction model handles it reliably.

Each correction includes tick, consumed sequence, local pose and velocities,
movement status/profile, and discontinuity epoch. Keep bounded input and proxy
history indexed by simulation tick. Replay only inputs above consumed `lastSeq`;
do not replay once on arrival and again on acknowledgement. Proxy history must
use states aligned to the correction tick, not the two-tick-delayed render state.
Do not mix future proxy positions with older local state. A projected world cannot
reproduce hidden contacts or unknown remote inputs exactly; this is explicitly
approximate prediction, even when the shared backend is deterministic.

If history is missing, the backlog exceeds its cap, a profile changes, an entity
appears/disappears or a discontinuity occurs, reset the prediction island to the
latest authority and replay only supported bounded history. Unknown future forces
wait for authority. Avoid importing stale contact caches after replacing proxies;
the evaluation must compare a fresh local prediction island against safe cache
restoration. Exact whole-world rollback is not a first-release promise.

Correct physical state immediately; smooth only presentation error, with a bounded
duration/distance and collision-aware snapping when easing would visibly traverse
a wall. Camera, sprite, local fog and cover use the same eased eye as today.
Remote bodies interpolate position and shortest-arc angle between compatible
states; spawn/despawn, teleport and gap boundaries do not interpolate through
walls or invent an object. Sleep freezes simulation, not visibility eligibility.
Authoritative snapshots alone decide pickup, hit, constraint and inventory effects.

Validation network profiles: 0/50/100/200 ms round trips, jitter, delayed/missing
application frames, reconnect, background-tab resume and catch-up. WebSockets are
ordered/reliable, so network loss manifests as delay/stalls rather than arbitrary
out-of-order messages on one connection. Include queued-input trimming/repeats,
hidden contacts and door-memory disagreement. Record correction count, maximum
and percentile error, time to settle, frame cost and bytes; no claim of exact
dynamic reconciliation from a static-wall-only test.

## Replay and compatibility rollout

Keep the axes from [26](26-recording-contract.md): simulation version for changed
rules/order; content ID for masses, radii, materials, loot and ability tuning;
schema/minimum reader for representation. Pin backend artifact/settings identity
for debug/resimulation separately. Do not allocate final version numbers until
implementation knows which branches have landed.

New recordings contain transforms, angles, lifecycle and tether/item state needed
to draw every retained tick. Extend display descriptors for new content without
making the renderer depend on today's tuning. Raise minimum reader when old
clients would misrepresent required physics visuals; an optional diagnostic blob
alone does not require it. Old recordings keep their old frames, body appearance
and map semantics; no engine initialization or content migration to view them.

Retain [26](26-recording-contract.md)'s frame digest, bounded writer, whole-frame
omission and sparse playback behavior. No extrapolation or reconstructed spring
motion across missing frames. Preserve exact received retained states through
seek and partial tails. Measure serialization CPU, uncompressed pending bytes,
compressed size and publication failure with body-heavy frames: storage backpressure
is isolated today, serialization CPU is not. Checkpoint blobs, if recorded for a
debug mode, use separate bounded cadence and cannot become required for playback.

## Failure and resource policy

Initialize the selected backend before admitting a physics match; report a clear
load failure instead of silently selecting another solver. Dispose room worlds,
temporary prediction worlds and WASM allocations on replacement/retirement.
Validate finite positions/forces/materials and bounded arrays at game/API entry.
Stable capacity rules reject optional spawns without consuming an item or cooldown;
mandatory objective/loot conservation follows [29.1](29.1-physics-design.md).

Non-finite or escaped body state fails a test and emits a bounded diagnostic in
development. At runtime contain it at a validated last-safe state, cancel affected
constraints and mark a correction; repeated faults fail the affected physics
match rather than changing solver settings. This is an exceptional recovery path,
not ordinary collision handling or permission to teleport across obstacles.
Do not claim the historical crowd slowdown fixed by installing middleware.
