## Selected direction and decision boundary

Use a **2D rigid-body world with a game-owned character motor**, damped springs
and maximum-distance ropes. Rapier 2D/WASM is the selected development direction
per user decision (17.3 entry 18). The custom solver is retired as a candidate;
its retained implementation/tests serve only as an archival comparison baseline.
Planck.js is the reserve if Rapier integration fails or becomes unsustainable.
This is an engine direction, not a measured pass, production implementation or
default-match activation. Character feel, loot, abilities, visibility and
authority remain game rules around the solver.

### #451 remediation gates (proposed for review)

The following gates turn the user's #451 answers into a bounded lab contract.
They replace the failed static and spring tolerances from the initial P0 run;
they do not change the recorded P0 results in [42.6](42.6-physics-comparison.md).
Passing revised lab measurements and the bounded integration proposal are in
[42.9](42.9-physics-remediation.md). #472 approved those settings and the P1 phase
matrix; the experimental P1 profile pins four substeps.

- Static geometry may show a temporary peak penetration of at most 0.5 world
  units, and each affected pair must recover to at most 0.05 within four ticks
  (0.2 seconds). Wall, door and playable-mask escape remains zero-tolerance.
- Actor and prop penetration caps remain at most 0.5 world units; maximum rope
  excess remains at most 1 world unit.
- Set a per-substep body speed cap to
  `0.8 * min(body minimum radius, 12) / substepSeconds`. At the contestant
  reference radius of 12, this is 384, 768 and 1,536 world units/second at 2, 4
  and 8 substeps. Boxes use their smaller half extent as minimum radius.
  Larger bodies do not get a faster cap. Refuse faster body
  speeds before solving for swept-query or explicit-teleport handling; this is
  a lab admission rule only until P1 enforces it after motors, forces and
  impulses.
- Use a 24-world-unit speculative contact distance (0.6 engine metres at the
  existing 40 world units/metre scale). Other Rapier settings remain unchanged
  from the comparison unless a reviewed report justifies another change.
- Replace the one-second 5%-of-analytic spring error gate with initial stretch
  15 world units and no growth above 15. Settle to at most 0.5 by 3 seconds and
  remain at or below that through 4 seconds. Evaluate Rapier native center
  springs at stiffness/damping pairs 20/4, 2,000/60 and 20,000/200. Reject a
  finite `maxForce` request rather than pretending the backend enforces it;
  native springs are evaluated uncapped. No hook force-cap or break policy is
  approved here. Review visual traces separately from human-feel playtests.
- Four substeps is the reviewed experimental P1 setting after #472. It does not
  establish production qualification or default activation.

The maximum authored live-map bounding extent is 51,840 by 25,920 world units
(36×18 zones); the other authored live sizes are 17,280 by 8,640 and 34,560 by
17,280 (14, 55). These are supported live-room extents, not a universal macro
playground limit. Use realistic supported coordinates for distant-map witnesses
rather than the synthetic million-unit coordinate probe.

A cylinder viewed from above is a disk. Actors need no tipping, rolling or
vertical stacking to deliver the requested contact. Dynamic props may translate
and rotate in the horizontal plane. The initial world has zero planar gravity.
Floor drag and motor traction must be explicit: a top-down solver has no floor
contact from which to infer them. Keep horizontal physics independent of later
height/floor rules. Full 3D, ragdolls, fluid simulation, rope wrapping,
arbitrary destruction and playable multi-floor transitions are outside this slice.
Spring-built deformable ("jelly") objects and player squish/crush are why springs
are in scope (user, 17.3 entry 18), tracked in #455. The user selected a lab-only
prototype first on 2026-10-25. [42.21](42.21-jelly-squish.md) records the isolated
native-spring ring, fixed-core compression signal and retained-state viewer.
Gameplay integration and crush consequences remain undecided.

## Ownership and units

Proposed `shared/physics/` owns the backend adapter, body/constraint lifetime,
contact filtering, deterministic command/event ordering and physics queries.
`shared/simulation/` owns decisions, ability states, damage, inventory and outcome.
`shared/shape.ts` stays an engine-independent geometry leaf. Existing movement
exports stay as compatibility/query facades until each consumer is migrated;
never run both old separation and the new solver over the same actor in one tick.
No engine handles in public game types, wire records or content definitions.
`map/kernel/` imports no game physics. Map authoring provides shapes/material
descriptors; the runtime builds engine colliders from them.

Use world units in gameplay APIs; start the comparison at 40 world units per
engine metre, seconds for velocity/forces, radians for angles and relative mass
units. Pin the chosen scale and solver settings with the rules. Existing 9/8
units-per-tick movement corresponds to 180/160 units/second at 20 Hz. Do not feed
those old per-tick values into an SI-style integrator unchanged. Test large maps
at their distant corners as well as at the origin before selecting the scale.

The adapter needs only: create/remove body and joint; set motor intent; enqueue
force/impulse; fixed step; read transforms/events; overlap/ray/shape casts;
checkpoint/restore; dispose. A room owns one world; no global mutable engine.
IDs are game IDs mapped privately to handles, with generation-safe lookup after
removal. Cache static geometry once; gate/material/obstacle mutations enter one
revisioned mutation path consumed by collision, sight and navigation. Separate
indexes for different queries are fine; separate mutation truth is not.

## Character contact and movement

These are provisional tuning assumptions, not newly approved balance numbers:

| Property | Initial contract / tuning starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Body | Upright circular footprint, rotation locked; aim is independent of physical angle |
| Body versus head | Body radius starts at 0.8 times the nominal rendered head radius, represented by separate descriptors; measure the current art rather than equating the legacy movement radius with head size |
| Small overlap | Visual heads may overlap while bodies touch. Actor-to-actor contact alone permits a small tolerance (prototype cap 0.5 world units); this does not weaken wall, door or void containment |
| Actor contact | Both roles and teammates collide; inactive/dead/extracted actors do not. No ordinary walking through a stationary player |
| Sliding | Zero actor-contact restitution and low tangential friction (start at zero). Preserve tangential movement at glancing contact |
| Mass / shove | Start at equal actor mass; bounded motor force prevents unlimited crowd bulldozing. A heavier gladiator or charge override is explicit later content tuning |
| Floor | Ground traction, braking and maximum motor acceleration are separate from contact friction; ice reduces traction/braking, slow terrain lowers desired speed |
| Impulses | External velocity survives motor updates; locomotion may recover toward target speed with bounded acceleration, never overwrite all velocity each tick |

Body and head sizes are separate match-pinned content fields; the ratio guides
tuning only. Never compute gameplay collision size from a sprite's runtime
dimensions or a cosmetic skin.

Use dynamic, rotation-locked character bodies as the first candidate, driven by
a bounded motor impulse toward desired velocity. Compare a swept character
controller only if dynamic motors cannot meet feel/contact gates; do not combine
an infinite-mass kinematic controller with arbitrary prop pushing and call that
equivalent. Clamp motor effort, external impulse and total supported speed under
separately named content limits; keep explosion/hook responses observable.

All living actor bodies solve together. Stable IDs determine tie-breaking,
including coincident centres; avoid random separation or permanent array-order
priority. The solve must preserve wall/void containment when crowd correction
pushes one actor into another. Narrow openings may legitimately queue people;
blocking is allowed, but indefinite numerical wedging is not. Bots must wait,
steer or replan rather than teleport through the queue.

Spawn/respawn/teleport needs a bounded free-placement search including active
actors and props. If no legal spot exists, defer placement and retry; do not
spawn inside a wall or eject another player. Defer a door closing while a body
occupies its sweep/closed footprint. Crushing damage is not implicit. On
teleport, death, extraction or transit: clear velocities as appropriate, remove
constraints, invalidate prediction history and publish a discontinuity. Ordinary
walking and contact never use a teleport fallback to cross geometry.

Movement footprint, combat hurt shape, interaction reach and rendered head are
distinct. Keep current combat hit rules until explicitly versioned; thinner
movement cylinders must not silently shrink hit targets. Navigation may remain
conservatively larger initially; update its clearance contract deliberately.

### Experimental P2 implementation (#439)

The explicit content/rules identities and provisional numbers live in [14](14-match-rules.md).
`simulation/actor-physics.ts` owns the profile validation, movement footprint and
centre-sampled floor motor selection. `movement.bodyRadius` remains the legacy
movement/combat convention; combat queries do not read the narrower profile.
Caller-provided character tuning and motor surfaces are copied and frozen before
world creation. P1 content keeps the previous motor and placement behavior.

`GameMap.motorSurfaces` contains at most 1,024 rectangular regions with unique
IDs, numeric priority and speed/traction/braking multipliers in [0,1]. The actor
centre at motor construction selects the highest priority containing region;
equal priorities use lexical ID. Rectangles include their left/top edges and
exclude right/bottom edges. Traction scales active steering acceleration, braking
scales recovery with no movement input, and speed scales target speed. Uncovered
floor uses multiplier 1. This does not add floor contact friction, damage, art,
prop drag or surface generation; P5 owns that expansion.

P2 placement checks static geometry, active actors and existing solid props,
including rotated prop colliders. Its bounded search retains P1's 24 rings of
32 candidates at four-unit intervals. Initial impossible placement rejects game
creation; an impossible gladiator respawn retries a later boundary. An impossible
relocation keeps its previous pose. Developer teleport rejects an occupied exact
destination; accepted teleports clear motion/motor/joints and invalidate queued
effects by generation. Removal on death/extraction/transit clears the body and
constraints. Private authoritative physics state marks placement/removal with
ID and generation; P3/P4 must consume these markers for projection/prediction.
Transit preflights its bounded landing before spending cooldown or emitting
effects, so a blocked destination leaves both intact. Each marker reaches exactly
one stepped capture: a marker made inside a step reaches that step's capture, and
one made between ticks (teleport, seating, creation) reaches the next step's,
appearing also in any unstepped capture before it. Markers are deduplicated by ID
and bounded to 128.
Closing an occupied gate is refused (the gate stays open); nothing retries it, and
closing can be tried again after the footprint is clear. A door interaction tests
player positions as well as bodies, so a transit that landed earlier in the same
phase, whose body has not moved yet, refuses the close before discovery observes it. This is the authored closed rectangular footprint,
not a new swinging-door geometry model. Queries propagate changed native body
positions before testing colliders, so a between-tick teleport cannot leave stale
occupancy. P8 adds local dynamic navigation below; general scenery mutation
remains outside the pinned authored-geometry contract.

All admitted actors are inserted by stable game ID. Coincident-centre solver
witnesses document deterministic ID ties, but illegal overlapping spawns are
never admitted as normal placement. Actor penetration is capped by the <=0.5
witness gate; static penetration retains the reviewed temporary/recovery gate,
with zero wall/door/void crossing. Authorized developer views draw separate
head/body/combat circles in world units from pinned display descriptors: cyan
head, amber body and pink combat radius.
Human feel is still unverified; evidence and limitations are [42.12](42.12-physics-p2.md).

## Collision and query matrix

| Pair / query | Initial behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Actor vs actor | Solid, low-friction contact with bounded slop |
| Actor vs solid prop | Mutual contact; motor force bounds pushing |
| Actor vs pickup | Sensor/query only; loose loot cannot block a doorway |
| Pickup vs pickup | No solid contact initially; avoids unstable loot piles |
| Pickup vs static world | Solid bounded motion; floor drag/sleep; no escape into void |
| Prop vs prop / static | Rigid contact; material friction/restitution; bounded sleeping |
| Hook / ordinary projectile | Swept query with explicit hit mask; owner exclusion and earliest time of impact, stable ID tie-break |
| Static windows, pipes, hedges | Preserve existing body/hand blocking and shot/sight pass-through semantics unless a new projectile profile explicitly changes them |
| Sight / bot discovery | Separate semantic queries; no automatic sight occlusion just because a body is solid |

Solid props initially do not become sight blockers. If later content needs movable
cover, it must implement observation/memory and sight invalidation as a separate
contract. Floor surfaces use an explicit deterministic priority where they
overlap; sample at body centre initially. Sensors report ordered transitions,
not direct inventory/damage callbacks during solving. Ordinary contact does not
cause damage; impact damage needs an opt-in profile with thresholds and cooldown.

Represent playable-mask holes and outer bounds as actual sweep/contact boundaries,
not just an endpoint `insideMap` test. Preserve legacy diamond handling. Convex
compound colliders must match authored shapes without internal seam snagging.
Bound collider count, vertex count and aspect ratios at ingestion; retain
non-colliding artwork separately. Fast actor/prop/actor relative motion, thin
walls and rotating elongated props are explicit anti-tunnelling tests.

## Springs, ropes and the first hook ability

A spring restores toward a rest length with damping; a rope enforces a maximum
distance without pushing when slack. Support both, not a chain of little rigid
segments. Store local anchors, rest/max length, stiffness/damping or equivalent
frequency/damping ratio, force limit, break condition and stable endpoint IDs.
Convert author-friendly frequency using reduced mass once the solver is chosen;
bound stiffness against the fixed step. Zero-length anchors and deleted/sleeping
endpoints must not create NaNs or dangling joints. Wake affected bodies on force,
contact, anchor change or constraint attachment.

Prototype a hook as a separately selectable experimental gladiator ability;
do not replace Warden/Specter/Striker kits silently. State machine:
`ready -> flying -> attached/pulling -> released -> cooldown`, with miss/blocked
flight returning to cooldown. Start with one enemy actor target and one active
tether per caster; no friendly grab, scenery anchor, multiple victim or rope
wrapping. Exact range, travel speed, force, duration and cooldown are pinned
prototype content, tuned in the lab before promotion.

The server sweeps the hook tip. On attachment, validate current target status,
range and unobstructed tether; do not pull a victim through a wall because a
historical hit was legal. Shorten a force-limited rope target with a damped pull;
retain a bounded victim steering motor. The caster is not an immovable world
anchor: equal/opposite response or a documented bounded caster motor must account
for the reaction. Contacts win over target-length wishes. Stop at a standoff
outside the caster body; break on obstruction, excessive separation, timeout,
death, extraction, teleport or endpoint removal. Release velocity is bounded,
not an accumulated spring-energy launch. A wall at a doorway, a closing gate,
two opposing hooks and a third actor between endpoints need witness cases.
Pressing skill again while attached requests release; it does not bypass cooldown.
Revalidate obstruction/status at the next-tick attachment boundary as well as hit.

Do not rewind the entire physical world for a hook. First prototype uses present
authoritative swept flight. Existing melee/rail lag compensation remains its own
hit-query policy; later hook compensation requires an explicit historical-hit
and current-attachment contract. Physical hit outcomes and joint creation are
server-only; the client may immediately animate the launch without claiming a hit.

## Loot, props and physical item extension

L0 (#442) implements the identity/atomic logical transition boundary as an
explicit engine-free selection; [14](14-match-rules.md) owns its rules, IDs,
split/merge survivors, capacity and mandatory overflow. [22.1](22.1-shared-simulation.md)
owns its modules, and [26](26-recording-contract.md) its schema 11 decision.
Physical body attachment, wake/sleep and authored physics profiles remain P5.

Item definition, item instance and world body are different identities. A carried
item has no physics body. A ground instance has at most one; pickup atomically
removes that body, transfers the exact state and destroys attached constraints.
Drop reverses this without copying value. Preserve quality, magazine/reserve
ownership, charge, stack counts and stable lineage across drop, death, pickup and
replay. Splitting a stack allocates deterministic child IDs and conserves quantity;
merging preserves a deterministic survivor. No ID allocation in solver callbacks.

Retain six ordinary slots and equipment-derived contestant abilities. Reload and
tier work can proceed independently of the solver after defining the item
contract. Physics must not decide ammo compatibility, rarity or pickup priority.
Retain existing pickup range, reach and self-drop cooldown until deliberately
changed. Resolve competing pickups by a documented stable player order. Sleeping
items remain pickable and visible when authorized; sleep is not despawn.

Ordinary drops settle with modest bounded motion and no actor blocking. Thrown
devices, pushable cover and physical weapons use separate authored profiles,
never infer physics from an icon or rarity. Cap active bodies/constraints, reject
optional spawns deterministically at the cap, and preserve the item in inventory
when its drop cannot be represented. Reserve capacity for mandatory death drops;
overflow must retain recoverable logical loot, never erase a charged objective
cell. Destruction/removal emits a typed fact before dependent bodies/joints vanish.

Explosions query a radius, apply game-defined cover/falloff and bounded impulses
in stable target order. Damage and impulse are separate outcomes (a shield need
not cancel knockback). Do not let impulses reach through walls merely because
the broad phase found a nearby body. First solid prop is an inert pushable object;
breakables, crush damage and explosive chains are later opt-in profiles. Player
squish/crush is designed in #455; a crushed body never resolves by crossing geometry.

### Experimental P5 implementation (#445)

The explicit profile and provisional values are owned by [14](14-match-rules.md).
`simulation/world-physics.ts` validates/freezes authored crate placements, reconciles
L0 ground IDs to `item:<id>` bodies, preflights optional body capacity and writes
solved item poses before interactions/discovery. The physics tick uses L0's atomic
slot/drop/rearrangement operations. Logical mandatory overflow is admitted later
in stable ID order when a body slot and legal footprint exist; it remains ordinary
pickable loot while waiting. Actor roster slots remain reserved for respawn.

The adapter's optional loot collision profile uses static-only contact groups.
Actor placement and gate occupancy exclude loot. Floor drag changes native damping
without waking sleepers; force/impulse wakes them. Complete projected body lists
and private typed placement/removal markers identify lifecycle boundaries; removal
is marked before deleting body/joints. Solver command callbacks never allocate IDs
or transfer inventory. Blast policy stays in `simulation/explosion-physics.ts` and
traps, separate from the low-level impulse adapter. Current static-map cover is an
explicit assumption; this slice does not introduce moving blast/sight cover.

## Ballistics and elevation boundary

First ship planar motion. Prepare an independent opt-in ballistic profile with
height `z`, vertical velocity, gravity, ground-plane ID and obstacle height ranges.
A thrown object's planar sweep and height interval must agree at time of impact;
testing only endpoint height allows tunnelling through cover. Landing, bounce,
fuse expiry and impact order need one rule, recorded in [29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md).
Legacy map records without height keep existing shot/body rules, not guessed
heights. The first ballistic issue is a lab contract/prototype, blocked from live
promotion until height authoring and roof/window semantics are settled. Do not
equate decorative roofs with collidable ceilings or build a multi-floor game by
adding a `z` field. This preserves a route to 2.5D without forcing it into the
first character-collision release.

### P7 lab prototype (#447)

`tools/physics/ballistics.mjs` implements the separately invoked
`ballistic-lab-1` profile. Nothing in shared simulation, content, maps or the
server imports it. No live simulation/content/schema version is allocated;
promotion needs a reviewed version and content decision with separate expectations.
The frozen fixture and ordinary projectile/window behavior remain unchanged.

The provisional witness is one non-rotating **cube** with half extent 2 in x/y
and vertical extent 4. `z` is its bottom height above the named flat ground plane,
not its centre. Planar velocity is constant; vertical position is
`z(t) = z0 + vz*t - 200*t*t/2`, in world units and seconds. A tick is 0.05 s.
One ground bounce has restitution 0.5; the next landing stops. Impacts stop without
planar reflection, damage or blast. The default launch is (20,50,20), velocity
(100,0,60), fuse age 2 s. These are laboratory assumptions, not balance approvals.
The witness budget is 200 ticks, with at most 1,024 explicit obstacles and 1,024 holes.
Launch admission caps z at 1,000, total speed at 2,000 units/s and fuse age at
10 seconds. Validation of stepped states permits the conservative energy envelope
(height at most 11,000 and speed at most sqrt(4,400,000)), so a valid launch does
not invalidate itself during ascent/descent. Positions, IDs and arrays are bounded;
non-finite values and mismatched ground-plane IDs are rejected.
Scene fields are limited to `groundPlaneId`, `bounds`, `holes`, `obstacles` and
`decorativeRoofs`; unknown fields are rejected to catch authoring typos. Roof art
is validated as at most 1,024 finite rectangles but contributes no contacts.
The ID `bounds` is reserved for the implicit outer edge, so authored obstacle and
hole IDs cannot be confused with boundary contacts.

Obstacles require unique IDs, an axis-aligned planar rectangle and closed
`[zMin,zMax]` solid height interval. Collision expands planar rectangles by 2 and
tests bottom height against `[zMin-4,zMax]`. Candidate times include all planar
entry/exit crossings and both roots of each vertical slab boundary. Testing those
times and the intervals between them detects overlap beginning *after* planar
entry, ascent/descent and apex tangency, even when both tick endpoints miss.
This is an analytic sweep for stationary boxes and this cube only; it does not
establish spherical, rotating or moving-target continuous collision detection.

The lab mask is an inset outer rectangle with expanded rectangular holes. Its
boundaries are height-independent terminal contacts, so throwing high cannot
escape into void. This intentionally bounded representation is not an adapter
for the game's arbitrary cell masks. Initial overlap terminates at time zero.
Decorative roof records are ignored for collision. Only an explicit height interval represents
a physical ceiling. A synthetic window consists of a solid sill and lintel with
an empty interval between; contact includes tangency. No floor transitions exist.

Legacy maps **without height** retain existing shot/body/window semantics. They
are never auto-converted to this profile; an explicit lab obstacle lacking height
is rejected. Physical height authoring, shape coverage and mask adaptation must
be reviewed before any real-map integration. Open product choices remain in
[17.3](17.3-product-gameplay.md); tie/state contracts are in
[29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md), executed evidence in
[42.18](42.18-physics-p7.md).

## Experimental P6 constraints and hook (#446)

`physics/constraints.ts` validates explicit `spring` and `rope` descriptors;
`physics/world.ts` applies their forces at rotated local anchors each fixed
substep. Descriptors name stable body IDs, local anchors, rest/maximum length,
stiffness, damping, a mandatory finite positive force cap and optional break
separation. Springs can push and pull; ropes exert tension only when stretched.
Coincident anchors apply zero force. Relative anchor velocities include angular
motion. Forces are equal/opposite, wake endpoints and share the existing body
speed bounds. Deletion/teleport removes constraints; excessive separation breaks
them. There is no positional projection or stored-energy release impulse.
Legacy descriptors without `kind` retain the native centre-rope implementation.

`simulation/hook.ts` owns the opt-in ability; tuning and identity are in [14](14-match-rules.md).
Flight sweeps against current geometry and moving actor paths through the
swept-query owner shared with physical projectiles (`simulation/swept.ts`); attachment is
validated again at the next boundary. Hook gameplay never runs in client prediction.
A force-limited rope is a soft distance target, so the historical native-rope
one-unit excess gate does not apply to this explicit capped mode: blocked contact
must win over shortening. Human hook feel and P9 promotion remain pending.
