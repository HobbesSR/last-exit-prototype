## Model and scope

A **2D rigid-body world with a game-owned character motor**, damped springs and maximum-distance
ropes. Character feel, loot, abilities, visibility and authority are game rules around the solver.
The legacy custom solver is retired; reviving it, or changing engine, needs a new reviewed decision
([21](21-stack.md)).

A cylinder viewed from above is a disk: actors need no tipping, rolling or vertical stacking.
Dynamic props translate and rotate in the horizontal plane. The world has zero planar gravity, so
floor drag and motor traction are explicit; a top-down solver has no floor contact to infer them
from. Horizontal physics stays independent of later height/floor rules. Full 3D, ragdolls, fluids,
rope wrapping, arbitrary destruction and multi-floor transitions are outside the model. Spring-built
deformable ("jelly") objects and player squish/crush are why springs are in scope (17.3 entry 18,
#455); they exist only as a lab prototype ([42.21](42.21-jelly-squish.md)), and gameplay
integration and crush consequences are undecided.

The largest authored live map is 51,840 by 25,920 world units (36×18 zones); the others are
17,280 by 8,640 and 34,560 by 17,280 (14, 55). These are supported live-room extents, not a macro
playground limit. Distant-map witnesses use realistic supported coordinates.

## Settings and witness gates

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Scale | 40 world units per engine metre; seconds for velocity and force, radians for angles, relative mass units. Gameplay APIs use world units |
| Substeps | Four fixed substeps per 20 Hz tick (12.5 ms); a CPU spike cannot change the count |
| Speculative contact distance | 24 world units (0.6 engine metres) |
| Body speed cap | `0.8 * min(body minimum radius, 12) / substepSeconds` (`shared/physics/motion-limits.ts`): 768 units/s at four substeps for the reference contestant radius of 12. Boxes use their smaller half extent; larger bodies do not get a faster cap. Motors, forces and impulses share the bound before and after each substep; faster initial motion is refused |
| Engine | Rapier pinned at 0.21.0 |

Tests hold these gates: static geometry may show a temporary peak penetration of at most 0.5 world
units, recovering to at most 0.05 within four ticks; wall, door and playable-mask escape is
zero-tolerance. Actor and prop penetration are capped at 0.5 world units, and a native rope's excess
at 1 world unit. Rapier documents cross-platform determinism only for identical versions,
initialization and insertion/removal order, and its CCD has limits for two moving CCD bodies, so the
game's own witnesses, not engine claims, are the acceptance tests. Repeatability is not rollback
determinism.

## Ownership

`shared/physics/` owns the backend adapter, body/constraint lifetime, contact filtering,
deterministic command/event ordering and physics queries. `shared/simulation/` owns decisions,
ability states, damage, inventory and outcome. `shared/shape.ts` stays an engine-independent
geometry leaf. Never run two solvers over the same actor in one tick. No engine handles appear in
public game types, wire records or content definitions. `map/kernel/` imports no game physics: map
authoring provides shape and material descriptors and the runtime builds colliders from them.

The adapter offers only: create/remove body and joint; set motor intent; enqueue force/impulse;
fixed step; read transforms/events; overlap/ray/shape casts; checkpoint/restore; dispose. A room owns
one world; there is no global mutable engine. IDs are game IDs mapped privately to handles, with
generation-safe lookup after removal. Static geometry is cached once; gate, material and obstacle
mutations enter one revisioned mutation path consumed by collision, sight and navigation. Separate
indexes for different queries are fine; separate mutation truth is not.

The map's static geometry (`mapGeometry` bodies) is one collider per shape, all on one shared fixed
body, because Rapier's step costs per fixed body as well as per collider (42.22, #670). Closed gates
come and go, so each keeps its own fixed body. Map geometry cannot be a constraint endpoint.
The world calls the physics pipeline's step itself and removes bodies through rapier.js's raw
sets, because `World.step` and `World.removeRigidBody` then re-sync rapier.js's handle maps by
visiting every collider across the wasm boundary, which made each step cost per collider in the
world (42.22). The world keeps those maps current instead: it creates and removes only through
calls that map and unmap their own handles, and Rapier creates none inside a step.
`tests/physics-world.test.js` checks the maps against the engine.
Soft bodies are the exception: the skipped sync is what maps them, so the step throws if the
world holds one. Using them means re-syncing whenever any exist (#732).

## Character contact and movement

The numbers are tuning in [14](14-match-rules.md), not approved balance.

| Property | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Body | Upright circular footprint, rotation locked; aim is independent of physical angle |
| Body versus head | Separate descriptors; body radius starts at 0.8 times the rendered head radius as a tuning guide |
| Small overlap | Visual heads may overlap while bodies touch. Actor-to-actor contact alone permits a small tolerance (0.5 world units); this does not weaken wall, door or void containment |
| Actor contact | Both roles and teammates collide; inactive/dead/extracted actors do not. No ordinary walking through a stationary player |
| Sliding | Zero actor-contact restitution and low tangential friction. Tangential movement survives glancing contact |
| Mass / shove | Equal actor mass; bounded motor force prevents unlimited crowd bulldozing. A heavier gladiator or charge override would be explicit content |
| Floor | Ground traction, braking and maximum motor acceleration are separate from contact friction |
| Impulses | External velocity survives motor updates; locomotion recovers toward target speed with bounded acceleration and never overwrites all velocity each tick |

Body and head sizes are match-pinned content fields. Never compute gameplay collision size from a
sprite's runtime dimensions or a cosmetic skin. Characters are dynamic, rotation-locked bodies driven
by a bounded motor impulse toward desired velocity; an infinite-mass kinematic controller with
arbitrary prop pushing is not equivalent. Motor effort, external impulse and total speed are clamped
under separately named content limits, and explosion and hook responses stay observable.

All living actor bodies solve together, inserted by stable game ID. Stable IDs break ties, including
coincident centres; there is no random separation or permanent array-order priority. Crowd correction
preserves wall/void containment. Narrow openings may legitimately queue people; indefinite numerical
wedging is not allowed. Bots wait, steer or replan rather than teleport through a queue.

Movement footprint, combat hurt shape, interaction reach and rendered head are distinct.
`movement.bodyRadius` remains the movement/combat convention and combat queries do not read the
narrower physics profile; a thinner movement cylinder must not silently shrink hit targets. Navigation
may stay conservatively larger and changes its clearance contract deliberately. Authorized developer
views draw separate head (cyan), body (amber) and combat (pink) circles from pinned display
descriptors.

### Motor surfaces

`simulation/actor-physics.ts` owns profile validation, the movement footprint and floor motor
selection. Caller-provided character tuning and motor surfaces are copied and frozen before world
creation. `GameMap.motorSurfaces` holds at most 1,024 rectangular regions with unique IDs, numeric
priority and speed/traction/braking multipliers in [0,1]. The actor centre at motor construction
selects the highest-priority containing region; equal priorities use lexical ID. Rectangles include
their left/top edges and exclude right/bottom. Traction scales steering acceleration, braking scales
recovery with no movement input, and speed scales target speed; uncovered floor uses 1. Surfaces add
no contact friction, damage, art or prop drag.

### Placement and lifecycle

Spawn, respawn, drop and teleport search for a free spot against static geometry, active actors and
solid props (including rotated prop colliders): 24 rings of 32 candidates at four-unit intervals.
Impossible initial placement rejects game creation; an impossible gladiator respawn retries at a
later boundary; an impossible relocation keeps its previous pose. Nothing spawns inside a wall or
ejects another player, and illegal overlapping spawns are never admitted. Developer teleport rejects
an occupied exact destination. Transit preflights its landing before spending cooldown or emitting
effects, so a blocked destination leaves both intact.

Teleport, death, extraction and transit clear motion, motor and constraints, advance the body's
generation (so queued effects cannot reach a reused body), invalidate prediction history and publish a
discontinuity. Ordinary walking and contact never use a teleport fallback to cross geometry.
Private authoritative state marks each placement/removal with ID and generation. Each marker reaches
exactly one stepped capture: one made inside a step reaches that step's capture, and one made between
ticks (teleport, seating, creation) reaches the next step's, appearing also in any unstepped capture
before it. Markers are deduplicated by ID and bounded to 256.

Closing an occupied gate is refused (the gate stays open); nothing retries it. A door interaction
tests player positions as well as bodies, so a transit that landed earlier in the same phase refuses
the close before discovery observes it. This uses the authored closed rectangular footprint, not a
swinging-door model. Queries propagate changed native body positions before testing colliders, so a
between-tick teleport cannot leave stale occupancy. Crushing damage is not implicit.

Visible dynamic bodies enter only a private local bot overlay
([22.6.4](22.6.4-dynamic-overlay.md)): static geometry stays pinned, dynamic motion does not rebuild
the authored mesh or change portal clearance, and the overlay waits or routes around local bodies
with no teleport recovery. General scenery mutation is outside the authored-geometry contract.

## Collision and query matrix

| Pair / query | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Actor vs actor | Solid, low-friction contact with bounded slop |
| Actor vs solid prop | Mutual contact; motor force bounds pushing |
| Actor vs pickup | Sensor/query only; loose loot cannot block a doorway |
| Pickup vs pickup | No solid contact; avoids unstable loot piles |
| Pickup vs static world | Solid bounded motion; floor drag/sleep; no escape into void |
| Prop vs prop / static | Rigid contact; material friction/restitution; bounded sleeping |
| Hook / ordinary projectile | Swept query with explicit hit mask; owner exclusion and earliest time of impact, stable ID tie-break |
| Static windows, pipes, hedges | Existing body/hand blocking and shot/sight pass-through semantics, unless a projectile profile explicitly changes them |
| Sight / bot discovery | Separate semantic queries; a body being solid does not occlude sight |

Solid props are not sight blockers. Movable cover would need its own observation/memory and sight
invalidation contract. Overlapping floor surfaces use explicit deterministic priority, sampled at
body centre. Sensors report ordered transitions, never direct inventory/damage callbacks during
solving. Ordinary contact causes no damage; impact damage needs an opt-in profile with thresholds and
cooldown.

Playable-mask holes and outer bounds are real sweep/contact boundaries, not an endpoint `insideMap`
test. A map without a playable area is rejected (#985). Convex compound colliders match authored
shapes without internal seam snagging. Collider count, vertex count and aspect ratios are bounded at
ingestion; non-colliding artwork is kept separately. Fast relative motion, thin walls and rotating
elongated props are explicit anti-tunnelling tests.

## Constraints and the hook

A spring restores toward a rest length with damping; a rope enforces a maximum distance without
pushing when slack. Neither is a chain of rigid segments. `physics/constraints.ts` validates explicit
`spring` and `rope` descriptors: stable endpoint body IDs, local anchors, rest/maximum length,
stiffness, damping, a mandatory finite positive force cap and optional break separation.
`physics/world.ts` applies their forces at rotated local anchors each substep. Coincident anchors
apply zero force; relative anchor velocity includes angular motion. Forces are equal and opposite,
wake both endpoints and share the body speed bounds. Deletion and teleport remove constraints;
excessive separation breaks them. There is no positional projection or stored-energy release impulse.
A descriptor without `kind` keeps Rapier's native centre rope, which has no force cap or break policy.
Zero-length anchors and deleted or sleeping endpoints must not create NaNs or dangling joints. At
most eight constraints exist at once.

`simulation/hook.ts` owns the hook ability. State machine: `ready -> flying -> attached/pulling ->
released -> cooldown`; a miss or blocked flight returns to cooldown. One enemy actor target and one
active tether per caster; no friendly grab, scenery anchor, multiple victims or rope wrapping.
Flight sweeps against current geometry and moving actor paths through the swept-query owner shared
with physical projectiles (`simulation/swept.ts`), in present authoritative time: the world is never
rewound for a hook, and melee/rail lag compensation stays its own policy.

On a hit, attachment is validated again at the next-tick boundary: current target status, range and
unobstructed tether, so a historically legal hit never pulls a victim through a wall. The pull is a
force-limited rope shortening toward a standoff outside the caster body; contacts win over the target
length, so the native rope's one-unit excess gate does not apply. The victim keeps a bounded steering
motor and the caster takes the reaction; it is not an immovable anchor. The tether breaks on
obstruction, excessive separation, timeout, death, extraction, teleport or endpoint removal. Release
velocity is bounded. Pressing skill again while attached requests release without bypassing cooldown.
Hit outcomes and joint creation are server-only; hook gameplay never runs in client prediction, and
the client may only animate the launch.

## Loot, props and physical items

[14](14-match-rules.md) owns item identities, split/merge survivors, capacity and mandatory overflow;
[22.1](22.1-shared-simulation.md) the modules; [26](26-recording-contract.md) the schema.

Item definition, item instance and world body are different identities. A carried item has no body;
a ground instance has at most one (`item:<id>`). Pickup atomically removes that body, transfers the
exact state and destroys attached constraints; drop reverses this without copying value. Quality,
magazine/reserve ownership, charge, stack counts and lineage survive drop, death, pickup and replay.
Splitting allocates deterministic child IDs and conserves quantity; merging keeps a deterministic
survivor. Solver callbacks never allocate IDs or transfer inventory.

Physics never decides ammo compatibility, rarity or pickup priority. Competing pickups resolve in
stable player order. Sleeping items stay pickable and visible when authorized; sleep is not despawn.
Ordinary drops settle with modest bounded motion and no actor blocking. Thrown devices, pushable
cover and physical weapons use separate authored profiles, never inferred from an icon or rarity.

`simulation/world-physics.ts` validates and freezes authored crate placements, reconciles ground IDs
to item bodies, preflights optional body capacity and writes solved item poses before
interactions/discovery. Optional spawns at the cap are rejected deterministically, and an item whose
drop cannot be represented stays in inventory. Actor roster slots stay reserved for respawn. Logical
mandatory overflow is admitted later in stable ID order when a body slot and legal footprint exist,
remaining ordinary pickable loot while it waits; a charged objective cell is never erased. Removal is
marked before body/joint deletion. Loot uses static-only contact groups, and actor placement and gate
occupancy ignore it. Floor drag changes native damping without waking sleepers; force or impulse
wakes them.

Explosions query a radius, apply game-defined cover/falloff and bounded impulses in stable target
order; blast policy lives in `simulation/explosion-physics.ts` and traps, apart from the low-level
impulse adapter. Damage and impulse are separate outcomes (a shield need not cancel knockback).
Impulses never reach through walls because the broad phase found a nearby body. Cover is the static
map. The solid prop is an inert pushable crate; breakables, crush damage and explosive chains would be
opt-in profiles, and a crushed body never resolves by crossing geometry (#455).

## Height and ballistics

Live motion is planar. Maps without height keep their shot/body/window semantics and are never
assigned guessed heights; decorative roofs are not collidable ceilings, and a `z` field does not make
a multi-floor game. A thrown object's planar sweep and height interval must agree at time of impact,
since testing only endpoint height tunnels through cover.

The `ballistic-lab-1` profile (`tools/physics/ballistics.mjs`, #447) is a lab prototype that nothing
in shared simulation, content, maps or the server imports, with no live version allocated. Its
witness, admission bounds and analytic sweep are in the
[archived text](archive/2026-11-13-physics-adoption/29.1-physics-design.md#p7-lab-prototype-447) and
[42.18](42.18-physics-p7.md); its tie rule is in [29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md). Live
promotion needs height authoring, roof/window semantics, mask adaptation and a reviewed version and
content decision; the open product choices are in [17.3](17.3-product-gameplay.md).
