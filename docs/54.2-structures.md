# 54.2. Structure types

Part of [54](54-region-types.md). The structure section: `checkpoint`, `park`, `hut`, `depot`, `plant` and `compound`. The catalogue table lists `checkpoint` and `park` in the friction family; their entries sit in this section. The rules every entry relies on and
does not repeat are in [54, "What every type shares"](54-region-types.md#what-every-type-shares);
the catalogue table and the status of the whole catalogue are in 54 too. Moved
from 54 without changing its content (#510).

## Structures

Buildings give concealment (P-11, F-18): solid walls and roofs hide what's
inside, and windows and doors let sight through.

### `checkpoint`

- **Role:** the half of the old `military` that isn't a walled base. A choke
  across a passage: barriers, a guard post and staggered chicanes that slow a
  crossing and break the sightline along it.
- **Strategy:** no decomposer. Chicane barriers across the line between its
  two farthest portals, alternating sides, with a small roofed post beside
  them. A one-portal region is a guard post alone.
  **Built (#220):** `map/micro/strategies/checkpoint.ts`. Unlike the spacing
  types it keeps its promise by pruning during the solve, since a chicane is
  meant to stand across the route.
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#checkpoint-builder).
  - **Side yards (#575):** residual cells outside the chicane corridor can
    hold supply crates, preserving the primary barriers/post and two-cell
    aisles. A full two-cell access ring around each crate leaves a local hunter
    bypass, so crates cannot seal a narrow side lobe against the boundary.
    Every added crate also keeps the portal promise; the existing loot
    clearance filter excludes its geometry. A one-portal checkpoint can
    have crates around its post.
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of every piece, indoors or out.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
  - **Too small:** a region with no post and no barrier placed goes to
    `open` (17 M24).
- **Shape needs:** *proposed*, a chicane needs two portals and 3 cells across
  the line, and a post needs a contained 6 × 5 footprint. Either alone is
  enough to build.
- **Formed by:** medium set pieces, where the old library's `military`
  pieces were.

### `park`

- **Role:** the old `park` and `tree` classes. Overgrown ground with tree
  clumps and hedges: soft cover, open sightlines, and hedge lines that wall
  off stretches of ground except at a gap.
- **Strategy:** no decomposer and no buildings. Clumps of trees (round
  obstacles) at a seeded density, and hedge runs that leave a gap a door wide.
  **Built (#221):** `map/micro/strategies/park.ts`, which keeps its promise by
  spacing, as `cover` and `plant` do.
  - **Hedge pocket (#698 under #529):** an optional single reusable 14 x 14
    connected hedge enclosure with a 2.5-cell east opening, tried only when
    `hedges` is positive. The builder preserves a two-cell apron and requires
    a hunter route from a portal into its interior; the route stays protected
    from later scatter. No fit, zero hedge weight, or no portal witness leaves
    the existing park grammar in place. Pocket counts are separate from the
    older hedge-run count, and the normal per-cell loot pass still follows.
    Some parks in the 24x12 and 36x18 maps fit it; the 12x6 seed-1 sample did not.
    See [42.25](42.25-park-pocket.md) for measurements and scope.
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#park-builder).
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of every piece.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
  - **Too small:** a region too small for a piece stays clear.
- **Parameters:** `density` and `hedges`, each from 0 to 1.
- **Shape needs:** none, as `cover`.
- **Formed by:** small set pieces (52). Its ground is weighted 0.15 in the fill (52, #221), as `cover`'s is, so a lone 6 × 6 tile of it holds a tree at most.

### `hut`

- **Role:** one small building in its yard, with walls, a door, windows and a
  roof. It is 52's worked case: four corner blocks across a tile junction form
  one 6 × 6 region, and the hut stands anywhere inside it.
- **Strategy:** a builder picks a contained rectangle for the building, puts
  its door where the yard keeps every portal joined, and leaves the yard around
  it. A route through the building counts only through unlocked doors. After
  siting the house, it may delegate useful boundary rectangles to `cover` while
  retaining the house, actual doorstep access and movable yard circulation (20.10).
  **Built (#129), with larger houses since L5 (#192, 56):**
  `map/micro/strategies/hut.ts`. It reasons in cells, where a hunter is a
  2 × 2 block of yard cells, using cell-level connectivity and circulation.
  - **Multi-house composition (#532, `types-51` / `hut-yard-4`):** large regions
    may hold up to eight houses before cover is considered. Each additional house
    keeps two-cell aisles and a full exterior access ring; cumulative siting checks
    preserve every earlier doorstep and portal connection (20.3.4). Compact or
    heavily cleared regions may still hold one house, or none when no safe site exists.
    All envelopes and doorstep demands remain retained during yard handoff.
    This delivers the hut capability; compound courtyard contents are implemented,
    and secondary exterior compound structure work is recorded under #532 below. Authored-map and
    builder-fill evidence is in [42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md#multi-house-huts-532).
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#hut-builder).
  - **Cord-aware yard adoption (#647, `types-48`):** after siting the house,
    register its whole box as a pessimistic parcel and its actual two-cell
    doorstep blocks as fixed access with interior terminals (20.10). Yard cords
    may move around the house to admit boundary-flush cover rectangles. There
    is no blanket perimeter collar or frozen standing-block route tree.
    Boundary rectangles are this hut implementation's selection policy, not an
    SDK restriction: each builder may also choose interior space to relinquish
    (17.2.10). This milestone does not foreclose an interior handoff policy.
    Corey accepted the measured retained-yard shares under the current
    geometry/access policy, with no percentage floor (17.2.10, 2026-10-29).
    Candidate scales are half the bounding extent, one third, and three cells,
    interleaving corner boxes and centered edge slabs. Up to 36 complete owned
    rectangles survive static house, doorstep and original-portal owned seam
    exclusions; rejected static candidates do not spend that cap. Each has at
    least a 3-by-3 pocket. Already transferred cells are excluded during probing.
    This caps probe count, not work per probe: partition and routing cost still
    grow with geometry and accepted batch size. Greedy fixed-order acceptance
    skips overlaps and can prevent a different set of smaller boxes; it does
    not optimize transferred area or compare alternate orders. Concave lobes without a complete
    candidate rectangle can remain intentionally open.
    Keys are `yard:<x>,<y>,<w>,<h>`; legacy reason `unused-yard-lobe` now
    denotes authored peripheral rectangles, rather than raw residual components.
    Cumulative probes check exact cover receiving feasibility and retained
    hunter routes, then publish one verified batch. Original portals stay with
    the hut. Refused candidates stay retained; an optional tracker/search miss
    leaves the independently sited house and original yard intact. Cover's
    exact-interface preview cache remains scoped to one invocation.
  - **Why that keeps the promise:** fixed doorstep approaches, the house envelope
    and original portals survive every accepted partition; movable continuous
    cords connect those terminals and every new crossing after margin inheritance.
    Final retained, child and containing geometry are independently checked in
    thorough mode. Core counts stay with the hut; incapable huts still take the
    terminal empty path. The measured adoption and its cost are in
    [42.3](42.3-generation-and-delegation-evidence.md#hut-cord-adoption-647).
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of the walls and the door, indoors or out. Retained hut
    loot rolls in canonical cell order and is clipped to retained ownership;
    delegated cover uses its existing
    loot behavior.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
- **Shape needs:** *proposed*, a contained 4 × 4 for the one-space house; the
  larger designs use their listed box sizes. The region needs no extra extent
  margin around the house.
- **Formed by:** the fill's hut quarters, and as a child of `block` (52).
- **Parameters:** none yet. Locked doors are deferred (M23).
- **Material:** the game's room shell in `builders.ts` (real windows, a door
  sized by the profile, optional shelves), and mapgen's `compound`.

### `depot`

- **Role:** industrial ground: warehouses and container aisles. Long
  sightlines down the aisles, hard corners at their ends.
- **Strategy:** no decomposer at first. A builder places warehouses on
  contained rectangles and container rows between them, aisles at least a
  door wide.
  **Built (#159, composition #533):** `map/micro/strategies/depot.ts` targets
  `density / 2` of reduced fill as warehouse envelopes (about 27.5% by default),
  subject to siting. Every piece has a complete two-cell exterior access ring
  and two-cell inter-piece spacing; warehouse doorsteps stay protected through
  final infill. Hints cannot override bypass clearance. Container yards remain
  a separate seeded pass; density zero and undersized fallbacks stay empty.
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#depot-builder).
  - **Residual crates (#570):** short unaddressed lane remnants can receive
    one-cell crates at the parent density. The two-cell aisles, portal routes
    and feasible row spans deliberately skipped by density stay clear. This
    direct canvas pass preserves warehouses and rows and excludes new crate
    geometry from loot sites; density zero stays empty.
  - **Historical timing:** The tree costs about 0.1 s on a 54-portal,
    6,480-cell depot, so depot builds it at every portal count (42).
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of every wall, door, shelf and container, indoors or
    out.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
  - **Too small:** a region with no contained 10 × 10 goes to `open` (17
    M24).
- **Shape needs:** *proposed*, at least 10 × 10.
- **Formed by:** enormous and small set pieces, where the old library's `industrial` sheds and yards were (52).
- **Parameters:** `density`, from 0 to 1 (0.55 by default), and `roomCells`,
  whole cells from 6 to 16 (8 by default).
- **Material:** the game's `example-depot`.

### `plant`

- **Role:** the half of the old `industrial` that isn't a warehouse. Big
  machinery blocks and pipe runs on a factory floor: hard cover, long lines
  between machines, and a few tight corners.
- **Strategy:** no decomposer. Machinery on a coarse grid, joined by low pipe
  runs, with an optional roofed shed.
  **Built (#219):** `map/micro/strategies/plant.ts`. Like `depot`, it keeps
  its promise by spacing.
  - **Residual bands (#577):** crates can fill unused machinery-band cells
    after machines, pipes and the shed. Skipped density slots, shed slots,
    lattice aisles and pipe gaps stay clear. A full two-cell access ring and
    piece spacing leave a local hunter bypass; primary geometry and shed
    traces remain unchanged (20.3.4).
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#plant-builder).
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of every piece, indoors or out.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
  - **Too small:** a region with no contained 10 × 10 goes to `open` (17
    M24).
- **Shape needs:** *proposed*, at least 10 × 10, as `depot`.
- **Formed by:** enormous and small set pieces, where the old library's
  `industrial` pieces were.

### `compound`

- **Role:** rooms in a ring around a walled court, with two or three gates.
  A pocket that rewards a contestant who knows its gates and traps one who
  doesn't. It is where dead ends live.
- **Strategy:** a builder puts the ring on the region's outline and the court
  inside, and places gates so every portal reaches the court. A region with a
  hole suits it whole, as 19's ring example does.
  **Built (#160), its ring one building design since L5 (#192, 56):**
  `map/micro/strategies/compound.ts`. No decomposer. It
  stands one compound in a contained box. The box can touch hinted perimeter
  while portal approaches and usable gate doorsteps stay clear.
  - **Builder mechanics:** [20.3.4](20.3.4-structure-builders.md#compound-builder).
  - **Courtyard composition (#532):** one keep and up to four cover pieces can
    occupy the court before exterior-yard delegation. Two-cell perimeter and
    inter-footprint bypasses preserve ring-door approaches and circulation;
    the keep's doorstep and a central standing block stay clear. The bounded
    HOUSE/cover grammar and small-court refusal policy are in 20.3.4.
  - **Exterior outbuilding (#532):** eligible compounds may add one largest-fit
    HOUSE before cover delegation, with a full two-cell collar and connected
    actual gates/doorstep. The shared yard transaction retains and populates
    both boxes. No-fit compounds keep their existing output. See 20.3.4.
  - **Loot:** each cell rolls its chance and takes its tier, wherever a loot
    disc stands clear of every wall and door, in the rooms, the court or the
    retained yard. Delegated cover uses its existing loot behavior.
  - **Cord-aware exterior-yard delegation (#647, `types-50`):** register the
    unchanged ring/court box as one pessimistic parcel. Actual two-cell gate
    approaches stay fixed; movable continuous cords replace blanket perimeter
    collars and frozen routes. Original portals remain retained.
    Select up to eight largest contained rectangles from actual owned yard
    minus the ring, gate stands and original portal seam cells. Discovery
    removes each rectangle before finding the next. Each root tries its full
    hunk, one-cell insets on both/per-axis sides, then 75%/50% side slabs.
    Alternatives can touch an actual ownership edge or lie inside the yard;
    the court is never offered. Deduplicate and require a 3-by-3 fill pocket
    before counting at most 48 eligible offers. Exact inherited cover previews
    decide receiving feasibility. The insets are candidate alternatives,
    not fixed circulation reservations. Greedy discovery and cumulative
    acceptance do not maximize area or optimize order; refused cells stay owned.
    Compound's optional handoffs stop above 128 original portals, before
    candidate/tracker work; the independently sited ring and yard remain intact.
    This remains a provisional adopter guard, not a shared SDK limit. The
    fixed-site #661 initialization witness succeeds through 140 portals and
    exhausts the segment budget at 150–170; it does not measure complete
    handoff execution above 128. Evidence and the keep/defer decision are in 42.3.
    Inherited non-fill strips may be included when the coordinator proves
    retained access after new margins; final owners and whole geometry receive
    independent thorough checks. Narrow remnants stay intentionally open.
    Keys are `yard:x,y,w,h`, tagged `unused-exterior-yard`; snapshots retain
    full footprints. Shared transaction/recovery is in
    [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md), coordination in
    [20.10](20.10-portal-cords.md), measured comparisons in 42.3.
  - **Core elements:** it sites none. Any its brief lists are left for the
    report.
  - **Too small:** a region with no usable contained box of at least 10 × 10
    or no ring with accessible gates takes terminal empty (20.7), preserving
    original ownership and requests without geometry or loot.
    Large original ownership does not imply usable fill: internal guaranteed
    margins can prevent every minimum ring box. The exact #340 diagnosis and
    reproduction and the later `military-quarter` content correction are recorded
    in [42](42-performance-history.md). The original constrained brief still falls
    back; [52](52-map-primitives-and-library.md) supplies suitable authored joins
    for the corrected blueprint.
- **Shape needs:** *proposed*, a compound of at least 10 × 10, with a court of
  at least 4 × 4, plus usable portal approaches and gate doorsteps.
- **Formed by:** medium set pieces, where the old library's walled `military` bases were (52).
- **Parameters:** `gates`, whole numbers from 1 to 4 (2 by default), one per
  side.
- **Material:** the game's `example-courtyard`, and mapgen's `courtyard` and
  `compound`.
