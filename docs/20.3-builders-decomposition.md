# 20.3. Builders and decomposition

Production canvas adoption (#430) starts with `cover`: each accepted cover piece
commits its whole assembly and template footprint through `createBuilderCanvas`
([20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#production-canvas)). Existing lattice, cluster-box,
boundary, route and loot decisions remain in the strategy. Further strategy
migrations are separate behavior-preserving steps; `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` and
cover's executor version remain unchanged for this output-preserving extraction.

Depot is the next adopter: each warehouse and container row commits as a whole
assembly, with its template box recording addressed intent. Its existing lane,
aisle, hint, route-tree, design-observer and loot decisions stay in the strategy.
A refusal after siting is an invariant failure, rather than another placement
fallback. This migration also preserves output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION`.

Hut, compound, park, checkpoint, hall, ruins and rubble follow depot (#465): siting
accepts a piece, then it commits, and a refusal is an invariant failure. A park clump and
a ruins room each commit as one assembly. Checkpoint commits a piece once its route proof
holds. Hall, ruins and rubble keep legacy world-absolute records, whose template boxes
are not their intent, so they pass their pieces' shapes as areas. A hall and its hint walls
commit as one assembly whose intent is the pillars and the walls rather than the
rectangle, because walls may stand between pillars or under a roof with none; a hall
with neither intends its rectangle. A ruins room's intent is its spans rather than
its box. `commitHintWalls` commits a builder's hint walls as one assembly. Walls meet at
corners, and ruins spans meet in a room's corners, so `separateAreas` stops each
rectangle's area short of an earlier one. Output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` are unchanged.

Plant is not yet migrated. Its pipe runs reach into the machines they join, which a
commit refuses as a collision with an earlier assembly; #490 rebuilds them to end on the
machine surface, after shape subtraction (#488) and contact between assemblies (#489).
Cover's post-filters commit through a fresh canvas with cover's protections (#491).
`coverAround` commits each piece it keeps as its own assembly, as `buildCover` did, and
`coverNear` commits the pieces it is given, then its guard. `coverAround`'s route check
stays in the filter, and a refusal is an invariant failure.

Siting then moved onto the canvas's queries (#460, [20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#production-canvas)).
Depot's `taken` grid, `buildCover`'s footprint grid and `coverNear`'s `AISLE` grid gave way
to `fits` and `spaced`, as did checkpoint's barrier spacing for its post. Park, hall, ruins and
rubble test candidates with `fits` instead of the fill mask and their protections. Cover's
loot standing room asks `spaced` too. Lattice slots, cluster boxes, hints, route proofs and
boundary spacing stay in the strategies. Output and `REGION_TYPES_VERSION` are unchanged.
Plant adopts the queries when it moves onto the canvas (#490).

## Builders and composition

`builders.ts` shares a parameterized room shell with actual windows, an unlocked
door sized by the active profile and optional shelves. Open ground proposes sparse cover/shelters;
depots propose warehouses and container aisles; courtyards propose a room ring
around clear ground; ruins propose discontinuous walls and rotated convex debris.
Placement counts and tests guard against a builder silently producing no content.

`entry` is deliberately small: after region geometry resolves, it calls the
reusable `spreadPoints` primitive. It samples a bounded half-cell lattice, keeps
only circles fully contained and clear of blockers/reservations, then selects
farthest points with non-overlap at the active contestant clearance. With a
required entrance it also checks each accepted point is reachable from that root.
The root is a reachability anchor, so only blockers can invalidate it. Macro
reservations are passable: entry points stay off them, but one beside the entrance
does not break placement. Spawning is the entry region's guarantee and loot is not,
so entry points are placed before the loot fill and see only geometry and macro
reservations. Loot then takes the leftover room and stays off every entry point; a
small entry region can hold its full spawn count and no loot. The loot budget never
moves an entry point (#21). Loot that blocks movement, such as a
container, would be a standard element placed through the builder and obey the
element rules, including the protected entrance approach.
It reports a shortfall when this sampled placement cannot meet the requested
count. That does not prove maximum packing capacity. It is a local spacing
result, not a proof that a complete match can support that many players.
Entry placement uses the region seed to break ties between equally scored
farthest-point candidates. It preserves the spacing objective and can legitimately
remain unchanged where the best positions are unique. Omitting the optional seed
on the reusable `spreadPoints` primitive retains canonical tie ordering.
Ruin decay now changes intact spans into smaller rotated rubble and adds scatter;
it is not a parameter of the other builders.

`map/micro/sdk.ts` exports the pure reusable layer: region-mask and shape
queries, collision/route helpers, `spreadPoints`, and `microMetrics`, with their
types. It also exports decomposition analysis, immutable region contexts,
candidate allocation, interface discovery, and independent plan validation.
Perimeter helpers include `hintedCells`, `boxesAgainstHints`, `boundaryBoxFilter`,
`portalMargin`, `hintStrength`, `hintsOfStrength`, `clipHints` and `wallHints`; builders use them as
soft placement guidance and to keep portal approaches clear.
These are mechanics: they describe cells and structural facts, but do not select
the caller's content strategy. It imports neither the builder catalogue nor live
game state, assigns no gameplay IDs, and does not choose a macro plan. It is a source-level
SDK for this project, not a separately published package.

## Design guidelines for builders (decided 2026-10-05/08)

[20.9](20.9-builder-structure.md) describes the usual order of a builder's
work and the SDK piece for each step. These guidelines are what the work
should look like.

None of these is checked or enforced; builders keep the portal promise as their
only obligation (20.1). They are art direction that a builder should usually
follow:
- **Fill the region.** Use as much of the space as the builder's style allows,
  rather than a thin scatter in a large region (#242). Diagnosis is a
  builder-test tool that counts large empty rectangles (3 x N and larger), not a
  rule applied while building (#288). For planning while building, a builder
  can keep an addressed-area record of what it has handled with intent,
  including ground left empty on purpose
  ([20.2](20.2-geometry-access.md#addressed-area)). A builder that cannot fill what it was
  given may eventually return the residue as sub-regions for decomposition to
  fill (#289; D1-D10 design direction accepted in
  [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md), answers in
  [17.2.9](17.2.9-delegation-questions.md); preparation and execution are
  implemented, and the hut-to-cover authorship pilot is complete in #330).
- **Use the hinted perimeter.** Place geometry on or beside perimeter segments
  hinted not passable (52, "Segment prescriptions"; #243), while leaving
  passable portal approaches clear. The brief carries optional `hints` (20.1),
  and `map/micro/hints.ts`, exported through `sdk.ts`, offers cells and boxes
  against those runs plus `portalMargin`. A builder may prefer mutual hints, which
  both sides state, by handing the helpers `hintsOfStrength(brief, 'mutual')` first
  (20.1, #406). These remain soft guidance: no builder
  is required to fill a hint or reject an otherwise valid placement because it
  lacks one. Current adoption covers `cover`, `depot`, `plant`, `park`,
  `checkpoint`, `hut`, `compound`, and `block`.

  A builder with nothing more particular planned for a hinted stretch can
  call `wallHints` (`map/micro/hint-walls.ts`, #407) after its own pieces. It
  stands plain `wall-run` placeables (20.8) in the cells beside every hint,
  against the line, except where a cell is outside the fill area (so portal
  margins stay clear), is addressed (20.2), or where its wall would overlap
  a shape the builder passes in `avoid`: its protected routes and its own
  pieces. What is left is walled in maximal stretches of `minLength` cells or
  more, and walls at a convex corner share its cell. It is opt-in: `hall`,
  `ruins` and `rubble` call it on every hint (Corey, #407), and `open` stays
  empty.

  `cover`, `depot`, `plant`, and `park` respect the automatic clear margins
  (20.6). With at most 32 portals, they also protect sampled hunter routes
  and portal approach strips; `depot` connects nearby portals
  into a deterministic spanning tree rather than reserving every first-portal
  diagonal. It retains the full portal approach strips and swept hunter
  clearance. This changes the reserved route layout, not the connectivity
  promise (#325). `depot` builds that tree at every portal count (#347): the
  boundary rule below let its warehouses seal portal approaches. `park` keeps
  its first-portal routes up to 32 portals and builds the nearby tree past
  that, for the same reason (#361). With more portals, `cover` and `plant` use
  a bounded conservative boundary rule; a #361 audit found it sealed no portal
  of theirs ([42](42-performance-history.md)). In that large-mask mode, a hinted
  attachment may touch one side or two adjacent sides while retaining the
  two-cell owned ring elsewhere; opposite-side attachments remain disallowed.
  This conservative mode does not prove every non-portal pocket remains
  connected. `checkpoint` validates the empty shape, then prunes trials against
  cached swept routes, searching again only where a trial touches its evidence.
  It shortens barrier end margins and prefers hinted post boxes. `hut` and
  `compound` use cell-level yard access checks. `block`
  clips parent hints to the lot or alley that owns each hinted boundary segment.

  `boundaryBoxFilter` caches the shared spacing check. In rectangles a box
  leaves a hunter aisle on at least one side of each axis. In concave masks it
  requires an owned ring except on fully hinted attachment sides. It does not
  check portal approaches, piece spacing or final reachability; the caller
  keeps those separately. A two-cell geometric approach at a portal is needed
  by the current hunter-standing check even though the authored empty margin
  is one cell. The validator still samples the whole portal (20.1).

## Child-region decomposition

`createRegionContext` accepts one connected four-neighbor polyomino and freezes a
canonical copy of its cells, reservations, required cells, forbidden cells and
entrance annotations. Analysis is cached by normalized footprint, and `child()`
creates a constrained child context by restricting those annotations. This keeps
context mechanics immutable while callers retain strategy intent.

`analyzeRegion` reports graph and cell facts: connected components, holes and
boundary ownership, articulation cells, maximal contained rectangles, four-neighbor
depth, local straight-run width, monotonicity and rectangularity. Depth and local
width are grid measurements only: neither is a true body-clearance measurement.
Candidate helpers also grow a bounded predicate/cost-controlled region and propose
straight, bounded-width neck cuts. They do not construct a skeleton or delete a
cut from the final ownership plan. Holes are legitimate topology, so a candidate
is not convexified or split into rectangles merely to make it easier to assign.

The caller supplies candidate footprints and generator contracts. Allocation
assigns non-overlapping candidates to compatible generators, rather than treating
geometric convexification as the decomposition objective. A contract uses roles,
tags, area bounds, hole policy and optional rectangularity as hard feasibility;
its `utility` returns named soft score components. `requiredTags` are enforced,
whereas `preferredGenerators` are only candidate metadata. Reserved, assigned and
forbidden cells have separate ownership roles. The remaining connected components
are first-class residual regions, scored for unused area, fragmentation, small
remainders, seams and piece count according to caller policy.

The allocator uses deterministic bounded beam/fork search over bitset claims. It
reports considered/truncated candidates, expansions, budget exhaustion and
per-candidate rejection or non-selection reasons. A retained result is explicitly
non-optimal: a feasible alternative may be absent because of overlap, utility or
beam pruning. The validator independently recomputes ownership, residuals, cuts,
interfaces and score accounting; an optional generator registry additionally
rechecks contract feasibility. It does not certify a physical route, doorway or
optimal allocation.

Shared boundaries are coalesced into straight interface runs. Runs at least the
configured width are portal opportunities for later negotiation, never physical
doors or traversability guarantees. Negotiation and execution of a flat plan are
described under [Dispatch and portal negotiation](20.4-hierarchical-generation.md#dispatch-and-portal-negotiation); recursive execution
remains unimplemented ([20.5](20.5-tools-and-next-boundaries.md#next-boundaries)).

The concrete example in `decomposition/example.ts` supplies the policy and four
consumer contracts. On the 252-cell neck example, defaults select two 120-cell
room footprints and a 12-cell connector grown from a neck cut. Their two shared
boundaries each have one three-cell straight run. The 252-cell ring example instead
selects the intact courtyard footprint, preserving its hole. The same allocator
can prefer less coverage when the caller increases residual-fragmentation cost;
this tradeoff has a dedicated test. None of these choices is hard-coded into the
allocator or asserted globally optimal.

Contexts are bounded to 4,096 cells and 64 cells per axis. Like the production
region path (20.2), analysis no longer limits distance from the origin (#330);
coordinates must be safe integers with room for its bounded scan offsets.
Analysis retains at
most 256 maximal rectangles. Allocation evaluates at most 128 candidates against
16 contracts, retains up to 32 beam states, assigns at most 16 pieces, and stops
after 20,000 expansions. The example uses smaller defaults. Cuts are full straight
cross-sections, not a medial-axis or graph-min-cut implementation. Entrance records
are preserved annotations; physical access obligations still need downstream
negotiation and collision checks. Reserved cells remain reserved in a child view;
later-stage reservation consumption and automatic recursive dispatch are not yet
provided.

`composeRegions(results)` joins a whole map's brief results, up to 4,096 of them, in
one cell coordinate system (51 stage 7, C2). It refuses a cell with two owners,
duplicate ids, mixed cell sizes, and any portal that isn't on its region's perimeter,
faces cells no region owns, straddles two regions, or isn't named alike, with the same
run, in the brief on its other side, and a portal id that names two pairs. It returns a `BuiltMap` (the kernel's type):
regions in id order and each shared portal's pair once, the same whatever order the
results came in. It checks briefs and ownership only, never geometry: a builder's
promise is its own to check (51 principle 9). `stampBuiltMap` and `builtMapCollision`
emit its geometry through the same adapter path as one region, each region's under its
id as its node. Core element sites stay in the map for the report (51 stage 8).
`builtMapCollision` is bounded by the regions' cells (`builtPlayableArea`), the same
bounds `map/live.ts` gives a live match (26). Before #204 it had none, so movement
fell back to the old diamond, and the edges of a chain map, where its spawns are,
counted as outside the map.

`adapter.ts` stamps a result's assemblies through `placeElement`. It retains roof
ownership and unlocked doors and exposes only the result's loot list as generation
spots. The labs' previews use `builtMapCollision`, the same adapter, for real
movement and collision queries. It does not start a multiplayer match or alter the default
generator, global RNG order, fixture, snapshot schema or recording version.
The caller owns placement within the arena's world boundary and collision checks
against content outside the supplied regions; local validation cannot establish those.

## Catalogue builder mechanics

Builders put down shared placeables, such as `warehouse`, `house` and
`cover-piece`, where a piece is reused. Each placeable owns what it is and the area
it demands; the builder decides where it goes ([20.8](20.8-placeables.md)).

The type roles, status, shape needs, fallback, content and access obligations remain in [54](54-region-types.md). Delegation execution and recovery are owned by [20.7](20.7-builder-delegation.md).

### `cover` builder

- **Slots:** they lie on a lattice one tile (6 cells) apart. Each slot is
  taken with chance `density`, by a cluster of one to three crates,
  barriers and containers inside a 4 × 4 box.
- **The aisle:** a cluster's bounding box keeps 2 clear cells from the next
  cluster. Its footprint must lie in the reduced fill, off every clear margin (20.6).
  Hinted boundary sites may use a perimeter cell instead of the old blanket
  edge ring.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the clear margins keep every portal's
  approach, and masks with up to 32 portals also protect a sampled hunter
  corridor while placing pieces. Above
  32 portals, a conservative boundary rule allows attachment along one
  hinted side or two adjacent hinted sides, but retains a 2-cell owned ring
  elsewhere and forbids attachment across opposite sides. This bounded mode
  is a spacing argument, not a proof that every non-portal pocket remains
  connected; `validatePortalReach` remains the independent promise check.
  The bounded #361 audit and its contrasting park/depot findings remain in [54](54-region-types.md#cover).

### `ruins` builder

- **Rooms:** slots lie on a lattice 6 cells apart, at a seeded phase. A slot
  is taken with chance `density` by a roofless room 3 or 4 cells a side,
  wholly on owned cells, so at least a doorway's width (52) parts two rooms.
- **Walls:** a quarter cell thick, as `hut`'s are, laid a cell at a time
  along the inside of the room's box. Each span falls with chance `decay`;
  half of the fallen lie as a rotated slab inside their cell, and the rest
  are gone.
- **The doorway:** two cells wide, on a side whose cells beyond are owned
  two deep, so no room is sealed off. A doorway at a corner takes the
  meeting wall's end span too, to stay two cells wide.

### `hall` builder

- **The rectangle:** the largest rectangle in the reduced fill (20.6) with
  both sides at least 6 cells. Ties go to the first in row order.
- **Pillars:** each stands a quarter cell inside a 2 × 2 block of cells, so
  it is 1½ cells square. Blocks lie `spacing` cells apart, centred across
  the rectangle, and the aisle between two pillars is `spacing` less 1½
  cells.
- **The stagger:** each line of blocks is offset from the last along one
  axis, drawn from the seed, by as near half a period as whole cells allow
  while sharing no factor with it: 5 cells at spacing 6, 7 at 10. So the
  lines visit every phase within a period's worth of them, and a sightline
  down the other axis meets a pillar. A stride sharing a factor would visit
  only some phases and leave a lane open the whole way (PR #176 review).
  That axis keeps clean lanes. Staggering both would
  pinch pillar corners together.
- **The roof:** with `roofed`, the rectangle is one enclosing element, so a
  roof hides what's under it as a building's does.

### `checkpoint` builder

- **The line:** from one portal's centre to another's, the farthest pair
  (the first in id order on a tie).
- **Barriers:** along the line's longer axis, starting 1 cell in plus a
  seeded offset of 0 to 2, then every 4 cells. Each is a half-cell wall of
  kind `ruin-wall`, so it blocks movement and sight. It reaches 4 cells
  either side of the line, or as far as the region goes, attaches to the
  region's edge on one side and stops 2 cells (a doorway) short of the
  other. The sides alternate along the line, from a seeded first side, so a
  body crossing slaloms. Where the region is wider than the barrier a body
  can go round it, which slows a crossing without sealing it. A station
  needs a run of at least 3 owned cells across the line, or it gets no
  barrier.
- **The post:** a roofed box 6 to 8 by 5 or 6 cells, its long side along the
  line, built as `depot`'s warehouse is (56): a roofed design with shelves
  on its floor, traced for the lab. It stands nearest the middle of the
  barriers, off the line. Its footprint must be owned and cannot occupy the
  one-cell portal margin; it may sit flush against a hinted perimeter run.
  Hinted boxes are tried before distance ordering. It keeps 2 cells clear of
  barriers. With one portal it ranks from a point 5 cells in from that portal,
  and with none from the region's middle.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the empty shape is checked once with the
  elective flood check (`validatePortalReach`, 20.1), and a region whose own
  shape fails is `open`. Swept routes connect nearby portals into a tree and
  join each portal's stands. A trial piece cannot block a threshold crossing;
  paths it cannot touch are reused, and affected paths are searched again.
  Barriers and posts are kept only while this evidence holds. Post sites are
  tried hinted first then nearest, up to 6. Failed pieces are dropped, with
  the previously accepted evidence retained. The independent final-geometry
  validator is unchanged and still checks the whole portal in tests.

### `park` builder

- **Slots:** a lattice 6 cells apart at a seeded phase. A slot's pieces stay
  in its first 4 cells, so 2 are aisle.
- **Hedges:** with chance `hedges` (default 0.2) a slot starts a hedge run,
  across or down into the next slot, 10 cells long, in a seeded row of the
  slot's band. Its two stubs are 0.4 cell thick, of kind `hedge`, which
  blocks movement and hands but not sight or shots, as `pipe` does
  (17.2.8 M34, `seeThrough` in `shared/movement.ts`). The gap is 2 cells (a
  doorway) at a seeded place that leaves at least a cell of hedge each side.
- **Clumps:** each other slot holds a clump with chance `density` (default
  0.6): 1 to 3 trees, each a circle of kind `tree` 1.5 cells across in a
  2-cell footprint, at seeded places in the slot's band that don't overlap.
  A tree blocks bodies and sight, so a clump is soft cover, not a wall.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** clumps and hedge runs keep 2 cells from
  other pieces and avoid portal approaches, and no piece touches a protected
  hunter route. Up to 32 portals, a route joins the first portal to each
  other one. Past that, `depot`'s nearby-portal tree joins them instead,
  since a route search per portal from the first costs up to 2 s on the
  largest parks. Park once used `cover`'s boundary rule there, which let a
  clump stand flush over a side portal's approach and seal it (#361, [42](42-performance-history.md)).
  Hints guide the slot phase and tree sites; they never require an obstacle.

### `hut` builder

- **The designs:** it tries a house of three spaces, then two, then one.
  If no house has a reachable doorstep, it takes the terminal empty path
  (20.7). Three spaces take a 6 × 6, 8 × 6 or 9 × 4
  box, two a 6 × 4 or 7 × 5, either way round, and one a 4 × 4.
  With automatic margins (20.6), compact single-room boxes also try
  4 by 3, 3 by 4 and 3 by 3 cells; walls and door widths stay the same.
- **The box:** each design tries its sizes, then contained boxes in a seeded
  order, with `boxesAgainstHints` candidates first. There is no extra
  two-cell extent prefilter: a house can fit flush with the region boundary
  when its footprint and doorstep fit. Candidates lie in the reduced fill,
  off every portal's clear margin (20.6), where a hunter stands straddling
  the seam. A bounded cell-level
  flood check keeps the yard's portal-connected standing blocks joined
  and requires a doorstep reachable from a portal. When the box has a
  2-cell owned ring, the yard connectivity follows by spacing; otherwise the
  flood check decides it directly.
- **The house:** it fills its box with quarter-cell walls and a roof. It has
  one outside door, a doorway wide, on the doorstep's side. The one-space
  house centres it and faces it with a window as wide. A larger house's
  spaces are allocated in the box (56 L5), at least 3 × 3 each, joined by
  interior doors, with windows where they fit. Its door and the doors
  joining every space must be placed, or the side isn't used. With one
  outside door, no route runs through the house, and every space is reached
  through it.

### `depot` builder

- **Lanes:** everything lines up on lanes 3 cells apart along one axis,
  drawn from the seed, at a seeded phase.
- **Warehouses:** `roomCells` long and three quarters of that across, at
  least 5, with quarter-cell walls and a roof. They stand at seeded places
  on the lanes until about `density` of a quarter of the ground is taken.
  **Designs (#218, 17.2.8 M34):** each box is allocated as a building
  design (56), trying in a seeded order a floor with an office and a store,
  or a floor with an office. The floor takes at least half the box and asks
  for a door on each end. The office asks for a door into the floor, a door
  out on the far end, where it can front the warehouse, and a window. The
  store asks for a door into the floor. A design is kept only if at least
  two doors lead outside, so the warehouse stays a way through, and every
  space is reached through doors. A box neither fits, such as
  `roomCells` 6, is a one-room design with a doorway-wide door centred on
  each end. Every warehouse is traced for the lab, and the trace names the
  designs passed over and why.
  Shelf islands a cell across run down the floor, keeping 2 cells from its
  walls and from each other. The interior is guidance and adds nothing to
  the portal promise (M29).
- **Container rows:** two or three 2-cell containers end to end, packed
  along each lane around the warehouses. Each place that fits one is taken
  with chance `density`, and the next starts 2 cells on, so the aisles run
  long and end in hard corners.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** pieces keep 2 cells from each other and
  stay out of portal approaches. Hinted warehouse boxes are tried first.
  Rectangular masks and concave masks use the same boundary-spacing and
  corridor rules as `cover` above, rather than a blanket edge ring.
  Routes join nearby portals in a deterministic spanning tree (#325) at any
  portal count, using Euclidean distance between hunter stands, not
  routed path length. Nearby stands can require a long detour in a concave
  mask, so this choice does not guarantee minimum total protected route length.
  The former first-portal star reserved diagonals
  throughout some large depots and rejected every warehouse footprint.
  The tree still joins every portal and protects the full approach strips,
  while leaving more contiguous space for warehouses and container rows.
  Depot once skipped the tree past 32 portals and relied on `cover`'s
  boundary rule. That let a warehouse stand flush over a portal's approach
  and seal it, once large depots had room for warehouses (#347). The bounded
  timing remains in [54](54-region-types.md#depot).

### `plant` builder

- **Slots:** a lattice 8 cells apart at a seeded phase. A slot's pieces stay
  in its first 6 cells, so 2 cells of aisle run between slots.
- **Shed:** with chance `shed` (default 0.5), a block of two by one, one by
  two or two by two slots, at the first block in a seeded order whose box
  stays owned and clear of portal approaches and protected routes. It is built as `depot`'s warehouse is: a
  roofed building design (56) with shelves on its floor, traced for the lab.
- **Machines:** every other slot holds one with chance `density` (default
  0.6): a block 2 to 4 cells a side at a seeded place in the slot, half of
  them with chamfered corners. Hints guide slot phase and box positions;
  candidates that fail the boundary or route clearance checks are left out.
- **Pipe runs:** neighbouring machines across or down are joined, with
  chance 0.7, along a row both face, when at least 3 cells part them. The
  game is 2D, so a pipe blocks movement but not sight or shots, as a window
  does (obstacle kind `pipe`, 29; 17 M34). Each run leaves one gap a door
  wide, 2 cells, at a seeded place, which both roles use. A gap only a
  contestant fits waits for the squeeze balance (P-04). Gantries wait for
  collision planes (16).
- **Why that keeps the promise:** every machine's and the shed's box keeps
  2 clear cells from other pieces, and so does every run's but for the two
  machines it joins. Portal approaches and concave-mask corridors or
  conservative boundary spacing are checked as `cover` does (20.3). A run
  stays in its machines' band and crosses only the aisle between them, so
  runs never meet. Machines and runs can wall ground in, but every run has
  its door-wide gap, so no ground is shut off. So a `plant` region keeps
  the portal promise exactly when its shape does.

### `compound` builder

- **The box:** sizes start at the largest contained rectangle, capped at
  24 × 24, then shrink by two cells per axis down to 10 × 10. At each size
  up to 16 hinted boxes precede a centered site and seeded alternatives.
  Small-yard alternatives are capped at 64; large fields try 12. Candidates
  must keep the yard's hunter standing blocks connected and have enough
  gate sides reachable from that yard. Gate feasibility uses the actual
  centered outer door stands, including both whole-cell neighbors of a
  half-cell center, rather than an arbitrary spot in a widened passage.
  There is no three-cell inset.
- **The ring:** roofed rooms 3 cells deep along the inside of the box, with
  quarter-cell walls. The box's edge is their back wall, so the compound's
  outer wall is the rooms'. Each side's middle, between the corners, is
  split into rooms 3 to 5 cells long, as evenly as whole cells allow, each
  with a door a doorway wide onto the court. The rooms and passages are the
  spaces of one design over the ring's cells, so two rooms share one wall,
  and each room is its own element with its own roof.
- **Dead ends:** a 3 × 3 room stands at each corner. It opens only into its
  neighbour along the north or south side, a room or a gate's passage.
- **Gates:** `gates` sides, drawn from the seed, each have a passage 2
  cells wide through the ring at a seeded place, from a door in the outer
  wall to the court. The passage widens to the corner where what's left
  would be too short for a room. A passage keeps its whole width: the walls
  it shares stand in the rooms beside it. It is open to the court along its
  length. The rest of the ring is closed.
- **The court:** the open ground inside the ring, at least 4 × 4. It holds
  nothing but loot.
- **Doors and passages:** every door is centred in its wall, and each
  passage is a doorway wide (52), so a hunter passes through them.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the builder floods the hunter's 2 × 2
  standing blocks in the yard around each candidate. Portal approaches
  must remain connected, and each chosen gate needs a reachable doorstep.
  Its existing ring design then connects every room to the court and gates.

### `block` builder

- **The split:** the region's bounding box is split, guillotine fashion,
  across its longer side at a seeded place. Each split leaves an alley 3
  cells wide between the parts, and splitting stops once no part is longer
  than 24 cells.
- **Lots:** each part tries rectangles at least 6 cells a side that may
  attach to one or two adjacent boundary sides. Hinted attachments take
  preference, then larger area. Other sides keep two owned cells for
  circulation; opposite-side attachments are refused. The three-cell
  splits between lots remain unchanged. A part with no viable rectangle
  stays alley.
- **Children:** each lot is a `hut` (no side over 12), `depot` (sides at
  least 10), `compound` (at least 16), `ruins`, or `cover` (at least 9)
  region. Its type is drawn from those its size suits, and it gets its own
  seed. The alleys, everything left, are one `open` region. Each child's
  zones are the block's, cut to its cells. Parent hints are clipped to the
  child owning each hinted boundary segment. `planBlock` returns the child
  briefs, and the block's result gathers the children's geometry, loot and
  manifest counts. Each element's label is prefixed with its lot.
- **Frontages:** each lot has two portals onto the alleys, the two whole
  sides at a seeded corner with two owned cells beyond both sides. Two,
  not one, so the lot is a passage its
  strategy must keep joined, rather than a pocket that owes nothing. A `hut`
  still fits in the far corner of the smallest lot. The alleys hold every
  frontage and every one of the block's own portals.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** a convex lot can make a notch at an edge
  or corner, but cannot bridge opposite boundary sides. Its other sides
  keep a hunter-wide path around it. Lots stay separated by three-cell
  splits; parent portal approaches stay in the alleys. Each lot keeps its
  own promise between frontages that face owned alley cells.

### `departure` builder

Exits are spread farthest-first, so they sit as
far apart as the region allows, and may share one corridor (17 M13's
assumption, keep the tip). Hunters' spawns are spread over the ground at
least the set-back from every exit. Cover and loot fill around both, as in
`arrival`. What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

### `charging` builder

The charger stands as near the region's middle as a
contestant fits and can walk to from the first portal. Cover and loot keep
off the standing room around it. Then, if no cover already stands within 2
cells of that room (*proposed*, a doorway's width), one container is added
there, keeping cover's aisles, where the region has room. A pad too small
for that, like the fixture's 2 × 2, takes its cover from its neighbours.
What doesn't fit is a shortfall for the report.

### `transit` builder

`map/micro/strategies/transit.ts` uses hunter clearance and first-portal access,
then keeps two cells of standing space free of cover and loot. Nearby cover
uses the existing cover strategy. Insufficient space leaves a report shortfall.
