16. What discovery learns by window and what by sight (2026-10-14, #230 / PR #354). #275 left open whether "discovered" means lit cells only or everything within a radius or viewport; #274 allowed either.
    - Corey: "I think there definitely needs to be a split between what is learned from being within a radius / rect window, and what requires line of sight. And so pretty much world geometry is rect window, although not necessarily dynamic state, but traps, interactables, loot and other dynamic elements would be line of sight."
    - Built (#354): world geometry is discovered within the largest client viewport (2400 × 1600) around a contestant, through walls and roofs; that is the minimap's fog. Loot needs line of sight: bots remember items they have seen and search against cells they have had in sight. Chargers, transit stations and the exit stay landmarks with a 4,000-unit beacon (17.2.7).
    - Not yet line of sight: bots read live door lock state when judging an item's building accessible, and bots don't consider traps at all. People's door state already follows sight (gate memory, 15).

17. Spectator vision and absent subjects (#388, 2026-10-21; F-19). Asked whether an absent followed subject should retain sight at their last position or switch to an omniscient view.
    - Let's see, the way I would think about it is that spectating either uses the vision of the player or uses omniscient vision depending on a spectating mode essentially. Support for both seeing the game as close to the player sees it as a spectator and free roam omniscient views are allowed, or maybe that becomes a policy thing in  the future, anti-cheat concerns and hte like are deferred while best practices are encouraged and maintaining optionality is desired. But essentially we need to move between player experience and omniscient spectating intelligently and I think that's a policy you can reason out.
    - Derived policy: preserve the explicitly chosen vision mode through subject absence; player vision has no current sight without its subject, while omniscient viewing remains available through V or free roam. Session defaults and authorization are owned by [24](24-networking-privacy.md); temporal camera behavior is owned by [25](25-pacing-and-rendering.md).

18. Physics design request (2026-10-22; F-14). Verbatim:
    - It's time to design and create a set of issues for a physics system, which is going to touch a lot of other systems like replay and a loot overhaul along with gladiator abilities and other new items that have physical components. For the most part I think we just need rigid body and springs for most of what I have in mind. I don't know if we should use middleware or roll our own. And there's client side prediction and other details to consider. Also players should collide with each other and slide on each other with a small amount of overlap, their cylinders thinner than their heads. Anyway, we need to get this system reasoned through. Go as far as you can without further input preparing the design and issues.
    - Proposed interpretation and assumptions, not further user answers:
      [29.1](29.1-physics-design.md) separates narrow body contact from overlapping
      head art, proposes planar bodies and springs/ropes, and preserves an opt-in
      height extension. [29.2](29.2-physics-state-networking.md) proposes versioned
      tick changes and bounded approximate prediction without hidden-state leaks.
      [41.1](41.1-physics-plan.md) records evaluation gates and provisional loot,
      reload and motor tuning. Middleware selection remains evidence-gated.
    - Answers after the P0 no-go (2026-10-22; #451, evidence in 42.6, PR #452), asked whether its gates express the requirements. Verbatim:
      1. Static penetration: "seems like some grace is fine."
      2. Maximum closing speed: "I'm trying to imagine a reason anything would go faster than a "screen" per tick. Like my hope would be that to be an extremely high upper bound. The question is, is that upper bound so high that it's not helpful? And perhaps we should ask, what can our choice of technology support and let that constrain mechanics."
      3. Springs: "the main reason I included springs is so we could have classic "jelly" like objects that can deform rigidly. In fact, one concept I'm toying with is players getting "squished" where there bodies are essentially shock absorbers across the diameter of their circles and if they deform to much they get "crushed". Super advanced detail though."
      4. Engine: "I very much like using optimized and feature rich libraries, so the only reason I would avoid going down that route is if there are unique features of our games that cannot be efficiently merged with Rapier."
    - Derived direction, for #451 to turn into 41.1 gates:
      - Rapier is the chosen direction. No game feature was found that cannot be merged with it: fog-limited prediction, state-only playback, snapshot correction, masks and doors, the hook, squish and the 2.5D layer either fit directly or need a game layer any solver would need. Retire the custom solver; Planck stays the reserve from 29.1. The main integration cost is geometry ownership: colliders come from the shape module, and navigation must agree with physics about what is passable (P8).
      - Static contact: temporary, recovering penetration is acceptable; escape through walls, doors or the mask is not.
      - Speed: a screen per tick (`MAX_VIEW` 2400×1600 units, about 32,000–48,000 u/s) is too high to constrain anything. Let the engine set the cap instead. Measured at the game's contestant radius (12), Rapier keeps two head-on bodies apart while they close by less than their combined radius per substep. It first tunnelled at 600/1000/2000 u/s each at 2/4/8 substeps, against predicted limits of 480/960/1920. For comparison, walking is 180 u/s (boost 261) and bullets 700–880. Anything faster than that cap is a hitscan check or a teleport, not a simulated body.
      - Springs serve jelly and squish, so the gate becomes stability under stiffness and believable settling, checked visually, not a 5% curve fit at one second. Evaluate Rapier's built-in joints for this. Squish is a later, advanced mechanic: a solid core plus a crush rule driven by contact pressure. Jelly and squish are tracked, deferred, in #455.
