# 52.1. Authoring tile perimeters for obstruction

Up: [52](52-map-primitives-and-library.md). These are working notes for whoever authors tiles
and set pieces, Corey or an agent. They explain how a tile's perimeter values decide what
builders can block, which the rule documents don't show on their own. The rules are owned
elsewhere: values and resolution in 52 and [51.4](51.4-resolution.md), margins in
[20.6](20.6-passable-margins.md), direction in
[17.2.11](17.2.11-navigation-structure-direction.md) and the answers in
[17.2.2](17.2.2-boundaries-and-prescriptions.md). The evidence is in
[42.34](42.34-edge-clearance.md) and [42.35](42.35-seam-pitch-probe.md). Figures are from
#688's `size-36-1` unless stated otherwise.

## The goal, in authoring terms

Corey wants players to have to go around things, and sometimes to back out of dead ends
(17.2.11). On the map, that needs **long stretches of region boundary with no guarantee**,
which builders on both sides can fill. A blocked stretch makes a detour only if the next
opening is several cells away. One-portal regions are where dead ends come from.

Density inside a region doesn't do this. Neither does a hint beside a portal, since the
portal is the way through.

## How a perimeter value becomes clear ground

The chain: stated values, then resolution, then regions and portals, then 20.6 margins,
then what a builder may fill.

- **A guaranteed stretch becomes a portal,** and every portal clears ground. 20.6 keeps
  one cell clear on each side of it and one cell past each end, on both owners' sides,
  and a third owner's too at a corner. A 3-cell portal clears 5 cells on each side, its
  strip plus two end caps, so about 10 in all. Builders may place nothing there.
- **A hint clears nothing.** It is guidance: builders may fill beside it, and nothing
  makes them.
- **The minimum portal is 2 cells** (`MIN_PORTAL_LENGTH = Math.ceil(2 * CELL_SCALE.hunterRadius)`,
  from `hunterRadius = 0.875` in `map/kernel/scale.ts`; `0.925` includes `clearanceMargin = 0.05`).
  Placement refuses a portal that is settled shorter than that.
- **One run gets one set of end caps.** One 3-cell passable stretch costs less clear
  ground than two separate ones, and leaves longer runs between them.

## Why today's tiles keep edges open

Every themed `*-ground` tile originally in `common-primitives@5` had the same perimeter on all four
sides: three hinted segments, then three passable (`hhhPPP`), aligned so that neighbours
meet half to half.

- **One hinted half leaves about one cell to fill.** The neighbouring portal's end cap
  takes the cell next to it, so a 3-cell hinted run usually has one fillable cell.
- **The opening is always close.** 94% of unguaranteed runs sit beside a guaranteed run,
  so even fully filled, the next opening is within a cell or so.
- **The builder-side ceiling is low.** If every builder filled every cell it may, the
  blocked share of unguaranteed edge would rise from 18% to about 41%, and crossings would
  stay straight (42.34).

**Direction (2026-11-07, 17.2.11 "Fewer portals per region"):** the half-and-half side stops
being the default, for fill tiles and set pieces alike. Portals are expensive, and regions
should have fewer of them. Keep whole 3-cell passable blocks where a side opens, and let
placement keep the regions connected to each other. Builders still keep each region's
portals reachable from one another (20.1). After #853 the pattern remains on 30.5% of fill-tile sides
by weight (36.2% are all `any`), on the open sides of the corner, through and edge variants,
and on every themed ground. #851 owned the change. Its experiment is in
[42.40](42.40-sparse-sides-experiment.md): set-piece borders are the lever, and the fill's
remaining portals come mostly from `any` sides, not half-and-half ones. Corey adopted
`sparse / alternate` (#895, `common-primitives@7`; see 52 "Sparse-sides adoption" and
[42.44](42.44-sparse-lighting-adoption.md)).

## The rules

Passability is matched (17.2.2, #849), the only rule since #922; a library states it as
`passabilityRules: "matched"` (51.4 owns the table). What it means for authoring:

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `passable` meets a hint where two tiles meet | placement refuses it: values match like with like |
| `any` meets anything | it takes the other side's value; `any` is the wildcard |
| `any` meets `any`, two different regions | passable, decided after aggregation |
| `any` meets `any`, one aggregated region | unstated: no internal guaranteed run, no 20.6 strips |
| unstated, between two regions inside a tile | `passable`; hint one side to close it |
| unstated, on a tile edge | `any` |
| unstated, inside one region | nothing reads it |
| `passable` authored explicitly inside one region | an internal guaranteed run with strips |

## Tips

**Pair hinted sides on purpose.** The solver must pair a fully hinted side (`hhhhhh`) with
another hinted side or with `any`, never with a passable half, so long closed seams form only
where two tiles agree.

Author variants in pairs or families that can meet each other, and weight them. Keep
the `hhhPPP` tiles eligible as a fallback. That alone doesn't guarantee a
solve: their sides must also fit what is beside them, in every orientation they allow, so
check that whole maps still place.

**Let placement keep the map connected.** The open-face rule (`open-face.ts`) refuses, during
the solve, any placement that seals off a component or leaves a portal too short, and
backtracks. A variant that would cut a region off simply isn't placed there. Don't hand-check
connectivity across combinations; check that the fallback tiles stay eligible wherever the
variants are, and that a seed batch still places in about the same number of attempts.
Matching adds seam and orientation constraints the open-face rule can only refuse late: a
set piece whose own slots can't meet (`setPieceSeamProblems`) never places, and clumps of
fully hinted tiles can cost many attempts.

**Expect fewer portals and more one-portal regions.** Thinning guarantees while keeping maps
valid put 53–66% of seam length in runs of 6 or more (against 4.9%), and raised one-portal
regions from 13 to 186–551 (42.35). Those dead ends are part of the goal, but hunters and
spawn-to-exit routes depend on the portals that remain. Check them in the measurement
before calling a variant good.

**Watch `any` tiles.** An all-`any` tile (`field`, the rubble banks) fits anything. Against
another `any` side of a different region, its whole 6-cell edge becomes a portal. Against a
hint it takes the hint. These are the most porous tiles on the map: in
`map/macro/src/chain/resolution.ts` (#895 batch rules), an `any`–`any` edge inside one
merged region clears nothing, while between regions it still opens the whole edge.

**Mark the in-tile region edges you want closed.** Under `resolution.ts`'s rules (#895),
an unstated segment between two different regions inside a tile becomes `passable`, and so
part of a portal (4,386 segments in the #851 pre-batch audit). `rubble-bank`'s rubble/open
edge, for example, would become fully passable where left half unstated unless a hint
blocks that edge.

**Use explicit internal `passable` sparingly.** Inside one region it still makes an internal
guaranteed run, with a clear strip one cell wide on each side and caps. That is the tool for
steering movement through a large merged region, and it costs fill wherever it's used
(the #895 batch removed the 16,689 internal `any`–`any` clearing segments, about 32% of
open cells cleared, from earlier libraries).

**Rotation reverses a side.** A tile allowed several orientations can read a side in the
opposite order: `hut-quarter`'s east side is `hhhPPP` at 0°, and the same side, turned to
the west at 180°, is `PPPhhh`. Under matching, a rotated
`hut-quarter` or rubble bank beside an unrotated themed tile mismatches half to half. Check
each allowed orientation against the tiles it is meant to meet.

**Set-piece edges are most themed edges.** Most themed tiles are weighted 0 and placed only
in set-piece slots. The `*-inward-*` variants already have fully hinted sides, for slots
facing the inside of a set piece. To close the outer edges of set pieces, change their
edge slots, not only the fill tiles.

**Don't rely on shortening end caps.** The caps let a hunter turn the corner at a portal.
42.35's witness found clipped caps leave a diagonal pinch narrower than the hunter, so cap
clipping is shelved. Make runs long by authoring, not by trimming margins.

**Hint strength is visible to builders.** A hint stated on both sides is `mutual`, and one
stated on one side only is `one-sided` (`hintStrength`, 51.4). A builder may prefer mutual
hints, so a seam both tiles hint is the stronger request.

**A hint is still not a wall.** `cover` and `park` now adopt perimeter reach
(`perimeterWallsIfAsked`, `reachPerimeter`, 20.10; #838, #849), while market, plant,
checkpoint, depot, block and compound still block only 0–1% of their edges (42.34).
For those builders, long hinted runs still stay walkable unless perimeter reach is
adopted alongside the authoring.

## Lessons from the experimental library (#849)

From the step-by-step experiment in [42.36](42.36-matched-library-experiment.md), with Corey
in the Map Lab:

- **Difficulty and weight multiply.** At each slot the solver tries designs in descending
  `(difficulty + offset) × random^(1/weight)`. Weight is authored; difficulty is derived from
  the prescriptions that constrain a neighbour. Corey prefers every authored weight at 1 and a
  `fillable` flag in place of weight 0, so difficulty and the offset (default 12) are the dials.
- **Rotations used to multiply a tile's draws.** Each allowed orientation was its own option
  with the tile's whole weight, so a four-rotation tile got four draws to a fixed tile's one.
  Under matching a tile's weight is now shared by its orientations (17.2.2, #850).
- **Tiles that agree with themselves cascade.** The themed grounds all state the same aligned
  `hhhPPP` sides and the highest difficulty. Let into the fill, they take most of it and bring
  the three-segment pattern back (Corey saw this in the Lab). They also give every set piece
  many portals, which makes interiors awkward: a future authoring effort (Corey, 2026-11-06).
- **Fully hinted tiles in the fill clump into sealed regions.** The `*-inward-*` tiles, hinted
  on up to four sides, score highest; in the fill they formed same-class regions with no way
  out, which the open-face rule refuses late, so placement slowed to a minute at 12 wide.
- **`any` edges open whole sides.** The rubble tiles were `any` all round, so wherever rubble
  met another region's `any`, the whole 6-cell side became a portal. Hinting the edge beside
  rubble closes rubble regions except at their authored crossings.
- **Corner tiles chain.** Two fully hinted sides at a corner with `any` elsewhere fit almost
  anywhere and continue closed runs round the corner: runs reached 24 cells.
- **A set piece's border is a fixed neighbour.** Under matching it admits only aligned or `any`
  tiles beside it, so closed-side variants thin out in the first ring around set pieces. Its
  edge slots are where to close set-piece borders.

## Checking a change

- **Structure:** `map/tools/seam-pitch-probe.mts` (42.35) and `map/tools/edge-clearance.mts`
  (42.34, 51.10) report run lengths, fillable stretches, flanking, portals and blocked share.
- **Effect:** 51.10's detour and dead-end measures, on #688's panel.
- **Landing:** follow 31's content batching rule: one adoption batch, one version bump and
  one recapture, and Corey decides adoption. Never regenerate the frozen fixture to make a
  change pass.
