# 42.80. In-process games without the mirror's applier (#1345)

Up: [42](42-performance-history.md). Design: [27.2](27.2-tick-architecture.md#test-api). Earlier points:
[42.79](42.79-core-frames-benchmark.md) (frames from the core's output, the baseline here), [42.78](42.78-tick-cutover-benchmark.md)
(the tick cutover) and [42.76](42.76-state-flip-benchmark.md) (end of the state flip).

## Paired tick and frame

An in-process game no longer runs the mirror's record applier. It keeps its clock, its phase and the players' knowledge
(memory and cues), and the receive boundary keeps presence itself. The simulation is unchanged: both characterization
checks reproduce the frozen fixture exactly. So the pair isolates the applier's share of `sim.step`. The bench steps an
in-process game, so this is the path that changed. A room still runs the applier until #1346.

| Median of five round means, ms/tick | `main` (`3fab6ba2`), runs 1 and 6 | This change, runs 2 and 5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| No traps: tick + frame | 1.867, 1.879 | 1.841, 1.865 |
| No traps: step | 1.688, 1.696 | 1.666, 1.687 |
| No traps: frame | 0.175, 0.179 | 0.169, 0.169 |

| Median of five round means, ms/tick | `main`, runs 4 and 8 | This change, runs 3 and 7 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Traps: tick + frame | 1.719, 1.728 | 1.681, 1.680 |
| Traps: step | 1.519, 1.518 | 1.491, 1.486 |
| Traps: frame | 0.196, 0.202 | 0.190, 0.190 |

The step is about 1% lower without traps and 2% lower with them. Without traps that is within the rounds' spread: tick
plus frame ranged over 1.83–1.99 ms on this side and 1.85–2.02 on `main`. With traps both of this side's pairs are
below both of `main`'s. The mirror's applier was a small part of the tick. The core's step dominates it, as 42.79 found.
Memory refresh (`sim.tick.memory`) moved from the mirror into `tick-knowledge.ts` and is unchanged, at 0.024–0.032 ms.
Both sides averaged 7.64 active contestants and 813 ground records without traps, and 7.11 and 813 with them. Those
counts are now read from each tick's frame on both sides.

Against the earlier points, tick plus frame on this map was 1.927 ms (`main` in 42.79) and 1.949 (that change),
2.886 at the cutover (42.78) and 5.831 at the end of the flip (42.76). With traps those were 1.689, 3.042 and 6.242. The
42.78 and 42.76 pairs ran different rules and bot traces, so they place this change on the series rather than measure
it.

## Method and limits

Windows, Node 24.15.0, release WASM. The baseline is `git archive 3fab6ba2`, `main` with #1348 merged. Its
`shared/core-wasm.generated.ts` was copied from this tree, since the change touches no Rust. The map cache is 42.79's
(`test-results/1344-bench-map.v8`, SHA-256 `b13d673b8b5fc101dca36b2b2d27cbc9d15c525a281a902d0b9f5b7a95c37ebe`).
Each run is five rounds of 300 warm and 600 timed ticks. Each tick takes a private frame (`snapshot`, which also queries
the bots' routes). The runs alternated: base, change, change with traps, base with traps, change, base, change with
traps, base with traps. Outside load was 7.2–10.6% of the machine, below the 25% threshold. The result is one map and
its bot trace.

Reproduce from the repository root with `node tests/bench-phase-three.mjs --cache=<cache> --root=<3fab6ba2-archive>`
and without `--root`, each again with `--traps`. [Raw rounds and contention](reports/issue-1345/benchmark.json).
