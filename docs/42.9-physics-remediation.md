## Decision proposed for review

Rapier 0.21.0 is the user-selected direction. Retire custom as a candidate, keeping
its source/tests only as the historical P0 baseline; Planck remains the reserve.
The revised numeric witnesses pass in Node and Chrome. Propose **four substeps,
40 world units/metre, and 24 units speculative contact distance**, with a bounded
integration target of **at most 27 actors, 128 awake bodies and eight tethers**.
This reduces the provisional 512-body/32-tether stress scope explicitly: clustered
512-body physics exceeds the unchanged p95 5 ms / p99 10 ms timing targets.
This is a synthetic timing envelope, not a production capacity guarantee.

P1 (#438) still needs review of these settings, revised numeric gates and its
draft old/new phase matrix in 29.2. Room integration, force/impulse speed guards,
geometry ownership, real-map correctness and whole-tick budgets remain P1/P2/P8
work. Native springs are uncapped evaluation-only; force-limited hook behavior
is not selected here. Jelly/squish remain deferred in #455.

## What changed, and why

- The user's static-contact grace becomes peak <=0.5 units and per-pair recovery
  to <=0.05 within four ticks. Zero wall/door/mask escape, actor/prop <=0.5 and
  rope excess <=1 remain required. Unrecovered static pairs fail the lab gate.
- The old 0.8-unit speculative contact distance did not prevent deep transient
  overlap even below a no-tunnelling speed. Set it to 24 units (normalized 0.6).
  This enables speculative contacts ahead of a capped pair's combined 19.2-unit
  relative translation per substep; it does not enlarge physical body shapes.
- Initial body-speed admission caps translation at
  `0.8*min(minimumRadius,12)/substepSeconds`: 384/768/1,536 u/s for r=12 or larger
  at 2/4/8 substeps. Larger bodies do not get faster. A one-percent excess is
  refused before stepping, with swept-query/explicit-teleport routing recorded.
  Small bullets at current 700–880 u/s therefore need swept queries, not small
  simulated contact bodies. This helper does not implement moving-target sweeps,
  production motor/impulse limits, or constraint-induced speed handling.
- Scale the 26 contact witnesses' geometry by 1.5 to contestant r=12 and use
  actual gladiator r=23 with legal placement. Preserve relative doorway clearance:
  the 20-unit opening becomes 30 for a 24-unit actor diameter. Add seven pair
  separation offsets, r=12/r=23 capped pairs, above-cap refusals and two stiff
  native springs. The original baseline scenarios/reports remain available.
- Use a distant synthetic wall near (51,820, 25,900), within the bounding scale
  of the largest authored live map, 51,840 × 25,920. Smaller authored maps are
  17,280 × 8,640 and 34,560 × 17,280 (14, 55; tile=6 cells × 48 units). These are
  rectangular envelopes of a diamond, not occupied-corner claims or a universal
  generator limit. Real generated collision geometry is still an integration gate.
- Replace the one-second analytic spring fit with native spring stability:
  initial stretch 15, no growth beyond 15, and extension <=0.5 from 3 through
  4 seconds, for stiffness/damping 20/4, 2,000/60 and 20,000/200. Native springs
  reject finite maxForce requests because this API does not enforce them.
  Game-owned force-limited ropes remain unchanged; no native rope claim follows.
- Fix path diagnostics to interpolate the shortest rotation arc. Rapier wrapping
  +pi to -pi previously invented a full rotation and a false box/wall crossing.
  Regression tests retain detection of a real rotating sweep through the wall.

## Correctness results

Each runtime has 117 rows: 111 simulated traces and six pre-step refusals.
The simulated set includes six invalid coincident-placement probes, which remain
failed numeric rows and are explicitly ineligible for promotion. All **111 eligible
rows** (105 simulations plus six refusals) pass. All simulated rows repeat exactly,
all 111 checkpoint continuations are exact in each runtime, and all 111 simulated
hashes match across Node/Chrome. Refusal results also repeat exactly. This is pinned
lab continuation evidence, not transform-only restoration or gameplay snapshots.

No eligible straight-path crossing is detected; static depth never reaches the

are sampled. The actor overlap bound also detects failures that the crossing
counter alone misses when endpoints overlap. Capped-pair regression tests check

At four substeps, native spring tail amplitudes from 3–4 seconds are 0.102902,
0.000009 and 0.000003 units respectively. The [recorded visual](reports/physics-451-springs.png)
shows bounded damped settling, without explosive growth. Visual QA inspected the
curves and recorded poses; this is not a human jelly/squish or hook feel test.
The uncapped stiff-spring experiment is outside the proposed capped gameplay
envelope and does not qualify constraint-driven actor motion for production.

Both runtimes pass 50 create/dispose cycles, including 128-item bursts, reused
IDs, attached-joint deletion and post-disposal guards. The tests separately cover
native joint deletion, ID reuse through snapshot restoration and exact continuation.
Prediction reruns 0/50/100/200 ms synthetic profiles, preserving their limits:
hidden contacts cause corrections (maximum position error 14.5182 units), and the
fresh projected island is not an exact authoritative world. No game networking,
rendering or privacy contract has been implemented by these probes.

## Timing and limits

171 Node and 21 Chrome physics timing cells, 18 populated query cells per runtime,
six actual legacy movement control cells, plus lifecycle/prediction were retained.
Warm-up 20 ticks, measured 100 ticks/cell; all report work and game controls ran
sequentially under the machine benchmark lock without competing tests from this
session. Node v24.15.0, Chrome 155.0.8059.85, Windows 10.0.26200, Ryzen 9 5900X.
Other machine users remain outside that scheduling guarantee.

At four substeps, the worst per-cell p95/p99 within the proposed <=27 actors,
<=128 bodies, <=8 tethers envelope is **3.4563/3.6930 ms in Node** and
**3.4000/3.5000 ms in Chrome** (Chrome uses the diagonal cells). These are observed
finite samples, not latency guarantees or whole-room tick costs.
For comparability, timing workloads retain P0's synthetic r=8 bodies and packing;
the revised correctness witnesses use game-sized actors. Production geometry,
actor sizes and larger contact islands require fresh whole-room measurements.

The large speculative distance costs more than the original P0 settings.
For 27 actors/512 awake bodies/32 tethers clustered at four substeps, the original
P0 p95/p99 was 2.7464/2.9250 ms; the new full sweep is 12.2887/12.6533 ms.
Three isolated repetitions confirm the cost:

| Repeat | 27/128/8 clustered p95 / p99 ms | 27/512/32 clustered p95 / p99 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 3.8109 / 4.1740 | 12.2347 / 13.8533 |
| 2 | 3.2495 / 3.7038 | 12.0892 / 12.8115 |
| 3 | 3.3591 / 3.6052 | 12.1507 / 12.8723 |

The earlier P0 number is a historical control, not a freshly isolated causal A/B.
Across the entire stress matrix the worst per-cell Node p95/p99 is
26.6813/28.8185 ms and Chrome 13.9001/14.2001 ms. Do not advertise the stress
envelope or silently reduce it inside a benchmark to obtain a pass. Sleeping
populations are measured separately and do not qualify awake-body capacity.
Native-spring-heavy capacity is not measured by the rope workloads.

Unchanged production game controls: server seed 4217/content-3/12×6, 3,126 ticks,
p95/p99 3.9477/6.7144 ms, zero ticks over 50 ms; client 720 frames with draw p95
1.0 ms and frame p95 0.5 ms. These controls run no production Rapier world and
cannot establish that the historical crowd slowdown is fixed, physics whole-tick
budget passes, or client prediction has been integrated.
