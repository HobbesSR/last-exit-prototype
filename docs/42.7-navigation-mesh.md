# 42.7. Free-space mesh, stage 2 (#422)

Status: measured 2026-10-22. Up: [42](42-performance-history.md). Design:
[22.6.1](22.6.1-portal-graph-and-mesh.md#bottom-level-free-space-triangulation). No production
planner change; bots are untouched.

## Result

The free-space mesh replaces the 40-unit grid as the portal graph's bottom level with no
loss. Across the same 28 maps as [42.5](42.5-portal-graph.md), every promised portal pair was
confirmed for both bodies, with doors closed and openable, and every portal's stands joined,
on the mesh and on the grid alike. Collision refused none of the mesh's paths (zero defects).
The passage witnesses of [42.1](42.1-navigation-experiment.md), which the 24- and 40-unit
grids disconnect, are connected (`tests/free-space.test.js`).

Mesh routes are any-angle: about 8% shorter than the grid's region tables on the median pair,
and spawn-to-exit routes come out within 2% of the unrestricted grid search's length rather than up to
10% over it. Building every region's mesh costs about 0.2 s per body at 12 × 6 and 1 s at
36 × 18, and 2.5 to 25 MiB. Region tables cost about the grid's with doors openable and half of
it with doors closed.

## What was measured

`node --expose-gc tools/navigation/mesh-report.mjs test-results/navigation-422.json`, under the
bench lock, nothing else running. Raw data, source hashes and hardware:
[reports/navigation-422.json](reports/navigation-422.json). AMD Ryzen 9 5900X, Node v24.15.0,
Windows/x64; revision `f83ffee`, clean (later commits on the branch only rewrap comments and
rename a parameter).

Seeds 1 to 20 at 12 × 6, 1 to 5 at 24 × 12, 1 to 3 at 36 × 18, live chain params, bundled
library. For each map, body and door state (closed, openable): build every region's mesh
(`regionMeshes`), then region tables and stand joins over the mesh (`meshReach`) and over the
grid (`gridReach`, as in 42.5), comparing the two tables pair by pair. Then three routes with
doors openable, on the mesh planner, the grid planner and the unrestricted `gridRoute`.

## Limits

- **Evidence is one-sided** (22.6, "Safety"). A confirmed pair is a path the body can travel.
  None was unconfirmed, so none needed a finer check.
- **Lengths are of real paths, not optima.** The corridor search costs crossings greedily, so
  a pair's corridor can go the long way round an obstacle: pairs up to 1.5 times the grid's
  table length remain, the grid's own tile route being shorter there. Routes still cross each
  portal at its middle stand.
- **Erosion costs exact-fit passages.** The mesh is eroded 0.2 units past the clearance, so a
  passage within 0.4 units of the body's diameter is closed, where the grid's sweep at exactly the
  clearance accepts it. No promised pair on these maps depended on one.
- **Table cost grows with portals squared.** Each pair is a funnel path and its sweeps, where
  the grid read lengths off one flood per portal; 36 × 18 tables cost about half of generation
  per body. Splitting large regions, or measuring an edge only when a planner needs it, would
  reduce it. Closed doors no longer cost extra: gates are outlines in the mesh, not swept per move.
- **One mesh per body radius.** Two bodies, two meshes per region and door state; a third
  radius would add one more. See 22.6 for why the single-mesh design was set aside.
- Mesh KiB counts typed arrays, not the JavaScript heap; the cache keeps meshes for the life of
  the map. No gameplay, tick or bot measurement: nothing consumes the mesh yet.
