# 24.2. Lossy-transport delta design (#1094)

Up: [24](24-networking-privacy.md). Encoding direction: [24.1](24.1-wire-encoding-design.md).
Evidence: [42.50](42.50-lossy-deltas.md). **Accepted and implemented** under #1095 (stage 3): the codec's
deltas, the server's delivery, the edge's datagram send, the client's apply, the event window and quantization,
with one change to decision 3 (its status note) ([24.5 "Stage 3's contracts"](24.5-transport.md#stage-3s-contracts)). Corey, on
#1094 (comment 15465, 2026-11-16), after the decision brief in comment 15447, verbatim: "I have
read the decision brief and I approve all recommendations." That covers the three decisions
below and the brief's defaults for events, gates and large whole frames. A player session with WebTransport is
sent this split; every other session keeps `state` on its WebSocket (24). No simulation, input order, recording
or fixture change is proposed.

## Recommendation

Split each player view's frame by how it changes, and give each part the delivery that suits it:
- **Reliable stream: slow layers.** Items, gates and own discovery stay on a reliable ordered stream
  (a WebTransport stream or today's WebSocket), with today's keyframe layers unchanged (24).
- **Datagrams: fast state.** Everything else goes on unreliable datagrams: the frame header,
  players, bodies, tethers, traps, projectiles, effects, events, hook flights, heard cues and own-only
  fields. Each datagram is a delta against the newest frame the client has acknowledged, the Quake
  3 / Fiedler scheme.

In 42.50 this split met every declared budget at 12x6 and 36x18:
- **Fit:** the largest steady datagram was 993 meter bytes, and none needed a whole-frame fallback.
- **Freshness:** p95 staleness fell from 199–249 ms to 99 ms on typical, and from 599 ms to 249 ms on
  poor.
- **CPU:** 21 µs per player view per tick at 36x18.
- **Memory:** 1.2–1.6 MB of history per room.

Putting the whole frame on datagrams instead fragmented 2–11% of 36x18 gladiator messages, cost
158 µs per view per tick, and needed a 40-fragment whole frame to join.

The split is the transport shape #1095 would build. Nothing in it helps over today's single
WebSocket, so it waits for #1095. #1131's exact codec comes first.

## Decisions (accepted 2026-11-16)

1. **Adopt the split as the target for #1095.** The alternatives were everything on datagrams (A,
   above) and today's reliable stream for everything (R). R keeps head-of-line stalls under loss:
   its p95 staleness is 1.5–2.5 times S's on typical, wifi, poor and bursty.
2. **Shared views stay on the reliable stream.** This covers dev, and the delayed spectators that
   are moving downstream (#1123). Per-socket baselines would turn one shared encode into one per
   distinct baseline: 16 sockets held 2–8 distinct baselines per tick.
3. **Quantization activation.** Quantize, with 24.1's candidate quanta, only datagram fields that
   client prediction does not collide with. Everything it does collide with stays exact
   until it is qualified (review 863 on PR #1137):
   - **Quantized now:** observed players' records (x, y, heading); bodies with an item (sensor loot,
     never a proxy); traps; projectiles; effects; hook flights.
   - **Exact pending qualification:** the viewer's own player record and own body, and every other
     body without an item (actors and solid props, the predictor's proxy candidates). The
     predictor builds each proxy's collision shape from its transmitted x, y and angle, so rounding
     a proxy changes the predictor's collision world even when own state is exact.
   - **Exact:** the prediction motor and all shapes. Items and gates stay unrounded on the reliable
     stream.

   This narrower activation (Sp) saved 16–17% of datagram bytes, for example 424 to 352 meter bytes
   at 36x18 contestant. Quantizing everything (Sq) saved 32%, to 287.

   The prediction witness does not qualify the wider form. It compared predicted poses and
   prediction cuts across 13 profiles:
   - **Alone:** each family moved the predicted pose by under one quantum and changed no cut.
   - **Together:** all families raised the pre-correction error by 0.00445 in the sliding-wall
     profile, over the declared 2/512.
   - **Not measured:** the predictor's own contacts were not compared. Angles and angular velocity
     were zero throughout, and no rotated solid prop was ever a proxy.

   Extending quantization to proxies and own state needs a witness that compares predictor-side
   contacts with exact delivery, with rotated solid props and moving actors as proxies, on a
   generated map.

   That witness is #1142. The alternative, waiting to activate everything at once after it, was not
   chosen: the narrower form loses nothing by going first, because its rounding never reaches the
   predictor.

   **Status (2026-11-19).** #1142's witness (42.52) did not qualify rounding own-body or proxy poses, so
   the narrower form is what is live ([24.5 "Delivery"](24.5-transport.md#stage-3s-contracts), Quantization),
   with one change from the list above: bodies with an item stay exact, since body poses are now float32
   metres and rounding them alone would cost the exact bodies more than it saves (24.5 gives the bytes).

The rest is design for the implementation, not a decision.

## Acknowledgement and baselines

- **Baselines.** Each player view has exactly one socket, so a per-socket acknowledged baseline adds
  no encodes: one diff and encode per view per tick, as today.
- **Acknowledgements.** The client acknowledges the newest complete frame it holds. The tick rides
  on every input packet: today's 20 Hz inputs, or #1096's tick-stamped inputs (options 1 and 3 are
  both being tried). The server deltas against the newest acknowledgement it has received.
- **History.** The server keeps 32 ticks (1.6 s) of transmitted frames per view, in their
  transmitted form (quantized where adopted). The oldest baseline used was 22 ticks (bursty), and
  14 on poor, so 32 leaves headroom. A client keeps at least as many received frames as the
  server's history.
- **Recovery.** A baseline outside the history, an unknown one, or none at all (join, reconnect,
  long outage) gets a whole frame, sent every tick until acknowledged. A client that cannot rebuild
  a message (a missing baseline) discards it and keeps acknowledging its newest. The server never
  assumes a datagram arrived.
- **Ordering.** Datagrams may arrive late, out of order or not at all. The client applies only a
  message newer than its newest frame, and never applies one against a baseline it does not hold.
  The 32-frame prediction history (29.2) reads the rebuilt frames, so missing frames still discard
  prediction as they do today.

## Message and coverage

One generic delta covers the whole authorized fast projection, rather than per-family mechanisms.
- **Header fields:** fields that differ, plus fields removed.
- **Keyed lists:** each list whose entries carry an id gets additions, changed fields of existing
  entries, dropped ids, and an explicit id order whenever `shared/keyed-list.ts`'s rebuild rule
  would not reproduce it.
- **Growing lists:** a list of primitives that only grew travels as its new tail.
- **Exactness:** a rebuilt frame equals the projection exactly, order and presence included. Every
  distinct message in 42.50 was checked this way, and all were exact.
- **Format:** the measured format (`tools/perf/wire-1094-delta-format.mjs`) is measurement code. Its
  production form is the selected codec's deltas, with changed-field masks (D,
  [24.5 "Stage 3's contracts"](24.5-transport.md#stage-3s-contracts)).

The delta reuses the projection allowlists (`shared/simulation/projection-contract.ts`) and the
keyed-list rebuild rule. The live wire and the `delta-1` recording remain separate contracts
(17.4 #16).

Each datagram names the reliable-layer keyframe versions it was built against, so nothing reads
across the two parts silently. Two cross-part rules follow from the evidence:
- **An item body whose item the client does not yet hold is not drawn.** It waits until the item
  arrives. This happened on up to 1.2% of sampled ticks on typical and wifi, and on up to 3.7% on poor.
  The body is already authorized, since it is projected only with an authorized item, so holding it
  discloses nothing.
- **Gates may lag fast state** by up to the reliable stream's stall: p95 50–100 ms on typical, 350–400
  ms on poor. The server already enforces actual doors against stale client memory (29.2). The
  prediction witness's stale-door profile shows the predictor running through such a door until the
  correction arrives. Moving gate state onto datagrams is the fix if this proves visible in play.

**Events** are the last six (`shared/simulation/loot.ts`). Over datagrams, an event reaches a client
only if some frame holding it arrives before six newer ones push it out. None were missed in 42.50,
but that workload has few events. Accepted: events stay in frames for at least the history window
(32 ticks) rather than moving to the reliable stream. This is implemented for the split delivery, with
de-duplication in the client's reader ([24.5 "Delivery"](24.5-transport.md#stage-3s-contracts), Events).

## Byte budget and overflow

The cap was budgeted at 1,200 bytes of datagram payload, a conservative figure: the real limit is the
path's QUIC datagram size. The edge fixed it at 1,100 bytes of message per fragment, which fits at QUIC's
minimum path MTU, so a whole message may take 4,400 bytes ([24.5 "Delivery"](24.5-transport.md#stage-3s-contracts)). Steady fast deltas used at most 993 meter bytes in
42.50, and D takes about two thirds of the meter's bytes, so no relevance priority or capped packing
is needed at these sizes. A whole fast frame is 0.66–2.4 KB:
- **Up to four fragments:** send it fragmented. The whole message is lost if any fragment is, and it
  is re-sent each tick until acknowledged.
- **Larger** (a bigger roster or map): send it on the reliable stream instead.

Fragmenting steady messages is the overflow fallback, and its rate should be counted in production.
If steady messages start to fragment (72x36, or larger rosters), the next step is priority packing
at record boundaries, which 42.49's capped probe showed D can do in one pass.

## Quantization boundary

- **Order.** Quantize after disclosure and before comparison, so a value moving within its quantum
  sends nothing.
- **Shared copies.** Frozen shared records (items and gates, #655) quantize once per record and keep
  identity, so retained history and identity comparison behave as they do exact.
- **Client side.** Prediction and presentation read the dequantized values exactly as the server
  transmitted them (24.1).
- **Unchanged.** Authority, hit rewind, inputs and recordings stay exact.
- **24's rebuilt-frame promise.** 24 says a rebuilt frame equals the projection. For adopted lossy
  fields that becomes: a rebuilt frame equals the projection *as transmitted*.
