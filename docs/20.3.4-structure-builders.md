# 20.3.4. Structure builders

Part of [20.3](20.3-builders-decomposition.md), which holds the shared builder layer, the design guidelines and what
every catalogue builder shares. Moved from 20.3 without changing its content (#510).

## `checkpoint` builder

- **The line:** from one portal's centre to another's, the farthest pair
  (the first in id order on a tie).
- **Barriers:** along the line's longer axis, starting 1 cell in plus a
  seeded offset of 0 to 2, then every 4 cells. Each is a half-cell wall of
  kind `ruin-wall`, so it blocks movement and sight. It reaches 4 cells
  either side of the line, or as far as the region goes, attaches to the
  region's edge on one side and stops 2 cells (a doorway) short of the
  other. The sides alternate along the line, from a seeded first side, so a
  body crossing slaloms. Where the region is wider than the barrier a body
  can go round it, which slows a crossing without sealing it. A station
  needs a run of at least 3 owned cells across the line, or it gets no
  barrier.
- **The post:** a roofed box 6 to 8 by 5 or 6 cells, its long side along the
  line, built as `depot`'s warehouse is (56): a roofed design with shelves
  on its floor, traced for the lab. It stands nearest the middle of the
  barriers, off the line. Its footprint must be owned and cannot occupy the
  one-cell portal margin; it may sit flush against a hinted perimeter run.
  Hinted boxes are tried before distance ordering. It keeps 2 cells clear of
  barriers. With one portal it ranks from a point 5 cells in from that portal,
  and with none from the region's middle.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the empty shape is checked once with the
  elective flood check (`validatePortalReach`, 20.1), and a region whose own
  shape fails is `open`. Swept routes connect nearby portals into a tree and
  join each portal's stands. A trial piece cannot block a threshold crossing;
  paths it cannot touch are reused, and affected paths are searched again.
  Barriers and posts are kept only while this evidence holds. Post sites are
  tried hinted first then nearest, up to 6. Failed pieces are dropped, with
  the previously accepted evidence retained. The independent final-geometry
  validator is unchanged and still checks the whole portal in tests.
- **Side-yard infill (#575):** after the barrier/post solve, untouched cells
  are tried in canonical cell order with an independent 0.35 per-cell chance
  for a one-cell `cover-piece` crate. Its whole footprint must fit the reduced
  fill, keep two cells from every committed piece, and stay outside the same
  two-cell line corridor excluded from post siting. Its footprint also needs a
  complete two-cell ring in the access mask. Together with piece spacing, this
  leaves a hunter-wide bypass around the crate, including in one-portal regions
  and side lobes outside the portal tree; a crate cannot close a narrow neck
  against the region boundary. Each candidate also passes the incremental
  hunter portal proof. Successful crates commit through the
  canvas and enter the existing loot clearance filter. Primary barriers, post
  designs and observer traces are preserved; a region with neither a post nor
  a barrier still takes its original `open` fallback. This is direct placement,
  with no delegated ownership or new public parameter. Measurements are in 42.3.
  Checkpoint and depot share the residual candidate walk, crate construction and
  canvas commit in `strategies/infill.ts`; each builder supplies its own siting
  and acceptance rules. Depot's placement is unchanged by that extraction.

## `park` builder

- **Slots:** a lattice 6 cells apart at a seeded phase. A slot's pieces stay
  in its first 4 cells, so 2 are aisle.
- **Hedges:** with chance `hedges` (default 0.2) a slot starts a hedge run,
  across or down into the next slot, 10 cells long, in a seeded row of the
  slot's band. Its two stubs are 0.4 cell thick, of kind `hedge`, which
  blocks movement and hands but not sight or shots, as `pipe` does
  (17.2.8 M34, `seeThrough` in `shared/movement.ts`). The gap is 2 cells (a
  doorway) at a seeded place that leaves at least a cell of hedge each side.
- **Clumps:** each other slot holds a clump with chance `density` (default
  0.6): 1 to 3 trees, each a circle of kind `tree` 1.5 cells across in a
  2-cell footprint, at seeded places in the slot's band that don't overlap.
  A tree blocks bodies and sight, so a clump is soft cover, not a wall.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** clumps and hedge runs keep 2 cells from
  other pieces and avoid portal approaches, and no piece touches a protected
  hunter route. Up to 32 portals, a route joins the first portal to each
  other one. Past that, `depot`'s nearby-portal tree joins them instead,
  since a route search per portal from the first costs up to 2 s on the
  largest parks. Park once used `cover`'s boundary rule there, which let a
  clump stand flush over a side portal's approach and seal it (#361, [42](42-performance-history.md)).
  Hints guide the slot phase and tree sites; they never require an obstacle.

## `hut` builder

- **The designs:** it tries a house of three spaces, then two, then one.
  If no house has a reachable doorstep, it takes the terminal empty path
  (20.7). Three spaces take a 6 × 6, 8 × 6 or 9 × 4
  box, two a 6 × 4 or 7 × 5, either way round, and one a 4 × 4.
  With automatic margins (20.6), compact single-room boxes also try
  4 by 3, 3 by 4 and 3 by 3 cells; walls and door widths stay the same.
- **The box:** each design tries its sizes, then contained boxes in a seeded
  order, with `boxesAgainstHints` candidates first. There is no extra
  two-cell extent prefilter: a house can fit flush with the region boundary
  when its footprint and doorstep fit. Candidates lie in the reduced fill,
  off every portal's clear margin (20.6), where a hunter stands straddling
  the seam. A bounded cell-level
  flood check keeps the yard's portal-connected standing blocks joined
  and requires a doorstep reachable from a portal. When the box has a
  2-cell owned ring, the yard connectivity follows by spacing; otherwise the
  flood check decides it directly.
- **The house:** it fills its box with quarter-cell walls and a roof. It has
  one outside door, a doorway wide, on the doorstep's side. The one-space
  house centres it and faces it with a window as wide. A larger house's
  spaces are allocated in the box (56 L5), at least 3 × 3 each, joined by
  interior doors, with windows where they fit. Its door and the doors
  joining every space must be placed, or the side isn't used. With one
  outside door, no route runs through the house, and every space is reached
  through it.

- **Multiple houses (#532):** before any yard handoff, repeat the same largest-design-first
  search until no house fits or eight houses stand. The first site's seeded channels stay
  unchanged; subsequent searches and house designs have separate per-house seeded
  channels and unique building/trace labels. Secondary houses no longer reuse the
  primary allocation seed. The fixed single-room design ignores that seed, so
  equal single-room boxes and door sides still repeat; multi-room allocations
  may also coincide by chance. This is independent seeding, not a uniqueness rule.
  Each additional footprint fits the canvas, keeps two cells from every earlier envelope,
  and has a complete two-cell ring in the access mask. A cumulative standing-block flood
  treats all house envelopes as blocked and preserves portal components and every earlier
  doorstep's portal access. All houses commit their actual doorstep blocks as access on
  one canvas. This deliberately conservative boundary policy may leave narrow lobes unused.
  Cover receives remnants only after this composition pass. Loot excludes all house geometry.

- **Yard handoffs (#647):** house siting stays cell-based; optional handoff
  admission uses continuous prospective-parcel cords (20.10). Keep every whole
  house envelope and its actual doorstep blocks, then probe bounded corner boxes
  and edge slabs, allowing routes to move before one cumulative dispatch.
  The old blanket perimeter/house collars and fixed route tree are retired.
  Candidate policy and refusal behavior are specified in 54.2; measurements
  and same-seed visual comparisons are in 42.3.

## `depot` builder

- **Lanes:** everything lines up on lanes 3 cells apart along one axis,
  drawn from the seed, at a seeded phase.
- **Warehouses:** `roomCells` long and three quarters of that across, at
  least 5, with quarter-cell walls and a roof. They stand at seeded places
  on the lanes until about `density` of a quarter of the ground is taken.
  **Designs (#218, 17.2.8 M34):** each box is allocated as a building
  design (56), trying in a seeded order a floor with an office and a store,
  or a floor with an office. The floor takes at least half the box and asks
  for a door on each end. The office asks for a door into the floor, a door
  out on the far end, where it can front the warehouse, and a window. The
  store asks for a door into the floor. A design is kept only if at least
  two doors lead outside, so the warehouse stays a way through, and every
  space is reached through doors. A box neither fits, such as
  `roomCells` 6, is a one-room design with a doorway-wide door centred on
  each end. Every warehouse is traced for the lab, and the trace names the
  designs passed over and why.
  Shelf islands a cell across run down the floor, keeping 2 cells from its
  walls and from each other. The interior is guidance and adds nothing to
  the portal promise (M29).
- **Container rows:** two or three 2-cell containers end to end, packed
  along each lane around the warehouses. Each place that fits one is taken
  with chance `density`, and the next starts 2 cells on, so the aisles run
  long and end in hard corners.
- **Short lane remnants (#570, `types-37`):** after warehouses and rows, the
  canvas's unaddressed components supply candidate cells on the same lane
  phase and axis. A one-cell `cover-piece` crate may occupy a remnant with
  chance `density`, keeping two cells from every placed footprint and avoiding
  the original protected routes and automatic margins. Independent per-cell
  draws leave primary placement and building traces unchanged. Feasible row
  spans skipped by their density roll are recorded as intentionally empty and
  receive no crates; density zero stays empty. Crates never occupy the two
  continuous cross-lane aisle cells. Loot uses the existing per-cell rolls,
  excluding the added geometry. `infillCrates` counts the new pieces. This is
  direct canvas placement, with no child ownership or delegation snapshot.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** pieces keep 2 cells from each other and
  stay out of portal approaches. Hinted warehouse boxes are tried first.
  Rectangular masks and concave masks use the same boundary-spacing and
  corridor rules as `cover` ([20.3.3](20.3.3-ground-and-friction-builders.md#cover-builder)), rather than a blanket edge ring.
  Routes join nearby portals in a deterministic spanning tree (#325) at any
  portal count, using Euclidean distance between hunter stands, not
  routed path length. Nearby stands can require a long detour in a concave
  mask, so this choice does not guarantee minimum total protected route length.
  The former first-portal star reserved diagonals
  throughout some large depots and rejected every warehouse footprint.
  The tree still joins every portal and protects the full approach strips,
  while leaving more contiguous space for warehouses and container rows.
  Depot once skipped the tree past 32 portals and relied on `cover`'s
  boundary rule. That let a warehouse stand flush over a portal's approach
  and seal it, once large depots had room for warehouses (#347). The bounded
  timing remains in [54.2](54.2-structures.md#depot).

### Opt-in depot comparison (#663)

`buildDepotExperiment(brief, 'fixed' | 'movable')` is a tool-only export; the
registered `buildDepot` path and public brief parameters are unchanged. Both
experimental controls share depot's seeded warehouse candidates, quota, design
selection, container lanes and deliberate density-skipped empty spans. They omit
the secondary crate pass to isolate primary composition. Subsequent sites may
diverge after an admission difference; this is not a matched-final-layout claim.

Both controls require each template box's complete two-cell exterior ring to fit
the access mask, and keep the original two-cell inter-piece spacing. This is a
stronger boundary rule than production, preserving local aisle/side-pocket bypasses
rather than replacing them with portal reach alone. This is experiment policy,
not a new shared SDK or production requirement. Actual exterior warehouse
doorstep demands remain clear; every whole-cell two-by-two standing-block centre
in those demands joins the portal witness, including a sole portal. Routing treats
the entire warehouse as blocked, so a through-building shortcut cannot substitute
for exterior access. Independent final-geometry checks also exercise doors and
contestant access into realized rooms.

The fixed control keeps production's nearby-portal route shapes. The movable
control uses 20.10's coordinator with private rectangular obstacle proxies for
pessimistic content envelopes; output retains the actual warehouse/container
geometry. Real assemblies are preflighted on a detached canvas before an atomic
routing write. New doorstep terminals rebuild a detached coordinator; ordinary
rows append atomically while reusing prior swept evidence. Empty intent has no
blocker. Refusal publishes neither geometry nor changed witness. This is direct
composition, without child ownership or handoff dispatch. Comparison tooling and
retained evidence are in 53.1 and 42.3; compatibility is in 53.

## `plant` builder

- **Slots:** a lattice 8 cells apart at a seeded phase. A slot's pieces stay
  in its first 6 cells, so 2 cells of aisle run between slots.
- **Shed:** with chance `shed` (default 0.5), a block of two by one, one by
  two or two by two slots, at the first block in a seeded order whose box
  stays owned and clear of portal approaches and protected routes. It is built as `depot`'s warehouse is: a
  roofed building design (56) with shelves on its floor, traced for the lab.
- **Machines:** every other slot holds one with chance `density` (default
  0.6): a block 2 to 4 cells a side at a seeded place in the slot, half of
  them with chamfered corners. Hints guide slot phase and box positions;
  candidates that fail the boundary or route clearance checks are left out.
- **Pipe runs:** neighbouring machines across or down are joined, with
  chance 0.7, along a row both face, when at least 3 cells part them. The
  game is 2D, so a pipe blocks movement but not sight or shots, as a window
  does (obstacle kind `pipe`, 29; 17 M34). Each run leaves one gap a door
  wide, 2 cells, at a seeded place, which both roles use. Each stub is its
  run less both machines' shapes, broken into convex pieces, so it ends on
  the machine's surface, a chamfer's included (#490). A gap only a
  contestant fits waits for the squeeze balance (P-04). Gantries wait for
  collision planes (16).
- **Why that keeps the promise:** every machine's and the shed's box keeps
  2 clear cells from other pieces, and so does every run's but for the two
  machines it joins. Portal approaches and concave-mask corridors or
  conservative boundary spacing are checked as `cover` does (20.3.3). A run
  stays in its machines' band and crosses only the aisle between them, so
  runs never meet. Machines and runs can wall ground in, but every run has
  its door-wide gap, so no ground is shut off. So a `plant` region keeps
  the portal promise exactly when its shape does.
- **Canvas:** the shed, machines and runs commit through the builder canvas
  (20.3.1), sited with its `fits` and `spaced` queries; a refusal is an
  invariant failure.
- **Residual machinery bands (#577):** after the primary solve, the shared
  `strategies/infill.ts` helper tries one-cell `cover-piece` crates in the
  canvas's unaddressed cells. Only the six-cell bands of non-shed slots whose
  original machine-density roll passed are eligible; skipped slots and the
  continuous two-cell lattice aisles stay clear. Independent per-cell channels
  20–22 use `density` for the crate chance without changing primary elements or
  shed observer traces. Each footprint keeps two cells from committed extents
  (pipe stubs use their separate areas), fits the reduced fill and existing
  route/boundary rules, and has a full two-cell ring in the access mask. The
  ring and piece spacing give a local hunter bypass even in unported side
  lobes; the pipe gaps remain clear. Density zero adds no crates and leaves
  the optional shed behavior intact. Added geometry enters the existing loot
  clearance filter; `infillCrates` counts the additions. This is direct canvas
  placement, with no delegation or new public parameter. Evidence is in 42.3.

## `compound` builder

- **The box:** sizes start at the largest contained rectangle, capped at
  24 × 24, then shrink by two cells per axis down to 10 × 10. At each size
  up to 16 hinted boxes precede a centered site and seeded alternatives.
  Small-yard alternatives are capped at 64; large fields try 12. Candidates
  must keep the yard's hunter standing blocks connected and have enough
  gate sides reachable from that yard. Gate feasibility uses the actual
  centered outer door stands, including both whole-cell neighbors of a
  half-cell center, rather than an arbitrary spot in a widened passage.
  There is no three-cell inset.
- **The ring:** roofed rooms 3 cells deep along the inside of the box, with
  quarter-cell walls. The box's edge is their back wall, so the compound's
  outer wall is the rooms'. Each side's middle, between the corners, is
  split into rooms 3 to 5 cells long, as evenly as whole cells allow, each
  with a door a doorway wide onto the court. The rooms and passages are the
  spaces of one design over the ring's cells, so two rooms share one wall,
  and each room is its own element with its own roof.
- **Dead ends:** a 3 × 3 room stands at each corner. It opens only into its
  neighbour along the north or south side, a room or a gate's passage.
- **Gates:** `gates` sides, drawn from the seed, each have a passage 2
  cells wide through the ring at a seeded place, from a door in the outer
  wall to the court. The passage widens to the corner where what's left
  would be too short for a room. A passage keeps its whole width: the walls
  it shares stand in the rooms beside it. It is open to the court along its
  length. The rest of the ring is closed.
- **The court (#532):** the ground inside the ring, at least 4 × 4. Before
  exterior delegation, try one HOUSE keep (6 × 6 with three spaces, 6 × 4 or
  4 × 6 with two, then 4 × 4 with one), then up to four cover pieces: container,
  barrier and two crates. Seeded site ordering is deterministic. A two-cell
  perimeter remains clear beside every ring door and gate opening; each new
  rectangular footprint also keeps two cells from every other footprint. These
  bypasses preserve circulation without routing through the keep. Its actual
  doorstep is protected by the canvas. The central two-by-two standing block
  stays clear. Candidates outside reduced fill are refused; small courts can
  remain empty or take only cover. This is a bounded grammar, not maximum filling.
  Keep allocation has its own observer trace; ring room/gate counters retain
  their original meaning. Loot excludes all added geometry.
- **Doors and passages:** every door is centred in its wall, and each
  passage is a doorway wide (52), so a hunter passes through them.
- **Why that keeps the promise:** the builder floods the hunter's 2 × 2
  standing blocks in the yard around each candidate. Portal approaches
  must remain connected, and each chosen gate needs a reachable doorstep.
  Its existing ring design then connects every room to the court and gates.

- **Exterior yard (#647):** after siting, register the entire ring/court box
  and actual gate approaches, then admit authored cover rectangles with movable
  yard cords. Selection is in 54.2 and recovery in 20.7. Siting and ring design
  above remain unchanged; the old blanket collar and frozen route tree are gone.
